## THE FY24 CONTRACTION - a documentable red flag
OB 31-Dec-2023 1,179.0 -> 30-Jun-2024 894.2, while Q4 FY24 revenue was 143.0 and Q1 FY25 128.26.

MTAR has never disclosed a reconciliation. This window coincides exactly with the FY24 guidance collapse.

## IS THE Rs 2,278.96 cr ORDER FROM BLOOM? - the honest answer
The company has NOT said. Anyone asserting Bloom is asserting beyond the disclosure. BUT:
(a) The filing wording "in continuation of its regular business relationship with the customer" explicitly
    rules out a NEW customer. The company has told you it is not a new customer.
(b) Segment arithmetic places it in clean energy fuel cell: that book went 1,321.9 -> 3,430.4 (+2,108.5);
    add ~252 of clean energy revenue consumed in Q1 -> implied clean energy inflow ~2,360. The 2,278.96 cr
    order alone is ~97% of that.
(c) The two May-2026 orders (2,278.96 + 467.30 = 2,746.26) are **94.9%** of the record Q1 inflow of 2,895.1.
    Only ~Rs 149 cr came from everything else, INCLUDING all of nuclear, aerospace and defence.
(d) Bloom is 55-65% of revenue (analyst estimate, not company disclosure). MTAR's own NAMED Bloom order of
    10-Sep-2025 was USD 43.87m; this one is **5.4x** that in USD.
=> This is NOT evidence of a second large fuel cell customer. On the balance of disclosed evidence it is a
   DEEPENING of existing single-customer concentration - which is exactly why the clean energy share of the
   book rose from 51.2% to 66.7%.
=> Residual possibility that must be stated: if it IS a genuinely different entity (a hyperscaler, a second
   fuel-cell OEM), that is the single most important undisclosed fact in the story and justifies a materially
   different view. The company chose confidentiality over resolving it.

## CAPITAL ALLOCATION VERDICT
MTAR has a genuine, capital-light, high-turn reinvestment outlet: Rs 500 cr of capex against a Rs 5,143 cr
backlog is capex of only ~10% of backlog. The hot box increment is cheap (Rs 50-60 cr for 8,000 units).
Working capital is releasing cash, not absorbing it (CFO 55.5 -> 101.3 -> 196.9, then 247.69 in one quarter).
BUT the reinvestment is overwhelmingly into ONE CUSTOMER'S capacity ladder, "fully synchronized with Bloom's
own capacity expansion". MTAR is not choosing among independent opportunities at a hurdle rate; it is matching
a single customer's build-out. **The capital allocation decision being made is a leveraged bet on one
customer's capex cycle, funded increasingly with debt.**
Borrowing limits raised 29-Jan-2026 to **Rs 800 cr company / Rs 900 cr including subsidiaries**, with
shareholder approval by postal ballot INCLUDING resolutions permitting **creation of charges on company assets**.
No QIP, preferential or rights issue. Funding is debt plus internal accruals.
Finance cost already inflecting: Q4 FY26 Rs 9.6 cr, **+62.7% YoY** from Rs 5.9 cr - before the programme is drawn.

## CAPACITY vs THE FY27 GUIDANCE
Required: Rs 876.0 x 1.80 = ~Rs 1,577 cr (band 1,533-1,621).
Q1 FY27 delivered 360.7; naive annualisation 1,442.8 = ~91% of the midpoint. BUT MTAR's revenue is H2-weighted
(FY26: H1 292.2, H2 583.8, a 2.0x skew), so straight-line annualisation understates. Guidance is reachable.
Clean energy: 12,000 units in place; **20,000 commissioning Oct-2026 - INSIDE FY27, in time for the H2 weighting.
That single milestone carries most of H2 FY27 revenue.** 30,000 by Mar-2027 is for FY28.
Nuclear: not in place as a dedicated facility, but nuclear is only 2.7% of revenue - low risk to FY27,
HIGH risk to FY28-FY30.
A&D: capacity not disclosed; ~Rs 104 cr of incremental revenue on a Rs 1,577 cr target is 6.6% - survivable.
Data centre: contributes nothing to FY27.
**THE BINDING CONSTRAINT IS WORKING CAPITAL, NOT MACHINES.** Growing revenue 80% at 200+ working capital days
needs roughly Rs 800-900 cr of incremental working capital funding - which is EXACTLY the size of the borrowing
limit that was raised. **The borrowing resolution was not primarily about capex.**
And above working capital sits the customer's shipment schedule. MTAR demonstrated in FY24 that it can hold
confirmed orders and still miss guidance by a third when the customer defers. The largest order in its history
has no execution schedule at all.
