### The headline findings

**1. Your hypothesis is confirmed and then some. Margin guidance is catastrophically worse than revenue guidance: 10% vs 40%.** Over ten scored margin commitments across nine quarters, management has hit exactly **one** - and that one was a single-quarter call (Q2 FY25, "over 20%", delivered 19.4%). **Every single full-year EBITDA margin commitment made in the last three years has been missed.**

**2. Capex and commissioning-date credibility (6%) is even worse than margin.** This is the most under-appreciated finding. Management has missed essentially every capex number and every plant commissioning date it has given. The oil & gas plant alone has slipped from "volume production FY26" (Aug-2024) to June-2026 to September-2026 to October-2026 - four successive commitments. The FY27 capex number went from **INR 25-30 cr** (Aug-2025) to **INR 250-300 cr** (May-2026) to **INR 500 cr** (Jul-2026) - a roughly 17x escalation in under twelve months.

**3. Working capital is the one genuinely credible area (80%)** - and it is the *only* area where management systematically **under-promises and over-delivers**. NWC guidance has been beaten in five consecutive quarters, going 229 to 267 to 260 to 172 to 59 days. This is a real, verifiable operational improvement and the CFO deserves credit for it.

**4. Revenue guidance "hits" are flattered by the ratchet.** Four of the ten revenue scores are hits, but three of those are FY26 beats against the *initial* 25% guidance - which management then raised to 30-35% mid-year and **missed**. Scored against **the final standing guidance at each year end**, management is **0 for 2**: FY25 guided INR 700 cr+ / 21% and delivered INR 676 cr / 17.9%; FY26 guided INR 900 cr+ / 21% and delivered INR 876 cr / 19.5%. Both years ended just below the promised threshold - a recurring pattern of narrow misses that a casual reader treats as "in line".

## 4. CONCALL FORENSICS

### 4.1 Language drift - the confidence arc

The tonal arc across eight calls is unusually legible, and it tracks the share of revenue coming from one customer rather than tracking delivery quality.

**Q3 FY25 (11-Feb-2025) - peak ambition, long-dated promises.** The MD lays out a three-year margin ladder unprompted:
> *"we project a 30% revenue growth in FY26 and similarly an equivalent kind of growth moving forward for the next two years as well, with a progressive improvement in EBITDA starting with 24% for next year, FY26, and... 200 basis points improvement each year moving forward as well, ending up with FY28 at 28% EBITDA percentage."*
> *"We'll be able to bounce back very comfortably with larger revenues and higher EBITDA margins."*

**Q4 FY25 (23-May-2025) - contrition, and the single most valuable exchange in the whole file.** Utkarsh Maheshwari (Equities Fund Manager, Reliance General Insurance) asked the question no one else had the standing to ask:
> **Maheshwari:** *"My question is more from the structural thought process. I mean, from every time what we have guided, we have been running somehow short of our guidance. So this time, do you think that our guidance is almost at the lowest point of the thought process and maybe we can outperform this?"*
> **MD:** *"That's exactly the idea. See, what you've said is right, but what you have to also understand is that we have done over the last three years, we have guided, but over the last one year, there were some misses in terms of guidance. But the current financial year, we have ensured that we have given a situation of the worst-case scenario where we stand in terms of various external and internal factors... So you can be rest assured that what we have guided for this financial year is what we'll be able to achieve for sure."*

And on the margin cut specifically:
> **Maheshwari:** *"Maybe margins. So, I mean, we were thinking 24% on. Now, we are guiding 21%. So could this be the bottom?"*
> **MD:** *"...We wanted to ensure that we guide in a manner that what we can definitely achieve... This is a basic minimum guidance. What we have given is a basic minimum guidance."*

**The company then missed that "basic minimum" 21% floor, delivering 19.5%.** This is the most damning single data point in the scorecard - an explicitly floor-framed, worst-case, "rest assured" commitment, missed.

**Q1 FY26 (06-Aug-2025) - hedged, and an unusually candid admission of sandbagging.** On clean energy growth:
> *"yeah, we say conservatively 15% to 20%, but we always say that. But we are expecting a lot more."*

This is management telling analysts on the record that its stated segment guidance is not its real expectation. Useful, but it makes every guidance number harder to score - and it cuts both ways, because the same management then missed the aerospace and nuclear numbers badly in the same year.

**Q2 FY26 (06-Nov-2025) - crisis management.** Q2 EBITDA collapsed to 12.5%. The tone turns defensive and combative toward tariffs:
> *"While there is a temporary dip in EBITDA in this quarter, this is a short-term phenomenon."*
> *"there has been a prolonged discussion on the tariffs with our customers during the quarter. And while doing that, it almost took three, three and a half weeks to push back the whole thing."*
> *"if we absorb the tariffs, then we don't stand a chance. So it doesn't make any sense. So that is something that the American companies have to figure it out."*
> *"we are very confident of achieving twice the sales in the second half with higher margins."*

**Q3 FY26 (30-Jan-2026) - vindication, and a shift to a capacity-constrained narrative.**
> *"as highlighted in our previous earnings call, we anticipated a stronger second half of the year, and we have delivered accordingly with phenomenal growth in Q3."*
> *"it's the other way around, right? It's not about orders, but it's about how much you can produce. That's where we are right now over the next three to five years."*

This is the pivot point. From here the story stops being "can we win orders" and becomes "we are the bottleneck" - which conveniently makes execution slippage sound like a quality problem rather than a failure.

**Q4 FY26 (13-May-2026) - escalation and invulnerability.** Guidance is raised from 50% to 80%:
> *"We are raising our guidance for FY 2027 from 50% revenue growth to 80%... plus minus 5%, with clear EBITDA margins of around 24% for the year."*
> *"We can also look ahead with confidence in spite of geopolitical crisis, as we are now well prepared and we are in much stronger position now."*
> On competitive risk: *"we are really not concerned with that because we know its learning curve is very steep in this and we have enough on our plate. **It doesn't really matter to us at all.**"*
> *"We will never face or the customer would never face any kind of bottleneck in terms of the timely deliverables."*

**Q1 FY27 (30-Jul-2026) - peak confidence, plus one revealing flash of irritation.** Asked by Jenish Karia (Union Mutual Fund) about US data-centre capex slippage risk:
> *"See, **all this is unwanted noise.** I really wanted to express this very clearly. You have seen how we have progressed as far as MTAR is concerned... Things are going in the right direction. **Absolutely, there is no issue at all.**"*

And:
> *"We are pretty confident to do better than the guidance given earlier to everyone."*

**Assessment:** confidence has risen monotonically since Q2 FY26 and is now at its highest level in the review period - but it is rising on the back of *order book* and *customer forecast*, not on the back of a delivery record. The 2025 contrition has entirely evaporated. Management is now more confident than it was in Feb-2025, when it set a margin ladder it subsequently abandoned. And the confidence is being expressed most forcefully precisely where disclosure is weakest (Bloom, capacity, data centre).

### 4.6 Customer concentration and de-risking - the commentary says down, the numbers say up

**What management has said, repeatedly, for two years:**
- 14-Aug-2024: *"All this will enable us to diversify well and **drastically reduce the customer concentration risk over the next two years for sure**."*
- 23-May-2025: *"multiple growth engines across various sectors"*
- 06-Nov-2025: *"we have diversified. We have diversified strong growth engines at every area."*
- 30-Jul-2026 (CFO): *"the company also focusing on the customer diversification strategy, increasing wallet share from the existing customers"* - note the internal tension in that single sentence: diversification *and* deeper penetration of the same customer, in the same breath.

**What actually happened.** The two years are up. Test it:
- Q4 FY26 MD guided FY27 revenue mix at *"close to about 70%"* clean energy.
- Q1 FY27 reported clean energy at INR 220.8 cr of INR 360.7 cr = **61%**.
- But the INR 100.4 cr "Products & Others" line is **substantially Bloom-linked too**. The MD confirmed on 06-Nov-2025 that ASP assemblies go to Bloom at *"almost like 125,000 units each quarter"*, that there are *"around 16"* ASPs per hot box, and that Bloom enclosures had gone from *"500 units per quarter"* to *"1,000 units per quarter"*. When Gaurav Nagori (Avendus Spark) probed exactly this on 30-Jul-2026 - *"This new product segment, I'm assuming, are the product which goes into this fuel cell assembly only, other than the hot boxes?"* - the MD's answer was evasive: *"No, not necessarily. We have the other products as well."*
- **Best estimate: true Bloom-linked revenue in Q1 FY27 is meaningfully above the 61% headline, plausibly 70-80%.**

Meanwhile the intended diversifiers all failed or slipped:
- **Fluence** (guided INR 250-400 cr) - dead, deleted from the deck.
- **Electrolyzers** - never commercialised, stopped being discussed.
- **Oil & gas / Weatherford** - guided at INR 150-180 cr in FY27 (Feb-2025), now a plant with peak revenue of **INR 40-50 cr** (May-2026), still not operational.
- **Civil nuclear** - orders eventually arrived (INR 684 cr book), but revenue is still INR 3.2 cr a quarter and execution has slipped twice.
- **Aerospace & defence** - real, but at INR 36.4 cr in Q1 FY27 it is 10% of revenue after three years of "phenomenal growth" commentary.
- **Data centre infrastructure** - genuinely new, but the first order is INR 45 cr and the qualification is unfinished.

**Conclusion: de-risking has been a narrative, not an outcome.** Two years after the MD promised concentration would fall "drastically", MTAR is more dependent on Bloom Energy than it was, is disclosing less about that dependence, and has retired every one of the alternative revenue streams it named as the diversifiers.

## 5. THREE SPECIFIC DATA-INTEGRITY FLAGS

These are not judgement calls - they are internal contradictions in the company's own disclosure.

### 5.1 The FY27 margin guidance was stated three different ways on the same day (30-Jul-2026)

- **MD, prepared remarks:** *"I would like to reiterate our confidence in achieving the guidance given earlier at 80% revenue growth for the current fiscal year with an **EBITDA margin of 22% ±100 basis points**."*
- **CFO, prepared remarks, minutes later:** *"EBITDA margins is at INR 85.1 crores in this quarter, which is actually 23.54% we achieved **as against yearly guidance of 24% we have given**."*
- **The Q1 FY27 investor presentation filed the same day:** *"FY27 revenue growth guidance of 80% with **EBITDA margin of 24% ± 100 bps**."*

Three sources, two different numbers, a 200 bps gap, on the company's single most important forward metric. **No analyst on the call caught it.** Either the MD misspoke on a headline guidance figure in prepared remarks, or the company quietly lowered FY27 margin guidance by 200 bps and its own CFO and deck did not get the memo. Given the 26% originally promised for FY27, this needs to be put to the company directly.

### 5.2 The CFO quoted identical ROCE figures for two different periods

- Q4 FY26 call (13-May-2026): *"Our ROC is at **17.2% versus 11.4%** in the last year."*
- Q1 FY27 call (30-Jul-2026): *"ROCE is at **17.2% versus 11.4%**, and we expected to reach 23% next year."*

The same pair of numbers, one quarter apart, for what should be different periods. Compounding this, the Q4 FY26 deck reportedly showed FY26 ROCE at 12.1% (down from 18.2%) while the Q1 FY27 deck showed 18.1% for Mar-26 (up from 11.4%) - directionally opposite readings of the same year. **Caveat: I read structured extracts of the decks, not the raw PDFs, so one of those deck figures may be a transposition in the extract.** The two verbatim CFO statements, however, are from full transcripts and are directly comparable.

### 5.3 Exports: "majority of our revenues" vs 18-19% in the deck

- MD, 13-May-2026: *"**Today, exports contribute to majority of our revenues**, reflecting the vision laid out way back in 2010."*
- CFO, same call: *"**more than 70% our revenue is from the exports only.** Imports are maximum 30%-35%."*
- The company's own investor presentations report **exports at 19% of 9M FY26 and 18% of FY26**, with domestic at 81-82%. (Q2 FY25 deck: domestic 74%, exports 26%.)

This is almost certainly a billing-entity artefact - Bloom hot boxes physically ship to the US but appear to be invoiced domestically - but the gap between "majority" / "more than 70%" and a reported 18% is very large, and it matters for anyone modelling FX sensitivity (the CFO explicitly leaned on rupee depreciation as a FY27 tailwind on the same call: *"more than 70% our revenue is from the exports only... Thereby we will gain on this also"*). **Verify against the raw deck and the geographic segment note in the annual report before relying on either number.**

### 5.4 Two further minor internal inconsistencies worth noting

- **Q4 FY25 (23-May-2025):** the MD said FY25 clean energy revenue was *"around INR 417 crores"* and, later on the same call, that fuel cells alone were *"around INR 450 crores plus last year"* - the sub-segment larger than its parent.
- **Q1 FY27 (30-Jul-2026):** within a single CFO answer, asset turnover on the INR 500 cr capex was given as *"minimum four to five times"* and then, two sentences later, *"at least 6x"*.
- **Working capital days are reported on two incompatible bases.** The CFO quoted **59 days** for Q1 FY27 and **172 days** for FY26 on the calls; the decks report **146 days** and **266 days** for what appear to be the same periods. Both series show improvement, but they cannot be compared to each other or to guidance without reconciling the definition. Prior guidance (200 / 220 / 235 days) was given on the call basis, which is why I scored it that way.

## 7. BOTTOM LINE FOR THE SCORECARD

**Overall guidance credibility: 26 hits out of 83 scored quantifiable claims = 31%.**

| Sub-score | Ratio |
|---|---|
| Revenue guidance | **40%** (and **0 for 2** against final standing full-year guidance) |
| EBITDA margin guidance | **10%** - one hit in ten, and that hit was a single-quarter call |
| Order inflow / order book | **19%** |
| Segment revenue | **45%** |
| Capex / capacity / commissioning dates | **6%** - the worst category |
| Working capital / balance sheet | **80%** - the only reliable area |

**What this means practically for the FY27 thesis:**

1. **Take the 80% ±5% revenue guidance seriously but not literally.** It requires ~INR 405 cr/quarter for Q2-Q4 FY27 against Q1's INR 360.7 cr - a 12% sequential step-up sustained for three quarters. The order book (INR 5,943 cr) supports it and the MD says he will beat it. But this same management has missed the *final* revenue guidance for two consecutive years by 3-4%, and both nuclear (INR 3.2 cr in Q1 vs a guided INR 150 cr+ year) and A&D (needs to double from INR 36.4 cr/quarter) are running well behind the pace the guidance implies. If clean energy alone has to carry the year, the 80% depends entirely on Bloom's delivery schedule, which the company will no longer discuss.

2. **The 24% FY27 margin is the most credible margin guidance MTAR has given in three years** - Q1 is at 23.6%, and the mechanism (operating leverage on a 28% fixed cost base) is real and arithmetically sound. But note that (a) gross margin is *falling*, so the whole gain is volume-dependent and reverses fast on any revenue disappointment, (b) this is still 200 bps below what was promised for FY27 in Feb-2025, and (c) **the MD himself said 22% on the last call.** Resolve the 22% vs 24% discrepancy with the company before modelling it.

3. **Discount every capex and commissioning date by at least two quarters.** The 6% hit rate here is not noise. The INR 500 cr capex programme with only INR 35 cr spent in Q1 is heavily back-loaded, and the phase 3 fuel cell expansion "by March 2027" sits directly on the critical path for the FY28 revenue ramp that the 80% FY27 base implies.

4. **The real risk is not the guidance - it is the disclosure regression.** MTAR has, in the last two quarters, stopped naming its largest customer, withdrawn its hot box unit capacity roadmap, deleted a guided customer (Fluence) from its deck without announcement, left three material questions unanswered per call, and published a headline margin number that its MD, its CFO and its own presentation state differently. That combination, at the exact moment single-customer dependence is at an all-time high and the equity is being re-rated on an 80% growth promise, is the thing to underwrite.

5. **Give credit where it is due.** The working capital transformation (229 to 59 days), the INR 247.7 cr quarterly operating cash flow, the near-zero net debt, the INR 5,943 cr order book, the record INR 2,895 cr single-quarter inflow and the genuine Kaiga nuclear win are all real, verified, and were delivered ahead of guidance. The FY26 H2 = 2x H1 promise was hit to two decimal places. This is not a company that cannot execute - it is a company that has never learned to forecast, and whose disclosure discipline is deteriorating as its stakes rise.
