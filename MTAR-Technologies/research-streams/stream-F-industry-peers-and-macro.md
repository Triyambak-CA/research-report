## A2. Indian civil nuclear - policy position as of Sep-2026

### A2.1 The law has passed. Both target Acts are repealed.

This is the single biggest factual update versus any pre-2026 note. **[NOT PRIMARY]** but corroborated across World Nuclear News, Enerdata, Law.asia, Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists, S&R Associates and newsonair.gov.in:

- The **Sustainable Harnessing and Advancement of Nuclear Energy for Transforming India (SHANTI) Act** was passed by Parliament and received Presidential assent on **21-Dec-2025**.
- It **repeals both** the Atomic Energy Act, 1962 **and** the Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage Act, 2010, replacing them with a single consolidated framework.
- It permits private entities to set up nuclear facilities and to carry out production, use and disposal of nuclear energy under a licence from the Central Government with AERB safety authorisation.
- Scope of private participation spans nuclear power generation, reactor supply and operation, nuclear fuel mining, and R&D across the fuel cycle.
- Civil liability has been aligned with the international liability regime.

**So the answer to "has it passed" is yes - as a single consolidated Act, not as two separate amendments.** Any draft section describing "the Atomic Energy Act amendment and the CLNDA amendment" as pending is out of date and must be rewritten.

### A2.2 But the operative rules were still in draft as of Sep-2026

- DAE notified **draft SHANTI Rules, 2026** on **17-Aug-2026**, alongside **draft SHANTI Regulations, 2026** issued by AERB.
- The consultation window closed at **20:00 on 04-Sep-2026**. Final rules were not notified as of 21-Sep-2026 on the evidence available.
- Draft framework: a **single-window composite licence** covering construction, operation and decommissioning; a screening process; an "in-principle approval" that must be secured **before** an applicant may negotiate with reactor technology suppliers or develop infrastructure.
- **Operator liability limits: Rs 100 crore for smaller facilities up to Rs 3,000 crore for large plants**, with mandatory insurance and financial security.

**This is the critical nuance for the report: the statute is live but the licensing machinery is roughly three weeks past its consultation close and not yet operative.** No private party can have been licensed yet. Everything private-nuclear is therefore still pre-revenue.

It is an order-of-magnitude indicator, not a precise ratio. Verify before publishing.**

### A5.3 Is MTAR's position in the chain shifting? Yes - and downward in margin terms

MTAR's product content with Bloom has broadened from machined parts to **power units (hot boxes), sheet metal assemblies, enclosures, ASP assemblies and cable harnesses** for SOFC and electrolyser systems **[NOT PRIMARY]**. On the Q1 FY27 call, an analyst noted MTAR was "trying to get into the enclosures and cable harness" to increase TAM with its largest client, and the MD confirmed it.

**This is revenue-accretive and margin-dilutive.** Sheet metal, enclosures and cable harnesses are lower-value-added than precision machining. The gross margin trail confirms it precisely:

| Period | MTAR gross margin (company definition) | Management's stated reason |
|---|---|---|
| FY25 | **49.4%** | - |
| FY26 | **47.7%** | "higher input and freight costs", "geopolitical uncertainties" |
| Q1 FY26 | 47.65% | - |
| **Q1 FY27** | **45.61%** | "revenue mix which has happened in this year" |

Source: Q4 FY26 call summary and Q1 FY27 transcript, CFO verbatim.

**Gross margin has fallen roughly 380 bps in two years while revenue has grown 53% on a TTM basis.** That is the signature of moving down the value chain, not up it.

**What offsets it:** operating leverage. EBITDA margin has moved **18.2% (FY25) to 19.5% (FY26) to 23.54% (Q1 FY27)** despite the gross margin decline. MTAR is converting scale into profit faster than mix is eroding it - so far.

**The structural question for the report:** MTAR is becoming a **larger, more indispensable, lower-gross-margin sub-system assembler** to one dominant OEM. The EBITDA improvement is a volume-leverage story with a finite ceiling, not a value-capture story. On TradingView's standardised gross-margin basis, MTAR at **27.55%** ranks **below every precision-engineering peer in the set except Dynamatic (25.59%), Walchandnagar (15.80%) and BHEL (13.24%)**, and far below Azad (59.23%).

## D3. CONTRADICTIONS BETWEEN OFFICIAL DATA AND MANAGEMENT COMMENTARY

This is the highest-value part of the report. Nine contradictions, ranked by materiality.

### **Contradiction 1 - HIGH MATERIALITY. Nuclear: "inflection point" versus 18 months of tender slippage and no revenue.**

**Management says:** MD, 30-Jul-2026: *"In Civil Nuclear power, the capabilities we have built over the past five decades with lot of foresight are translating into meaningful opportunities moving forward... We believe Civil Nuclear vertical has transitioned to a more consistent and sustainable growth trajectory, with substantially reduced cyclicality compared to the past."* Q4 FY26 investor communication: *"expecting significant orders in Civil Nuclear Sector in the near future."*

**The record shows:**
- **Sumant Kumar of Motilal Oswal, on the same 30-Jul-2026 call:** *"For this civil nuclear power segment, our order book execution is still not picking. When can we expect this execution is going to happen? Which year?"* MD's answer: *"Second half of this year, the execution will commence."* **So a Rs 600-684 crore nuclear order book has been generating negligible revenue.**
- **BSR tender slippage:** RFP Dec-2024, deadline 30-Sep-2025, extended to 31-Mar-2026, then being **rewritten** to align with SHANTI (Jan-2026). No award as of 21-Sep-2026. **At least 18 months of slippage on the flagship private nuclear tender.**
- **Mahi Banswara timing, from the MD himself:** *"The timeline, I cannot really say. Probably, it's a process by itself... They'll finalize the tenders and then it's a one-year process, I guess... probably these orders might fit in in the next financial year"* - i.e. **FY28, not FY27**.
- **SHANTI Rules were still in draft** with consultation closing 04-Sep-2026. No private operator can be licensed yet.
- **BSMR-200 has only in-principle approval**, with financial sanction still pending Cabinet.

**How to write it:** the *policy direction* is unambiguously positive and the Kaiga 5&6 order is real and large. But the **gap between policy announcement and MTAR cash flow is measured in years, not quarters**, and management's "inflection point" language describes order intake, not revenue. Nuclear contributed no separately-disclosed revenue in FY26 and begins executing only in H2 FY27 on management's own admission.

## D6. THE THREE THINGS I WOULD LEAD WITH

1. **MTAR is a ~3% revenue-capture supplier to a customer trading at 362x earnings, and MTAR's own gross margin is falling as it wins more content.** Company-defined gross margin went 49.4% to 47.7% to 45.61% across FY25, FY26 and Q1 FY27 while revenue grew 53% on a TTM basis. The expansion into enclosures, cable harnesses and sheet metal assemblies is revenue-accretive and margin-dilutive. On a standardised basis MTAR's 27.55% gross margin is roughly half Azad Engineering's 59.23%. **The EBITDA improvement from 18.2% to 23.54% is operating leverage, which has a ceiling, not value-chain ascent, which does not.**

2. **The nuclear story is real but the cash flow is years out, and the flagship tender has slipped at least 18 months.** SHANTI is law, the rules are still in draft, BSMR-200 lacks Cabinet sanction, the BSR tender is being rewritten with no award, Mahi Banswara orders land in FY28 on the MD's own account, and a Motilal Oswal analyst told management to its face on 30-Jul-2026 that nuclear order-book execution "is still not picking". The Kaiga 5&6 order is genuine and large. Everything beyond it is policy, not revenue.

3. **Capacity arithmetic is the binding constraint on the clean-energy thesis.** If Bloom goes to 5 GW at roughly 77,000 hot boxes, MTAR's disclosed FY28 capacity of ~30,000 units covers about 40%. Bloom says it has "no dependence on a single supplier or country", a Taiwanese second source exists, and MTAR's MD declined to give a share number or a capacity number, citing an NDA. **Capex guidance jumped from Rs 250-300 crore to Rs 500 crore in eleven weeks - that revision is currently the only observable proxy for how large the phase-3 commitment has become, and it is the number to track next quarter.**
