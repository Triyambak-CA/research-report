### 2.2 Is the counterparty Bloom Energy or a new customer? - THE CRITICAL QUESTION

**Direct answer: the company has not said, and I found no source in which it or anyone else confirms the name. It is NOT DISCLOSED.** Anyone asserting it is Bloom Energy is asserting beyond the disclosure.

However, three independent lines of evidence point strongly to it being **an existing clean-energy fuel cell customer, of which Bloom Energy is the only one of remotely this scale**:

So the honest answer to "Bloom or new customer" is: **the company has told you it is not a new customer.** The open question is only *which* existing customer.

**(b) Segment arithmetic places it in the clean energy fuel cell line** [D]:
- Clean energy fuel cell/hydel book at 31-Mar-2026: 1,321.9 (51.2% of 2,581.9)

- The only orders of scale announced in the quarter were the 2,278.96 cr (14-May-2026) and 467.30 cr (22-May-2026). The 2,278.96 cr figure alone accounts for ~97% of the implied clean energy inflow.

**(c) The quarter's total inflow reconciles almost entirely to the two May-2026 orders** [D]:

**(d) Corroborating customer-concentration context:** Bloom Energy is stated by multiple secondary sources to be **55-65% of MTAR's revenue**, with MTAR supplying an estimated **50-70% of Bloom's hot box requirement** [S - broker/analyst commentary, not company disclosure]. MTAR's own named Bloom order of 10-Sep-2025 was USD 43.87m. The 14-May-2026 order is **5.4x** that size in USD.

**Verdict:** The evidence says this is an existing international clean-energy customer and the size, currency, segment placement and "continuation of regular business relationship" language are all consistent with Bloom Energy. **But MTAR has not named it, and I flag any statement that it is definitively Bloom as an inference, not a fact.** The investment-relevant point is the opposite of the bull case: this is **not** evidence of a second large fuel cell customer. It is, on the balance of disclosed evidence, **deepening of the existing single-customer concentration**, which is precisely why the clean energy share of the order book rose from 51.2% to 66.7%.

**Residual possibility that must be stated:** if it *is* a genuinely different international entity (e.g. a hyperscaler or a second fuel cell OEM), that would be the single most important undisclosed fact in the story, and it would justify a materially different view. The company chose confidentiality over resolving it. **A named counterparty is not available.**

**1. Scale the clean energy fuel cell (hot box) business to match the customer's build-out.**
> Stated: capacity from ~8,000 units to **12,000 by end-FY26** (commissioned), to **20,000 by end-CY2026** (Phase 2, Oct-2026), to **30,000** thereafter (Phase 3, commissioning Mar-2027). Move **entire Bloom operations to an SEZ near the airport**. Anticipates **~INR 4,000 cr (INR 40 billion) of clean energy order inflows**. [P-via-S]

**Judgement: pure extension of demonstrated capability.** This is the same product, the same customer, the same process, more of it. MTAR has done 8,000 units; going to 30,000 is a scale problem, not a capability problem. Capital cost per incremental unit is low (50-60 cr for 8,000 units). **No new capability required. The risk is entirely demand-side and concentration, not competence.**

**2. Convert the civil nuclear order book into revenue, starting H2 FY27.**
> Stated: Kaiga 5 & 6 supply (504 cr, deliveries to Feb-2030); refurbishment orders (RAPS-4, MAPS-2 coolant channels, 126.74 cr on 26-Aug-2026); a **"robust pipeline for refurbishment orders"**; **"a significant opportunity from the proposed four reactors at Mahimaswada"**; a **dedicated nuclear facility** being prepared; **"exponential growth in this segment from FY27"**; execution **commencing H2 FY27**. Management had guided to ~INR 1,000 cr of nuclear inflow "in the next 3 to 6 months." [P-via-S]

**Judgement: extension of demonstrated *component* capability at an undemonstrated *volume*, plus a capability gap on programme management.** MTAR has decades of NPCIL component heritage - coolant channels, fuel handling, grid plates - so the metallurgy and QA accreditation are real and hard to replicate. **But it has never executed 679 cr of nuclear backlog.** FY26 nuclear revenue was **23.6 cr**. Going from 23.6 cr to executing a 679 cr book with deliveries to 2030 is a **28.8x step** in a regulated, inspection-gated supply chain. MTAR has **not** disclosed the hiring, QA headcount or NPCIL-qualified capacity to support it, and the dedicated facility is still "being prepared." **The ~1,000 cr inflow guidance was also missed** (630.7 cr landed). **This strand requires execution capability the company has not yet demonstrated at scale.**

**3. Double aerospace and defence revenue in FY27, with further ramp over three to four years.**
> Stated: **"expects to double revenues in the current fiscal year"**; **"volume production commenced in aerospace division with certain customers"**; further ramp-up over the **next 3-4 years**. [P-via-S]

**Judgement: extension of demonstrated capability, but the orders are not in the book.** A&D revenue FY26 was **103.8 cr**; doubling means ~208 cr. But A&D backlog grew only **5.3%** in Q1 FY27 (361.5 to 380.6) [D] while the total book doubled. The FY27 doubling must be fed by orders **not yet announced**. The capability (ISRO cryogenic engines, DRDO-linked assemblies) is genuine and long-standing. **The transition from first-article/prototype to volume production only began in FY26** - by management's own words - so there is roughly one year of volume-manufacturing evidence.

**4. Build an oil and gas vertical.**
> Stated: **INR 70 cr of the INR 100 cr FY26 capex** directed to an oil & gas facility; management expects "additional support from its nuclear, aerospace **and oil & gas** segments over the coming quarters." [P-via-S]

**Judgement: capability the company has not demonstrated, and has not disclosed hiring for.** MTAR's EOU is described as serving "fuel cell, **oil**, energy and aerospace multinationals," so there is some existing oil-sector exposure. But **there is no separately reported oil & gas revenue line, no named oil & gas customer, and no oil & gas order announcement in the 24-month window.** 70 cr of capex has gone into a vertical with **zero disclosed revenue and zero disclosed orders.** This is the least-evidenced use of capital in the FY26 programme and deserves a direct question to management.

**5. Establish a data centre infrastructure vertical.**
> Stated: first order **INR 35.56 cr on 01-Apr-2026** from an international energy-sector entity for "various products for data centre infrastructure"; MD: **"We expect additional orders from this customer upon successful delivery of these products"**; on the Q1 FY27 call, the segment is **"still in its early stages, with only initial products in progress and a meaningful ramp-up not expected until the next year."** [P-via-S]

**Judgement: requires capability that has not been described, let alone demonstrated or hired for.** MTAR has not disclosed **what the products are**. Without that, it is impossible to say whether this is an extension (precision fabrication, sheet metal, heavy enclosures - all things MTAR does at Adibatla) or genuinely new (power electronics, liquid cooling thermal engineering - things it does not do and has not disclosed hiring for). **The absence of a product description on a vertical the market is capitalising heavily is the most conspicuous disclosure gap in the growth story.** Note also the counterparty is an **"energy sector"** entity, which is more consistent with fuel-cell-adjacent hardware than with a hyperscaler relationship.

**6. Reduce customer concentration through diversification (nuclear, aerospace, BESS).**
> Stated: diversification into nuclear, aerospace and **battery energy storage systems (BESS)** [S - analyst characterisation, I could not verify a BESS order or capability disclosure from MTAR itself].

**Judgement: stated, but the disclosed data moves the other way.** Clean energy went from **51.2% to 66.7%** of the order book in one quarter. FY26 revenue was **70.3%** clean energy. **The diversification strategy is real in intent and is failing in outcome**, because the core is growing faster than the new verticals. That is a good problem, but it should not be presented as diversification. **I found no MTAR disclosure of a BESS order or BESS capability - flag as unverified.**

**7. Improve margins through operating leverage and mix.**
> Stated: FY27 EBITDA margin guidance **22-24% (+/-100 bps)**, later characterised as ~24% for the year; "sequential margin improvement in the coming quarters due to higher operating leverage and a favourable product mix." [P-via-S]

**Judgement: supported by evidence.** FY26 EBITDA margin **19.5%** [D: 171.2/876.0]; Q1 FY27 **23.54%** [P-via-S]. The improvement is already delivered in one quarter. This strand is an extension of demonstrated performance.

## 9. THE FIVE FINDINGS THAT MATTER

1. **The order book more than doubled in one quarter on two orders from unnamed international counterparties, one of which has no execution schedule.** INR 2,278.96 cr (14-May-2026) plus INR 467.30 cr (22-May-2026) equal 94.9% of the record Q1 FY27 inflow. The larger order's timeline is explicitly "to be decided at a later stage."

2. **This is concentration, not diversification.** Clean energy went from 51.2% to 66.7% of the order book in the quarter. The civil nuclear book was flat in absolute terms. The company's stated diversification strategy is being outrun by its core.

3. **The execution gap was real and has genuinely narrowed - but the test has not been taken.** Opening-book-to-next-12-months conversion ran 54-68% through FY24-FY25 and reached 89-116% by FY26. But net order inflow over the two quarters to 30-Jun-2024 was approximately **negative INR 13.5 cr** while the book shrank 24%, and FY24 revenue came in 32% below original guidance. The FY27 task is 2.7x FY25 revenue on a book whose largest component has no delivery schedule.

4. **Civil nuclear is 26.3% of the order book and 2.7% of revenue, with execution not yet started.** The nuclear inflow guidance of ~INR 1,000 cr in 3-6 months produced INR 630.7 cr. Data centre is one INR 35.56 cr order, not a reported segment, products undisclosed. **Only the Bloom fuel cell business is proven.**

5. **The mandatory capex analysis cannot be completed on public evidence I could reach, and what can be computed is unflattering.** No audited capex line was obtainable. On total assets as the denominator, incremental EBIT FY24-FY26 over incremental capital is roughly **7.5% pre-tax, below a 13-14% cost of equity** - though the denominator is contaminated by working capital and treasury, and the numerator excludes FY26 capex that does not produce until FY27. **INR 500 cr of FY27-FY28 capex is unallocated by project, funded by debt and internal accruals, against borrowing limits just raised from ~180 cr of actual debt to 800/900 cr of authority, with charges on company assets approved.** The FY26 annual report cash flow statement and PP&E note are the documents that settle this, and they were blocked.
