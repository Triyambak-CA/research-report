## 7. The Bloom Energy relationship from the other side - the critical bear input

**This section contains the most decision-relevant material in the report.**

### 7.1 What Bloom says about its supply chain - and it is not what MTAR bulls assume

**Bloom Energy Q2 2026 earnings call, 28-Jul-2026. REPORTED.** Verbatim-as-summarised positions:
- *"Bloom has built a resilient supply chain with broadly available materials by design, **multiple qualified suppliers across multiple countries for every critical input**, and **no single supplier determining their operations**."*
- *"**No single supplier and no single country determines the company's destiny**, and every part of the supply chain is prepared to scale with growth."*
- *"The demand environment is robust, and they are **neither gated by capacity nor their supply chain**."*
- *"Since the beginning of the year, they have been **continuously adding to their American manufacturing capacity in copy exact increments** and will continue to do so **ahead of committed orders**."*
- *"Their 2026 guidance is **not going to have any dependence on any single project**."*
- Sources: https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-bloom-energy-tops-q2-2026-forecasts-shares-jump-after-hours-93CH-4818294 ; https://equibles.com/stocks/be/calls/2026-q2 ; https://www.marketbeat.com/earnings/reports/2026-7-28-bloom-energy-co-stock/

**Bloom Q1 2026 call. REPORTED**: management said its **current manufacturing footprint will allow it to deliver 5 GW of product annually**. https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-bloom-energy-q1-2026-sees-earnings-surge-93CH-4643282

**This is the single most important finding in this report.**

The Indian bull narrative rests on MTAR being Bloom's **sole-source, irreplaceable** hot box supplier ("the operational linchpin", "one of the most deeply embedded supplier relationships in global clean energy manufacturing" - Bastion Research). **Bloom's own management, on the record, to its own shareholders, says the opposite about its supply chain generally: multiple qualified suppliers, multiple countries, no single supplier determines its destiny.**

Three ways to read this, and honesty requires presenting all three:
- **(a) Bloom is de-risking its narrative post-Hunterbrook** and the statement is defensive boilerplate about *critical inputs* (i.e. scandium and raw materials) rather than about *sub-assembly manufacturing partners* like MTAR. This is the most probable reading and it substantially softens the point.
- **(b) Bloom genuinely has qualified alternate hot box sources** it has not publicised, in which case MTAR's pricing power and sole-source premium are overstated.
- **(c) Both**: MTAR is sole-source *today* and Bloom is deliberately building optionality.

**I could not resolve which.** No Bloom filing, call or press release that I reached **names MTAR at all.** That asymmetry is itself the finding: **MTAR discloses Bloom as 55-70% of its revenue; Bloom does not appear to disclose MTAR anywhere.** The relationship is existential for one party and unremarkable for the other. **That is what a bear should press on.**

### 7.2 Bloom's manufacturing footprint - the re-shoring question

**REPORTED / VERIFIED (10-K and company releases):**
- **Fremont, California**: 164,000 sq ft opened Jul-2022, $200m investment, **expandable to 5 GW of production capacity**. https://www.bloomenergy.com/news/bloom-energy-inaugurates-high-volume-electrolyzer-production-line/
- **Newark, Delaware**: **owned**, 178,000 sq ft, **"designed specifically for copy-exact duplication as it expands."** Source: Bloom Energy Form 10-K FY2025, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1664703/000162828026006516/be-20251231.htm
- Capacity **doubling from 1 GW to 2 GW by end-2026**, backed by ~**$100m** facility investment.
- **Visibility on 25 GW of deployments** (stated Aug-2026). https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/18/bloom-energy-says-it-has-visibility-on-25-gigawatt/
- Brookfield partnership **expanded from $5bn to $25bn**.
- **Oracle**: up to **2.8 GW** of fuel cell systems, **1.2 GW already contracted and under deployment**, announced Apr-2026.

**The phrase to sit with is "copy exact".** Bloom is explicitly building a *replicable* US manufacturing template and adding capacity **"ahead of committed orders"**. Combined with a US policy environment that rewards domestic content, **the structural direction of travel is toward more US in-sourcing, not less.** Bloom has not announced in-sourcing of hot boxes and I found no statement that it intends to. But the architecture being described is the architecture you build if you eventually want to.

### 7.3 Has Bloom announced India manufacturing of its own?

**Negative finding - REPORTED.** I found **no announced Bloom Energy manufacturing plant in India.**
- **Bloom Energy (India) Private Limited** exists - CIN **U74999KA2004PTC049952**, Bengaluru, incorporated 2004. **UNVERIFIED (vendor data)**: revenue **₹100 cr - ₹500 cr for FY ending 31-Mar-2025**, 1-year revenue CAGR 19%, **EBITDA CAGR 310%**. https://tracxn.com/d/legal-entities/india/bloom-energy-india-private-limited/ ; https://www.zaubacorp.com/BLOOM-ENERGY-INDIA-PRIVATE-LIMITED-U74999KA2004PTC049952
- **Inference (UNVERIFIED)**: a ₹100-500 cr Indian subsidiary with 310% EBITDA CAGR is most likely an engineering/R&D and procurement centre, and is the entity through which Bloom manages Indian suppliers. **An escalating Indian EBITDA base is weakly consistent with Bloom building direct Indian capability.** This entity's filings (MCA) deserve a proper look; I could not access them.

### 7.4 Has Bloom diversified suppliers?

**REPORTED**: Bloom asserts **"multiple qualified suppliers across multiple countries for critical inputs"** and an *"ecosystem of long-term supply partners that scales with the company"*, using **"custom suppliers who have adopted the 'Bloom Way' mentality."** No specific new hot box supplier was named. **No evidence of MTAR being displaced was found.** But no evidence of exclusivity was found either.

### 7.5 What MTAR is actually scaling to

- **REPORTED**: MTAR executing a phased expansion of **hot box assemblies from 8,000 to 12,000 units annually by end of FY26**, initial capex ~**₹40 cr**. https://bastionresearch.substack.com/p/have-you-heard-of-mtar-technologies
- **REPORTED**: Q1 FY27 call - fuel cell capacity **multi-fold expansion commissioning by March 2027.**
- **Inference**: Bloom going 1 GW → 2 GW and MTAR going 8,000 → 12,000 hot boxes (a 1.5x) in the same window **do not obviously match**. Either MTAR's share of Bloom's hot box requirement is falling, or the March-2027 "multi-fold" expansion is the catch-up, or the unit-to-GW conversion is not linear. **This is a specific, answerable question for management and I have not seen it asked.**
