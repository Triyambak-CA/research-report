That is the headline, and it is the one most commentary stops at. The series says three further things that the endpoints alone hide.

Domestic institutions are selling harder than the promoters are

Domestic holding peaked at 29.96% in December 2025 and was 22.35% by June 2026: minus 7.61 points in two quarters. Promoters fell 1.24 points over the same two quarters. Public holding fell from 27.19% to 23.51%. The foreign bid of 12.24% to 24.80% across those two quarters was filled by domestic institutions first, the public second, and the promoters third. A reader told only that "promoters are selling into an FII bid" has been given the smallest of the three sellers.

The December 2024 drop was a placement to domestic institutions, not a reclassification

This report previously flagged a 5.00 point promoter fall in the December 2024 quarter with no matching block deal in the accessible record, and suggested the most likely explanation was a reclassification of promoter-group members to public. The full register rules that out. Public holding did not rise: 38.49% to 38.39%, a fall of 0.10 points. Domestic institutions rose 17.28% to 23.17%, up 5.89 points, in the same quarter. Promoter minus 5.00, foreign minus 0.80, public minus 0.10, domestic plus 5.89. It nets to zero. The stake moved to domestic institutions. It was a sale or a negotiated placement, not a relabelling, and the earlier hypothesis in this report was wrong.

The one promoter purchase is now datable and sized

Promoter holding did not fall in every quarter. In the March 2025 quarter it rose, 31.42% to 31.77%, up 0.35 points. That is the single promoter buy this report found in the dealing record and could not quantify, recorded only as "February 2025, unquantified". It is 0.35% of equity, bought at roughly one fifth of the price at which the selling of May to September 2026 was done. It is the only quarter in three years in which the promoter stake increased.

The retail froth test this report could not run, run - and it fires

The shareholder count fell steadily for seven quarters, from 3,35,068 in June 2024 to 2,09,273 in March 2026. In the June 2026 quarter it rose 23.5% to 2,58,386: 49,113 new shareholders in a single quarter. That is the quarter in which the stock went parabolic, traded 13.4 times its entire share count in one month, and was placed in trade-to-trade surveillance. Public holding fell over the same quarter, 24.59% to 23.51%. More holders owning a smaller share, arriving at the top. A sharp rise in the retail shareholder count is the textbook froth marker, and it is present here at the exact quarter the surveillance framework caught the trading pattern.

Promoter dealing, netted

Eight open-market sales by seven different promoters over 24 months, totalling 2,88,741 shares or about 0.94% of equity, indicatively ₹190-210 cr. Against exactly one promoter buy, in February 2025, unquantified, at a price roughly one fifth of where the selling later happened. Six of the eight sales, 83% of the shares, went out between May and September 2026 - at and after the all-time high, and while the stock was under Stage IV surveillance. The most recent was on 16-Sep-2026. Section 7 sets out the full netting, the inter-se gifts and the pledge trail.

Delivery percentage and index events

Any delivery percentage published for MTAR after 25-Jun-2026 is trivially 100% and carries no information, because trade-to-trade settlement makes delivery compulsory. For the period before that, low delivery is one of the recognised triggers for the surveillance framework MTAR was placed in - which is circumstantial evidence that delivery was poor during the parabolic run, though it is not confirmed.

The Midcap 150 entry is an unusual, dated, mechanical event

MTAR enters the Nifty Midcap 150 with effect from 30-Sep-2026, announced around 16-Sep-2026 as one of seven entrants. Mechanically, funds tracking the midcap index must buy while smallcap trackers must sell. The unusual part is the setting: MTAR will join a midcap index while in trade-to-trade settlement with a 5% price band and ninety-six per cent less liquidity than it had three months ago. Passive funds will be executing rebalance trades into a 100%-delivery, circuit-limited book. That is a recipe for a dislocated rebalance in either direction, and it falls on 30-Sep-2026.

MTAR is currently in the smallcap 250 and 500, the microcap 250, the total market index and a defence index. No inclusion in or candidacy for the major global index families was found.

Analyst positioning

Seven analysts cover the stock and all seven rate it Buy, giving a consensus of Strong Buy - a distribution with no dissent at 162x earnings. Motilal Oswal published a ₹7,550 target on 31-Jul-2026. JM Financial initiated with a Buy and a ₹9,375 target around 08-Sep-2026, modelling a 66% revenue compound rate and 92% profit compound rate through FY29. A separate consensus service shows an average twelve-month target of ₹7,358 across four analysts, with a high of ₹10,007 and a low of ₹3,650.

Institutional initiation is genuinely recent: IIFL from August 2025, ICICI Securities from January 2026, Avendus Spark from May 2026, with Motilal Oswal escalating to a more senior analyst. That is the discovery phase, not abandonment. Notably, the analyst who asked the most rigorous questions across three calls did not appear on the most recent one.

Read the target dispersion, not the average

The low target is 48% below the current price and the high is 42% above it. When the covering analysts' own range spans a threefold difference in value, the honest reading is that nobody on the street has a confident view either.
