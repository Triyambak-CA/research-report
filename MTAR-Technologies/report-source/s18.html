Order book, execution record and growth strategy

The order book shrank 24% in six months, and nobody has explained it

Closing book fell from 1,179.0 at 31-Dec-2023 to 894.2 at 30-Jun-2024 while the company billed 143.0 and 128.3 in those two quarters. Net order inflow across the half-year was therefore approximately negative ₹13.5 cr. That is either near-zero gross inflow, or inflow offset by cancellation, descoping or reclassification. MTAR has never published a reconciliation. The window coincides exactly with the FY24 guidance collapse below.

A blanket purchase order is an umbrella agreement against which the customer calls off quantities. It is a different asset from a scheduled contract, and the filing says so in its own words. Together with the ₹467.30 cr order of 22-May-2026, these two unnamed-counterparty orders are 94.9% of the record quarter's inflow. Only about ₹149 cr came from everything else, nuclear and defence included.

Is it Bloom Energy?

The company has not said, and no source names it. Anyone asserting Bloom is asserting beyond the disclosure. But four things are on the record. First, the filing's own wording rules out a new customer. Second, the clean energy fuel cell book rose ₹2,108.5 cr in the quarter; adding back roughly ₹252 cr of clean energy revenue billed gives implied clean energy inflow of about ₹2,360 cr, of which this one order is roughly 97%. Third, MTAR's own named Bloom order of 10-Sep-2025 was USD 43.87 mn; this is 5.4 times that in dollars. Fourth, no other international customer of remotely this scale has ever been disclosed.

The investment-relevant conclusion runs against the bull case: this is not evidence of a second large fuel cell customer. On disclosed evidence it is a deepening of the existing concentration. The residual possibility must be stated honestly, because it is the most important unknown in the story: if the counterparty genuinely is a different entity - a hyperscaler, a second fuel cell maker - that single fact would justify a materially different view. The company chose confidentiality over resolving it.

Where disclosure stops

Consideration was disclosed for every order in the last 24 months. What was withheld is who and when. Counterparty is undisclosed on ₹3,045.8 cr of the large-order flow. Execution period is undisclosed on ₹2,542.96 cr of it. And the roughly ₹800 cr of further orders management announced on the earnings call of 30-Jul-2026 has no traceable exchange filing, no counterparty and no execution period at all.

Two things are true at once, and the bull and bear cases each tell only one of them. The criticism that the order book grew while revenue lagged is empirically correct for FY24 and FY25, where conversion ran at 54% to 68%. And the improvement through FY26 is genuinely real: 116% conversion means MTAR earned ₹1,080 cr of revenue off a ₹930 cr opening book, executing new orders within the same year.

The spread is the entire investment question

Apply FY26's 89% conversion to the ₹5,143 cr book and you get revenue far above anything management has guided. Apply FY24's 54% and you get roughly ₹2,780 cr. The difference between "MTAR executes like FY26" and "MTAR executes like FY24" on this book is about ₹1,800 cr of annual revenue. Nothing in the disclosure tells you which it will be, because the largest item in the book has no delivery schedule.

The stated reason for the FY24 cut is the single most important sentence in MTAR's disclosure history: "deferment of shipment plans against confirmed orders from Clean Energy to the next fiscal year." The orders existed. The customer deferred. MTAR's revenue is not in its own control at the point of shipment, and the company has said so.

What is proven, and what is an option

Proven · clean energy fuel cell

70.3% of FY26 revenue. Delivered against a published phased contract schedule. Repeat orders documented. Capacity ladder 8,000 to 12,000 (done) to 20,000 (Oct-2026) to 30,000 (Mar-2027) hot boxes. This is proven customer concentration, not proven capability breadth.

Partially proven · aerospace and defence

11.8% of FY26 revenue, a real business. But management's own words are that "volume production commenced" only in FY26, so there is about one year of volume-manufacturing evidence. The FY27 doubling must come from orders not yet in the book, which grew only 5.3% in Q1.

Option · civil nuclear

26.3% of the March book against 2.7% of revenue, a 28.8x book-to-revenue ratio. Execution had not begun at the Q1 FY27 call; the dedicated facility is still "being prepared". The nuclear inflow guidance was missed. No SMR or Bharat Small Reactor contract exists anywhere in the record; today's nuclear revenue is conventional heavy-water reactor supply and refurbishment.

Speculation · data centre

One order of ₹35.56 cr, 0.7% of the book, from an unnamed energy-sector customer, with the products not even described in the filing. Not a reported segment. Management: "still in its early stages... meaningful ramp-up not expected until the next year." The genuine data centre linkage is indirect and runs through Bloom.

MTAR is an order book told entirely in rupees

Hot boxes actually shipped per year: not disclosed, in any year. Capacity utilisation: not disclosed, any facility, any year. Nuclear and aerospace unit counts: not disclosed. There is no operating-count bridge from capacity to shipments to revenue, which means utilisation cannot be verified or even derived from public disclosure. You have the denominator (12,000 units) and no numerator.

Is the capacity there for 80%?

FY27 at guidance needs about ₹1,577 cr. Q1 delivered ₹360.7 cr; naive annualisation gives ₹1,443 cr, but MTAR's revenue is heavily second-half weighted (FY26 was ₹292 cr in H1 against ₹584 cr in H2), so the guidance is reachable. Physically, the clean energy line has 12,000 units in place and Phase 2 at 20,000 commissioning in October 2026, inside the year and in time for the weighting. That single milestone carries most of second-half FY27 revenue. Nuclear is not needed for FY27 at 2.7% of revenue, but it is needed for FY28 onward and its facility is not yet built.

The binding constraint is working capital, not machines

Growing revenue 80% on a working-capital cycle management itself targets at 200 to 220 days requires roughly ₹800-900 cr of incremental working-capital funding. That is exactly the size of the borrowing limit the board raised on 29-Jan-2026: ₹800 cr for the company and ₹900 cr including subsidiaries, approved by postal ballot together with resolutions permitting the creation of charges on company assets. The borrowing resolution was not primarily about capex.
