Section 19

Data-aggregator cross-check

This edition has what the first one could not get: the paid aggregator panel, and the filings to test it against. The panel earned its place. It also got three of its four product labels wrong, which is the more useful lesson.

Method disclosure - what changed between the two editions

The 21-Sep-2026 edition of this report was researched from an environment whose network policy blocked screener.in, the exchange sites, the rating agency and the company's own pages. Every series in this section was reconstructed by hand from transcripts and differenced quarterly figures, and the section said so.

On 22-Sep-2026 the premium aggregator panel was pulled from a logged-in session, the aggregator's public company page was fetched directly, and the primary documents were downloaded and read: the FY2026 and FY2025 annual reports, the Q1 FY27 investor presentation and earnings call transcript, and the ICRA rating rationales of 07-Jul-2026 and 13-Oct-2025.

Nothing from an aggregator is presented here as a primary source. Every aggregator figure is labelled and dated as such. Where an aggregator and a filing disagree, the filing wins and the disagreement is reported as a finding rather than smoothed. Price and market data remain as at 21-Sep-2026, the first edition's date, so that the valuation work in Section 14 is not silently re-anchored.

The two order-book numbers in circulation are eleven weeks apart, and both are right

A reader comparing this report against a screening tool will find ₹5,143 cr here and ₹2,581.9 cr there, and conclude one of them is wrong. Neither is. ₹2,581.9 cr is the closing book at 31-Mar-2026, the FY26 year end, which is the latest annual figure an aggregator carries. ₹5,143.3 cr is the closing book at 30-Jun-2026, the latest reported quarter. The difference is one quarter, in which the company took in ₹2,895.1 cr of orders and billed ₹360.7 cr. ICRA, writing on 07-Jul-2026, gives both halves: an order book of ₹2,582 cr at 31-Mar-2026, and "in May 2026, MTL received additional orders worth about ₹2,279 crore and ₹467 crore from its international customer(s)".

Taken at face value, those labels put civil nuclear at 70% of FY26 revenue in a company whose civil nuclear line billed ₹3.2 cr in the most recent quarter. Four independent tests establish the true mapping:

  • Each row's last four values match the company's own sector charts in the Q1 FY27 investor presentation, FY23 through FY26, exactly.
  • The four rows sum to 876.90 against FY26 revenue of 876.21, and to 676.00 against FY25's 676.0. Both tie, which also fixes the dating: the rows are right-anchored at FY2026, running FY2019 to FY2026.
  • ICRA, 07-Jul-2026: clean energy "including civil nuclear power and fuel cells" is "nearly 73% of total revenue in FY2026". Fuel cells 615.4 plus civil nuclear 23.6 is 639.0, which is 72.9%.
  • ICRA, same document: more than 70% of FY26 revenue came from Bloom Energy. Fuel cells alone are 615.4, which is 70.2%.

Whether the rotation happened in the panel or in the handling of its output cannot be determined from here, and this report does not assert which. The operative point is unaffected: a machine-extracted operating series must have every label mapped back to the company's own disclosure before any of it is used. The values in this panel were worth having. The labels would have inverted the entire business description.

A correction to this report, not to the aggregator

The 21-Sep edition of this section reported that the aggregator implied FY26 operating cash flow of ₹169.0 cr against the company's ₹196.9 cr, and concluded that "any screen ranking MTAR on aggregator free cash flow is working with a number that is roughly 14% light on the operating line". That was too broad. The ₹169.01 cr figure belongs to one market-data terminal. The screening aggregator's cash-flow table gives ₹197 cr, and the audited statement gives ₹196.90 cr. The two agree; the terminal is the outlier. Free cash flow follows: ₹63.30 cr for FY26, not the ₹35.4 cr printed before, which inherited the wrong operating line.

This also clears a trap worth naming, because it catches reports written soon after results season: aggregators routinely ingest the annual cash-flow statement months after the profit and loss, leaving a stale prior-year figure sitting under a current-year label. Tested here, and it passed - the cash-flow block is current to FY26.

The reconciler is one line on the balance sheet. Contract liabilities - advances from customers - went from ₹43.34 cr to ₹252.65 cr, up ₹209 cr or 482% in a single year. Every measure built on net working capital nets that money off and therefore improves. The cash conversion cycle, which counts only receivables, inventory and payables, does not see it at all, and therefore worsens: the components moved the wrong way, debtor days 113 to 140 and inventory days 369 to 399.

So the working capital improvement, the fourth-quarter cash flow surge and the record order book are not three facts. They are one fact seen three ways. Customers paid in advance against the new book. That money is a liability, it is spent on the inventory needed to execute, and it reverses as the work is delivered. A screen showing "working capital days improved from 168 to 69" is showing a customer-funded balance sheet, not an operational one.

Where a mechanical screen still gets MTAR wrong
  • Order book read as revenue visibility. Screens treat a book-to-sales ratio near six as quality. Forty-four percent of this book is one blanket purchase order with no disclosed execution timeline and no named counterparty, which is a different asset class from a scheduled contract.
  • Working capital read from whichever ratio the screen happens to carry. Two ratios on one page point in opposite directions for the same year.
  • Free cash flow read as a turning point. FY26's ₹63.3 cr is genuine and is the first meaningful positive year, but the aggregator's own FY20-FY26 series sums to minus ₹161 cr, and this report's FY22-FY26 figure on a stricter capex basis is minus ₹280 cr. One year does not reverse that, and the year that did it was funded by customer advances.
  • Return on capital compared across a peer set. Three bases, 11.1% to 17.2%, for one company in one year.
  • Liquidity filters will not catch the surveillance status. Ninety-day average volume of 873,885 shares looks normal; the ten-day average of 114,732 does not. The stock is in trade-to-trade settlement and a screen using the longer window will not see it.
  • Credit quality read from the rating alone. ICRA upgraded to A+ on 07-Jul-2026. In the same document it replaced a downgrade trigger the company had just breached with a different metric. Section 7 sets that out.
