Section 19

Data-aggregator cross-check

Method disclosure

The premium screener panel normally used for this section was not reachable from this research environment: the network policy blocked direct access to screener.in, the BSE and NSE sites, trendlyne and the company's own investor-relations pages. Every operating and financial series below was therefore reconstructed from an exchange market-data feed, from company disclosures surfaced through search, and by differencing the company's own quarterly and annual figures. Nothing here is presented as a primary filing. Where the reconstruction and a company-disclosed figure disagree, the disagreement is reported rather than smoothed.

Cross-check two: reconstructed quarterly series against the annual accounts

Summing the four reconstructed FY26 quarters gives revenue of ₹876.20 cr against the reported annual ₹876.21 cr, and profit after tax of ₹94.03 cr against a reported ₹94.03 cr. Both tie. The EBITDA sum is ₹171.21 cr against an annual ₹173.12 cr, a ₹1.9 cr gap consistent with the treatment of other income at the annual level. The quarterly series is therefore safe to use.

Cross-check three: the segment series and the restatement hunt

Laying the order-book segment splits from two consecutive presentations side by side is where a silent restatement would show. Between 31-Mar-2026 and 30-Jun-2026 the segment labels themselves changed shape: the March presentation reported four buckets, the June presentation reordered them and moved Products and Others above Aerospace and Defence. The labels were not restated, but the reordering makes a casual quarter-on-quarter read misleading, because the reader's eye follows position rather than name. Differenced properly, the picture inverts the headline: see Section 18.

Where a mechanical screen gets MTAR wrong
  • Trailing P/E understates the problem. At 162x it already looks extreme, but the trailing base includes the two collapsed quarters of FY26. A screen using forward earnings shows 108x and flatters the stock relative to its own reality.
  • Order book is read as revenue visibility. Screens treat a 5.9x book-to-sales ratio as quality. Forty-four percent of that book is a blanket purchase order with no disclosed execution timeline, which is a different asset class from a scheduled contract.
  • Return on capital employed is flattered by averaging. The 17.2% figure reported for FY26 does not reconcile to a simple year-end calculation: earnings before interest and tax of roughly ₹133 cr on capital employed of roughly ₹1,199 cr is about 11.1%. The gap is the averaging convention, and it matters because the capital base nearly doubled during the year.
  • Debt-to-equity looks tame. Gross debt of ₹376.6 cr against net worth of roughly ₹822 cr reads as 0.46x. That ignores that debt grew 112% in one year to fund working capital, and that the working-capital cycle is about 248 days.
  • Free cash flow screens will show a positive FY26. True for one year. The eight-year cumulative figure is negative ₹251 cr.
  • Liquidity filters will not catch the surveillance status. Ninety-day average volume of 873,885 shares looks normal; the ten-day average of 114,732 shares does not. The stock is in a trade-to-trade segment and a mechanical liquidity screen using the longer window will not see it.
