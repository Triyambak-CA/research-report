Against final standing guidance, management is nought for two

Four of the ten revenue scores are hits, but three of those are FY26 beats against the initial 25% guidance, which management then raised to 30-35% mid-year and missed. Scored against the guidance actually standing at each year end: FY25 was guided at "₹700 crores plus" with a 21% margin and delivered ₹676 cr at 17.9%; FY26 was guided at "₹900 crores plus" with a 21% margin and delivered ₹876 cr at 19.5%. Both years ended just below the promised threshold. A casual reader records that as "in line".

The pattern is legible. A high multi-year ladder is set when the story is being sold - in February 2025 the MD volunteered 24% for FY26, 26% for FY27 and 28% for FY28, unprompted. Three months later, once the year had actually started, the FY26 number was cut 300 basis points to 21%. Then even the cut number was missed. Every full-year EBITDA margin commitment made in the last three years has been missed. The structural break happened in FY24, when margin fell from 27.1% to 19.8%, and it has been guided away every year since without ever being repaired.

The single most damning exchange in the file

On the call of 23-May-2025, Utkarsh Maheshwari of Reliance General Insurance asked the question nobody else had the standing to ask: "from every time what we have guided, we have been running somehow short of our guidance. So this time, do you think that our guidance is almost at the lowest point?"

The MD's answer: "That's exactly the idea... we have given a situation of the worst-case scenario... So you can be rest assured that what we have guided for this financial year is what we'll be able to achieve for sure." And on the margin specifically: "This is a basic minimum guidance."

The company then missed that explicitly floor-framed, worst-case, rest-assured 21% commitment, delivering 19.5%.

On the last two calls management never once said the name. In the Q1 FY27 transcript the word appears exactly once in the entire document, and an analyst said it. Management uses "the customer", which appears nineteen times. This coincides exactly with three other things: the withdrawal of the hot box capacity roadmap, the non-disclosure agreement becoming the standard answer, and Bloom-linked revenue reaching its highest ever share. Disclosure on the single most important customer fell to zero at precisely the moment dependence on it peaked.

The capacity roadmap was withdrawn

In the Q2 FY26 and Q3 FY26 calls MTAR gave precise hot box unit capacity with dates: 8,000, then 12,000, then 16,000, then 20,000, then 30,000. From Q4 FY26 those numbers were withdrawn and replaced with the word "multifold", citing a confidentiality agreement. Four different firms - Systematix, Lucky Investments, Avendus Spark and Axis Max Life - have asked since, and the answer has been some version of "I can't specify the numbers because of the NDA being signed." An investor can no longer model the company's single largest revenue driver.

Deflection markers - counting "NDA", "can't quantify", "too premature", "let's see how it goes" - have roughly quintupled, from 2 on the Q3 FY25 call to 11 or 12 on each of the last four.

Failed guidance is not withdrawn here. The row is deleted.
  • Fluence, the battery storage customer, was guided at ₹250 cr plus for FY27 in February 2025, then ₹200-400 cr over two to three years, and was asked about on seven consecutive calls. It was then removed from the investor presentation with no announcement. Management explained it only because an analyst noticed the omission: "I see that customer has been dropped from the customer list." The MD's reply: "We have dropped it because we have enough on our plate right now."
  • Electrolysers were discussed on essentially every call for two years, always "a little more time", and then simply stopped being mentioned.
  • The SSLV programme suspension was disclosed only when an analyst asked: "we put a pause to it because we have so much on hand right now."
  • The FY28 28% margin target has never been formally withdrawn. Asked directly about it on 30-Jan-2026 by Motilal Oswal, the MD answered about FY26 and FY27 and never addressed FY28.
  • A nuclear production-linked incentive scheme "valued at ₹18,000-20,000 crore in the upcoming Union Budget", flagged on 30-Jan-2026, has never been mentioned again.

Anyone following this company must compare the investor decks quarter on quarter, because the call will not tell you what was removed.

Who answers

The promoter MD answers between 74% and 87% of everything on every call. There is no professional chief executive; Parvat Srinivas Reddy holds both roles. The CFO is brought in almost exclusively for working capital, debt, tax and cash flow, and is usually invited in by name. The hardest questions therefore go to the promoter and get the softest answers: the confidentiality deflections, the market-share non-answer, the FY28 margin dodge and the "unwanted noise" dismissal are all his. CFO airtime has risen from 12% to 25%, which coincides exactly with working capital becoming the one genuine good-news story - the single area where a professional answered and delivered.

Coverage is improving faster than disclosure

Named questioners per call ran at four to nine through FY25, peaked at fourteen on the Q3 FY26 call, and sits at nine now. The composition matters more than the count. The FY25 calls were dominated by boutiques and small portfolio managers. Institutional initiation is recent and visible: IIFL from August 2025, ICICI Securities from January 2026, Avendus Spark from May 2026, with Motilal Oswal escalating to a more senior analyst. On the buy side, TRUST Mutual Fund's chief investment officer appeared personally, alongside Union Mutual Fund, Axis Max Life and Lucky Investments. The questions have got materially sharper - Avendus probing whether the "products" line is really just more Bloom exposure, Axis Max Life doing bottom-up capacity arithmetic from Bloom's own 5 GW plan. The market is starting to ask the right questions. Management is not yet answering them.

The tone arc, and one quarter that revealed everything

Confidence has risen monotonically since the Q2 FY26 low and is now at its highest in the review period - but it is rising on order book and customer forecast, not on a delivery record. The 2025 contrition has entirely evaporated.

The revealing quarter is Q2 FY26, when EBITDA margin collapsed to 12.5%. The cause, in the MD's own words, was a standoff with the largest customer over US tariffs: "it almost took three, three and a half weeks to push back the whole thing" and "if we absorb the tariffs, then we don't stand a chance... that is something that the American companies have to figure it out." This is the only quarter in which MTAR has publicly pushed back on its largest customer, and it cost a quarter of earnings.

The sentence that named the size of the prize

On the same call, explaining why the customer had room to absorb tariffs, the MD said: "our operating margins are at 40%. So 60%, there's a lot of scope for Bloom to reduce those costs as much as possible." He disclosed his own gross margin on a public call and, in the same breath, told his customer's procurement team how much room there was to take. Pricing pressure is not a theoretical risk in this relationship; it has already cost one quarter's earnings once.

By Q4 FY26 the posture had hardened into invulnerability. Asked by IIFL whether the customer might qualify additional vendors, the MD replied: "we are really not concerned with that... It doesn't really matter to us at all." And on the most recent call, asked about the risk of US data-centre capital expenditure slipping, his answer was: "See, all this is unwanted noise. I really wanted to express this very clearly... Absolutely, there is no issue at all."

Three contradictions inside the company's own disclosure

One: the FY27 margin was stated three ways on the same day

On 30-Jul-2026 the MD's prepared remarks said "an EBITDA margin of 22% plus or minus 100 basis points". Minutes later the CFO's prepared remarks said "as against yearly guidance of 24% we have given". The investor presentation filed that same day said "24% plus or minus 100 bps". Two hundred basis points apart, on the company's single most important forward metric, and no analyst on the call caught it.

Two: the same return on capital, quoted for two different periods

On 13-May-2026 the CFO said "our ROC is at 17.2% versus 11.4% in the last year". On 30-Jul-2026 he said "ROCE is at 17.2% versus 11.4%, and we expected to reach 23% next year". The identical pair of numbers, one quarter apart, for what should be different periods.

Three: exports are either 18% of revenue or more than 70%

On 13-May-2026 the MD said "today, exports contribute to majority of our revenues" and the CFO said "more than 70% our revenue is from the exports only". The company's own presentations report exports at 19% of nine-month FY26 and 18% of FY26. This is most likely a billing-entity artefact, with hot boxes physically shipping to the United States but being invoiced domestically. It matters because the CFO leaned on rupee depreciation as an FY27 margin tailwind on the same call. Anyone modelling currency sensitivity needs to resolve which number is the economic one.

Two smaller inconsistencies are worth recording because they speak to review discipline. On 23-May-2025 the MD said FY25 clean energy revenue was "around ₹417 crores" and, later on the same call, that fuel cells alone were "around ₹450 crores plus" - the sub-segment larger than its parent. And within a single CFO answer on 30-Jul-2026, asset turnover on the ₹500 cr capex was given as "minimum four to five times" and then, two sentences later, "at least 6x".

Where management genuinely delivers

Working capital is the one area of systematic under-promising

Net working capital days have gone 229, 267, 260, 172, then 59 on the company's call basis. Inventory days fell from 208 to 145 and receivable days from 140 to 82. Operating cash flow was ₹247.69 cr in the June 2026 quarter alone. Gross debt of ₹423.6 cr sits against ₹379 cr of investments, so net debt is roughly ₹45 cr - the company is, on its own account, close to debt free.

The CFO's explanation is specific and checkable: renegotiated commercial and credit terms, faster delivery to customer premises, daily and weekly monitoring of each working-capital element, and a goods and services tax refund programme targeting about ₹70 cr a year. "Everything is done organically." Guidance in this category has been beaten five quarters running, the only such record in the file.

Two cautions. A meaningful part of the 59-day figure is customer advances on the new blanket orders. Contract liabilities rose ₹209.3 cr in FY26, and holding them flat would have made FY26 net working capital 248 days rather than 161. That is a real cash benefit but not necessarily a durable run rate - management's own year-end target is below 100 days, not 59. And the company reports working capital on two incompatible bases: the calls quote 59 and 172 days where the decks show 146 and 266 for what appear to be the same periods. Both series improve; neither can be compared to the other without the definition.

The margin mechanism, and why it reverses fast

In Q1 FY27 gross margin fell 204 basis points, from 47.65% to 45.61%. EBITDA margin rose 541 basis points, from 18.13% to 23.54%. The entire margin expansion is operating leverage on a fixed cost base the CFO puts at about 28% of revenue. Nothing in it comes from pricing or mix, both of which moved against the company.

That is arithmetically sound while revenue is growing 130%. It is also the reason the 24% target is fragile: if growth decelerates, the leverage runs backwards at the same speed, from a gross margin that is already declining. No analyst on any of the nine calls has stress-tested this.
