Valuation

The correct order of operations is to establish what the price already assumes before forecasting anything. On central assumptions, ₹7,040 requires MTAR to compound revenue at roughly 59% a year for ten years. That is the whole valuation discussion; everything else is detail.

Why the primary multiple here is EV/sales, not P/E

For a business whose earnings base has swung from ₹4.25 cr to ₹50.23 cr in three quarters, trailing P/E measures the base, not the business. EV/sales is the honest anchor because revenue is the variable actually inflecting and because margin is the contested assumption. At 20.1x trailing sales and 13.9x the company's own guided FY27 revenue, MTAR is priced above almost every listed Indian capital-goods or defence comparable on a sales basis - and it earns a lower margin than most of them.

One nuance the bear case must concede: MTAR has never been cheap. Even at the April-2025 panic low of ₹1,155.60 it traded at 67x trailing earnings. A de-rating to 45-50x is not unprecedented - it happened in FY23 - but it has happened exactly once in five years.

Reverse DCF: what ₹7,040 already assumes

Method: ten-year explicit free cash flow to the firm, tax at 25.17%, terminal growth 5%, terminal return on capital set one point above the cost of capital. The model is solved for the flat ten-year revenue growth rate that makes the discounted value equal today's enterprise value of ₹21,699 cr. Two variables are left open because they are the genuine uncertainties: the sustained EBIT margin, and the sales-to-capital ratio, which is how many rupees of revenue each rupee of new capital buys.

The number that breaks the grid

MTAR's own realised capital efficiency is nowhere near the right-hand columns. FY26 revenue of ₹876 cr sits on capital employed of roughly ₹1,199 cr, a sales-to-capital ratio of 0.73x. On an incremental basis it is worse: between FY23 and FY26 revenue rose ₹302 cr while capital employed rose roughly ₹499 cr, an incremental sales-to-capital of about 0.61x. Every rupee of new sales has needed roughly ₹1.6 of capital.

The realistic column is therefore 1.0x or worse, not 2.0x or 3.0x. Read at 1.0x and a 21% EBIT margin, the current price requires a 64% revenue CAGR for ten consecutive years. That takes FY36 revenue past ₹80,000 cr - more than double what Hindustan Aeronautics earns today.

Stated plainly: there is no corner of this grid that a careful analyst can defend. The most generous combination available - a 28% EBIT margin the company has never delivered, capital efficiency four times better than it has ever achieved, and a cost of capital below any defensible estimate - still demands 40% compounding for a decade.

Read the band honestly

At ₹7,040 the stock trades at 80% of the way from the bear case to the bull case. The market is not pricing the base case; it is pricing close to the bull case. Probability-weighted, the band centres well below the current price - and every one of the three scenarios still embeds an exit multiple above the Indian capital-goods median of roughly 34x. None of these is a pessimistic multiple.

Sell-side consensus, for contrast

Seven analysts cover the stock and all seven rate it Buy, giving a consensus of Strong Buy. Motilal Oswal published a target of ₹7,550 on 31-Jul-2026. A separate consensus service shows an average twelve-month target of ₹7,358 across four analysts, with a high of ₹10,007 and a low of ₹3,650. The dispersion is the signal, not the average: the low target is 48% below the current price and the high is 42% above it. When the analyst community's own range spans a three-fold difference in value, nobody on the street has a confident view either.
