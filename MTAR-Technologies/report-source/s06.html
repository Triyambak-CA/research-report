Section 06

Earnings quality and forensic checks

There is no fraud signature here. There is no aggressive tax, no dilution, no exceptional credits, no accounting policy churn, and three consecutive clean audit opinions. What there is, instead, is a company whose reported numbers are unusually sensitive to inventory, currency and the timing of customer advances, and whose own documents publish the same figure several different ways.

Earnings quality score: 5 out of 10

The score is not lower because every classic fraud test passes cleanly and the audit trail is unblemished. It is not higher because three of the biggest swing factors in FY26 profit - a currency gain, an inventory-sensitive gross margin, and a fourth-quarter cash inflow - are each individually material and none is separately disclosed, and because the company has published its own return on capital at values ranging from 9.65% to 18.2% for the same years.

Management has been candid about the cause on calls: "working capital days increased to 267 from 229 sequentially, mainly due to delayed receivables from conflict-affected regions" and, more specifically, "delayed payments from conflict regions, particularly Israel". That is a named, concentrated collection risk on a live customer relationship, disclosed verbally, with no corresponding expected-credit-loss disclosure retrievable.

Test two: the foreign exchange finding - the biggest single item in this section

₹25 crore of FY26 profit is currency, not operations

On the Q4 FY26 earnings call management stated a "positive foreign exchange gain of ₹25 crores in FY26 due to currency depreciation", alongside "export revenues exceed 70% of total, benefiting from rupee depreciation".

Scale the ₹25 cr against FY26ValueForex as a share
Profit after tax₹94.03 cr26.6%

Year-on-year profit increase₹41.14 cr60.8%

Roughly 61% of MTAR's entire FY26 profit growth is attributable to a stated currency gain. And it cannot be sitting in other income, which runs at ₹0.5-3.7 cr a quarter and annualises to well under ₹10 cr. It is therefore either a translation benefit inside revenue or netted inside other expenses - and either way it is inside the reported EBITDA margin. FY26's margin recovery from 17.85% to 19.52% is substantially a currency effect, and it will not repeat if the rupee stabilises. The rupee weakened 8.66% against the dollar over the year to 21-Sep-2026.

The annual report says currency risk is managed by a "natural hedge; majority of revenue in foreign currency". A natural hedge only works when costs are also in foreign currency, and MTAR's cost base is overwhelmingly Indian. An earlier annual report describes the same risk as managed through hedging. Those are two different approaches to the same exposure.

Test three: where the cash actually came from

Two consecutive quarters of cash flow at three times EBITDA is not a working capital improvement

Q4 FY26 produced ₹218.9 cr of operating cash flow - 111% of the entire year's total - on ₹61.8 cr of EBITDA, a conversion of 354%. Q1 FY27 then produced ₹247.69 cr on ₹85.05 cr of EBITDA, a conversion of 291% and 68.7% of that quarter's entire revenue. No manufacturing business converts 69 paise of every revenue rupee into operating cash in a quarter unless it is receiving advances.

The corroboration is consistent. The quarter carried ₹2,895.1 cr of record order inflow including a ₹504 cr contract with a state nuclear operator, and Indian nuclear public-sector contracts routinely carry 10-20% mobilisation advances. A call one quarter earlier flagged "initiatives underway to secure customer advances". Investments rose from ₹238.69 cr at March to ₹379 cr at June.

Customer advances are a liability, repayable in work. They inflate operating cash flow, they mechanically reduce net working capital days because they sit in current liabilities, and they reverse as the order executes. The headlines "working capital days down to 146" and "net debt minimal at ₹44.6 cr" are most probably artefacts of the same advance.

March-2026 net working capital is reported as 146 days, 172 days and 266 days depending on which of three documents you read - and two of those three were published on the same day. The reconciliation is arithmetic:

The "improvement" from 266 days to 146 days is substantially an artefact of switching the denominator from trailing actual revenue to forward-annualised quarterly revenue. Absolute net working capital appears roughly unchanged. The separate 59-day figure quoted by the chief financial officer on the same call cannot be reconciled on any basis identified here.

None of this means the underlying improvement is fictional - receivable days genuinely fell from 140 to 82 and inventory days from 208 to 145 on the company's own quarterly basis, and the cash arrived. It means the single most prominent operational claim in the presentation is measured on a basis that flatters it, and that no comparison to prior guidance is valid without stating which basis is in use.

Test six: the investment pile and its negative carry

₹238.69 cr of short-term investments appeared on the balance sheet during FY26, essentially all of it in the fourth quarter, against roughly ₹1.9 cr a year earlier. The company held it while paying an effective 10.60% on ₹376.57 cr of debt. Indian bank deposits yielded roughly 6-7%. That is an indicative negative carry of about ₹9 cr a year, roughly 10% of FY26 profit, and no rational treasurer does it voluntarily.

Which means the investments are probably not free cash

The overwhelmingly likely explanation is that the deposits are restricted - margin money lodged against letters of credit and bank guarantees, or a ring-fenced customer advance. If that is right, true net debt at March-2026 is ₹376.57 cr, not ₹137.9 cr, and the management framing that there is "literally no debt in the company" does not hold. The interest-income test that would settle it cannot be performed, because interest income is not separately disclosed anywhere. If interest income on nearly ₹239 cr of investments is not visible in FY26 other income, that is a serious finding.

Test seven: contingent liabilities, related parties and the auditor

These three tests could not be completed because the relevant notes to the accounts were not retrievable. That is stated plainly rather than assumed clean.

  • Contingent liabilities: no quantum, trend or nature for any year FY22 to FY26. The only adjacent statements are boilerplate assertions that no pending legal or regulatory action affects going concern. One separate source puts FY25 contingent liabilities at ₹2.27 cr, 0.3% of net worth, which would be negligible. Given export-heavy revenue and heavy raw material import, customs and tax refund disputes are the likely material category.
  • Related party transactions: the schedule is not accessible for any year. A broker's analysis of the FY25 annual report contains sections headed "major related party transactions with promoter-owned entities" and "guarantees given to related parties" - confirming such transactions exist - but not their quantum or direction. This is the single most important unresolved item in this report. The only available statements are the standard assertions that transactions were at arm's length.
  • Statutory auditor: not named in any accessible source. Tenure, fee trend, CARO clauses and any emphasis of matter are all unobtainable. No change or resignation is reported anywhere, which is an absence of evidence rather than evidence of absence.
What did change: both the cost auditor and the internal auditor, simultaneously

The FY26 results approved the appointment of new cost auditors and new internal auditors for three years from FY27. The outgoing firms are not named. A simultaneous change of both, in the year the company's inventory accounting drives its reported gross margin and a ₹25 cr currency gain drives its profit growth, is worth a question. In the same window the compliance officer resigned, and the FY26 annual report itself admits "minor delays in regulatory filings".

What the score of 5 means

This is not a company with an accounting problem. It is a company with a disclosure discipline problem that has not kept pace with its own growth - three compliance officers in two years, a deck that contradicts the call it was published beside, a headline working-capital claim measured on a shifting denominator, and the three largest swing factors in a year's profit each undisclosed as separate lines. At a 47x multiple none of that would matter much. At 162x, where the entire investment case rests on the reported numbers being exactly what they appear to be, it matters a great deal.
