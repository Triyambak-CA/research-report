Section 06

Earnings quality and forensic checks

There is no fraud signature here. There is no aggressive tax, no dilution, no exceptional credits, no accounting policy churn, and three consecutive clean audit opinions. What there is, instead, is a company whose reported numbers are unusually sensitive to inventory, currency and the timing of customer advances, and whose own documents publish the same figure several different ways.

Earnings quality score: 5 out of 10

The score is not lower because every classic fraud test passes cleanly and the audit trail is unblemished. It is not higher because three of the biggest swing factors in FY26 profit - a currency gain, an inventory-sensitive gross margin, and a fourth-quarter cash inflow - are each individually material and none is separately disclosed, and because the company has published its own return on capital at values ranging from 9.65% to 18.2% for the same years.

Test two: the foreign exchange finding, corrected against the filing

This finding has been revised. The earlier edition placed the gain in the wrong part of the profit and loss account

The 21-Sep-2026 edition reported a ₹25 cr currency gain, reasoned that it could not be sitting in other income "which runs at ₹0.5-3.7 cr a quarter", and concluded it must therefore be inside EBITDA, making FY26's margin recovery "substantially a currency effect". The FY2026 annual report was not reachable from that environment. It has since been read. The gain is in other income, below EBITDA, and it is ₹20.66 cr. The claim that the operating margin improvement is a currency effect is withdrawn.

On the Q4 FY26 earnings call management stated a "positive foreign exchange gain of ₹25 crores in FY26 due to currency depreciation", alongside "export revenues exceed 70% of total, benefiting from rupee depreciation". Note 21 to the consolidated accounts gives the audited figure and its position.

Strip the currency line and pre-tax profit still grows 51%, which is the honest way to state it: the operating improvement is real, and the reported profit growth is flattered by roughly a third.

The natural hedge is more real than this report previously allowed

The earlier edition argued that management's "natural hedge" description fails because "MTAR's cost base is overwhelmingly Indian". The directors' report discloses both legs for FY2026: foreign exchange earnings of ₹726.77 cr against foreign exchange outgo of ₹426.46 cr. Currency outgo is 58.7% of currency earnings. MTAR imports a great deal of what it exports back out, so the net long position is about ₹300 cr, roughly 34% of revenue, not the near-total exposure the earlier wording implied. The company also states it hedges through forward contracts, which reduces the open position further. ICRA's formulation is the precise one: margins are exposed "to the extent of the unhedged position", and that position is not disclosed.

What remains unresolved is the translation effect inside revenue. With 82.96% of FY26 contract revenue billed outside India, a weaker rupee lifts reported rupee revenue on unchanged dollar volumes. That effect is real and is nowhere quantified in the filing, so it is neither claimed nor dismissed here.

Test three: where the cash actually came from

Two consecutive quarters of cash flow at three times EBITDA is not a working capital improvement

Q4 FY26 produced ₹218.9 cr of operating cash flow - 111% of the entire year's total - on ₹61.8 cr of EBITDA, a conversion of 354%. Q1 FY27 then produced ₹247.69 cr on ₹85.05 cr of EBITDA, a conversion of 291% and 68.7% of that quarter's entire revenue. No manufacturing business converts 69 paise of every revenue rupee into operating cash in a quarter unless it is receiving advances.

The corroboration is consistent. The quarter carried ₹2,895.1 cr of record order inflow including a ₹504 cr contract with a state nuclear operator, and Indian nuclear public-sector contracts routinely carry 10-20% mobilisation advances. A call one quarter earlier flagged "initiatives underway to secure customer advances". Investments rose from ₹238.69 cr at March to ₹379 cr at June.

Customer advances are a liability, repayable in work. They inflate operating cash flow, they mechanically reduce net working capital days because they sit in current liabilities, and they reverse as the order executes. The headlines "working capital days down to 146" and "net debt minimal at ₹44.6 cr" are most probably artefacts of the same advance.

March-2026 net working capital is reported as 146 days, 172 days and 266 days depending on which of three documents you read - and two of those three were published on the same day. The reconciliation is arithmetic:

The "improvement" from 266 days to 146 days is substantially an artefact of switching the denominator from trailing actual revenue to forward-annualised quarterly revenue. Absolute net working capital appears roughly unchanged. The separate 59-day figure quoted by the chief financial officer on the same call cannot be reconciled on any basis identified here.

None of this means the underlying improvement is fictional - receivable days genuinely fell from 140 to 82 and inventory days from 208 to 145 on the company's own quarterly basis, and the cash arrived. It means the single most prominent operational claim in the presentation is measured on a basis that flatters it, and that no comparison to prior guidance is valid without stating which basis is in use.

Test six: the investment pile, and the hypothesis this report got wrong

This test has been revised. The investments are free, and the adverse case does not hold

The 21-Sep-2026 edition reasoned that ₹238.69 cr of investments appearing in the fourth quarter, held against debt costing an effective 10.60%, made no sense unless the money was restricted - margin money against letters of credit, or a ring-fenced customer advance - and concluded that "true net debt at March-2026 is ₹376.57 cr, not ₹137.9 cr". It also said the interest-income test that would settle it could not be performed. The FY2026 annual report settles it, and the answer is no.

The ₹215.34 cr sits in a single liquid debt mutual fund, quoted, carried at fair value through profit or loss, and freely redeemable. It is not margin money. ICRA reaches the same conclusion independently from its own access to the company, describing "free cash and liquid investments of ₹218.5 crore as on March 31, 2026". Net debt at 31-Mar-2026 is therefore about ₹153 cr - gross debt of ₹369.22 cr less ₹215.34 cr of liquid funds and ₹0.91 cr of cash - close to the ₹137.9 cr this report reported and nowhere near the ₹376.57 cr adverse case. That case is withdrawn.

The interest-income test can now be performed, and it says the money arrived at the very end of the year

Interest on bank deposits and others was ₹1.00 cr in FY26 against ₹1.03 cr in FY25 - it fell - while the investments balance went from nil to ₹215.34 cr and total liquid resources reached ₹218.5 cr at the year end. A full year's interest of ₹1.0 cr on a closing balance of ₹215 cr is a yield under 0.5%. Only one reading fits both numbers: the balance did not exist for most of the year. It arrived in the fourth quarter, alongside the ₹209 cr increase in customer advances, and the ₹0.34 cr fair value gain on the fund is consistent with a holding period of weeks rather than months. The negative-carry argument in the earlier edition also falls away, because the company did not carry this money against its debt for a year. It carried it for a quarter, and it was not its own money.

The off-balance-sheet exposure is the item worth keeping. Bank guarantees and letters of credit outstanding more than doubled, from ₹72.67 cr to ₹149.95 cr, which is the right order of magnitude for an importer running ₹426.46 cr of foreign currency outgo, and which the earlier edition listed as unobtainable.

The auditor is named, and it is a Big Four network firm

The statutory auditor is S.R. Batliboi & Associates LLP, Chartered Accountants, a member firm of the EY network. The opinion on both the standalone and the consolidated statements for FY26 is unqualified. The one observation of substance in the CARO annexure is that the audit-trail server backup was not maintained in India for part of the year, April to August 2025 - a records-location compliance point, not an accounting one. Internal auditors are M/s Seshachalam & Co, Hyderabad. The earlier edition listed the auditor as "not named in any accessible source"; it is named now, and the identity is a mild positive rather than a neutral.

What did change: both the cost auditor and the internal auditor, simultaneously

The FY26 results approved the appointment of new cost auditors and new internal auditors for three years from FY27. The outgoing firms are not named. A simultaneous change of both, in the year the company's inventory accounting drives its reported gross margin and a ₹20.66 cr currency gain drives a third of its pre-tax profit growth, is worth a question. In the same window the compliance officer resigned, and the FY26 annual report itself admits "minor delays in regulatory filings". Note that this concerns the cost and internal auditors; the statutory auditor is unchanged.

What the score of 5 means

This is not a company with an accounting problem. It is a company with a disclosure discipline problem that has not kept pace with its own growth - three compliance officers in two years, a deck that contradicts the call it was published beside, a headline working-capital claim measured on a shifting denominator, an export-share chart off by a factor of four, and return on capital published five different ways for one year. The audited accounts, where they have now been read, are clean: an unqualified opinion from a Big Four network firm, a related-party note with nothing in it, contingent liabilities at 0.28% of net worth, and other income decomposed exactly as the notes require. The problem is not the accounts. It is everything the company says about the accounts in between filing them. At a 47x multiple that would matter little. At 162x, where the entire investment case rests on the reported numbers being exactly what they appear to be, it matters a great deal.
