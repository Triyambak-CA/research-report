External headwinds and tailwinds

The currency point deserves its own sentence

If the rupee had been flat in FY26, MTAR's profit would have grown roughly ₹16 cr rather than ₹41 cr, and the celebrated margin recovery from 17.85% to 19.52% would have been largely absent. That is not an accounting criticism - a translation gain on genuine export revenue is real money. It is a forecasting one: a currency move is not a repeatable earnings driver, and the FY27 model should not assume one.

Global linkages

Trade policy. More than 70% export exposure into the United States makes MTAR structurally exposed to any tariff action on Indian engineering goods, and the Q2 FY26 episode showed the pass-through fight is real and costly. Separately, because MTAR imports from the United States, Germany and China, it sits on both sides of the trade-policy question.

China. Trade-data aggregation shows MTAR importing from China. Its customer is facing a securities class action alleging exactly that Chinese material reached it through intermediaries. MTAR occupies that intermediary layer. Nobody has examined what MTAR imports from China or whether any of it is relevant. This is an unquantified linkage, not a known risk.

The AI capital expenditure cycle. This is the true macro driver and MTAR reaches it two steps removed. Global AI data-centre capacity additions are put at 30-40 GW by 2027 by MTAR's own customer. Between that number and MTAR's revenue sit the customer's share of the fuel-cell market, its procurement decisions, and MTAR's share of those. MTAR captures about 3% of its customer's revenue, and on the company's own FY27 guidance that ratio does not improve.

Tailwinds that are real but slower than the narrative

  • Indian nuclear liberalisation. The statute passed on 21-Dec-2025 and replaced both prior Acts. But the rules were still in draft with consultation closing on 04-Sep-2026, no private party can yet be licensed, and the flagship private reactor tender has slipped at least eighteen months and is being rewritten. The direction is unambiguous; the cash flow is years away.
  • Indian defence indigenisation. ₹1.39 lakh crore of the FY27 capital acquisition budget, about 75%, is earmarked for domestic industry. MTAR earned ₹103.8 cr of aerospace and defence revenue against that funnel.
  • Grid constraints in the United States. The demand for on-site fuel cell power is driven by multi-year interconnection queues, not by subsidy. That is a more durable driver than a policy incentive and it is the strongest structural argument in the bull case.
