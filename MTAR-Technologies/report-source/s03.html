Competitive landscape and peer benchmarking

MTAR is the most expensive name in the set on the two multiples that do not depend on an earnings base

At 97.2x enterprise value to EBITDA and 26.19x book, MTAR is not merely the most expensive of the eight - its price to book is more than twice the next highest, Paras at 15.59x. It earned that position with a 295% one-year move against 90% for the next-best performer. The cheapest genuinely comparable business in the set, Triveni Turbine, trades at 52.5x earnings and 42.2x EBITDA with a higher return on capital.

The three-year record is the number the current run-rate hides

MTAR's revenue compounded at 15.2% from FY23 to FY26, ranking sixth of eight, against 33.7% for Azad and 36.7% for Unimech. FY24 revenue grew 1.2%. FY25 grew 16.4%. The current acceleration is one year old. The company being valued at the highest multiple in its peer group has the second-slowest three-year growth record in it.

What each competitor is actually trying to do

CompanyStated milestones
Azad EngineeringThe closest comparable business model - qualification-led precision components for energy turbines and aerospace, export-led, concentrated multinational relationships with GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi Heavy, Rolls-Royce and Baker Hughes. Cut raw material cost to sales from 12% to 5%, the mirror image of MTAR's gross margin decline. Same business physics, better margins, broader customer base, slower growth.

This is what happens to an Indian defence name when the order conversion stops, and it happened this year, in this sector.

The unlisted and international competition MTAR actually faces

On the clean energy side the relevant competitor is not Indian. Third-party research identifies a Taiwanese supplier also manufacturing hot boxes for the same customer. On the Q1 FY27 call an analyst put the arithmetic directly to management - a 5 GW customer implies roughly 77,000 hot boxes, so "even if you exclude the Taiwanese player", MTAR would be asked for more than 50,000 a year against a disclosed path to 30,000. The Managing Director declined to give a number, citing confidentiality, and declined separately to give a market share, saying only "we hold majority of the share in that".

Elsewhere in solid oxide: FuelCell Energy has exited after a USD 64.5 mn impairment. Ceres Power licenses technology rather than manufacturing and took its first royalties in 2026. Doosan Fuel Cell began mass production on Ceres technology at a 50 MW Korean facility in July 2025, targeting the same data-centre market. The field is thinning at the component level and consolidating around one dominant original equipment maker - which is good for that maker's supplier today and structurally risky for it tomorrow.

The framing that resolves this section

MTAR is not out-executing Azad Engineering on quality of business. Azad earns 37.7% EBITDA margins and 59.2% gross margins with a broader customer base and more than double MTAR's three-year growth record. MTAR is out-executing on volume cycle position: it is levered, through one customer, to the single fastest-growing industrial demand pool in the world. The MTAR premium is a cycle premium, not a quality premium - and it is being paid at twice the price to book of anything else in the sector.
