Section 07

Promoter, governance and capital allocation

The promoter group has roughly halved its stake since listing and was still selling on 16-Sep-2026. Set against that: zero equity dilution in five years, a stable chief executive and finance chief, and clean audit opinions. Both halves are true and both matter.

Who the promoters are

MTAR was incorporated in 1970 by P. Ravindra Reddy, K. Satyanarayana Reddy and P. Jayaprakash Reddy, independent technocrats who built a private supplier to the Department of Atomic Energy, ISRO and DRDO. Its first project was coolant channel assemblies for reactor cores, taken on after the state-owned HMT Ltd declined the work as too complex. That heritage is genuine and it is the foundation of the nuclear and space qualifications the company still trades on.

The promoter group today is not a single family but a dispersed extended Reddy-family bloc, and the distribution of ownership inside it is unusual.

Netted across every leg - open-market sales, the single buy, inter-se transfers and pledges - the promoter group is unambiguously a net seller. Seven promoters sold, one bought; the aggregate stake fell 7.06 percentage points from September 2024 to June 2026 and kept falling; and 83% of the shares sold went out between May and September 2026, at and after the all-time high, while the stock was under the most severe stage of exchange surveillance.

A pledge created and released on the same day is a settlement mechanic, not financing

Saranya Loka Reddy created a pledge over 1,05,000 shares with Aditya Birla Money Limited on 28-Aug-2026 and released the same 1,05,000 shares with the same counterparty on the same day, disclosing both in separate letters dated 31-Aug-2026. The net change in encumbrance is nil. The purpose is not disclosed, and a same-day create-and-release of this shape is characteristic of collateral posted against a margin or settlement obligation rather than a loan against securities. It sits inside the window in which six of the eight promoter sales were executed. This report does not assert a connection; it records that the two happened in the same fortnight.

The Kavitha Reddy Gangapatnam pledge of 32,000 shares to a wealth distributor is different in character - that shape is a personal loan against securities - and it has not been released. This promoter sold twice in May and June and then pledged in September, monetising by both routes at once.

The pledge percentage in circulation cannot be reconciled to anything filed

A subscription data aggregator reported 3.52% of promoter holding pledged as at 22-Sep-2026. The FY2026 annual report discloses no promoter pledge at all, and the only pledge reference in it concerns the company rather than the promoters. The three filed disclosures above net to 0.10% of equity currently encumbered. The encumbrance trail this report assembled peaks near 1.60%. None of these reaches 3.52%, and the quarterly shareholding pattern filings that would settle it have not been read. Neither figure is stated here as fact. A separate report headlined "promoters confirm no share encumbrance in FY26" is flatly inconsistent with the disclosures above and should be disregarded.

Absolute encumbrance levels are low on every reading and are not a solvency concern. The reason to chase the number is not leverage. It is that promoters who are selling, pledging and posting collateral in the same fortnight are doing three things with the same shares, and the aggregate picture is the one that matters.

Key management: one seat is stable, one is not

The Managing Director has been in place throughout. The Chief Financial Officer, Gunneswara Rao Pusarla, has been in post since 02-Nov-2021, nearly five years, and his predecessor was retained as a financial advisor rather than departing outright. Both are genuine positives.

Three Company Secretaries in under two years

Shubham Sunil Bagadia left on 31-May-2024 with no stated reason. Naina Singh served from 01-Jun-2024 to 20-Jan-2026, about nineteen and a half months, citing "pursuit of a better career opportunity". Priyanka Agarwal took the seat on 02-Feb-2026.

The Company Secretary is the Compliance Officer under the listing regulations - the person who signs off disclosures, insider-trading compliance and shareholding patterns. Rapid turnover in that specific seat is a recognised governance warning. Compounding it, the incumbent is described as having about four years of total experience, gained at a practising company secretary firm and one micro-cap. Appointing a four-year Compliance Officer to a ₹22,000 cr company - in the same window in which it was placed under the most severe surveillance stage and its promoters sold heavily - is a capacity concern independent of anyone's competence.

On the board, the two confirmed exits in the period were both non-executive or nominee rather than independent: Gangapatnam V.S.K. Reddy on 13-Dec-2024, and the Florintree nominee after that fund's exit. Rohith Loka Reddy was appointed on 19-Mar-2025. The board runs to about nine directors and the chair appears separate from the MD, which is a positive if confirmed. Independence ratio, independent director names and expertise, attendance records and audit committee composition could not be obtained and are therefore not assessed here rather than assumed clean.

The re-rating is not earnings

FY23 earnings per share were ₹33.62. FY26 earnings per share were ₹30.57 - 9.1% lower, three years later. Over the same period the share price went from about ₹1,582 to ₹7,040, up 345%. Every rupee of that move is multiple expansion. FY24 and FY25 were earnings recessions, with profit falling 45.7% and then a further 5.7%.

The credit rating, and a sensitivity that changed when it was breached

ICRA upgraded MTAR's long-term ratings to [ICRA]A+ (Stable) from [ICRA]A (Stable) on 07-Jul-2026 and reaffirmed the short-term rating at [ICRA]A1. The rated amount was enhanced from ₹670 cr to ₹1,470 cr, a 119% increase in one step. It is the first rating movement in over three years: [ICRA]A (Stable) was held at 04-Apr-2023, 16-Jun-2023, 23-Sep-2024 and 13-Oct-2025.

The upgrade rests on scale, the order book and demand, and those are real. Reading the two most recent rationales side by side shows something the July document alone does not.

The metric named as the downgrade trigger was breached, and replaced, in the document that delivered the upgrade

In October 2025 the agency named total debt to operating profit above two times as the specific metric that would trigger a downgrade. The July 2026 document reports that ratio at 2.2 times for FY2026, and in the same document the named trigger becomes interest coverage below five times, which is not breached.

Two readings, and both belong here. The agency's wording is "on a sustained basis", and one year at 2.2x after a year at 1.5x is not a sustained breach; the upgrade is defensible on scale and order book alone. Against that, the substitution of an unbreached sensitivity for a breached one, inside the document that upgrades the rating, is visible only if you read consecutive rationales together, and nobody reading the July document by itself would see it. This report draws no conclusion about the agency's judgement. It records what the two documents say.

Headroom on the new trigger is thin. Interest coverage is 5.8x against a threshold of 5.0x. Borrowings doubled to ₹369 cr during FY26, the interest line is running at ₹39 cr on a trailing basis against ₹29 cr for FY26, and ₹160 cr of capital expenditure is already contracted. Coverage holds only while operating profit grows at least as fast as the interest bill.

Three further things the rationales disclose that the company does not. Repayment obligations are ₹44.5 cr in FY2027 and ₹60 cr in FY2028. Average fund-based working capital utilisation was 39.2% over the twelve months to May 2026, so there is drawing headroom. And the ₹218.5 cr of cash and liquid investments at 31-Mar-2026 is described as free, which is what settles the question Section 6 previously left open.

Where the evidence stops

The first of the two items flagged here in the 21-Sep-2026 edition has been resolved. The related party schedule and the guarantees note were read from the FY2026 annual report on 22-Sep-2026 and both come back clean: total related-party flows of ₹12.5 cr or 1.4% of revenue, three quarters of it management and board pay, key managerial remuneration falling, and guarantees to related parties nil at 31-Mar-2026 against a maximum of ₹19.50 cr during FY25 to the wholly owned subsidiary Gee Pee Aerospace and Defence Private Limited. Section 6, test seven, has the schedule. There is no promoter-entity supply chain and no circular funding.

What is still open is the second item, and it is genuinely open. Promoter-level litigation, political exposure and prior failed ventures have not been checked. The offer document's litigation section was never opened and no search of court or regulatory records has been run. The absence of a finding there is an absence of evidence, not a clean bill of health, and nothing in this enrichment pass changes that.

Also unresolved: the current pledge position. The FY2026 annual report discloses no promoter pledge, and the only pledge reference in it concerns the company rather than the promoters. A data aggregator reports 3.52% of promoter holding pledged as at 22-Sep-2026. The encumbrance trail above peaks near 1.60% and falls to about 0.52% before the September 2026 pledge of 32,000 shares. Nothing in hand reconciles to 3.52%, and the quarterly shareholding pattern filings that would settle it have not been read. Neither figure is stated here as fact.

What is known and positive: contingent liabilities are ₹2.27 cr at both FY25 and FY26, 0.28% of net worth, now read from the filing rather than a secondary source, with no disputed tax demands disclosed. The FY26 audit opinion from S.R. Batliboi & Associates LLP is unqualified on both the standalone and the consolidated statements.
