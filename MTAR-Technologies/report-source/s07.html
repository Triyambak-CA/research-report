Section 07

Promoter, governance and capital allocation

The promoter group has roughly halved its stake since listing and was still selling five days before this report. Set against that: zero equity dilution in five years, a stable chief executive and finance chief, and clean audit opinions. Both halves are true and both matter.

Who the promoters are

MTAR was incorporated in 1970 by P. Ravindra Reddy, K. Satyanarayana Reddy and P. Jayaprakash Reddy, independent technocrats who built a private supplier to the Department of Atomic Energy, ISRO and DRDO. Its first project was coolant channel assemblies for reactor cores, taken on after the state-owned HMT Ltd declined the work as too complex. That heritage is genuine and it is the foundation of the nuclear and space qualifications the company still trades on.

The promoter group today is not a single family but a dispersed extended Reddy-family bloc, and the distribution of ownership inside it is unusual.

The operating executive owns less than two of his own relatives

The Managing Director holds 3.88%, less than K. Vamshidhar Reddy at 5.70%, less than Madhavi Mitta at 4.50%, and less than his own mother at 4.22%. There is no single controlling shareholder. That means the economic alignment of the person actually running the company is materially weaker than the headline "29% promoter holding" suggests.

Promoter holding fell from 36.42% to 31.42% in the December 2024 quarter. Five percentage points is roughly 15.4 lakh shares, worth about ₹255 cr at that quarter's price. No block deal of that size appears anywhere in the accessible record, and the stock traded a narrow ₹1,470 to ₹1,824 range through the quarter with no volume spike - a sale of that size would almost certainly have printed as a block. The most likely explanation is a promoter-group reclassification to public rather than a sale, and a suggestive but non-causal coincidence is that Gangapatnam Venkata Satish Kumar Reddy resigned as a non-executive director effective 13-Dec-2024, from a promoter family branch. This is unresolved and it is the first thing to check in the filed December 2024 pattern.

Against those eight sales there is exactly one promoter buy in the whole 24-month window, around 20-21 February 2025, whose name, quantity and price were all in a source that could not be opened. February 2025 traded between ₹1,206.75 and ₹1,675 - roughly one fifth of the price at which the group was selling fifteen months later.

There were also inter-se gifts on 23-Feb-2026 at nil consideration: the MD transferred 2,00,000 shares to his mother, taking him from 4.53% to 3.88%, and Saranya Loka Reddy transferred 97,000, taking her from 1.95% to 1.63%. Aggregate promoter holding was unchanged and no open-offer obligation was triggered. Gifting within a family for estate or tax planning is entirely routine. The sequence worth recording, without alleging intent, is that the recipient of those gifts then sold 20,000 shares on the open market on 16-Sep-2026.

Netted across every leg - open-market sales, the single buy, inter-se transfers and pledges - the promoter group is unambiguously a net seller. Seven promoters sold, one bought; the aggregate stake fell 7.06 percentage points from September 2024 to June 2026 and kept falling; and 83% of the shares sold went out between May and September 2026, at and after the all-time high, while the stock was under the most severe stage of exchange surveillance.

Pledges: low in absolute terms, rising again

Visible encumbrance peaked at about 1.60% of equity, was released down to roughly 0.52%, and then rose again on 17-Sep-2026 when Kavitha Reddy Gangapatnam pledged 32,000 shares, about 0.14%, to 360 ONE Distribution Services. Absolute levels are low and are not a solvency concern. Two qualitative points stand: pledging to a wealth distributor is characteristic of a personal loan against securities rather than business funding, and this promoter sold twice in May and June and then pledged in September - monetising by both routes at once. One further item is unresolved: a report headlined "promoters confirm no share encumbrance in FY26" is flatly inconsistent with the pledge disclosures covering May to September 2026, and the conflict cannot be settled without the filings.

Key management: one seat is stable, one is not

The Managing Director has been in place throughout. The Chief Financial Officer, Gunneswara Rao Pusarla, has been in post since 02-Nov-2021, nearly five years, and his predecessor was retained as a financial advisor rather than departing outright. Both are genuine positives.

Three Company Secretaries in under two years

Shubham Sunil Bagadia left on 31-May-2024 with no stated reason. Naina Singh served from 01-Jun-2024 to 20-Jan-2026, about nineteen and a half months, citing "pursuit of a better career opportunity". Priyanka Agarwal took the seat on 02-Feb-2026.

The Company Secretary is the Compliance Officer under the listing regulations - the person who signs off disclosures, insider-trading compliance and shareholding patterns. Rapid turnover in that specific seat is a recognised governance warning. Compounding it, the incumbent is described as having about four years of total experience, gained at a practising company secretary firm and one micro-cap. Appointing a four-year Compliance Officer to a ₹22,000 cr company - in the same window in which it was placed under the most severe surveillance stage and its promoters sold heavily - is a capacity concern independent of anyone's competence.

On the board, the two confirmed exits in the period were both non-executive or nominee rather than independent: Gangapatnam V.S.K. Reddy on 13-Dec-2024, and the Florintree nominee after that fund's exit. Rohith Loka Reddy was appointed on 19-Mar-2025. The board runs to about nine directors and the chair appears separate from the MD, which is a positive if confirmed. Independence ratio, independent director names and expertise, attendance records and audit committee composition could not be obtained and are therefore not assessed here rather than assumed clean.

The re-rating is not earnings

FY23 earnings per share were ₹33.62. FY26 earnings per share were ₹30.57 - 9.1% lower, three years later. Over the same period the share price went from about ₹1,582 to ₹7,040, up 345%. Every rupee of that move is multiple expansion. FY24 and FY25 were earnings recessions, with profit falling 45.7% and then a further 5.7%.

Where the evidence stops

Two items are unresolved and both would change the assessment if they came back badly. First, a broker's analysis of the FY25 annual report contains sections headed "major related party transactions with promoter-owned entities" and "guarantees given to related parties" - confirming that promoter-owned entities do transact with the listed company - but the quantum and direction could not be obtained. That is the single most important open question in this report. Second, promoter-level litigation, political exposure and prior failed ventures were never checked, because the offer document's litigation section could not be opened and the research budget ran out. The absence of a finding there is an absence of evidence, not a clean bill of health.

What is known and positive: contingent liabilities at FY25 were ₹2.27 cr, just 0.3% of net worth, which is negligible and argues against any large litigation at the company level. Audit opinions for FY26 were clean, with no qualification and no emphasis of matter reported.
