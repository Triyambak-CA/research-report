Executive summary

MTAR is a genuine operating inflection wrapped in a valuation that requires a decade of near-impossible compounding. The quarterly revenue run-rate has gone from ₹135.6 cr to ₹360.7 cr in three quarters, and that is real, audited and reported. The order book is 6.8x last year's revenue. It is also the case that the record order book is one blanket purchase order from one undisclosed customer with no delivery schedule, that cumulative free cash flow over five years is negative ₹252 cr, and that the price sits above every valuation MTAR has ever traded at.

The three strongest positives

  1. The operating inflection is real and audited. Quarterly revenue: ₹135.6 cr, ₹278.0 cr, ₹306.1 cr, ₹360.7 cr. EBITDA margin from 12.5% to 23.6%. Profit after tax up 364% year on year. Every one of those numbers is in a filed result.
  2. A genuine incumbency that is hard to replicate. MTAR has been a qualified nuclear supplier since 1969, in a supply chain where new entrants face a seven-to-ten-year qualification with no guarantee of award. On the clean energy side it has a fifteen-year relationship and is described in third-party research as the primary hot box supplier. The ₹504 cr Kaiga 5 and 6 fuel handling award is the largest nuclear order in the company's history and it is real.
  3. The balance sheet has not been abused. Zero equity dilution in five years, share count fixed at 3,07,59,600. No qualified placement, no warrants, no rights issue. An unqualified opinion from S.R. Batliboi & Associates LLP. Effective tax rate tight to statutory across eleven quarters. The related party note, read on 22-Sep-2026, is genuinely clean: total flows of ₹12.5 cr or 1.4% of revenue and most of it management pay, guarantees to related parties nil, and key managerial pay down 15.7% in a year profit rose 78%. Contingent liabilities are 0.28% of net worth.

The three sharpest risks

  1. The record order book is one blanket purchase order with no schedule. ₹2,278.96 cr, filed 14-May-2026, counterparty undisclosed, "execution timelines will be decided at a later stage". It is 44% of the June order book, and with one other unnamed order it is 94.9% of the record quarter's inflow. Clean energy went from 51.2% to 66.7% of the book in the quarter management called diversified.
  2. The cash has never been there. Cumulative free cash flow FY22 to FY26 is negative ₹252 cr against ₹367 cr of reported profit, on the audited FY26 figure of positive ₹63.3 cr. Through nine months of FY26 operating cash flow was minus ₹22 cr on ₹109 cr of EBITDA. The entire year's cash arrived in the fourth quarter, at 354% of that quarter's EBITDA, and Q1 FY27 repeated it at 291%. That is the signature of customer advances, and the FY26 balance sheet now confirms it: contract liabilities went from ₹43.34 cr to ₹252.65 cr, up 482% in one year. Advances are a liability repayable in work, not earnings. The same ₹209 cr is also what makes the company's "working capital days halved" headline true.
  3. The price. 162x trailing earnings, 97x enterprise value to EBITDA, 26x book. The reverse discounted cash flow requires a 55-60% revenue compound rate for ten years on central assumptions, against a realised incremental sales-to-capital ratio of 0.61. FY26 earnings per share of ₹30.57 are still below FY23's ₹33.62. The entire re-rating is multiple expansion.
Two dated events in the last week of September 2026

MTAR enters the Nifty Midcap 150 with effect from 30-Sep-2026, forcing passive rebalancing into a book that is in trade-to-trade settlement with a 5% price band and ninety-six per cent less liquidity than it had in June. Separately, the lead plaintiff deadline in the United States securities class action against Bloom Energy falls on 28-Sep-2026. Neither is a thesis, but both are dated and both can move this stock more than fundamentals will in that window.

The one line

This is a bet on one American fuel-cell maker's AI data-centre order book converting into Indian hot boxes, priced as if MTAR compounds revenue at 55-60% a year for a decade, and it breaks if the blanket purchase order does not become firm call-offs on the schedule the market has assumed.
