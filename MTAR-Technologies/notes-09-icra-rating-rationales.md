## 2. The finding: the downgrade trigger was breached and replaced in the same cycle

This is the sharpest thing in either document, and it needs stating carefully because the
inference is mine, not ICRA's.

**13-Oct-2025 rationale, negative factors:**
> "Specific credit metrics that may downgrade MTL's ratings include **TD/OBDITA of more than 2
> times on a sustained basis**."

**07-Jul-2026 rationale, key financial indicators:**
> Total debt/OPBDITA (times): FY2025 **1.5**, FY2026 **2.2**

**07-Jul-2026 rationale, negative factors:**
> "Specific credit metrics that may downgrade MTL's ratings include **Interest Coverage of below
> 5x on a sustained basis**."

So: the metric ICRA named in October 2025 as the downgrade trigger stood at 2.2x in the year
being rated, against a stated trigger of "more than 2 times". In the same document the trigger
metric was changed to interest coverage, and the rating was upgraded.

Two honest readings, and the report should carry both:
- ICRA's wording is "on a sustained basis". One year at 2.2x after a year at 1.5x is not
  sustained, and the upgrade rests on scale, order book and demand, which are real.
- The replacement of a breached sensitivity with an unbreached one, in the same document that
  delivers the upgrade, is the kind of thing that only shows if you read consecutive rationales
  side by side. Nobody reading the July document alone would see it.

**Headroom on the new trigger is thin.** Interest coverage was 5.8x in FY26 against a trigger of
below 5x. Interest cost is running at Rs 29 cr for FY26 and Rs 39 cr on a trailing twelve month
basis, and borrowings more than doubled from Rs 177 cr to Rs 377 cr in the year. Coverage is
holding only because EBITDA is growing at a similar rate.

## 3. Gaps in the report that these documents close

**(a) The counterparty of the Rs 2,278.96 cr blanket order.** The report lists this as an open
item: "The counterparty of the Rs 2,278.96 cr order". ICRA, 07-Jul-2026:

> "In May 2026, MTL received additional orders worth about **Rs. 2,279 crore and Rs. 467 crore
> from its international customer(s)**."

ICRA elsewhere in the same document states that **more than 70% of FY26 revenue came from a
single customer, Bloom Energy Corporation**. It does not name the counterparty of the two May
orders, and "customer(s)" leaves open whether it is one party or two. So this narrows the
answer without closing it: the order is from an **international** customer, in a book where one
international customer is more than 70% of revenue. That is a much tighter statement than the
report could make, and it must not be written up as confirmation that it is Bloom.

It also supplies the second order's size. The report says a further "~Rs 800 cr has no
traceable exchange filing"; ICRA puts a second May-2026 order at **Rs 467 cr**.

**(b) Whether the investments balance is restricted margin money.** The report's open item asks
whether Rs 238.69 cr of investments is restricted, noting that if so, true net debt is
Rs 376.6 cr rather than Rs 137.9 cr. ICRA, 07-Jul-2026:

> "the company has healthy liquidity position with **free cash and liquid investments of
> Rs. 218.5 crore** as on March 31, 2026"

ICRA calls it free. On a primary source, the report's adverse case does not hold. This closes
in the company's favour and the report should say so plainly.

**(c) The debt maturity profile**, which stream-A had only "partial, from calls only":
repayment obligations of about **Rs 44.5 cr in FY2027 and Rs 60 cr in FY2028**. Average
fund-based working capital utilisation was **39.2%** over the twelve months to May 2026, so
there is drawing headroom.

**(d) Capex.** "capex plans of around **Rs 250-300 crore in the medium term**, which would be
funded by debt and internal accruals." This matches the Q4 FY26 call guidance in the report's
scorecard, now confirmed to a third party.

**(e) Customer concentration, quantified from a primary source, two years running.**
FY2025: "more than 70% of its revenues from a single customer - Bloom Energy Corporation".
FY2026: "more than 70% of its revenues being generated from a single customer, i.e., BEC, US".

## 4. The segment label question, resolved arithmetically

ICRA, 07-Jul-2026, environmental considerations:
> "most of its revenue (**nearly 73% of total revenue in FY2026**) coming from this segment,
> including **civil nuclear power and fuel cells**"

The screener premium Insights rows were transcribed with labels that do not survive this test.
Taking the Insights FY26 values and MTAR's actual product groups:

| Product group | FY26 Rs cr | % of 876.21 |
|---|---|---|
| Clean energy, fuel cells | 615.40 | 70.2% |
| Clean energy, civil nuclear | 23.60 | 2.7% |
| **Clean energy total** | **639.00** | **72.9%** |
| Aerospace and defence | 103.80 | 11.8% |
| Products and others | 134.10 | 15.3% |
| Total | 876.90 | 100.1% |

72.9% against ICRA's "nearly 73%". And 70.2% for fuel cells against ICRA's "more than 70% from
BEC". Both tie. The transcribed labels had the fuel-cell line and the civil-nuclear line
attached to the wrong values, and the aerospace line attached to a clean-energy label.

**To be confirmed against the FY26 annual report's Ind AS 115 revenue disaggregation note
before anything is printed.** The arithmetic is strong but it is still inference from an
aggregator's rows plus a rating agency's percentage.

## 6. Working capital, a fifth basis from a primary source

ICRA states net working capital to operating income:

| | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|
| NWC/OI | 62% | 44% |
| Day equivalent (x365) | 226 | 161 |

ICRA agrees with the direction the company claims: working capital intensity fell materially in
FY26. It disagrees on the level with everyone, including the company. See notes-08 section 5:
the same year now has six published measures, from 44% of revenue to 419 days.

ICRA's own caution, which the company's presentation does not carry:
> "The overall working capital intensity is expected to remain high in the medium term due to a
> growing order book and a relatively long manufacturing cycle and lead time."

| | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Operating income (Rs cr) | 676.0 | 876.2 |
| PAT (Rs cr) | 52.9 | 94.0 |
| OPBDITA/OI | 17.9% | 19.5% |
| PAT/OI | 7.8% | 10.7% |
| Total outside liabilities / tangible net worth | 0.6 | **1.1** |
| Total debt / OPBDITA | 1.5 | **2.2** |
| Interest coverage | 5.4 | 5.8 |

Total outside liabilities to tangible net worth **almost doubled, 0.6 to 1.1**. Gearing went
0.2x at 31-Mar-2025 (0.3x at 31-Mar-2024) to **0.46x** at 31-Mar-2026. The balance sheet
levered up hard in the year the order book trebled.

  P. Jayaprakash Reddy. Incorporated as a private limited company in 1999 from a 1970
  partnership; listed March 2021; eight manufacturing units in Hyderabad.
