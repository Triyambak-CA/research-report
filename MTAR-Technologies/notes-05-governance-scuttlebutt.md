## THE BLOOM RELATIONSHIP FROM THE OTHER SIDE - the sharpest finding in the file
Bloom Energy Q2 2026 call, 28-Jul-2026, management on the record to its own shareholders:
  "multiple qualified suppliers across multiple countries for every critical input"
  "**no single supplier determining their operations**"
  "**no single supplier and no single country determines the company's destiny**"
  "neither gated by capacity nor their supply chain"
  adding US capacity "in **copy exact** increments... **ahead of committed orders**"
  "2026 guidance is not going to have any dependence on any single project"
Bloom Q1 2026: current manufacturing footprint will allow delivery of **5 GW of product annually**.
Newark, Delaware plant (owned, 178,000 sq ft) is "designed specifically for **copy-exact duplication** as it expands".
**NO Bloom filing, call or press release that could be reached NAMES MTAR AT ALL.**
=> MTAR discloses Bloom as 55-70% of its revenue. Bloom does not appear to disclose MTAR anywhere.
   The relationship is existential for one party and unremarkable for the other.
Three readings, all honest: (a) defensive boilerplate about critical raw inputs post-Hunterbrook, not about
sub-assembly partners - most probable, and it softens the point; (b) Bloom genuinely has qualified alternate
hot box sources, in which case MTAR's sole-source premium is overstated; (c) both - sole source today while
Bloom deliberately builds optionality. Not resolvable.
Unit maths that does not obviously reconcile: Bloom going 1 GW -> 2 GW while MTAR goes 8,000 -> 12,000 hot
boxes (1.5x) in the same window. Either MTAR's share is falling, or the Mar-2027 "multi-fold" expansion is
the catch-up, or units do not map linearly to GW. **A specific, answerable question nobody has asked.**
No Bloom manufacturing plant announced in India. Bloom Energy (India) Pvt Ltd (Bengaluru, inc. 2004) has
vendor-reported revenue of Rs 100-500 cr for FY25 with a 310% EBITDA CAGR - most likely an engineering and
procurement centre, and weakly consistent with Bloom building direct Indian capability.

## HUNTERBROOK "Bloom's Big Lie" - 08-Jul-2026
Allegations: Bloom told investors it has "no China supply chain" and is "not dependent on China for scandium",
while relying on Chinese-sourced scandium. Four China-linked routes traced: scandium oxide shipped directly to
the Delaware plant, plus scandium-bearing ceramics and powders through intermediaries in **Thailand, Japan and
South Korea**. Method: global shipping records, corporate filings, satellite imagery. A sales rep of Hunan
Oriental Scandium (claims >50% of the global fuel-cell-grade scandium oxide market) quoted: "We are also BE's
largest supplier of scandium." Capacity maths: 5 GW of fuel cells a year needs ~220 tonnes of scandium oxide
against projected global supply of ~240 tonnes.
Bloom 8-K rebuttal 09-Jul-2026: "false and misleading"; holds sufficient non-China scandium oxide inventory for
current demand and backlog; visibility to support 25 GW/yr.
Class action: class period 27-Feb-2025 to 08-Jul-2026; ss.10(b)/20(a) and Rule 10b-5; **lead plaintiff deadline 28-Sep-2026**.
Jim Chanos publicly arguing an AI energy bubble (Jun-2026).
UNCONFIRMED and flagged: one outlet reported Oracle's "Project Jupiter" (2.45 GW of Bloom fuel cells) rejected
by regulators a second time and a one-year New York data-centre moratorium - could not corroborate.
Other reporting says Black Hills is continuing the 1.8 GW Wyoming project directly with the large-load customer,
targeting service in early 2028, with Crusoe out as developer. Morgan Stanley: the Crusoe pause does not break
the AI power thesis.
