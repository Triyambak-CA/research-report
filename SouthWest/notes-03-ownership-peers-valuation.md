## I. VALUATION WORK (my own computation, 28-Aug-2026)

### Reverse DCF - what Rs 209 already assumes
Inputs: CMP Rs 209.06, 3.27 cr shares, mcap Rs 684 cr, debt Rs 80 cr, cash Rs 12 cr, **EV Rs 752 cr**. FY26 EBIT Rs 47.3 cr, NOPAT Rs 36.0 cr. TTM EBIT Rs 55.0 cr, NOPAT Rs 41.8 cr. WACC 13.5% (Ke 14.5%, Kd 9.5% pre-tax, market weights). ROIC 20% (= FY26 ROCE). Terminal growth 5%, terminal ROIC 15%.

- **No-growth value** (perpetuity, zero reinvestment): Rs 266 cr on FY26 NOPAT, Rs 310 cr on TTM. That is **35-41% of the Rs 752 cr EV**. So roughly **60% of the market value is growth not yet delivered.**
- **Implied growth:** to justify Rs 752 cr the market needs **26.2% EBIT CAGR for 10 years** (on TTM NOPAT), or **19.1% for 15 years**. On the FY26 base: 30.4% for 10 years, or 21.5% for 15.
- **THE KEY POINT:** growth = reinvestment rate x ROIC. At a 20% ROIC, a 26.2% growth rate requires a **reinvestment rate of 131% of NOPAT** - the company must reinvest more than it earns, every year, for a decade. Free cash flow is negative for the whole explicit period and essentially 100% of the value sits in the terminal value. That is not a modelling artefact: FY26 FCF was Rs -33 cr and 3-year cumulative FCF is Rs -18.5 cr. **At its own ROIC, self-funded growth caps out at 20%; the price demands 26%. The gap must come from debt or equity issuance that the current share count does not reflect.**
- Sensitivity (implied 10-yr EBIT CAGR on TTM NOPAT):
  - WACC 12.0%: ROIC 16% -> 23.1% | ROIC 20% -> 17.7% | ROIC 25% -> 15.4%
  - WACC 13.5%: ROIC 16% -> above 60% | ROIC 20% -> 26.2% | ROIC 25% -> 21.1%
  - WACC 15.0%: ROIC 25% -> 27.8%; below that the model does not solve inside a 60% cap
  Read plainly: only at a 12% discount rate does the implied growth (17.7%) fall BELOW management's own 20% guidance. At any higher discount rate the price demands more than management promises.
