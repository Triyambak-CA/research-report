# H. The commissioned industry report problem

Sources: ICDR Schedule VI (consolidated to 21-Mar-2026); ESDS Software Solution RHP dated 24-Aug-2026 (downloaded from SEBI, local esds-rhp.txt) as market-practice evidence.

## H.1 What the regulation actually requires
- Sch VI Part A (10)(A) Industry Overview: "If extract of any industry report is disclosed in the offer document, the complete industry report shall be provided as part of the material documents" (inserted by amendment; present in current text). So the FULL report is inspectable - and in practice hosted on the issuer's website.
- Sch VI Part A Instructions (a): source and basis of ALL statements and claims must be disclosed; terms like "market leader"/"leading player" only if substantiated by a cited source. Instruction (e): no forward-looking statements that cannot be substantiated.
- There is NO ICDR requirement to disclose the FEE paid to the industry-report provider, and NO track-record requirement for the provider (contrast with the BRLM price-information table, file G). NOTHING VERIFIED on any SEBI-mandated standard disclaimer text in the regulations themselves.

## H.2 What market practice (SEBI-observation-driven) delivers
Evidence from the ESDS RHP (representative of current practice): the report is described as "exclusively commissioned and paid for by our Company" in the definitions, the Industry Overview lead-in, Presentation of Information, AND a dedicated risk factor (ESDS risk factor no. 62: "Statistical and industry data... derived from an industry report exclusively commissioned and paid for by our Company for the purpose of the Issue. Reliance on information from the industry report for making an investment decision in the Issue is subject to [inherent risks]"); the full report is stated to be available on the company website. The commissioning disclosure is therefore reliably present in any recent DRHP/RHP, but characterise it as issuer disclosure practice under SEBI review, not a numbered ICDR mandate.

## H.3 How the analyst should discount it and cross-check
- Treat every market-size, CAGR and "company is among the leading..." claim as an ISSUER CLAIM: the provider was selected and paid by the issuer, the terms of reference are set by the issuer, and TAM definitions are routinely drawn so the issuer ranks top-3. First test: check whether the report defines the market at a level of granularity that exactly flatters the issuer (e.g. "managed cloud services for BFSI in tier-2 India" rather than "cloud services").
- Cross-check against: (1) the industry chapters of OTHER DRHPs/RHPs in the same sector (different issuers commission CRISIL vs F&S vs Technopak - triangulate their market sizes; they frequently disagree by 2-3x); (2) official data - MOSPI/ASI, RBI sectoral credit, ministry dashboards, IBEF/NITI Aayog/Economic Survey (existing skill section 20); (3) listed peers' annual-report industry sections and concall commentary; (4) the issuer's OWN restated revenue growth vs the claimed industry CAGR - a company growing at 12% in a market claimed to grow at 25% is losing share, whatever the narrative says.
- Read the provider's own disclaimer in the full report (material documents): it invariably disclaims responsibility for forecasts and states reliance on issuer-supplied information - quote that disclaimer in the research report when the issuer leans on the numbers.
- Check provider identity and repetition: the same boutique provider appearing across many IPOs of one BRLM stable is a red flag; CRISIL MI&A / CARE / F&S carry more process credibility than unknown boutiques, but the incentive problem is identical.
