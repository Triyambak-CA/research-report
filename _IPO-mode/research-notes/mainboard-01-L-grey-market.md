# L. Grey market premium (GMP)

## L.1 Regulatory status
- The IPO grey market has NO statutory or regulatory basis. It is off-exchange forward dealing in securities that are not yet listed, among private counterparties, settled informally. Under the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act 1956 (ss.13/16/18 framework), contracts in securities other than spot-delivery contracts or contracts on/permitted by a recognised stock exchange are impermissible; grey-market "kostak"/"subject-to-sauda" deals sit outside that framework and are legally unenforceable. (Statutory framing; specific enforcement actions on IPO grey markets: NOTHING VERIFIED.)
- SEBI does not publish, endorse, regulate or recognise GMP. No SEBI circular or regulation references GMP as an input to anything.
- SEBI HAS commented on it obliquely and negatively: the Nov-2025 SEBI consultation paper preceding the ICDR (Amendment) Regulations 2026 observed that because offer documents are voluminous, "potential investors often rely on secondary and unregulated sources of information, such as grey market trends and unverified social media posts" - and offered the revamped draft abridged prospectus precisely to redirect investors to regulated disclosure (as quoted in the Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas client alert dated 24-Mar-2026 on the Amendment Regulations; SECONDARY source quoting SEBI's paper). Senior SEBI officials have repeatedly cautioned against GMP reliance in public remarks (media-reported; UNVERIFIED to a specific citable document).
- SEBI's 02-Sep-2024 flipping study (file K) documents the behaviour GMP feeds: allotment-and-flip within a week, strongest when listing pops exceed 20%.

## L.2 Defensible position for a research report
- Do NOT cite GMP as valuation evidence, a price target, or "expected listing gain". It is an unregulated, unverifiable, thin, manipulable quote published by aggregator websites with no audit trail; quoting it in a research deliverable lends it the credibility SEBI's own consultation paper says it should not have, and sits badly with a two-way-pitch, no-rating format.
- Permissible narrow use, if used at all: ONE line in the flow/positioning section, clearly framed - "aggregator-reported GMP of about Rs X (roughly Y% over cap price) as on <date>; unregulated, unverifiable, treated as a sentiment datapoint only, not evidence of value" - and only to make a POSITIONING argument (e.g. a high GMP predicts heavy retail/NII oversubscription, lottery-driven allotment, and week-1 flipping supply per the SEBI study; a GMP collapse between anchor day and listing warns of a weak debut). Never let it touch the valuation section; reverse DCF at the cap price is the anchor, GMP is crowd noise around it.
- If the caller's compliance posture is conservative (SEBI Research Analyst Regulations 2014 context - reports must have a reasonable basis), the cleaner choice is to omit GMP entirely and model expected supply from the SEBI flipping-study base rates instead.
