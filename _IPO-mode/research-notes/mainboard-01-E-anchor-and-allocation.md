# E. Anchor book and allocation

Source: SEBI (ICDR) Regulations 2018, consolidated as amended to 21-Mar-2026 (local copy icdr-2026-03-21.pdf), read 01-Sep-2026.

## E.1 Anchor investor rules (mainboard, book-built)
- Definition Reg 2(1)(c): anchor investor = a QIB applying for AT LEAST Rs 10 CRORE in a mainboard public issue (Rs 2 crore for SME).
- Sch XIII para (10)(b) and Reg 32(3): UP TO 60 PER CENT of the QIB portion may be allocated to anchors, on a DISCRETIONARY basis.
- Investor-count buckets - Sch XIII (10)(c)(I), AS SUBSTITUTED by the ICDR (Third Amendment) Regulations 2025 w.e.f. 01-DEC-2025:
  - allocation up to Rs 250 crore: minimum 2, maximum 15 anchors, minimum allotment Rs 5 crore each;
  - above Rs 250 crore: minimum 5 anchors; maximum 15 for the first Rs 250 crore PLUS an additional 15 (raised from 10) for every additional Rs 250 crore or part thereof; minimum allotment Rs 5 crore each.
  (Pre-01-Dec-2025 text: max 2 anchors up to Rs 10 crore; 2-15 for Rs 10-250 crore; +10 per additional Rs 250 crore - footnote 793.)
- Reservation inside the anchor portion - Sch XIII (10)(d), AS SUBSTITUTED w.e.f. 01-DEC-2025: 40 PER CENT of the anchor portion reserved: 33.33 per cent for DOMESTIC MUTUAL FUNDS and 6.67 per cent for LIFE INSURANCE COMPANIES AND PENSION FUNDS; undersubscription in the insurer/pension slice may go to domestic MFs. (Earlier rule: one-third reserved for domestic MFs - footnote 794.)
- Price discovery: anchor bidding opens ONE DAY BEFORE the issue opening date (Sch XIII (10)(e)); allocation completed the same day ((10)(g)); if the discovered issue price is HIGHER, anchors pay the difference; if LOWER, no refund - anchors keep paying their higher price ((10)(h)).
- Disclosure timing - Sch XIII (10)(i): number of shares allocated to anchors AND the allocation price must be given to the exchanges for publication on the exchange website BEFORE THE ISSUE OPENS. This is the anchor list PDF on BSE/NSE.
- Exclusions - Sch XIII (10)(k): no lead-manager associates (with carve-outs for their sponsored MFs, insurers, AIFs, pension funds, non-individual FPIs) and no person related to promoter/promoter group (deemed related if shareholder/voting agreement, veto rights, or board nomination right).
- Anchor lock-in: 50% for 90 days, 50% for 30 days, from allotment (Sch XIII (10)(j)).
- Margin: anchors pay full margin on application like others; balance within 2 days of issue closure (Sch XIII (10)(f), (11)).

## E.2 Allocation splits - Reg 32
- Reg 6(1) route (profitability track record met): Reg 32(1) - RII not less than 35%, NII not less than 15%, QIB not more than 50% (5% of QIB portion for mutual funds; MFs also eligible in balance QIB).
- Reg 6(2) route (profitability test NOT met): Reg 32(2) - QIB NOT LESS THAN 75%, NII not more than 15%, RII NOT MORE THAN 10%. Plus Reg 6(2) obliges the issuer to REFUND the full subscription if it fails to allot 75% of the net offer to QIBs.
- Eligibility test that decides the route - Reg 6(1): (a) net tangible assets >= Rs 3 crore in EACH of the preceding 3 full years (restated, consolidated), of which not more than 50% monetary assets (monetary-asset test waived for pure OFS); (b) AVERAGE OPERATING PROFIT >= Rs 15 crore over the preceding 3 years WITH operating profit in EACH of the 3 years; (c) net worth >= Rs 1 crore in each of the 3 years; (d) if name changed in the last year, 50% of revenue from the new-name activity.
- Signal value: a 6(2) filing means the company failed at least one of these - most commonly the Rs 15 crore average operating-profit / positive-every-year test. Retail gets only 10%; the issue lives or dies on institutional appetite. Always state the route in the report; the route is disclosed on the cover page and in "Other Regulatory and Statutory Disclosures" (eligibility paragraph).

## E.3 NII sub-categorisation and allotment mechanics
- Reg 32(3A) (inserted w.e.f. 01-Apr-2022): within the NII portion - ONE-THIRD reserved for applications > Rs 2 lakh and up to Rs 10 lakh ("small NII/sNII"); TWO-THIRDS for applications > Rs 10 lakh ("big NII/bNII"); spillover between the two sub-categories permitted.
- Reg 49(4A) + Schedule XIV Part B illustration: each NII allottee gets NOT LESS THAN the minimum NII application size, subject to availability; when oversubscribed, allottees are SELECTED BY DRAW OF LOTS and each winner gets the minimum application size; remaining shares (if any) on a proportionate basis. So NII allotment is lottery-first, not proportionate - large NII applications no longer buy proportionate allocation.
- Retail: allotment of at least the minimum bid lot per investor, remainder proportionate (Reg 49(4)).
- Minimum retail application value: Rs 10,000-15,000 band (Reg 47(2)).
- Minimum allottees for any public issue: 1,000 (Reg 49(1)). Minimum subscription: 90% of the offer through offer document, except pure OFS (Reg 45(1)).

## E.4 Analyst checks (concrete)
- Pull the anchor allocation PDF from BSE/NSE the evening before opening. Compute: (i) domestic MF share of the anchor book vs the 33.33% floor - a book barely at the floor, padded with sundry AIFs, NBFC treasuries and round-trip family offices, signals weak institutional conviction; (ii) count distinct DECISION-MAKERS, not schemes - 12 schemes of one AMC is one decision; (iii) flag anchors who habitually appear in every IPO and exit at T+30 (cross-check their names against the 30-day unlock behaviour in prior issues).
- Check whether marquee long-only names (large-cap active MFs, insurance, EPFO-linked pension managers) are present vs momentum vehicles.
- Post-30-day and post-90-day: check bulk/block deal data on the exchanges for anchor exits at unlock (names are public from the allocation PDF).
- For 6(2) issues: treat the 75% QIB floor as a structural fragility - if QIB book is thin on day 3, withdrawal risk is real.
