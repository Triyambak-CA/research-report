# J. THE EVIDENCE BASE ON SME IPO OUTCOMES

## J.1 SEBI's own data - Consultation Paper on Review of SME segment framework, dated 19-Nov-2024 (PDF saved sebi-cp-sme-nov2024.pdf; comments closed 04-Dec-2024; led to Board PR 37/2024 of 18-Dec-2024 and the 08-Mar-2025 ICDR amendments)
All figures below are FROM the consultation paper (data as on 15-Oct-2024 unless stated):
- Scale: 745 SME companies listed (NSE 417 + BSE 328), combined market cap approx Rs 2 lakh crore. Since inception: 565 exclusive listings on NSE, 524 on BSE; 322 migrated to main board (NSE 140, BSE 182).
- Fund raising: FY24 = 196 SME IPOs raising > Rs 6,000 crore (NSE 138 issues Rs 4,621 cr; BSE 58 issues Rs 1,474 cr). FY25 till 15-Oct-2024 = 159 IPOs, > Rs 5,700 crore. OFS component historically small even pre-cap (e.g. NSE FY24: Rs 273 cr OFS vs Rs 4,348 cr fresh).
- SUBSCRIPTION-MULTIPLE INFLATION (para 19): applicant-to-allottee ratio rose from 4X (FY22) to 46X (FY23) to 245X (FY24). This is the single best number for the "manufactured frenzy" thesis.
- Listing outcomes (para 18, listings in FY24 + FY25-to-date): NSE - 224 of 255 listed at a premium, 31 at discount; of the 224 listing-day gainers, 185 still showed gains vs issue price on 15-Oct-2024, 39 had turned to losses. BSE - 91 of 100 listed at premium; 65 of the 91 still gainers. NOTE: this snapshot was taken near the top of the SME cycle; it shows listing-pop persistence, not long-run alpha. No official long-horizon (3-5 yr) SME return study found - nothing verified on long-run SME IPO returns from an official source.
- GOING DARK / DEATH RATE (paras 11): NSE - 12 of 417 suspended; 15 with ZERO trades in the preceding month. BSE - 28 of 328 suspended; 35 with zero trades in the preceding month. So roughly 5-10% of BSE SME counters are suspended or entirely untraded at any time.
- Post-listing behaviour: preferential issues are the dominant follow-on route (169 preferential issues raising Rs 2,049 cr over FY22-FY25TD, vs 3 FPOs and 2 QIPs); heavy bonus/split activity (98 bonus issues + 13 splits over the window) - classic float-and-sentiment management.
- RPT intensity (para 22, top-50 SME entities FY23 annual reports): 1 in 2 SME listed entities had RPTs > Rs 10 crore; 1 in 5 had RPTs > Rs 50 crore; 1 in 2 had RPTs > 10% of consolidated turnover; 1 in 7 had RPTs > 50% of turnover.
- Named failure modes (para 21): diversion of IPO/rights proceeds to promoter-controlled shell companies; circular trades among related/connected parties to inflate revenue and "create positive sentiment to induce investors".

## J.2 Named enforcement actions, 2024-2026 (order dates verified from SEBI website listings/press; classification of facts from the orders as reported)
1. TRAFIKSOL ITS TECHNOLOGIES LTD (BSE SME, approx Rs 45 cr IPO per press reports, Sep-2024): SEBI halted the LISTING ITSELF (interim order Oct-2024) over a plan to pay ~40% of proceeds to a third-party software "vendor" that was a shell with no financials; order of 03-Dec-2024 directed REFUND of all application money with interest within a week (SEBI order page: "Order in the matter of Trafiksol ITS Technologies Ltd", Dec-2024); Final Order Aug-2026 (SEBI enforcement page, aug-2026). First case of an SME IPO cancelled between allotment and listing.
2. DEBOCK INDUSTRIES LTD (NSE SME 2018, migrated to mainboard 2022): interim ex-parte order 23-Aug-2024 (corrigendum 28-Aug-2024); confirmatory order Dec-2024; final order Aug-2026 - penalties totalling Rs 29.57 crore on the company, MD Mukesh Manveer Singh and 9 others; findings: majority of sales/purchases fictitious, circular transactions engineered the MAINBOARD MIGRATION, forged bank statements, rights-issue proceeds routed via related entity to promoters and overseas; promoter barred 7 years.
3. SYNOPTICS TECHNOLOGIES LTD + FIRST OVERSEAS CAPITAL LTD (lead manager) (NSE SME, Jul-2023 IPO): SEBI interim order May-2025 (reported 06-May-2025) - approx 54% of issue proceeds (~Rs 19 cr) siphoned on/around listing day to entities at the MERCHANT BANKER'S direction, disguised as issue expenses; FOCL barred from new assignments; SEBI stated ~20 other FOCL-managed SME IPOs under examination (~Rs 100 cr). The landmark case for LEAD-MANAGER-DRIVEN diversion.
4. VARYAA CREATIONS LTD (BSE SME, fixed-price IPO Rs 20.10 cr, listed 30-Apr-2024): SEBI interim order 14-May-2025 - approx 71% of proceeds diverted to three unrelated entities on the instructions of the lead manager (Inventure Merchant Banker Services barred); promoter shares frozen.
5. ADD-SHOP E-RETAIL LTD (+ White Organic Agro) (BSE SME Sep-2018, migrated to mainboard Oct-2020): SEBI interim order May-2024 (reported 06-May-2024) barring 12 entities - over 46% of reported sales fictitious, RPTs without audit-committee approval, circular sales to inflate revenue and stock price.
6. Context actions: SEBI chairperson's public caution on SME segment ("unrealistic picture painted by promoters... bonus, splits, preferential allotments as sentiment tools"), reported 28-Aug-2024; SEBI advisory to auditors of SME companies reported 23-Aug-2024; Rachana Infrastructure administrative warning dated 19-May-2026 for inadequate SME IPO prospectus disclosures (minor but shows post-listing prospectus scrutiny).

## J.3 What this justifies in the skill
- The base rate of fraud-adjacent behaviour in the SME segment is materially higher than mainboard: SEBI's own consultation paper says diversion and circular-revenue cases are the REASON for the 2025 tightening.
- Distinct SME failure modes to test for, each now precedent-backed: (a) proceeds diverted via fake vendors/objects (Trafiksol); (b) revenue inflated via circular RPTs to engineer listing or migration (Debock, Add-Shop); (c) the LEAD MANAGER as the architect of diversion (Synoptics/FOCL, Varyaa/Inventure); (d) sentiment management via bonus/split/preferential after listing (SEBI CP data + chair's caution).
- Subscription multiples are a manipulated signal: 245X average applicant inflation means "oversubscribed 300 times" carries near-zero information about quality.
