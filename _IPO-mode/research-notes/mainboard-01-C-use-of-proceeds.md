# C. Use of proceeds forensics

Source: SEBI (ICDR) Regulations 2018, consolidated as amended to 21-Mar-2026 (local copy icdr-2026-03-21.pdf), read on 01-Sep-2026.

## C.1 General Corporate Purposes cap
- Reg 7(2): the amount for GCP as mentioned in the objects in the draft offer document and offer document SHALL NOT EXCEED 25 PER CENT of the amount being raised by the issuer.
- Definition Reg 2(1)(r): "general corporate purposes" include identified purposes for which no specific amount is allocated, or any amount so specified towards GCP.

## C.2 Unidentified inorganic growth cap
- Reg 7(3) (inserted by ICDR Amendment Regulations 2022 w.e.f. 14-Jan-2022):
  - GCP + objects where the issuer HAS NOT IDENTIFIED an acquisition or investment target: COMBINED cap of 35 PER CENT of the amount being raised.
  - Unidentified acquisition/investment objects ALONE: cap of 25 PER CENT of the amount being raised.
  - Both limits fall away if the acquisition/strategic-investment target IS identified and specific disclosures are made in the DRHP and offer document.

## C.3 Repayment/prepayment of borrowings as an object
- Permitted. Sch VI Part A (9)(A)(2): if an object is loan repayment, the document must disclose (a) name of lender, brief terms and conditions, amount outstanding; and (b) a CERTIFICATE FROM THE STATUTORY AUDITOR certifying that the loan being repaid was UTILISED FOR THE PURPOSE FOR WHICH IT WAS AVAILED (clause (b) inserted w.e.f. 31-Jan-2023; a peer-reviewed CA may certify periods not audited by the current statutory auditor or subsidiary loans, proviso inserted w.e.f. 08-Mar-2025).
- Analyst check: match the named lenders/amounts against the borrowings note in the restated financials; a "repayment of borrowings" object where the largest facilities are working-capital lines that will be redrawn is quasi-GCP. Where loans being repaid were lent by promoter-group entities or carried promoter guarantees, the repayment releases the promoter, not the company - check RPT chapter for cross-holding of that debt.
- Working-capital object: Sch VI Part A (9)(A)(5) requires basis of estimation, holding-norm assumptions, projected current assets breakup - the ONLY forward-looking numbers in the whole document; test the receivable/inventory day assumptions against the restated history.
- Interlock with lock-in: Reg 16(1) provisos - if the MAJORITY of fresh-issue proceeds is for capital expenditure, promoter lock-ins EXTEND (18 months -> 3 years on minimum contribution; 6 months -> 1 year on excess). "Capital expenditure" includes civil work, fixed assets, land, plant and machinery, and (w.e.f. 08-Mar-2025) repayment of loans taken for such capex. So the objects mix directly determines the supply calendar.

## C.4 Monitoring agency - Reg 41
- Reg 41(1): mandatory if the issue size EXCLUDING THE OFS PORTION exceeds Rs 100 CRORE. Monitoring agency = a CREDIT RATING AGENCY REGISTERED WITH SEBI (substituted from the earlier "public financial institution or scheduled commercial bank" - footnote 100). Exemption: issues by banks, public financial institutions, insurance companies.
- Reg 41(2): quarterly report in Schedule XI format until 100 PER CENT of proceeds utilised (earlier the words "excluding GCP proceeds" were omitted - i.e. GCP utilisation IS now monitored; Schedule XI item 5 requires "details of utilisation of proceeds stated as General Corporate Purpose (GCP) amount").
- Reg 41(3): board of directors and management must comment on the monitoring agency's findings in the Schedule XI report.
- Reg 41(4): issuer must publicly disseminate the report within 45 DAYS from each quarter end - on its website and filed with the stock exchanges.
- Analyst use post-listing: the quarterly Schedule XI filings on the exchange are the audit trail for objects vs actuals; deviations also flow to the audit committee/shareholder approval route under LODR Reg 32.

## C.5 OFS proceeds
- OFS proceeds go to the SELLING SHAREHOLDERS, not the company. Confirmed structurally in the current text: (i) Reg 41(1) computes the monitoring threshold "excluding the size of offer for sale by selling shareholders" because there is nothing for the company to utilise; (ii) Sch VI cover-page format (Sch X price band ad and Part E abridged prospectus) requires per-selling-shareholder "aggregate proceeds from offered shares"; (iii) Reg 45(1) exempts OFS from the 90 per cent minimum subscription requirement; (iv) Sch VI Part A (15)(A)(b): in an OFS the dividend for the entire year is payable to the transferees - the shares are pre-existing. Under s.28 of the Companies Act 2013 an OFS is a sale by members through the prospectus; the issuer merely facilitates.
- Analyst framing for an OFS-heavy issue: compute fresh-issue percentage of total issue. If OFS > 50 per cent: (a) the "objects" chapter governs only the small fresh slice - valuation support from "growth capital" narratives is weak; (b) monitoring agency may not even be triggered if fresh portion <= Rs 100 crore; (c) the WACA disclosure for the selling shareholders (B.4) tells you the sellers' realised multiple - state it; (d) issue expenses are typically shared pro-rata, check the fee-sharing disclosure in objects/issue-expenses.
