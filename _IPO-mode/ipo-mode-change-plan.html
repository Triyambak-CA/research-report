1. The change list

The surface is smaller than expected, because the design system and the validator are both section-count agnostic. I confirmed this by reading validate.py: it derives the section count from the chips in the document and only enforces contiguous numbering plus table-of-contents sync. A 28-section IPO report validates cleanly with no code change.

Recommendation

One overlay file, not a sibling skill. A separate india-ipo-deep-dive would mean duplicating or symlinking roughly 30 KB of design system and validator for no benefit, and it would let the two drift apart. 2. Step 0 gets a new question

The skill currently resolves a company name to a listed ticker. It needs to branch first, because the answer changes which spec file applies:

StatePrimary sourcePath
ListedAnnual reports, concalls, exchange filingsExisting 20 sections, unchanged
Mainboard IPO, draft or final document filedOffer document plus price band advertisementIPO mode, mainboard variant
SME IPO, draft document filedOffer document plus exchange filingsIPO mode, SME variant, higher scepticism prior
Announced but nothing filedNoneSTOP No primary source exists. Say so rather than assembling a report from media coverage.

Listing status and platform are both on the cover page of the offer document, so this costs one lookup.

4. New sections

Eight shared additions, then three that exist only for SME. Each is written to slot into the spec in the same imperative style as the existing sections.

N1 - Offer structure and eligibility route

State the fresh-issue versus offer-for-sale split in rupees and percent, and the eligibility route the issuer used. A mainboard issuer filing under the alternative route has failed the profitability test, and that single fact also forces three-quarters of the book to institutions and caps retail at a tenth. Verify the route in both the draft and final documents - it can change between them.

SME delta: recompute every eligibility metric yourself. Flag any that clears its threshold by less than a quarter, or clears only in the qualifying years. State whether the issuer would have failed the other exchange's test.
N2 - Use-of-proceeds forensics

Recompute each object against its statutory cap. A number sitting exactly at a cap is a design choice, so say so. For loan-repayment objects, list the lenders and match them to the borrowings note, flagging promoter-linked debt and guarantee releases. For working-capital objects, audit the holding-norm assumptions against actual history. Name the monitoring artefact that will exist after listing and diarise it.

SME delta: repaying promoter or related-party loans from proceeds is prohibited outright, directly or indirectly, so trace for indirect routes. Verify that capex vendors and quotation-givers are real companies with real filings.
N3 - Basis for issue price audit

Extract every key performance indicator, its definition and its certifier, and confirm the three-year lookback covering metrics shown to pre-IPO investors was honoured. Tabulate the weighted average cost of acquisition across each mandated window, and state the floor and cap as multiples of it. Then stress-test the issuer's own written justification for that gap against actual KPI growth. Pull the price band advertisement, which arrives two working days before bidding opens.

SME delta: this regime applies to SME issues too. If the chapter is absent or perfunctory, that is a compliance red flag, not a regime difference.
N4 - Post-listing supply calendar

Build the unlock table from the capital structure chapter. For each tranche give the share count, the percentage of post-issue capital, the holder's identity, that holder's cost multiple at the offer price, and how many days of expected traded value the tranche represents. Flag any issue where a large block unlocks early on a high multiple over cost.

Mainboard and SME ladders differ materially, and SME lock-ins are the longer of the two. Both ladders go into the spec as tables.
N5 - Anchor book and subscription quality

From the exchange's anchor allocation disclosure, compute the domestic mutual fund share and the insurer and pension share against their reserved floors, count how many genuinely distinct decision-makers are present, and flag books dominated by sundry alternative funds or serial flippers. Read the final-day subscription split across institutional, non-institutional and retail. After listing, check bulk and block data for anchor exits by name.

SME delta: anchors appear only in book-built issues and the minimum ticket is far smaller, so anchor names are a weaker signal. Check the issue type before applying book-building mechanics at all.
N6 - Draft-to-final document diff

Diff the two filings: eligibility route, issue size, fresh and offer-for-sale mix, the roll of financial periods, changes to restated numbers in overlapping years, risk factors added, changes to the KPI set or peer set, capital structure events, and any change of auditor or banker. Report the deltas as what the review surfaced and what the issuer re-engineered. Roughly an hour of work, and almost nobody does it.

SME delta: there is no regulator observation letter, so diff the draft against the final prospectus filed with the registrar, and use the 21-day public comment window as an additional input.
N7 - Intermediary track record

The offer document must carry the lead manager's price performance on their recent issues, benchmark-adjusted at 30, 90 and 180 days. Compute the median and the share of their issues trading below offer at six months, and put that base rate next to your own valuation conclusion. Check the auditor's peer-review status and any change of auditor before filing.

SME delta: the lead manager underwrites the issue and takes a minimum stake on its own account, and arranges the market making. Treat it as a principal with its own incentives, not an agent, and say so in the report.
N8 - Restatement and litigation sweep

Table the audited-versus-restated reconciliation for each year and explain every material adjustment. Recompute the litigation materiality floor yourself and compare it with the policy threshold the board chose. Sum litigation against the company, promoters, directors and subsidiaries as a share of net worth. Note the group-company blind spot and pull registry data to fill it.

The obligation to disclose loss-making and negative-net-worth group companies was removed in Aug-2021. Silence about the promoter group is not evidence of health.

SME only

N9 - Liquidity and exit-ability note

Trailing six-month average daily value, the share of days actually traded, and the position size exitable at a tenth of daily volume. Surveillance status and history. What happens to the position if the counter enters a restricted-trading framework. State the lot-size floor, since a holding cannot be sold below one lot except to the market maker.

N10 - Migration scorecard

Compute the distance to every mainboard migration gate on both exchanges. Classify as realistic within three years, plausible, or never. If never, the terminal state is an illiquid half-yearly-reporting counter, and the valuation must say so.

N11 - Governance cliff box

A mandatory plain-words paragraph in every SME report: after listing this company publishes results and a shareholding pattern only twice a year, most corporate-governance obligations do not apply to it, and earnings calls are voluntary and unlikely. Conviction must be discounted for a six-month information vacuum.

Open design decision

This takes a mainboard report to about 27 sections and an SME report to about 31. That may be too long. Before writing the spec, decide whether to fold some additions into existing sections - the supply calendar could live inside Section 13, the restatement table inside Section 6 - or accept the longer report. My inclination is to fold N4 into 13 and N8 into 6, keeping six genuinely new sections. Worth your call.

6. Traps to append

The two research tracks threw up 27 candidates between them. After removing the overlaps, expect roughly 20 written entries. None are drafted yet. The ten below are the ones that most change how a report reads:

  1. Oversubscription is not conviction. SEBI's own study of 144 issues found 54 percent of allotted shares outside the anchor book were sold within a week, and non-institutional investors sold 63 percent. On the SME side the applicant-to-allottee ratio inflated roughly sixty-fold in two years.
  2. The industry chapter is paid for by the issuer. Total addressable market definitions are drawn to flatter. Cross-check against the official macro sources in Section 20, which is exactly what that section is for.
  3. The price band is not in the prospectus. It arrives by advertisement two working days before bidding, together with the cost-multiple disclosures. Schedule the valuation work around that date.
  4. Offer-for-sale money is not the company's money. A headline issue size tells you nothing about what the business actually receives. Only the fresh slice is governed by the objects narrative.
  5. Silence about the promoter group is not good news. The duty to disclose loss-making and negative-net-worth group companies was deleted in Aug-2021.
  6. The pre-IPO margin inflection is suspect by default. It often coincides exactly with clearing an eligibility threshold. Check the restatement reconciliation before believing it.
  7. Grey market premium is not evidence. Unregulated, unverifiable and manipulable. A sentiment footnote at most, never an input to valuation.
  8. Do not read the draft when the final exists - and do not assume nothing changed between them. In the worked example the eligibility route itself flipped.
  9. SME rules from before Mar-2025 are stale. Application size, allottee minimum, the general-corporate-purposes cap, lock-in periods and the monitoring threshold all changed.
  10. A listing-day price cap is not price discovery. On SME the opening price is capped, so a cap-hit listing tells you demand exceeded that cap and nothing more.

7. What we could not verify

Both agents were instructed to flag rather than guess. These carry into the spec as explicit gaps, and should be re-checked before anyone relies on them:

  • No SEBI document addresses grey market premium directly. The position rests on inference from the securities law framework plus consultation language, not a citable rule.
  • The applicability date of the KPI industry standards is from secondary summaries; the circular number and date are primary.
  • No requirement exists to disclose the fee paid for the commissioned industry report. The "commissioned and paid for" language is market practice, evidenced from an actual filing.
  • The SME secondary-market lot-size floor after the 2025 application-size change: nothing verified. The 2012 circular still appears to govern.
  • A conflict in the SME rules between auditor certificates said to accompany quarterly results and a half-yearly results obligation. SEBI has not clarified it.
  • SME earnings-call prevalence is a practice observation. No official count exists.
  • No official long-horizon SME return study. The available regulator data is a single cycle-top snapshot.
  • SME practice findings rest on three prospectuses only. Do not over-generalise them.
One citation corrected

The two agents cited the same litigation-materiality rule differently. I checked the actual regulation text: it sits at Schedule VI Part A, item (12)(A)(1)(v), substituted with effect from 08-Mar-2025. The SME agent's reference to item (14) was wrong and is corrected here. The banker price-information table at item (14)(R) checks out.

8. Corrections to my first read

What I said earlierWhat the regulation actually says
A third of the anchor book is reserved for domestic mutual fundsChanged on 01-Dec-2025. Forty percent of the anchor portion is now reserved, split between mutual funds and life insurers and pension funds.
A capex-heavy issue may shorten promoter lock-inInverted. A capex-majority fresh issue lengthens promoter lock-in substantially, on both the minimum contribution and the excess.
The offer document gives a full picture of loss-making group companiesRemoved from the regulation in Aug-2021. Names and top-five financials only, the latter hosted on the group companies' own websites.
Whether the KPI and cost-of-acquisition regime applies to SME was the open questionIt applies. Its absence from an SME prospectus is a compliance red flag, not a regime difference. This was the single biggest open item and it resolves in favour of keeping the valuation work intact.
