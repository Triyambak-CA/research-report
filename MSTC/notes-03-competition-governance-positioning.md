## C1. Competition - MANAGEMENT'S OWN WORDS (Q3 FY25 call, 11-Feb-2025, Bhanu Kumar, Director Commercial)
"the entry barriers to e-commerce business is very, very low. So anyone having a computer and some
software skills can set up an e-commerce business venture. So I have competition from almost on a daily
basis from anyone, fly-by-night operators to established players like M Junction Services and C1 India.
So these are big companies who have been in this business for a pretty long time. THEY ARE OUR BIGGEST
COMPETITORS."
On losing business: "the NPA property business, we had lost to a software development team. Actually, the
public sector banks, on their own, they had set up a portal with some technology partner, and they are
operating it themselves. So whenever any organization decides to have its own portal, then that is
definitely it takes away a lot of our revenue."
Why PSBs left: MSTC's portal lacked integrated financing modules, property marketing, and commission-agent
onboarding - "they need one big ecosystem".
On Coal India: CIL "decided that they should have an absolute control on the sale of coal also ... they do
have 2 service providers for this ... But then there were some technical issues ... they decided that it is
better to have a third-party service provider." => CIL tried in-housing, failed, came back. That is the
single best evidence FOR the moat, and it is also proof the client can attempt it at will.
Client stickiness: "somebody like Indus Towers or Bharti Airtel, they are totally dependent on us ... some
groups like Tata's and Reliance and all, they do have multiple service providers."
Stated pull factor: "we are probably having the biggest buyer base as far as scrap sale is concerned, and
there's no one matching that kind of numbers." (unverifiable, management claim)
Admitted under-pricing: "just for a couple of projects for the government of India, maybe we are
under-pricing" (Q1 FY26).

## C4. THE COAL EXCHANGE - the single biggest structural swing factor
- Ministry of Coal notified the COAL EXCHANGE RULES, 2026 on 04-Jun-2026.
- Coal Controller Organisation (CCO) is the registering and regulating authority; authorisations for 25 years.
- Shifts coal sales from a "one-to-many" auction-agent model to a "many-to-many" exchange model, with
  delivery-based spot trading on standardised contracts.
- 16 third-party agencies (TPAs) empanelled for coal sampling/analysis as of 31-Jul-2026.
- MSTC response (Q1 FY27 IP, 13-Aug-2026): "MSTC is in the process of examining the feasibility of setting
  up Coal & other Mineral exchanges."  mjunction has already submitted framework recommendations.
- MANAGEMENT'S OWN COAL NUMBERS ARE CONTRADICTORY:
    Q1 FY26 call (14-Aug-2025, Bhanu Kumar): "we earned about INR4 crores from coal auctions in the first
      quarter ... roughly about INR3 crores to INR4 crores per quarter is the revenue from coal auctions"
      (including Meghalaya, PDCL, Jharkhand, not just CIL) => ~Rs 12-16 cr/yr = 3-4% of revenue.
    Q4 FY26 call (30-May-2026, CMD): "Coal does form a significant part of our revenue stream."
  These cannot both be right. Note that commercial coal BLOCK auctions (10 mines FY26, 22 blocks in
  rounds 13-14, 17 mines in round 15) are event-fee mandates separate from coal e-auction/linkage.
  Either way, MSTC has never disclosed coal revenue in any filing. UNRESOLVED - flag as a data gap.

## C7. Regulatory and tax exposures
- SECTION 201(1)/201(1A) TDS DEMAND: notice u/s 156 received 15-Apr-2025 from the Income Tax Officer
  (TDS) for AY 2024-25 - Rs 178.40 cr (tax Rs 150.56 cr + interest Rs 27.85 cr). MSTC: "no material
  impact ... the demand is appealable and company will appeal." NOT included in the FY25 contingent
  liability schedule (post-balance-sheet). NEVER discussed on any concall. NO analyst has ever asked.
  Section 201 = assessee-in-default for failure to deduct or pay TDS. For a platform of MSTC's GMV this
  points to TDS on facilitated transactions (194-O / 194Q / 206C territory). If the position is upheld
  the exposure is RECURRING across years, not a one-off. FY26 status is unknown - the FY26 annual report
  is not yet published (AGM 24-Sep-2026). THIS IS THE SINGLE LARGEST UNPRICED RISK.
- Disclosed contingent liabilities FY25 Rs 395.03 cr (FY24 Rs 306.52 cr, +28.9%) = 53.5% of FY25 net
  worth; add the Rs 178.40 cr Sec 201 demand and it is Rs 573.43 cr = 78% of FY25 net worth, or 63% of
  FY26 net worth of Rs 903.94 cr.
- Credit rating: ACUITE A+ (Stable) long-term / ACUITE A1+ short-term, REAFFIRMED 27-Apr-2026, on
  Rs 110 cr of PROPOSED (i.e. undrawn, unlisted) bank facilities - Rs 10 cr long-term + Rs 100 cr short
  term. Rated by Acuite (a smaller agency), and a CARE Ratings letter also exists (17-Mar-2026).
  NOTE: a company with zero real debt and Rs 1,328 cr of cash carrying only an A+ long-term rating is
  itself informative - the agency is capping on business risk (small scale, mandate concentration,
  contingent liabilities), not on leverage. There is no rated debt outstanding, so the rating carries
  little information about default risk and no rationale document with risk commentary was filed.
- MSTC filed the "Large Corporate" initial disclosure (Annexure A) on 09-Apr-2025 and 09-Apr-2026 - a
  formality; it has no listed debt.
