Top 3 Positives

  • The collapse everyone is pricing happened to the wrong number. Options notional average daily turnover is still 23% below FY24, and that is the figure in every headline. But NSE levies transaction charges on premium turnover, and premium average daily turnover in the June-2026 quarter was Rs 6,43,518 million, an all-time high, above FY24, FY25 and FY26. Revenue grew 13.1% and transaction charges grew 14.9% in that quarter. The regulatory reset compressed contract counts, not the fee base.
  • A near-monopoly that regulation makes almost impossible to attack. 92.99% of cash equity turnover, 99.79% of equity futures, and the largest derivatives exchange in the world by contracts traded for seven consecutive years. A new entrant needs SEBI recognition, Rs 100 crore of net worth and, decisively, liquidity, which cannot be licensed. The last serious challenger now litigates against NSE rather than competing with it.
  • Extraordinary economics with no capital behind them. Nil borrowings, a 78.81% operating EBITDA margin in the June quarter, return on capital employed of 42.8% even in a bad year, and 84% of profit paid out as dividend. The structurally growing lines, listing fees, data, indices and colocation, all grew 12% to 33% in the year transaction charges fell.

Top 3 Risks

  • Sixty per cent of revenue sits in the one product the regulator is deliberately restraining, and the restraint is not finished. Equity options are 60.2% of revenue from operations. SEBI has tightened four times since Oct-2024 and the Union Budget raised securities transaction tax on options premium again with effect from 01-Apr-2026. The regulator that shrinks this revenue line also approves NSE's fees, its board and its listing.
  • The settlement is paid but not final, and that is not how it is being reported. NSE paid Rs 1,491.21 crore on 31-Jul-2026, and the Supreme Court closed its appeals on 03-Sep-2026. But NSE's own RHP says SEBI's acceptance is in principle only and that "the settlement order by SEBI in this regard is yet to be passed", and the Court expressly kept the questions of law open as against other respondents. No SEBI settlement order has been published as at 21-Sep-2026.
  • The cap price already assumes the recovery continues uninterrupted for a decade. Roughly 19% compound earnings growth for ten years, from a base where the most recent full year fell 15.5% and the recovery is one quarter old. At 12.5x book for a business whose regulator caps what it may charge, there is no margin for the reset resuming.

"This is a bet on the tollbooth of Indian capital markets compounding with a financialising economy, priced as if one quarter of recovery has settled the question of whether the regulator's restraint was an interruption or a reset, and it breaks if SEBI tightens again before the annuity lines grow large enough to matter."

SECTION 01

Industry and Sector Analysis

NSE does not compete in an industry so much as operate a piece of national infrastructure. The question is not market size but who controls the tollbooth's pricing, and the answer is SEBI, not NSE.

Source Warning - The Industry Chapter Is Paid For

The industry chapter rests on a report titled "Industry report on Capital Markets and Market Infrastructure Institutions in India" dated 08-Sep-2026, prepared by Redseer Strategy Consultants Private Limited and, in the offer document's own words at page 155, "commissioned by and paid for by our Company exclusively in connection with the Offer". The same passage records that "Neither we, nor the BRLMs, nor any other person connected with the Offer has independently verified any third-party statistical, financial and other industry information in the Redseer Report", and that excerpts "may have been re-ordered by us for the purposes of presentation". The DRHP carried an earlier version of the same report dated 16-Jun-2026.

This does not mean the chapter is ignored. For an unlisted issuer it is the single richest source of industry data available, much of it drawn from SEBI, the Reserve Bank and the IMF, and this report uses it extensively and attributes it throughout. What it does mean is that its framing, its base years and any addressable-market definition are treated as issuer claims to be tested rather than as findings. Section 17 builds the independent counterweight from official sources, and where the two disagree both are shown.

What the industry actually is

India has two stock exchanges of consequence, NSE and BSE, two depositories, NSDL and CDSL, and a handful of specialised venues, MCX in commodities and IEX in power. Every one is a Market Infrastructure Institution under the SEBI (Stock Exchanges and Clearing Corporations) Regulations 2018, a category carrying obligations no ordinary company faces: a public-interest board, SEBI approval for senior appointments, ownership caps on shareholders, and regulatory control over what may be charged.

That last point is the one most often missed. This is not a free-pricing business. Transaction charges are subject to regulatory oversight, and SEBI has intervened directly, most consequentially in requiring a uniform, non-slab fee structure from Oct-2024, which compressed the effective yield exchanges earned from high-volume members. A monopolist who cannot set his own price is a very particular kind of monopolist, and every valuation multiple in this report has to carry that fact.

! The Most Important Distinction In This Report

Options notional turnover and options premium turnover are not the same number, and NSE earns on the second one. Notional average daily turnover is still 23% below FY24 and that is the figure behind every "derivatives collapsed" headline. Premium average daily turnover in the June-2026 quarter was Rs 6,43,518 million, the highest of any period disclosed, above FY24's Rs 6,17,788 million. SEBI's curbs worked by raising contract sizes and cutting weekly expiries, which mechanically shrinks notional while leaving the premium pool intact. Anyone modelling NSE's revenue off notional turnover is modelling the wrong base.

But the record is not a share win, and the report will not let it read as one. The offer document's own peer table shows BSE's options premium turnover grew 96.3% year on year in the June quarter against NSE's 15.9%. The premium pool grew 33.1%. NSE reached a record because the pool expanded, while its share of that pool fell from 78.6% to 68.5% in twelve months. Section 3 sets out the full series. Both things are true: the fee base recovered, and NSE is losing share of it quickly.

The regulatory timeline that did the damage

SEBI's index-derivatives framework, circular SEBI/HO/MRD/TPD-1/P/CIR/2024/132 dated 01-Oct-2024, phased in from 20-Nov-2024: minimum contract value raised from roughly Rs 5 lakh to Rs 15 lakh, weekly expiries cut to one per exchange per index, an additional 2% extreme loss margin on expiry days, and calendar-spread benefit removed on expiry. Intraday position-limit monitoring followed on 01-Apr-2025 and end-of-day limits on 01-Jul-2025. A single weekly index-expiry day was then fixed, Tuesday for NSE and Thursday for BSE, for contracts expiring on or after 01-Sep-2025. NSE discontinued Bank Nifty, Nifty Financial Services, Nifty Midcap and Nifty Next 50 weeklies as a direct consequence.

On top of that, securities transaction tax on options premium rose from 0.0625% to 0.1% with effect from 01-Oct-2024, and again to 0.15% from 01-Apr-2026, with futures moving from 0.02% to 0.05%. The tax increases are a second, independent squeeze on the same activity, and the second one only began in the current financial year.

Who captures value across the chain

The chain runs issuer, exchange, clearing corporation, depository, broker, investor. NSE owns three of those links: the exchange, NSE Clearing outright, and the Nifty index franchise. Value capture is being redistributed by regulation rather than competition. NSE was compelled to cut its NSDL stake from 24.00% to 15.00% under Regulation 21 of the SEBI (Depositories and Participants) Regulations 2018, and did so by selling into NSDL's own IPO, with NSDL listing on 06-Aug-2025. NSE Clearing must maintain a Core Settlement Guarantee Fund of roughly Rs 130.8 billion out of profits that would otherwise be shareholders'.

Barriers to entry, and the price of them

The barriers here are close to absolute and they are legal rather than economic. A new exchange needs SEBI recognition, Rs 100 crore of net worth, a public-interest board and, decisively, liquidity, which cannot be licensed. The last serious attempt, MSEI, failed and now litigates against NSE rather than competing with it. But the same regulation that keeps competitors out caps what the incumbent may earn, who may own it and who may sit on its board. That trade is the defining feature of the sector and it runs through every section that follows.

Business Model and Value Chain

A vertically integrated, multi-asset exchange owning trading, clearing, data and indices. Three reporting segments, but in practice one revenue engine with several small annuity businesses attached.

How the money actually arrives

A trading member executes a trade on the NSE platform and NSE levies a transaction charge on the value of that trade. For options the charge is levied on premium turnover. That single mechanical fact explains why FY26 options revenue fell only 1.9% while notional volumes fell 17.4%, and why June-quarter options revenue rose 15.6%.

Competitive Landscape and Peer Benchmarking

The issuer names exactly one listed peer. That choice is defensible and also convenient, because the one peer it names trades on a higher multiple than the one it is asking for.

Auditing the issuer's peer set

In Basis for Offer Price the company compares itself with BSE Limited and nothing else. The justification offered is that BSE is the only other multi-asset Indian exchange spanning cash, derivatives, mutual funds and debt with a comparable revenue mix. On the merits that is fair: CDSL is a depository, MCX is a single-commodity venue, CAMS is a registrar. None is a stock exchange.

But a single-peer table with a peer on a trailing price to earnings of roughly 47x sets a comfortable ceiling. NSE is asking for about 42x at the cap on trailing-twelve-month earnings. Against that single comparator the issue looks cheap. Against the global exchange set it does not. Both views belong in the report, so both are given.

Competitive Advantage: the 6 Cs Moat Framework

One of the widest moats available in Indian equities, and one of the few where the moat's owner does not control the toll. Rate the moat strong and the pricing power weak, and both are true.

CostStrong

The marginal cost of matching one more trade is effectively zero, and fixed costs are spread over the largest order flow in the country. Operating EBITDA margin of 78.81% in the June quarter, on a cost base where total expenses excluding the Core Settlement Guarantee Fund contribution were Rs 59,999.03 million against Rs 187,133.70 million of income in FY26. No competitor at a quarter of the scale can match the unit economics.

CapacityStrong

Capacity is technological, not physical, and NSE has demonstrated it at the highest contract volumes in the world for seven consecutive years. Colocation racks nearly doubled in two years, from 934 to 1,868, which is capacity being filled by paying customers rather than built on hope. The constraint is resilience, not throughput, and the 24-Feb-2021 outage is the reminder.

CapitalStrong

Nil borrowings across all three restated years and the stub. Return on capital employed of 42.8% in a bad year. The business funds a Rs 130.8 billion Core Settlement Guarantee Fund, an 84% dividend payout and its entire capital expenditure out of operating cash. It has never needed external equity, which is precisely why this offer raises none.

ChannelModerate

Distribution runs through roughly 1,400 trading members, and the top ten are 46.78% of revenue. NSE does not own the investor relationship. In practice members cannot move flow to an illiquid venue, so the concentration has never bitten. But the channel is the one place a regulator-created alternative, such as a protected expiry day on a rival exchange, can redirect volume without NSE doing anything wrong.

Customer captivityModerate

The deepest moat here is liquidity: an order book attracts orders because it already has orders. Nifty is the benchmark 435-index family with Rs 8.95 trillion of passive assets tracking it, and switching costs for an algorithmic firm with racks in the NSE data centre are physical as well as commercial. Downgraded from strong on the evidence. NSE's share of the options premium pool fell from 96.7% in FY24 to 68.5% in the June-2026 quarter once SEBI allocated BSE a separate weekly expiry day. Captivity that survives competition but not a regulator's calendar is not captivity in the sense this framework means, and it is 60% of revenue that proved movable.

Compliance moatStrong

SEBI recognition under the SECC Regulations, a Rs 100 crore net-worth floor, public-interest directors and fit-and-proper tests on shareholders. Entry is not merely expensive, it is discretionary in the regulator's hands. The identical regulation is also the principal risk, which is the paradox of this asset.

The single most important moat source, and its half-life

Liquidity network effect, and it has no natural expiry. No incumbent exchange anywhere has lost a deep liquidity pool to a competitor on price. The half-life is therefore not set by competition but by regulation, and there the record is explicit: SEBI has already redirected options flow to BSE through the expiry-day allocation, forced NSE's NSDL stake down from 24% to 15%, mandated a uniform fee structure, and restrained the derivatives product that is 60% of revenue.

The honest statement of the moat is this: the franchise is close to unassailable commercially and entirely assailable administratively. The options share series is the proof rather than the theory: nearly thirty points moved in two years without a single competitive act by BSE. An investor here is not underwriting competitive risk. They are underwriting Indian regulatory policy for a decade, and buying at a multiple that assumes it stays broadly benign.

The company presents a Normalised Operating EBITDA Margin of 76.23% for FY26 against a reported 66.85%. Its own definition adds back, among other items, "Additional Contribution to Core SGF" and "SEBI Settlement Fees".

A cost incurred in every year of the disclosed record, on six distinct matters, is not self-evidently non-recurring. The eligibility route, which is a one-line quality gate

The offer is made under Regulation 6(1), not the Regulation 6(2) fallback. That means NSE satisfied the profitability test: net tangible assets of at least Rs 3 crore in each of the preceding three years, average operating profit of at least Rs 15 crore over three years with each year positive, and net worth of at least Rs 1 crore in each year. For a company earning Rs 10,302 crore of annual profit those bars are trivial, but the route still matters: a Reg 6(2) issuer has failed the profit test, and that would have forced 75% of the net offer to institutions and capped retail at 10%.

Because this is a Reg 6(1) issue, the allocation is the standard one: retail at least 35%, non-institutional at least 15%, qualified institutional buyers not more than 50%, with up to 60% of the institutional portion available to anchors. Retail therefore sets a meaningful part of the price here, which Section 21 shows mattered.

The document does not give a reason. The pattern of the cut does. The two sellers who did not reduce their tranches are the two most recent institutional entrants, Canada Pension Plan Investment Board at a cost of Rs 324.13 a share and Aranda Investments at Rs 62.38.   • There will be no monitoring agency reports.   • The usual diversion risk is nil. The most common Indian enforcement failure mode, proceeds diverted away from stated objects, is structurally impossible in a pure offer for sale. That is a real, if negative, comfort.

The SECC ownership cap, which every buyer must understand before bidding

A Company Nobody Is Allowed To Control

Under the SECC Regulations: public shareholding must be at least 51%; an individual resident investor may hold no more than 5%; certain institutional categories may hold up to 15%; a non-resident investor is capped at 5% with aggregate non-resident holding not exceeding 49%; and holders above a threshold must be fit and proper. Minimum net worth for the exchange is Rs 100 crore.

The consequences for a shareholder are concrete and they cut both ways. There will never be a takeover bid, a control premium, or an activist block. No strategic buyer can accumulate, so the usual backstop under a falling share price does not exist. Equally, no one can raid the company, and the free float is structurally permanent. Any valuation applying a control-premium or takeover-optionality argument to NSE is applying a framework the law forbids.

Regulatory advantages, which are real and are the other half of the ledger

The same framework that constrains NSE protects it absolutely. Entry requires SEBI recognition, which is discretionary. Fee competition is bounded because the fee structure is regulated for everyone. The clearing corporation requirement makes a low-cost challenger structurally impossible. And SEBI's own interest in systemic stability makes it a poor regulator to bet against the incumbent: the measures of the last two years restrained a product, they did not threaten the franchise.

Concentration risks, stated plainly

Single product, equity options, 60.2% of revenue. Single geography, India, essentially 100%. Single regulator. Top ten members 46.78% of revenue. Single primary data centre region. And now a single listing venue which is also the only competitor.

Return on the capital deployed

The honest computation is difficult because revenue is not attributable to capex in a business where the marginal trade is free to process. Two approaches, both stated with their limits.

Direct attribution, the one line where it works. Colocation is the only revenue line that maps to a physical asset. Rack revenue grew from Rs 1,338.14 million in FY24 to Rs 2,051.75 million in FY26, an increase of Rs 713.61 million, against racks growing from 934 to 1,680. Annualising the June-2026 quarter gives roughly Rs 2,376 million on 1,868 racks. That is approximately Rs 1.27 million of annual revenue per rack, at very high incremental margin since the data centre is already built. The associated capex is not separately disclosed, so a true internal rate of return cannot be computed. Stated as not computable rather than invented.

Aggregate incremental return. Profit before tax from continuing operations went from Rs 111,842.80 million in FY24 to Rs 138,955.79 million in FY26, an increase of Rs 27,112.99 million, against cumulative capex over the two years of Rs 30,604.72 million. That is a crude incremental return well above any plausible cost of equity, but it is contaminated: the earnings change is driven overwhelmingly by market volumes and by the settlement charge, not by the capex. The number is presented and then discounted, because for this business capex is not what generates the return.

The capital allocation verdict

NSE has no reinvestment outlet at its incremental return, and it knows it. Total capex of Rs 17,547.83 million in FY26 is 17% of profit. The remainder cannot be productively deployed inside the business, the regulator has explicitly objected to deploying it outside the business, and so it is being distributed: payout has risen 53.6%, 71.1%, 84.1% across the three years. That is the correct answer and the company is giving it.

The implication for valuation matters. A business distributing 84% of earnings cannot compound book value quickly, so the return to a shareholder is dividend plus whatever earnings growth the market delivers. It is a yield-plus-growth asset dressed as a growth asset, and at 42x earnings the yield is under 2%.

Here there are 29 separate fund houses and 27 separate insurance and pension entities, which is 56 independent institutional judgements, and both reserved categories were filled well above their floors rather than scraped. There is no sign of a book padded out with sundry alternative investment funds or short-horizon vehicles, and domestic long-only money, which is the most diligence-intensive participant in an Indian IPO, took the largest share voluntarily.

One governance check the regulation requires: lead-manager associates and promoter-related institutional buyers may not hold anchor allocation. With no promoter, the second limb is moot. On the first, the offer document discloses that Morgan Stanley India, ICICI Securities and SBI Capital Markets are restricted to marketing only precisely because of their associate relationships, which is the mechanism working as intended. See Section 23.

The Base Rate That Governs How To Read Any Of This

SEBI's study of 144 initial public offers from Apr-2021 to Dec-2023 found that 54% of shares allotted outside the anchor book were sold within one week of listing: 63.3% by non-institutional investors, 42.7% by retail, rising to 67.6% where listing gains exceeded 20%. Subscription measures flip demand, not conviction. A 1.16x book means the issue was covered. It says nothing about whether the buyers intend to hold, and the base rate says most will not. This report does not convert any subscription multiple into an expected listing gain or a price target, in the text, in a table, or by implication.

21c. Grey market premium, recorded once and relied on for nothing

Aggregator-reported grey market premium of approximately Rs 43 to Rs 55 per share as at 20-Sep-2026, 10:00 pm, which is roughly 2.4% to 3.1% of the cap price of Rs 1,785. Source: IPO aggregator platforms. Unregulated, unverifiable, recorded as a sentiment observation and not as evidence of value.

The grey market has no statutory basis. It is off-exchange forward dealing among private counterparties, legally unenforceable, quoted by a small number of dealers who profit from the trades they quote, and thin enough to move. SEBI does not publish, endorse or recognise it. It does not enter Section 19 in any form: no implied listing price, no expected return, no adjustment to fair value, no scenario weighting.

Its one legitimate analytical use is the direction of travel, and here the direction is stark. The premium peaked at approximately Rs 285 around 04-Sep-2026 and fell to Rs 43 to Rs 55 by 20-Sep-2026, a decline of roughly 81% between the filing of the red herring prospectus and the final day of bidding. A premium that collapses through the bidding window is a warning worth reporting even where the level is untrustworthy, because the direction is observable.

The coherent pattern, sourced to the underlying facts rather than to the premium. Four disclosed facts point the same way: retail undersubscribed at 0.72x; overall subscription of only 1.16x; the grey market premium down roughly 81% in a fortnight; and an unlisted dealer quote around Rs 2,059 on 17-Sep-2026 that sits far above both the cap price and the implied grey market price, which means the unlisted market has been converging downward toward the offer price rather than the offer pricing up toward it. Together these describe an issue that is fully priced, thinly covered, and unlikely to see a large listing pop. That is a positioning statement drawn from four sourced facts, not a forecast drawn from a dealer quote, and it is offered as such.

Against that, the cap price is below the certified one-year cost of acquisition (Section 18) and the anchor book is genuinely high quality. Those two facts point the other way. Both sets belong in the pitch in Section 25, and neither is suppressed.

! Conflict - Three Lead Managers Are Restricted To Marketing Only

The offer document discloses that three of the syndicate cannot perform the full role of a book running lead manager:

  • SBI Capital Markets is itself a selling shareholder, offering 8,780,590 shares. It was added as a seller by the addendum of 10-Aug-2026, after the draft.
  • Morgan Stanley India is an associate of MS Strategic (Mauritius), a selling shareholder offering 11,000,000 shares.
  • ICICI Securities is an associate of ICICI Lombard General Insurance, a selling shareholder.

Under Regulation 21A of the SEBI (Merchant Bankers) Regulations and Regulation 23(3) of the SEBI ICDR Regulations, all three "will be involved only in the marketing of the Offer". Each has signed the due diligence certificate and each is disclosed as a book running lead manager.

The disclosure is complete and the restriction is the regulation working exactly as designed. The point worth making is narrower and it is about how the offer reads from outside: three houses whose affiliates are selling the shares are nonetheless named on the cover as lead managers, and a reader who sees twenty banks on a prospectus will not, without reading the footnote, know that three of them are on the other side of the trade.

The lead managers' own price information

The offer document must carry each banker's record on its recent issues, showing price movement against the benchmark at 30, 90 and 180 days, covering three financial years and up to ten issues per banker. The disclosed issue counts for the principal houses across FY25 and FY26 are: JM Financial 27, Kotak Mahindra Capital 19, SBI Capital Markets 15, Citigroup 13, J.P. Morgan 10, Morgan Stanley 10, HSBC 9, Anand Rathi 3.

A limitation stated rather than glossed. The per-banker distributions of issues trading at a discount or premium at each interval are disclosed in the document across many pages, but this report has not recomputed a median benchmark-adjusted return per pricing banker from the underlying per-issue tables. Insufficient data for conviction on any individual banker's base rate, and the figure is therefore not asserted. What can be said is structural: a banker's own disclosure is bounded to at most ten issues and to three years, so it is by construction a favourable sample, and it should never be read as a complete record.

The more useful observation for this particular issue is that with twenty houses mandated, no single banker's track record is determinative of the pricing. The price was set by a committee of twenty in consultation with the company, and the outcome, a book of 1.16x with retail undersubscribed, suggests it was set at the top of what the market would bear.

Pre-Mortem and Anti-Thesis

The strongest case against, argued sincerely. It is imagined to be 2029 and the position has lost half its value. This is how it happened.

The short-seller's case, in eight points

  1. You bought a regulated utility at a growth multiple. 42x earnings and 12.5x book for a business whose fees are set under regulatory oversight, whose board requires regulatory approval, whose products need regulatory clearance, and whose largest revenue line the regulator has spent two years deliberately shrinking. Utilities do not get 42x.
  2. The recovery you underwrote was one quarter long. The June-2026 quarter was the entire evidential basis for believing FY26 was a trough. It was a single stub with one comparative, no seasonality could be assessed, and the comparative quarter was itself weak. If it was a volatility-driven exception, the base case never existed.
  3. The franchise was already being taken apart while you were reading the recovery. NSE's share of the options premium pool went 96.7%, 87.4%, 74.7% and 68.5% across FY24 to the June-2026 quarter. The record premium turnover you underwrote was pool growth of 33.1%, not a share win. BSE grew its premium turnover 96.3% year on year against your 15.9%, and its revenue 63.5% against your 13.1%. When the pool stopped growing, the share loss showed up in the revenue line immediately.
  4. Sixty per cent of revenue depends on people who lose money. SEBI's own data: 96 lakh individual traders, 91% of them losing, aggregate losses of Rs 1,05,603 crore in FY25, up 41% year on year. That is not a customer base, it is a policy problem with a countdown on it. The regulator has already acted four times and raised securities transaction tax twice. It will act again.
  5. The float cliff is 18.6 times the offer. With no promoter, ICDR Regulation 16 does not apply and there is no long-dated lock. Roughly 94.9% of the company comes free on one day around 22-Mar-2027, against a float of 5.11%. Any holder who wants out faces a market that cannot absorb even a fraction of it.
  6. Nobody can rescue the price. The SECC Regulations cap individual holdings at 5%, institutions at 15%, and non-residents at 5% individually and 49% in aggregate. There will never be a takeover, a control premium or an activist block. The usual floor under a falling share price does not exist here by law.
  7. The book told you at the time. Retail was undersubscribed at 0.72x. Overall subscription was 1.16x for the largest offer for sale in Indian history. The grey market premium fell about 81% in the fortnight to the close. The issue was cut 15% before it opened and it still only just covered. Every one of those signals was available before allotment.
  8. The margin was flattered and you accepted it. The company reported a normalised operating EBITDA margin of 76.23% against a reported 66.85%, and the largest add-back was SEBI settlement fees, a cost it incurred in all three restated years across six separate matters, rising every year to Rs 2,174 crore in total. A recurring cost presented as non-recurring.
  9. A fifth of the profit was never the exchange at all. Rs 27,708 million of investment income, 19.9% of pre-tax profit, is treasury yield on a corpus that mostly is not shareholders' money. When rates fell, so did it, and no amount of market activity replaced it.

Thesis-killers: specific, observable, dated

  • Options premium average daily turnover falls below Rs 5,50,000 million in any quarter. That is below the FY26 average and would mean the June-2026 recovery did not hold. This is the single most important number to watch.
  • Any further SEBI measure restricting index derivatives, or a third securities transaction tax increase in the Union Budget of Feb-2027.
  • NSE's options premium share keeps falling from the 68.5% recorded in the June-2026 quarter. This thesis-killer has in effect already triggered on the latest disclosed data, the share having gone 96.7%, 87.4%, 74.7% and 68.5%. What remains to be seen is whether BSE stabilises near a third of the pool or keeps taking share. A reading below 60% would be decisive.
  • The SEBI settlement order is not passed, or is passed on different terms, or any of the appeals kept alive at the Supreme Court produces an adverse finding of law.
  • Disorderly selling around the six-month lock-in expiry in March 2027.
  • Departure of the Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer within a year of listing, given the Tata Sons speculation of Sep-2026 and the absence of a promoter anchor.

SECTION 25

Investment Pitch: Two-Way, No Rating

Both cases, argued as strongly as the evidence permits. No rating, no recommendation, no conviction level. The reader decides.

The 90-second case for applying at the band

You are buying the tollbooth of the world's fastest-financialising large economy, at a discount to what private money paid for it last year, and at a discount to its own listed peers.

The cap price of Rs 1,785 is 0.91x the certified one-year weighted average cost of acquisition of Rs 1,954.51, and below the entire disclosed one-year transaction range of Rs 1,860 to Rs 2,015. Three unlisted dealers quoted Rs 1,984 to Rs 2,059 during the bidding week. In almost every Indian IPO the public pays a large multiple of recent insider cost; here the public is paying less. At 42.1x trailing earnings NSE is also cheaper than BSE at 47.0x, MCX at 53.9x and CDSL at 61.6x, while being 3.4x BSE's revenue and 4.2x its profit.

The feared collapse happened to the wrong number. Options notional turnover is down 23% from FY24 and that is what every headline reports, but NSE charges on premium turnover, and premium average daily turnover in the June-2026 quarter was Rs 6,43,518 million, the highest of any period in the document. Revenue grew 13.1%, transaction charges 14.9%, and the reported operating margin of 78.81% came in above the normalised figure, meaning the settlement drag has gone. The honest qualifier, which Section 3 makes in full: NSE reached that record while losing options share, because the pool grew 33.1%. A buyer is betting the pool keeps growing faster than the share loss.

The overhang that stopped this listing for a decade has been paid. Rs 1,491.21 crore settled on 31-Jul-2026 and the Supreme Court closed NSE's appeals on 03-Sep-2026. The 2016 attempt died of this; the 2026 attempt did not.

The moat is as close to absolute as Indian equities offer: 92.99% of cash turnover, 99.79% of equity futures, largest derivatives exchange in the world for seven years, nil debt, 42.8% return on capital employed, an 84% payout, and a structural engine, 132.37 million unique investors growing 13.9% a year, that has nothing to do with derivatives. And the anchor book agreed: 29 mutual fund houses across 98 schemes and 27 insurers and pension funds took both reserved categories well above their floors.

The 90-second case for avoiding

You are paying 42x earnings and 12.5x book for a company whose regulator decides its prices, its products, its board and the size of its biggest market, and that regulator has spent two years shrinking it on purpose.

The reverse discounted cash flow is unambiguous: at Rs 1,785 the price embeds roughly 19% compound earnings growth for ten years, then 5% forever, which requires normalised profit to reach about Rs 68,000 crore, six times today. The cap sits 76% of the way from the bear case to the bull case. A probability-weighted value of about Rs 1,290 is 28% below the cap. There is no margin of safety at this price for the reset resuming.

And it might resume. Sixty per cent of revenue is equity options. SEBI's own data shows 96 lakh individual derivative traders, 91% losing money, Rs 1,05,603 crore of aggregate losses in FY25 and rising 41% a year. Securities transaction tax on options premium has gone from 0.0625% to 0.15% in eighteen months. Four rounds of curbs are in force. Nothing about that trajectory looks finished, and one quarter of recovery is a thin basis for assuming it is.

The market told you during the bidding. Retail was undersubscribed at 0.72x. The whole book covered only 1.16x, after the issue had already been cut 15.1%. The grey market premium fell roughly 81% in the fortnight to the close. Those are four independent, dated signals of price resistance.

The moat is visibly eroding in the one product that matters. NSE's share of the options premium pool fell from 96.7% in FY24 to 68.5% in the June-2026 quarter, and BSE grew its premium turnover 96.3% year on year against NSE's 15.9%. A regulator handed BSE a weekly expiry day and took nearly thirty points of share off the incumbent in two years. That is what the moat is worth when the threat is administrative.

And the exit is structurally hard. About 94.9% of the capital unlocks on a single day around 22-Mar-2027, roughly 18.6 times the entire IPO float, while the SECC ownership caps ensure no strategic buyer, no control premium and no activist can ever provide a floor. You are buying a permanent minority position in a company nobody is allowed to control, ahead of the largest single-day supply event the stock will ever face.

If it is not interesting at the band, what would make it interesting

The base case of Rs 1,200 and the probability-weighted value of about Rs 1,290 are the honest reference points from Section 19. A price in the Rs 1,200 to Rs 1,300 range would imply roughly 14% long-run earnings growth rather than 19%, which is defensible on the evidence actually available rather than on the evidence hoped for. Section 20 identifies the mechanism most likely to produce such a price and the date it is most likely to arrive: the lock-in expiry around 22-Mar-2027.

The other route to the same place is evidence rather than price. Two or three consecutive quarters of options premium turnover holding above Rs 6,00,000 million, with no further regulatory measure and the annuity lines pushing past 20% of revenue, would convert the base case into something closer to the bull case and would justify the current multiple on its own terms. That evidence begins arriving with the first post-listing results in the December-2026 quarter.

Time horizon

The bull case is a five to ten year proposition, because it depends on India's investor base compounding and on the annuity lines scaling from 14% of revenue toward the global exchange norm. Neither happens in four quarters. Anyone whose horizon is shorter than that is not investing in the thesis in this report, they are taking a view on a listing pop, and Sections 19 and 21 both decline to support that.

The One-Line Summary

This is a bet on the tollbooth of Indian capital markets compounding with a financialising economy, priced as if one quarter of recovery has settled the question of whether the regulator's restraint of its largest product was an interruption or a reset, and it breaks if SEBI tightens again before the annuity lines grow large enough to matter.

No rating is given. This report contains no buy, sell or hold recommendation, no conviction level and no price target offered as advice. That refusal is the point of the document, not an omission from it. The evidence is set out above, on both sides, with its sources and its gaps named. The decision belongs to the reader.
