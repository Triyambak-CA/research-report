Top 3 Positives

  • The collapse everyone is pricing happened to the wrong number. Options notional average daily turnover is still 23% below FY24, and that is the figure in every headline. But NSE levies transaction charges on premium turnover, and premium average daily turnover in the June-2026 quarter was Rs 6,43,518 million, an all-time high, above FY24, FY25 and FY26. Revenue grew 13.1% and transaction charges grew 14.9% in that quarter. The regulatory reset compressed contract counts, not the fee base.
  • A near-monopoly that regulation makes almost impossible to attack. 92.99% of cash equity turnover, 99.79% of equity futures, and the largest derivatives exchange in the world by contracts traded for seven consecutive years. A new entrant needs SEBI recognition, Rs 100 crore of net worth and, decisively, liquidity, which cannot be licensed. The last serious challenger now litigates against NSE rather than competing with it.
  • Extraordinary economics with no capital behind them. Nil borrowings, a 78.81% operating EBITDA margin in the June quarter, return on capital employed of 42.8% even in a bad year, and 84% of profit paid out as dividend. The structurally growing lines, listing fees, data, indices and colocation, all grew 12% to 33% in the year transaction charges fell.

Top 3 Risks

  • Sixty per cent of revenue sits in the one product the regulator is deliberately restraining, and the restraint is not finished. Equity options are 60.2% of revenue from operations. SEBI has tightened four times since Oct-2024 and the Union Budget raised securities transaction tax on options premium again with effect from 01-Apr-2026. The regulator that shrinks this revenue line also approves NSE's fees, its board and its listing.
  • The settlement is paid but not final, and that is not how it is being reported. NSE paid Rs 1,491.21 crore on 31-Jul-2026, and the Supreme Court closed its appeals on 03-Sep-2026. But NSE's own RHP says SEBI's acceptance is in principle only and that "the settlement order by SEBI in this regard is yet to be passed", and the Court expressly kept the questions of law open as against other respondents. No SEBI settlement order has been published as at 21-Sep-2026.
  • The cap price already assumes the recovery continues uninterrupted for a decade. Roughly 19% compound earnings growth for ten years, from a base where the most recent full year fell 15.5% and the recovery is one quarter old. At 12.5x book for a business whose regulator caps what it may charge, there is no margin for the reset resuming.

"This is a bet on the tollbooth of Indian capital markets compounding with a financialising economy, priced as if one quarter of recovery has settled the question of whether the regulator's restraint was an interruption or a reset, and it breaks if SEBI tightens again before the annuity lines grow large enough to matter."

SECTION 01

Industry and Sector Analysis

NSE does not compete in an industry so much as operate a piece of national infrastructure. The question is not market size but who controls the tollbooth's pricing, and the answer is SEBI, not NSE.

Source Warning - The Industry Chapter Is Paid For

The industry chapter rests on a report titled "Industry report on Capital Markets and Market Infrastructure Institutions in India" dated 16-Jun-2026, prepared by Redseer Strategy Consultants Private Limited and commissioned by NSE exclusively for this Offer. The offer document records that neither the company nor the book running lead managers have independently verified it. Addressable-market definitions in a commissioned chapter are drawn to flatter. It is treated here as an issuer claim to be tested, and Section 17 builds the independent counterweight.

What the industry actually is

India has two stock exchanges of consequence, NSE and BSE, two depositories, NSDL and CDSL, and a handful of specialised venues, MCX in commodities and IEX in power. Every one is a Market Infrastructure Institution under the SEBI (Stock Exchanges and Clearing Corporations) Regulations 2018, a category carrying obligations no ordinary company faces: a public-interest board, SEBI approval for senior appointments, ownership caps on shareholders, and regulatory control over what may be charged.

That last point is the one most often missed. This is not a free-pricing business. Transaction charges are subject to regulatory oversight, and SEBI has intervened directly, most consequentially in requiring a uniform, non-slab fee structure from Oct-2024, which compressed the effective yield exchanges earned from high-volume members. A monopolist who cannot set his own price is a very particular kind of monopolist, and every valuation multiple in this report has to carry that fact.

Where the cycle sits

The Indian capital market is in a structural expansion and a simultaneous regulatory contraction. Through FY26 the two pulled in opposite directions on NSE's revenue. In the June-2026 quarter they stopped doing so.

! The Most Important Distinction In This Report

Options notional turnover and options premium turnover are not the same number, and NSE earns on the second one. Notional average daily turnover is still 23% below FY24 and that is the figure behind every "derivatives collapsed" headline. Premium average daily turnover in the June-2026 quarter was Rs 6,43,518 million, the highest of any period disclosed, above FY24's Rs 6,17,788 million. SEBI's curbs worked by raising contract sizes and cutting weekly expiries, which mechanically shrinks notional while leaving the premium pool intact. Anyone modelling NSE's revenue off notional turnover is modelling the wrong base. This is the recurring trap of one metric disclosed on two bases, and here it is the difference between a broken thesis and an intact one.

The regulatory timeline that did the damage

SEBI's index-derivatives framework, circular SEBI/HO/MRD/TPD-1/P/CIR/2024/132 dated 01-Oct-2024, phased in from 20-Nov-2024: minimum contract value raised from roughly Rs 5 lakh to Rs 15 lakh, weekly expiries cut to one per exchange per index, an additional 2% extreme loss margin on expiry days, and calendar-spread benefit removed on expiry. Intraday position-limit monitoring followed on 01-Apr-2025 and end-of-day limits on 01-Jul-2025. A single weekly index-expiry day was then fixed, Tuesday for NSE and Thursday for BSE, for contracts expiring on or after 01-Sep-2025. NSE discontinued Bank Nifty, Nifty Financial Services, Nifty Midcap and Nifty Next 50 weeklies as a direct consequence.

On top of that, securities transaction tax on options premium rose from 0.0625% to 0.1% with effect from 01-Oct-2024, and again to 0.15% from 01-Apr-2026, with futures moving from 0.02% to 0.05%. The tax increases are a second, independent squeeze on the same activity, and the second one only began in the current financial year.

Who captures value across the chain

The chain runs issuer, exchange, clearing corporation, depository, broker, investor. NSE owns three of those links: the exchange, NSE Clearing outright, and the Nifty index franchise. Value capture is being redistributed by regulation rather than competition. NSE was compelled to cut its NSDL stake from 24.00% to 15.00% under Regulation 21 of the SEBI (Depositories and Participants) Regulations 2018, and did so by selling into NSDL's own IPO, with NSDL listing on 06-Aug-2025. NSE Clearing must maintain a Core Settlement Guarantee Fund of roughly Rs 130.8 billion out of profits that would otherwise be shareholders'.

Barriers to entry, and the price of them

The barriers here are close to absolute and they are legal rather than economic. A new exchange needs SEBI recognition, Rs 100 crore of net worth, a public-interest board and, decisively, liquidity, which cannot be licensed. The last serious attempt, MSEI, failed and now litigates against NSE rather than competing with it. But the same regulation that keeps competitors out caps what the incumbent may earn, who may own it and who may sit on its board. That trade is the defining feature of the sector and it runs through every section that follows.

SECTION 02

Business Model and Value Chain

A vertically integrated, multi-asset exchange owning trading, clearing, data and indices. Three reporting segments, but in practice one revenue engine with several small annuity businesses attached.

How the money actually arrives

A trading member executes a trade on the NSE platform and NSE levies a transaction charge on the value of that trade. For options the charge is levied on premium turnover. That single mechanical fact explains why FY26 options revenue fell only 1.9% while notional volumes fell 17.4%, and why June-quarter options revenue rose 15.6%.

SECTION 03

Competitive Landscape and Peer Benchmarking

The issuer names exactly one listed peer. That choice is defensible and also convenient, because the one peer it names trades on a higher multiple than the one it is asking for.

Auditing the issuer's peer set

In Basis for Offer Price the company compares itself with BSE Limited and nothing else. The justification offered is that BSE is the only other multi-asset Indian exchange spanning cash, derivatives, mutual funds and debt with a comparable revenue mix. On the merits that is fair: CDSL is a depository, MCX is a single-commodity venue, CAMS is a registrar. None is a stock exchange.

But a single-peer table with a peer on a trailing price to earnings of roughly 47x sets a comfortable ceiling. NSE is asking for about 42x at the cap on trailing-twelve-month earnings. Against that single comparator the issue looks cheap. Against the global exchange set it does not. Both views belong in the report, so both are given.

Three observations the issuer's single-peer table cannot show. First, NSE's ~61% net margin is at the very top of the global set, matched only by CME and HKEX, and that is before the normalisation the company applies. Where the competitive threat actually is

Not from a new entrant. From BSE, in one specific product, enabled by a regulator. NSE's own disclosure shows options premium market share of 74.71%, meaning BSE holds roughly a quarter of the premium pool. NSE Clearing's settlement share fell from 94.24% to 88.42% in cash and from 95.67% to 91.04% in futures and options between FY25 and FY26.

The cause is disclosed in NSE's own risk factor: clearing-corporation interoperability, and the fixing of a single weekly index-expiry day per exchange, Tuesday for NSE and Thursday for BSE, for contracts expiring on or after 01-Sep-2025. NSE itself flags further share erosion as a live risk, not a historical event. That is the honest framing: BSE did not out-compete NSE, it was handed a protected expiry day. Whether that share is durable depends on whether SEBI keeps the arrangement, which no analyst can forecast.

Insufficient data for conviction on where options share settles. Two quarters of post-01-Sep-2025 data exist and the June-2026 quarter shows NSE's premium turnover at a record, which suggests the pool grew rather than NSE recovering share. Both can be true at once.

SECTION 04

Competitive Advantage: the 6 Cs Moat Framework

One of the widest moats available in Indian equities, and one of the few where the moat's owner does not control the toll. Rate the moat strong and the pricing power weak, and both are true.

CostStrong

The marginal cost of matching one more trade is effectively zero, and fixed costs are spread over the largest order flow in the country. Operating EBITDA margin of 78.81% in the June quarter, on a cost base where total expenses excluding the Core Settlement Guarantee Fund contribution were Rs 59,999.03 million against Rs 187,133.70 million of income in FY26. No competitor at a quarter of the scale can match the unit economics.

CapacityStrong

Capacity is technological, not physical, and NSE has demonstrated it at the highest contract volumes in the world for seven consecutive years. Colocation racks nearly doubled in two years, from 934 to 1,868, which is capacity being filled by paying customers rather than built on hope. The constraint is resilience, not throughput, and the 24-Feb-2021 outage is the reminder.

CapitalStrong

Nil borrowings across all three restated years and the stub. Return on capital employed of 42.8% in a bad year. The business funds a Rs 130.8 billion Core Settlement Guarantee Fund, an 84% dividend payout and its entire capital expenditure out of operating cash. It has never needed external equity, which is precisely why this offer raises none.

ChannelModerate

Distribution runs through roughly 1,400 trading members, and the top ten are 46.78% of revenue. NSE does not own the investor relationship. In practice members cannot move flow to an illiquid venue, so the concentration has never bitten. But the channel is the one place a regulator-created alternative, such as a protected expiry day on a rival exchange, can redirect volume without NSE doing anything wrong.

Customer captivityStrong

The deepest moat here, and it is liquidity. An order book attracts orders because it already has orders. Nifty is the benchmark 435-index family with Rs 8.95 trillion of passive assets tracking it, which locks in index licensing and the derivatives that settle against those indices. Switching costs for an algorithmic firm with racks in the NSE data centre are physical as well as commercial.

Compliance moatStrong

SEBI recognition under the SECC Regulations, a Rs 100 crore net-worth floor, public-interest directors and fit-and-proper tests on shareholders. Entry is not merely expensive, it is discretionary in the regulator's hands. The identical regulation is also the principal risk, which is the paradox of this asset.

The single most important moat source, and its half-life

Liquidity network effect, and it has no natural expiry. No incumbent exchange anywhere has lost a deep liquidity pool to a competitor on price. The half-life is therefore not set by competition but by regulation, and there the record is explicit: SEBI has already redirected options flow to BSE through the expiry-day allocation, forced NSE's NSDL stake down from 24% to 15%, mandated a uniform fee structure, and restrained the derivatives product that is 60% of revenue.

The honest statement of the moat is this: the franchise is close to unassailable commercially and entirely assailable administratively. An investor here is not underwriting competitive risk. They are underwriting Indian regulatory policy for a decade, and buying at a multiple that assumes it stays broadly benign.

SECTION 06

Earnings Quality, Forensics and the Restatement Reconciliation

The accounting is clean. The presentation is not neutral. Those are different findings and both belong here.

The restatement reconciliation, which is the highest-yield artefact in any offer document

There are no restatement adjustments. Audited and restated profit and equity are identical for all three years. No prior-period items, no accounting-policy changes requiring adjustment, no audit qualifications. The restatement is a regrouping into SEBI's disclosure format and nothing more.

That is a genuinely good result and it should be said plainly. In most offer documents this table is where the bodies are. Here it is empty.

Emphasis of matter, and what it says

The statutory auditors' examination report dated 04-Sep-2026 carries emphasis of matter paragraphs for FY24, FY25, FY26 and the June-2025 quarter. The FY26 paragraph reads, verbatim:

"We draw attention to Note 36(b) ... relating to the orders passed by the Securities and Exchange Board of India in relation to preferential access to tick-by-tick data at the Holding Company's Colocation facility, Dark Fiber point to-point connectivity and governance and conflict of interest matters ... The Holding Company has recognised a provision of Rs 13,912.07 million during the year ended 31 March 2026, in addition to an amount of Rs 1,000.00 million provided for earlier ... The future outcome of the above matters is uncertain at this stage. Our opinion is not modified in respect of this matter."

An emphasis of matter is not a qualification. The opinion is clean. But it is the auditor pointing at the one number a reader must understand, and it is carried in four consecutive periods.

! Red Flag - The Normalisation Removes a Cost That Has Recurred Every Year

The company presents a Normalised Operating EBITDA Margin of 76.23% for FY26 against a reported 66.85%. Its own definition adds back, among other items, "Additional Contribution to Core SGF" and "SEBI Settlement Fees".

SEBI settlement fees charged to profit: Rs 726.48 million in FY24, Rs 6,701.84 million in FY25, Rs 14,315.57 million in FY26. That is Rs 21,743.89 million, or Rs 2,174 crore over three years, rising every year, across six separate matters: the Feb-2021 technical glitch, the Trading Access Point matter, inter-CCP collateral at NSE Clearing, a general inspection covering Feb-2021 to Mar-2022, co-location and dark fibre. Settlement fees were 4.2% of other expenses in FY24 and 37.8% in FY26.

A cost incurred in every year of the disclosed record, on six distinct matters, is not self-evidently non-recurring. Nor is the Core Settlement Guarantee Fund contribution, which is a standing regulatory obligation on a clearing corporation. Use the reported margin as the base case and treat the normalised margin as the company's advocacy.

The counter-argument, stated fairly: in the June-2026 quarter reported operating EBITDA margin of 78.81% was ABOVE the normalised 77.75%, meaning there was nothing material to add back. If that persists, the company's characterisation will have been right and the FY24 to FY26 settlements will look like the clearing of a legacy book. One quarter does not settle it.

Other income dependence

Investment income inside revenue (Rs 8,416.91 million) plus investment income below the line (Rs 19,290.79 million) totals Rs 27,707.70 million, which is 19.9% of FY26 profit before tax. Decomposed rather than left as a single ratio: this is treasury yield on a large corpus, not arbitration awards or mark-to-market windfalls, so it is high quality but rate-sensitive. A 200 basis point fall in short rates would take roughly Rs 5,500 million off pre-tax profit with no change in market activity at all. Separately, "other income: others" swung from Rs 1,498.34 million in the June-2025 quarter to Rs 96.13 million in June-2026, a 93.6% fall, which flatters the stub's year-on-year revenue comparison and slightly depresses its profit comparison. That is why stub profit grew 6.7% while revenue grew 13.1%.

Auditor

Current statutory auditor: Walker Chandiok & Co LLP. Previous: Price Waterhouse & Co Chartered Accountants LLP, which held office from the 29th to the 34th annual general meeting and ceased on 24-Aug-2026 on "Completion of term of appointment". That is statutory rotation under the Companies Act, not a resignation, and it is not a red flag. It is recorded because an auditor change one month before an offer document always has to be checked, and here it checks out.

Note also that one subsidiary with total assets of Rs 512,168.80 million, which is NSE Clearing, is audited by a different auditor and the group auditor relies on that report. For a clearing corporation holding member collateral, the reliance is worth naming.

Contingent liabilities and tax

Claims against the group not acknowledged as debts stood at Rs 324.90 million at 31-Mar-2026, apart from the specific matters. Tax litigation runs to 81 cases and Rs 8,671.60 million, of which the largest single item is an NSE Clearing appeal against disallowance of the Core Settlement Guarantee Fund contribution, an addition of Rs 14,410.00 million producing a demand of Rs 3,945.50 million, partly stayed at Rs 3,156.40 million. A competition claim by MSEI of Rs 8,569.90 million sits before the NCLAT and is stayed. NSE Clearing has given an inter-CCP collateral guarantee to ICCL of Rs 85,000.00 million.

Related party transactions

Small relative to scale and unremarkable in direction. The largest flows in FY26 are a dividend of Rs 78.00 million received from NSDL, managing director remuneration of Rs 158.88 million, a dividend of Rs 24.80 million from Power Exchange India, and modest service charges with NSDL. There is no pattern of value leaving the listed entity. With no identifiable promoter there is no promoter-related-party channel at all, which removes the single most common Indian governance risk.

Earnings quality score

7 / 10. Pushing it up: zero restatement adjustments, an unmodified audit opinion, nil debt, no promoter-related-party channel, small contingent claims relative to net worth, and treasury income that is genuine yield rather than accounting. Holding it down: a headline "normalised" margin that removes a cost incurred in all three years across six matters; an operating cash flow line so contaminated by custodial clearing balances that the standard conversion test cannot be run; a fifth of pre-tax profit from the treasury rather than the exchange; and a non-operating write-off of Rs 399.99 million on the Open Network for Digital Commerce investment in FY24, which was written down to Re 1 because a section 8 company cannot distribute profit, a fact knowable before the investment was made. Not a 9, because the presentation is advocacy. Not a 5, because the books themselves are clean.

Governance and Capital Allocation Track Record

There is no promoter. That removes the dominant Indian governance risk and replaces it with a different one: a board the regulator effectively appoints, at a company the same regulator has repeatedly penalised.

The Structural Fact That Reframes This Section

"OUR COMPANY DOES NOT HAVE AN IDENTIFIABLE PROMOTER" appears on the cover of the offer document. There is no promoter shareholding, no pledge, no promoter remuneration beyond executive pay, no royalty, no promoter group, and no minimum promoters' contribution under ICDR Regulation 14. Every standard promoter-governance test in this framework returns nil. That is genuinely unusual and, on balance, a strength.

The board

Twelve directors: seven Public Interest Directors and five non-independent directors. The Public Interest Directors include the Chairperson Shri Srinivas Injeti, a retired Indian Administrative Service officer; Rajesh Gopinathan, former managing director of Tata Consultancy Services; Retd Justice Abhilasha Kumari; Prof Mamata Biswal; Prof G. Sivakumar of IIT; P.R. Ramesh, former chairman of Deloitte India; and Rajeev Vasudeva, former chief executive of Egon Zehnder. The executive side is Ashishkumar Chauhan as Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer, with Executive Directors Viral Mody and Sanjay Shorey appointed in May-2026.

That is, on its face, a strong board. But the appointment mechanism is the point: directors are appointed under the SECC Regulations with SEBI's prior approval, and Public Interest Directors are effectively regulator-nominated. LIC, holding 10.72%, proposed Shri Dinesh Pant to the board by representation letter dated 05-Mar-2026 under section 6A of the Life Insurance Corporation Act, and that appointment too required SEBI's prior approval under Regulation 24 of the SECC Regulations.

The governance question for a minority buyer is therefore not "will the promoter expropriate me" but "whose interests does a regulator-approved board serve when the regulator's policy objective and the shareholders' revenue conflict". That conflict is not hypothetical here. It is the derivatives curbs.

Remuneration

Managing director and chief executive remuneration of Rs 137.6 million is disclosed in the management chapter for FY26, while the related-party note shows Rs 158.88 million. The two figures are on different bases, most likely basic against total including variable pay and perquisites. The difference is flagged rather than resolved, because the document does not reconcile them and the report will not guess. Either figure is roughly 0.15% of profit, which is not a governance concern on scale.

The capital allocation record, judged on outcomes

  • Forced divestments, executed well. NSE reduced its NSDL holding from 24.00% to 15.00% by selling into NSDL's IPO, realising Rs 13,846.74 million from sale of associates in FY26 and Rs 14,484.41 million in FY25.

The historic capital-allocation errors were diversifications away from the core, they were modest in size, they have been unwound at a profit, and the regulator has made clear it will not permit a repeat. This is a good capital-allocation record arrived at partly by external discipline.

Offer for sale, in full, and what that means

One hundred per cent of this offer is a secondary sale. The company receives nothing. At the cap price roughly Rs 22,562 crore leaves the buyers' pockets and goes to selling shareholders, and the balance sheet of National Stock Exchange of India Limited is precisely the same the day after listing as the day before. Net asset value per share is Rs 142.40 before and Rs 142.40 after. There is no dilution, and there is no capital raised.

ipo-mode flags any offer for sale above 60% of issue size as a monetisation event requiring the sellers to be named. This is 100%, so Section 20 names them all.

The eligibility route, which is a one-line quality gate

The offer is made under Regulation 6(1), not the Regulation 6(2) fallback. That means NSE satisfied the profitability test: net tangible assets of at least Rs 3 crore in each of the preceding three years, average operating profit of at least Rs 15 crore over three years with each year positive, and net worth of at least Rs 1 crore in each year. For a company earning Rs 10,302 crore of annual profit those bars are trivial, but the route still matters: a Reg 6(2) issuer has failed the profit test, and that would have forced 75% of the net offer to institutions and capped retail at 10%.

Because this is a Reg 6(1) issue, the allocation is the standard one: retail at least 35%, non-institutional at least 15%, qualified institutional buyers not more than 50%, with up to 60% of the institutional portion available to anchors. Retail therefore sets a meaningful part of the price here, which Section 21 shows mattered.

Why the offer was cut, and what the cut tells you

The document does not give a reason. The pattern of the cut does. The two sellers who did not reduce their tranches are the two most recent institutional entrants, Canada Pension Plan Investment Board at a cost of Rs 324.13 a share and Aranda Investments at Rs 62.38. The sellers who cut hardest are state-owned insurers and banks sitting on costs between Rs 0.32 and Rs 5.26: Stock Holding Corporation cut 4,702,500 shares, General Insurance Corporation 4,470,500, Bank of Baroda 3,295,875 and MS Strategic 5,000,000.

Read plainly, an issue sized down by 15% between draft and final, with the lowest-cost sellers cutting most, is an offer being sized to demand. That reading is consistent with what the book did, and Section 21 takes it up.

No pre-IPO placement was undertaken. Minimum public offer requirements under Rule 19(2)(b) of the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Rules are met on the applicable slab for an issuer of this size, which permits a public offer below 25% with a commitment to reach minimum public shareholding within the prescribed period.

Use-of-Proceeds Forensics

There are no proceeds. This section is not skipped, because the absence is the finding.

! The Company Receives Nothing

The offer document states it directly: "Our Company will not receive any proceeds from the Offer." There is no fresh issue, no objects of the issue, no schedule of deployment, and no monitoring agency, because ICDR Regulation 41 requires one only where the fresh issue exceeds Rs 100 crore and here the fresh issue is nil.

What normally goes in this section, and what replaces it

In a normal report this is where management's only enforceable forward commitments are audited: objects against statutory caps, general corporate purposes against the 25% ceiling, loan repayment against the indebtedness note, working-capital holding norms against history, capex against orders placed. None of it exists here. There is nothing to audit because nothing was promised, and nothing was promised because no money is being raised.

Three consequences a reader should carry forward:

  • There will be no monitoring agency reports. The quarterly monitoring-agency report, public within 45 days of each quarter end, is the artefact that normally proves or disproves an issuer's stated objects in later years. For NSE it will never exist. The scoreboard that Section 10 would ordinarily be built to track is simply absent.
  • The usual diversion risk is nil. The most common Indian enforcement failure mode, proceeds diverted away from stated objects, is structurally impossible in a pure offer for sale. That is a real, if negative, comfort.
  • The capital structure is unchanged. No dilution, no change in net worth, no change in net asset value per share, no change in the share count. A buyer is purchasing an existing share from an existing holder at a negotiated price, with the exchange taking no part in the economics.

Offer expenses, which are the only cash flows the offer actually creates

The selling shareholders bear the offer expenses in proportion to the shares each sells. The company bears only the listing fees, the audit fees attributable to non-offer work, and expenses relating to product advertising consistent with past practice. That allocation is the correct one for a pure offer for sale and it is worth confirming, because a company bearing the cost of its shareholders' exit would be a transfer of value away from the buyer.

So who is actually being paid

At the cap price of Rs 1,785, approximately Rs 22,562 crore is transferred from new public shareholders to twenty-three existing selling shareholders. The weighted average cost of acquisition for the largest of them is between Rs 0.32 and Rs 324.13 a share. Section 18 computes what multiple of insider cost the public is paying, and Section 20 names every seller and what each realises. That, and not a use-of-proceeds table, is the substance of this offer.
