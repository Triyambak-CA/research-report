### The single most important analytical point in this report
**Options PREMIUM average daily turnover in Q1 FY27 of Rs 643,517.81 million is an all-time
high**, above FY26 (576,617.54), above FY25 (624,486.60) and above FY24 (617,788.39). Options
NOTIONAL turnover remains 23% below FY24.

NSE charges transaction fees on PREMIUM turnover, not notional. So the headline that
"derivatives volumes collapsed after the SEBI curbs" is true of notional and FALSE of the base
on which NSE earns. The curbs raised contract sizes, which mechanically shrank notional, while
premium turnover recovered to a record. Anyone modelling NSE off notional turnover is modelling
the wrong number. This is known trap 2, the same metric disclosed on two bases, and it is the
difference between a broken thesis and an intact one.

Caveat to state in the report: one quarter is not a trend, seasonality across the remaining
three quarters of FY27 is unknown and cannot be assessed from a single stub, and Q1 FY26 was
itself a weak comparator. **Insufficient data for conviction on the full-year FY27 path.**

## THE SETTLEMENT - reconciled from two primary sources, and NOT as widely reported

Two independent adversarial verifications were run. Both contradicted the press account.

**What is established:**
1. NSE paid in full on **31-Jul-2026**. The total is Rs 14,912.07 million (Rs 1,491.21 crore),
   being Rs 12,235.59 million for co-location and Rs 2,676.48 million for dark fibre. This
   splits as roughly Rs 7,764.7 million already on deposit with SEBI plus Rs 7,147.37 million
   paid across. Note that Rs 7,147.37 million is EXACTLY the "Settlement fees payable to SEBI"
   current provision carried at 31-Mar-2026, so the balance sheet and the payment reconcile.
2. SEBI's acceptance, as described in the RHP at pages 446-447, is **IN PRINCIPLE ONLY**: SEBI
   "vide email dated July 30, 2026 has in principle agreed to accept the terms of the
   settlement", and the RHP risk factor at page 31 records that this is "subject to Regulations
   28 and 31 of the SEBI (Settlement Proceedings) Regulations, 2018".
3. **The RHP states in terms: "The settlement order by SEBI in this regard is yet to be
   passed."** That is the issuer's own language on 10-Sep-2026.
4. No SEBI settlement order has been published as at 21-Sep-2026. The SEBI settlement orders
   listing runs to 16-Sep-2026 with no NSE co-location or dark fibre entry. (Do not confuse it
   with the September 2026 National Spot Exchange order, a different entity.)
5. The **Supreme Court order is dated 03-Sep-2026**, not 18-Sep-2026 as widely reported. The
   Record of Proceedings reads "Date : 03-09-2026", the bench was Justice J.B. Pardiwala and
   Justice K. Vinod Chandran, and the disposal was expressly "in terms of the settlement
   arrived at". The Court RECORDED a settlement; it did not adjudicate.
6. **Not all appeals were disposed.** The order disposed C.A. No. 6471/2023 and C.A. No.
   4273/2024. It records that "Civil Appeal Nos.6472-6478 of 2023 and Civil Appeal Nos.
   4274-4286 of 2024 qua all other respondents ... will remain alive and all the questions of
   law qua such respondents are kept open." The remainder were listed on 09-Sep-2026.
7. The RHP describes co-location interlocutory applications dated 17 and 18-Aug-2026 as
   "currently pending for hearing" before the Supreme Court.

**The residual risk, stated plainly:** the money has been paid and the Supreme Court has closed
NSE's own appeals, but the formal SEBI settlement order has still not been passed, and the
questions of law are expressly open as against other respondents. A settlement under Reg 28 and
31 of the Settlement Regulations is not final until the order issues. This is a genuine,
documented, residual item that almost all coverage of this issue has recorded as "settled and
closed". The report should say so in those terms.

**Tension to disclose, not paper over:** the signed Supreme Court order indicates the 03-Sep-2026
disposal covered a co-location appeal (C.A. 6471/2023) as well as dark fibre (C.A. 4273/2024),
while the RHP's co-location paragraph still describes interlocutory applications as pending.
Both are primary sources. The report states what each says and flags the tension rather than
resolving it by assertion.
