#### Risk Factor 4: IT Infrastructure, Systems, and Technology Risks
(Page 29)

Disruptions, failures, or inadequacies in our IT infrastructure, systems, or software, including those provided by third- party vendors, could adversely affect our business, reputation and results of operations, and may subject us to regulatory action or the imposition of financial disincentives by SEBI.

Our business depends on the continuous, reliable, and secure performance of our information technology infrastructure,
computers, communications networks, and trading and settlement software. With trading being fully electronic, technological
robustness of market infrastructure is critical. Any disruption, whether from software malfunctions, connectivity failures, or
order-matching errors, has the potential to affect market integrity and investor confidence at scale. According to the Redseer
Report, landmark episodes in global markets illustrate this vulnerability: the May 2010 ‘Flash Crash’ in US equity and
derivatives markets, the Knight Capital Group incident in August 2012 wherein the inadvertent reactivation of a deprecated
order routing algorithm resulted in significant financial losses, fat-finger order entry errors that triggered sudden price
dislocations in European and Asian markets, and settlement failures and order-routing latency events reported periodically
across exchange groups globally. These events underscore that even mature systems remain prone to operational stress. Any
failure, inadequacy, or disruption of our IT infrastructure, whether due to hardware or software defects or malfunctions,
cyberattacks, phishing, distributed denial-of-service attacks, operational or human error, insufficient capacity, natural disasters,
power outages, disruptions in telecommunications services, unauthorised access, fraud, military or political conflicts, terrorist
attacks, computer viruses, ransomware, malware, or issues with third-party service providers, could significantly disrupt our
business operations and erode market confidence. For example, our critical systems have, in the past, experienced disruptions
that have led to the shutdown of our operations for a few hours. On February 24, 2021, issues in the links with telecom service
providers impacted our Storage Area Network (“SAN”) system deployed in production and near disaster recovery site, resulting
in the primary SAN becoming inaccessible to host servers. While the trading system was not impacted, outage of risk, clearing
and settlement systems at NCL, index and surveillance systems at our Company led to a complete halt in all trading segments
for nearly five hours and 24 minutes. For further details, see “- Our Company has been, and continues to be, subject to
enforcement actions, penalties, adjudication proceedings, which may or may not get settled with SEBI in respect of alleged
violations and regulatory matters with applicable SEBI regulations. The outcome of current and potential future proceedings
is uncertain and could have a material adverse effect on our business, reputation, financial condition, results of operations,
cash flows and prospects” on page 27. We have since implemented remedial measures, including architectural change of system
design, shifting from SAN-based to host-based replication in key systems, strengthening disaster recovery and redundancy,
adding additional service providers for replication links, and limiting link utilisation to safeguard failover capacity.
We have also experienced technology-related issues affecting our systems in Fiscals 2026, 2025 and 2024, including bugs in
the corporate announcement submissions, issues with market data broadcast for specific securities, and periods during which
the NSE India website was inaccessible over the internet. There have also been certain technical lapses in the futures and options
asset class, including market data dissemination failures on secondary channels and instances where open interest values in
equity derivatives were computed and published incorrectly. Operational lapses include delays in the transition from special
pre-open sessions to the normal market for specific securities, delays in trade feeds to clearing corporations, as well as instances
where incorrect price ranges occurred due to erroneous base prices of certain securities. Recurring failures of this nature could
potentially have a significant impact in the future.
To address these risks, SEBI in 2021 (through the SEBI circular titled “Standard Operating Procedure for handling of technical
glitches by Market Infrastructure Institutions (MIIs) and payment of “Financial Disincentives” thereof” dated July 5, 2021),
mandated a standard operating procedure for handling technical glitches, prescribing substantial financial disincentives. We
have in the past received warnings from SEBI for non-compliance with this circular. For instance, SEBI issued a letter dated
February 26, 2024 to our Company wherein we had received an administrative warning from SEBI with respect to a non-
alignment of our internal standard operating procedure with the standard operating procedure mandated by SEBI by way of its
