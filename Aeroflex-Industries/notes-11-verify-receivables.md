## CLAIM A - FEMA-overdue export receivables vs ageing schedule: PARTLY CONFIRMED

### What the AR says (verbatim substance)

| FY24 | 3,234.65 (32.35 cr) | Note 45 XI, pdfp 98 / pr 113 | Note XIV, pdfp 143 / pr 158 | "such extension has not been approved" |

| FY26 | 3,921.57 (39.22 cr) | Note 44.14, pdfp 138 / pr 159 | Note 45.14, pdfp 196 / pr 217 | "such extension has not been made" |

Text: "The Holding company has over due receivables against the export realisation of goods for INR equivalent to ... due to the various business reasons ... in process of availing extension from RBI through its authorized dealers for the overdue realisations however till the date of the balance sheet such extension has not been made."

- Figures: CONFIRMED.
- "Beyond the FEMA realisation period": INFERENCE, not stated. The AR never says FEMA or names a period. The inference is reasonable, because an AD-bank extension is only needed once the realisation period is exceeded. Label it [I].
- "Without RBI extension": CONFIRMED (not approved / not made as at balance sheet date).
- "30-46% of trade receivables": slightly off. The note is a holding-company note, so the right denominator is standalone gross receivables: FY24 9,464.56 (pdfp 128), FY25 12,094.83 (FY25 pdfp 189), FY26 12,533.51 (pdfp 176). That gives **34.2% / 44.1% / 31.3%**, a range of 31-44%. The 30-46% range uses consolidated denominators.

- **The "far less is shown as past due" wording is wrong as written.** The schedule has no "not due" column. Its first bucket lumps not-yet-due amounts with items less than 6 months past due. The schedule therefore never shows a total past-due figure. What it does show is that at most 9.0 / 12.2 / 14.0 cr is more than 6 months past due.
- **The reconciliation gap is real.** The clocks differ: the FEMA period runs from the date of export, and the ageing runs from the due date. For the two to reconcile, about 23 / 41 / 25 cr of FEMA-overdue bills must sit less than 6 months past due. Under a 9-month rule, that needs credit terms longer than about 3 months from shipment.
- **The evidence on credit terms does not support long terms, but it does not pin them either.** On the Q4FY24 call (07-May-2024, p9 of 20) the MD said terms are often "payment on delivery" and "sometimes our payment comes within 50 to 90 days". On the Q2FY24 call (p7-8) he said US terms are "slightly on the credit basis" and "higher". On the Q2FY25 call he blamed a working-capital increase on Red Sea transit (30 days becoming 45-50 days) and "revising some payment terms". Consolidated receivable days run at about 106-116 (notes-01). Nothing here supports terms long enough to close a 23-41 cr gap, but there is no stated contractual term either.
- **FEMA period.** It was 9 months from the date of export through FY24, FY25 and most of FY26. RBI PR 2025-2026/1510 (14-Nov-2025) extended it to 15 months, and the RBI release of 31-Mar-2026 says the relaxation continues. I did not establish whether the 15 months covers every bill outstanding at 31-Mar-2026. Either way, FY26 gets worse, not better:
  - Under 9 months, a FEMA-overdue bill should sit in the over-6-months buckets, which total 14.0 cr.
  - Under 15 months, it should sit in the over-1-year buckets, which total 10.6 cr.
  - Both are set against 39.2 cr. A company still reporting 39 cr "overdue" after the window widened also suggests the note was not re-measured on the new rule. [I]
- **The benign reading the evidence cannot exclude.** The amount may be EDPMS bills the AD bank has not yet closed, where the books show the receivable as settled, netted, short-realised or written off. In that case it is a FEMA paperwork and compounding exposure, not an ageing misstatement. Nothing in the AR says the 39.22 cr sits inside trade receivables. "Overdue receivables against the export realisation" is suggestive, not conclusive.
- **Loss allowance.** CONFIRMED, but for FY26 consolidated only: allowance 58.10 (Note 10, pdfp 119 / pr 140) against more than 1 year past due of 823.43 + 207.19 + 29.95 = 1,060.57 (10.61 cr). All balances are classed "considered good". The allowance was nil in FY24 and FY25.

**Publishable form (A):** "The holding company reports export receivables overdue for realisation, with no RBI/AD extension, of Rs 32.35 / 53.37 / 39.22 cr (FY24-26), 31-44% of standalone receivables. Its ageing schedule shows only Rs 9.0 / 12.2 / 14.0 cr more than 6 months past due. Nothing the company has said about credit terms ("payment on delivery", payment "sometimes" in 50-90 days, receivable days about 106-116) explains the difference, so the two do not reconcile unless the overdue bills are not carried as book receivables, and the AR does not say which." Do NOT say the ageing shows "far less past due", and do not assert the ageing is misstated.

## CLAIM B - concentration denial vs MD statement: PARTLY CONFIRMED (facts true; the implied Ind AS 108 breach is NOT established)

- **Quote.** CONFIRMED verbatim: "The group has no concentration of credit risk as the customer base is widely distributed." It appears in FY26 Note 41 B consolidated (pdfp 136 / pr 157) and in the standalone statements (pdfp 194 / pr 215). The same sentence is in FY24 (pdfp 95 / 140) and FY25 (pdfp 152 / 205). It is three-year boilerplate.
- **Absence of a para 34 disclosure.** CONFIRMED.
  - Segment notes: FY26 Note 42 (pdfp 137) and standalone Note 43 (pdfp 194) say "single segment therefore segment-wise reporting is not applicable". Accounting policy L (pdfp 108 / pr 129) says the same. No entity-wide disclosure follows: no revenue by customer and no 10% statement.
  - Every "customer" hit in the financial statements was checked. None is a major-customer disclosure.
  - OCR check: only pdfp 2 (the cover) has fewer than 80 words, so no image-only page hides a note.
  - Ind AS 108 para 31-34 entity-wide disclosures do apply to single-segment entities. If any customer was 10% or more of FY26 revenue, the omission is a breach.
- **The MD's 25-26%.** The literal words are CONFIRMED (Q4FY26 call, 06-May-2026, p11 of 20): "our biggest customer in the international market contributes about 25% to 26% of our sales right now". The denominator, however, is AMBIGUOUS. The question was about "the U.S. business ex of skid assemblies".
  - FY26 AR p18-19 (pdfp 11) gives exports at 69% of revenue and the USA at 49% of exports, so the USA is about 34% of revenue.
  - Read as 25.5% of US sales, the customer is about 8.6% of total revenue. That is BELOW 10%, and no para 34 disclosure would be due.
  - Read as 25.5% of exports, it is about 17.6% of revenue. Read as 25.5% of total sales, it is 25.5%.
  - The rating files on disk (company/Credit_Rating.txt, ratings/crisil-17oct22.html) contain no customer-share data, so nothing pins the denominator.
  - Context: the Aug-2023 prospectus put the TOP FIVE customers at only 24.10% of FY23 revenue. One customer at 25% of total sales by FY26 is possible, but it is not corroborated anywhere I found.
- **Skid limb.** It does not support the claim for FY26.
  - The FY26 AR (pdfp 32) says SFN skids "contributed approximately 5% of consolidated revenue" in FY26, so the skid customer could not trigger para 34 in FY26 on skids alone.
  - Skid share: the Q2FY27 deck skid table (slide 17) gives Q2FY27 skid sales of Rs 50.03 cr against revenue of Rs 207.36 cr, which is **24.1%**. H1FY27 is 82.42 / 352.74 = 23.4%. The claim's "24% of Q2FY27" is CONFIRMED.
  - Single-customer status is CONFIRMED: "this particular business is dedicated for one customer" (Q3FY26 call), and "we are working with one customer exclusively for India for the liquid cooling business ... this very large U.S. multinational company" (Q2FY27 call, p6 of 16).
  - This is an FY27 issue. It bears on the FY27 AR, not on the FY26 AR.
- **Credit risk vs revenue.** "No concentration of credit risk" is an Ind AS 107 statement about receivables at the balance-sheet date. It is contradicted only if that customer's share of 31-Mar-2026 receivables was material, which is not disclosed.

**Publishable form (B):** "The AR repeats, every year since FY24, that the customer base is 'widely distributed', and gives no top-customer or 10% disclosure. On the Q4FY26 call the MD put the largest international customer at about 25-26% of 'our sales', The MD did not say what that percentage was of; the question was about the US business. If it is of total revenue (or even of exports, about 17.6% of revenue), Ind AS 108 para 34 required a disclosure the AR does not contain. If it is of US sales (about 8.6% of revenue), no disclosure was due. The skid business (5% of FY26 revenue, 24% of Q2FY27) serves a single customer, which will test the FY27 AR." Do not assert an FY26 breach. Do not cite the skid customer against the FY26 AR.
