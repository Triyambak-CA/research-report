Section 05

Financial analysis: nine quarters and four years

For three years Aeroflex grew revenue 12-18% a year at a 19-23% operating margin, then in the six months to Sep-2026 it grew 81%. Two things happened at once: a new product, liquid-cooling skids for one data-centre customer, went from nothing to a quarter of revenue, and the core export business recovered from a flat FY26. Reported profit growth in H1 FY27 (+159%) runs well ahead of both, because a change of depreciation method from 01-Apr-2026 lowered the charge by Rs 8.41 cr in six months, all booked in one quarter. The tables below keep the three apart.

! A change of depreciation method, booked in one quarter

From 01-Apr-2026 Aeroflex moved from written-down-value to straight-line depreciation, "based on the advice of technical experts" (Q2 FY27 call p5), treated as a change of estimate under Ind AS 8. Q1 FY27 had already been published on the old method (depreciation Rs 7.84 cr). The whole six-month saving, Rs 8.41 cr, was then taken in Q2, whose charge fell to Rs 0.45 cr (results note 5). Year-to-date measurement under Ind AS 34 makes that the standard treatment and the deck footnoted it, but the effect is large: a normal straight-line quarter carries about Rs 4.1-4.3 cr of depreciation, and the old method about Rs 8.9 cr. The MD read out the 157% profit growth before mentioning the change, and no analyst asked about it (notes-04 F4). __DEPNOTE__

Revenue: volume, mix and geography

The company does not disclose volumes or realisations by product, so a clean volume-versus-price split is not possible. What it does disclose points one way. Screener's extraction of hose production (7.79 mn metres in FY20 to 11.87 mn in FY24) and the company's own capacity and utilisation statements (hose utilisation 75% in FY25, 70% in FY26, 65% in Q2 FY27 on a larger base) say the core grew by volume until FY24 and by mix since: assemblies (hose plus end fittings, a higher-value sale) rose from about a third of sales in FY24 to 52% in FY26 (Q4 FY24 call p20; Q4 FY26 deck p12). Exports fell from 83% of sales (H1 FY25) to 60% (Q2 FY27). That is the skid business, which is entirely domestic, landing on top of an export base that grew only 6.2% in FY26 before recovering about 46% in H1 FY27 (notes-06, derived from deck percentages). Americas are 60-66% of exports.

Margins: what moved them

Gross margin stepped up five points in FY25 (36.6% to 41.6%) and has held at 42-44% since. The company attributes it to value-added mix. The FY26 operating-margin gain has a second source. Aeroflex books exchange gains, realised and unrealised, inside revenue from operations as "other operating revenue": Rs 6.37 cr in FY25 and Rs 15.03 cr in FY26 (AR FY25 note 20; AR FY26 note 21, printed p147). Its own Rs 99.74 cr EBITDA includes them. Excluding forex, EBITDA margin was 19.65% in FY25 and 19.84% in FY26: about 137 of the 156 bps of reported margin gain, roughly 88%, was the rupee. EBITDA excluding forex still grew 16.6%, a little ahead of revenue, so the core business grew; profit before tax excluding forex fell 7% (Rs 63.58 cr to Rs 59.05 cr) because depreciation more than doubled. The classification is permitted and separately disclosed, but it is the flattering choice, and it means a stronger rupee would cut operating profit, not "other income". The FY26 headline that EBITDA beat guidance therefore leaned on currency.Employee cost doubled in two years (Rs 9.06 cr in Jun-25 to Rs 18.03 cr in Sep-26) as the skid line was staffed; other expenses rose in step. Operating leverage is real but small so far: H1 FY27 EBITDA margin was 23.8% against 21.2% a year earlier, while revenue grew 81%. Skids appear to earn about the company average; management has never given a skid margin (notes-04 Part 2).

A cycle shortened by paying suppliers later reverses when growth slows. Overdue export receivables are taken up in Section 6. The first bad-debt provision of any size (Rs 1.50 cr, standalone cash flow) appeared in H1 FY27.

Cash flow and the balance sheet

Over FY24 to H1 FY27 the business generated about Rs 175 cr of operating cash and spent about Rs 283 cr on fixed assets, an acquisition and leases. The gap was filled by the IPO (Rs 136 cr net to the company) and the Feb-2026 preferential (Rs 53 cr net). Free cash flow has been negative in three of the last four periods. At 30-Sep-2026 the company had Rs 32.5 cr of cash, deposits and investments against Rs 0.85 cr of borrowings and Rs 23.14 cr of lease liabilities, so it is net cash by about Rs 8.5 cr, down from about Rs 61 cr six months earlier. Cash fell because bank deposits of Rs 47 cr were spent on capex. The next phase, Rs 202 cr by Mar-2028, is to be funded by "internal accruals, bridge loan financing and equity fund raise if required" (Q2 FY27 call p4), nine months after the MD said "we don't plan to take on any debt" (Q3 FY26 call p10).

Off balance sheet: no guarantees given for others are disclosed; contingent liabilities include an income-tax demand of Rs 41.76 cr and GST demands of Rs 7.19 cr and Rs 1.36 cr (Section 9), together about 10% of net worth and not provided for. Leases rose from Rs 7.83 cr to Rs 23.14 cr in H1 FY27 with a new facility whose location is not stated in any filing.
