Section 09

Regulatory and company-specific risks and advantages

Aeroflex carries Rs 51.04 cr of disclosed tax and other contingent claims, 11.4% of net worth, almost all of it arising from events before or around its IPO, and none provided for. A further Rs 77.61 cr GST notice disclosed in FY25 has vanished from the FY26 accounts without explanation. Add export receivables overdue for realisation for three years, a customer base the accounts call "widely distributed" while the MD describes a large single customer, all plants in one state, and no credit rating since 2023. No SEBI order, NCLT case or environmental notice was found.

The income-tax demand: legally weak, still cash-relevant

The company's case is strong on its face: s.41(1) taxes the remission of a liability that was earlier allowed as a deduction, and unpaid bank interest is deductible only on payment (s.43B), so interest that was never paid was probably never allowed. Most bank no-dues letters are dated Aug-Sep 2018, which points to AY 2019-20, not AY 2018-19 (prospectus p.268; notes-05 F1). The MD: "as per our advisers and our lawyers, we are extremely confident" of reversal (Q4FY26 p13). Against that: an appeal normally needs a pre-deposit or stay, a s.270A penalty if levied adds 50-200% of the tax on the addition, and neither AR FY24 nor AR FY25 mentioned a reopening that must have begun with a s.148 notice before the Mar-2026 order (inference on timing). The GST s.74 demand is the more likely of the two to stick in part (inference).

Separately, the FY26 loss allowance of Rs 0.58 cr covers 5% of receivables more than a year past due, and H1FY27 standalone booked a new Rs 1.50 cr bad-debt provision while receivables rose from Rs 129.82 cr to Rs 203.83 cr.

Industry-specific regulatory dependencies

  • US tariffs. The Americas are 60-66% of exports. HS 8307.10.60 was listed as a Section 232 steel derivative from Aug-2025; 8307.10.30 (with fittings) was not; the Apr-2026 restructuring's annex treatment of 8307 is not verified; a 10% Section 301 duty has applied to India since 24-Jul-2026. The rate Aeroflex pays today is not established (Section 1). Management's "10% to 15%" (Q4FY26 call) is neither confirmed nor refuted.
  • RoDTEP. 0.5% of FOB on HS 8307 (secondary; not checked against DGFT), scheme valid to 31-Dec-2026. On FY26 exports of Rs 294.01 cr, worth about Rs 1.5 cr a year (inference).
  • Export realisation rules. See above; the 15-month window from 14-Nov-2025 eases the test.
  • Input-side regulation. BIS quality control order on stainless flats (exemption reported to 31-Mar-2027) and an anti-dumping case on cold-rolled imports (both secondary, unverified) could raise the cost of the Chinese coil Aeroflex mainly uses.
  • No licence, price control or PLI applies to the products. Skids sell to a private customer; no regulatory approval of the skid itself is in the record.

Regulatory tailwinds

  • Budget 2026-27: a tax holiday to 2047 for foreign companies providing cloud services from Indian data centres, and a 15% cost safe harbour for related Indian data-centre providers. Demand-side support for Indian data-centre build; nothing for cooling hardware.
  • Capital goods scheme naming "fire-fighting equipment" (Budget para 21), an end-use management names. No hose-specific measure.
  • US-India framework (Feb-2026) cut the announced reciprocal rate from 25% to 18% and removed the additional 25%; no signed deal by 03-Nov-2026.
  • No QCO, anti-dumping duty or mandatory standard protects Aeroflex's own products in India. The protection it has is its cost base, not regulation.

Concentration

Customer

Asked about "the U.S. business ex of skid assemblies", the MD said "our biggest customer in the international market contributes about 25% to 26% of our sales right now" (Q4FY26 call p11, 06-May-2026). The denominator is ambiguous: about 25% of revenue if total sales, about 17-18% if international sales; either is above the Ind AS 108 para 34 threshold of 10%. Only a US-sales reading (about 8.6% of revenue) falls below it. AR FY24 to FY26 say "no concentration of credit risk as the customer base is widely distributed" and give no major-customer disclosure. The prospectus put the top five customers at 24.1% of FY23 revenue. From FY27 the single skid customer adds a second concentration: 24.1% of Q2FY27 revenue (skids were 5% of FY26, so this falls on FY27 reporting, not the FY26 AR).

Plant, geography, supplier

All plants are in Maharashtra: Taloja (hose), Palava (bellows and composite hose, per AR FY25) and Chakan, Pune (skids and Hyd-Air). The Americas are 66% of Q2FY27 exports, so US trade policy is a single-country risk on about 40% of revenue (inference: 60% exports x 66%). All skids sell to Indian data centres. The dominant input, stainless coil, is mostly sourced from China, and nickel is wholly imported into India. No second-source or hedging disclosure exists.

What the agencies flagged that the decks do not. Every rationale named working capital as the main weakness: CRISIL's 194 days of gross current assets in FY22 and Infomerics' operating cycle rising from 63 to 101 days. That risk is live again, with receivables up Rs 74 cr in H1FY27 and payables funding the gap (Section 6). The other two recurring risks are stainless price pass-through and debt-funded capex, and the Rs 202 cr plan with "bridge loan financing" brings the last back. The same FY22 year was measured at 194 days by CRISIL and 133 days by Infomerics; the difference is not reconciled. With no current rating and no bank lines, no outside party now tests these numbers.
