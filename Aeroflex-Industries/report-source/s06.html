Section 06

Earnings quality and forensic checks

The business under the numbers is real and growing: excluding exchange gains, FY26 EBITDA rose 16.6%, and cash from operations has covered 80% of profit over four years. What is weak is how much of the reported improvement comes from presentation and policy choices, and how much the accounts leave unsaid. Nearly 90% of FY26's margin gain is exchange gain booked inside revenue, and profit before tax excluding that gain fell 7% because depreciation more than doubled. FY27's profit is lifted by a switch to straight-line depreciation worth about Rs 17 cr a year, although on the old method H1 FY27 profit still more than doubled. Overdue export bills of Rs 39.22 cr do not reconcile with the ageing schedule, a Rs 77.61 cr GST notice disclosed in FY25 is absent from the FY26 accounts, and the statutory audit costs Rs 3 lakh. Forex explains about 137 of the 156 bps margin gain. The core business did not shrink: ex-forex EBITDA grew 16.6%, a little faster than ex-forex revenue. The 7% fall in ex-forex PBT is the depreciation step-up from new capacity, Hyd-Air's assets and a new lease, not weaker operations. The classification is permitted (Schedule III does not define other operating revenue, and the line is separately labelled), and part of the gain is economic for a business that exports about 69% of product sales against rupee costs. It is still the more flattering of the two permitted presentations: it lifts reported EBITDA and margin, it includes unrealised year-end restatement, the receivables are unhedged, and it reverses if the rupee strengthens. Neither the deck nor the Q2-Q4 FY26 calls mentioned it. The Q2 FY27 forex figure is not disclosed. Analysts should take forex out of EBITDA before comparing years. Earlier forex losses went below the line as exceptional items, but those were on a foreign-currency term loan, a financing item, so the different line is defensible.

The depreciation switch, as a quality item

Straight-line from 01-Apr-2026: permitted, large, and stated wrongly in the results note

Section 5 sets out the mechanics. For earnings quality, four points matter. First, size: the change lowered H1 FY27 depreciation by Rs 8.41 cr, all booked in Q2 (charge Rs 0.45 cr), and annualises to about Rs 17 cr of pre-tax profit, 23% of FY26 PBT; the gap widens as the Rs 202 cr Phase-2 capex is capitalised. Second, it rests on a 25-year life for plant and machinery, longer than the 15 years Schedule II indicates for general plant, with no technical evaluation disclosed. Third, the results note misstates the after-tax effect as Rs 8.23 cr (a 2% tax rate; the standalone figure is larger than its own pre-tax effect). The true effect at 25.168% is Rs 6.30 cr. The P&L books broadly the right deferred tax, so reported profit is not overstated by the note's error. Fourth, the growth it adds is the smaller part: on the old method, profit was Rs 30.26 cr in Q2 FY27 (+113% against +157% reported), Rs 49.06 cr in H1 (+129% against +159%) and Rs 83.18 cr over the trailing twelve months. The auditors' limited reviews for Q2 FY27 do carry an emphasis-type paragraph on the change, unlabelled, ending "Our conclusion has not been modified in respect of this matter" (the consolidated paragraph gives Q2 depreciation as Rs 44.17 lakh against Rs 44.76 lakh in the P&L, a typo). The CFO handover approved at the same 28-Oct-2026 meeting is the scheduled end of a two-year term that began 28-Nov-2024: a coincidence of timing, with no evidence of a link. The FY26 AR, authorised 05-May-2026, says nothing about the coming change.

That period was 9 months from export through FY24, FY25 and most of FY26, and 15 months from 14-Nov-2025 (RBI press release). Either way FY26 does not reconcile: under 9 months the bills should sit in the over-6-months buckets (Rs 13.99 cr), under 15 months in the over-1-year buckets (Rs 10.57 cr), and both are set against Rs 39.22 cr. Closing the gap needs credit terms of more than about 3 months from shipment. The MD has described terms as often "payment on delivery" and "sometimes ... within 50 to 90 days" (Q4 FY24 call); nothing stated supports terms that long, though no contractual term is disclosed either. A benign reading the evidence cannot exclude: these may be EDPMS bills the bank has not yet closed, where the books already show the receivable settled, netted or short-realised. That would make it a FEMA paperwork and compounding exposure, not an ageing problem. The AR does not say which, and does not say the Rs 39.22 cr sits inside trade receivables. This report does not assert the ageing is misstated.

Provisioning is thin. At 31-Mar-2026 (consolidated) Rs 10.61 cr was more than a year past due, 2.6 times the Rs 4.09 cr a year earlier, against a loss allowance of Rs 0.58 cr; every balance is classed "considered good", and the allowance was nil in FY24 and FY25. The first sizeable bad-debt provision, Rs 1.50 cr, came in H1 FY27 (standalone cash flow). The ECL provision matrix and loss rates are not disclosed.

Concentration: a missing disclosure on most readings, not an established breach

Every AR since FY24 says "The group has no concentration of credit risk as the customer base is widely distributed", and none has a top-customer or 10%-of-revenue statement. On the Q4 FY26 call (06-May-2026, p11) the MD said "our biggest customer in the international market contributes about 25% to 26% of our sales right now". He did not say of what; the question was about the US business excluding skids. Read as total sales, that customer is about 25.5% of revenue; read as exports, about 17.6%; read as US sales, about 8.6%. On the first two readings Ind AS 108 para 34, which applies to single-segment companies too, required a major-customer disclosure the FY26 AR does not contain; on the third, none was due. The prospectus put the top five customers at 24.10% of FY23 revenue, so one customer at a quarter of total sales would be a large shift, uncorroborated elsewhere. The skid business serves one US multinational exclusively, but it was 5% of FY26 revenue, so it bears on the FY27 AR (24% of Q2 FY27 revenue), not FY26. The credit-risk sentence is an Ind AS 107 statement about receivables at the balance-sheet date and is contradicted only if that customer's share of year-end receivables was material, which is not disclosed.

The Rs 77.61 cr GST notice: absent, status unverified

The FY25 AR disclosed it in the notes and in CARO. The FY26 AR mentions it nowhere: not in the contingent-liability note, not in CARO, and no call transcript refers to it. No filing on disk records a court order disposing of it. There is a defensible reason CARO could drop it, since a stayed, unadjudicated notice is arguably not a "demand", but Ind AS 37 still requires a contingent liability to be disclosed unless the outflow is remote, and a writ against a refund-recovery notice is not obviously remote. The right word is unexplained, not hidden. First question for management.

  • The income-tax matter surfaced only with the order. A s.147 order in Mar-2026 for AY 2018-19 implies a reopening notice issued earlier, yet neither the FY24 nor the FY25 AR mentions pending reassessment [inferred]. The order of 25-Mar-2026 was disclosed on 27-Mar-2026; the company admits it missed the 24-hour window because "concerned personnel were on leave ... on account of Ashtami and Ram Navami". The FY26 secretarial compliance report still records no deviation.
  • The GST credit on IPO costs ties to the IPO arithmetic. The company bore Rs 25.99 cr of offer expenses: Rs 22.39 cr charged to securities premium and Rs 3.60 cr taken as GST credit. The s.74 demand is Rs 3.597 cr, the same figure to within Rs 0.2 lakh. If the credit falls, about Rs 7.2 cr plus interest becomes a cost. Section 74 is the fraud or suppression section; the use of it is the department's allegation, under appeal. The AR dates the order 30-Mar-2026 for FY 2023-24; the Reg 30 intimation dates it 07-Apr-2026 for Apr-2021 to Mar-2024.
  • Whether the company also carried the promoter's share of IPO costs is a question, not a finding. The prospectus promised pro-rata sharing with the selling shareholder (46.15% company), yet deducted the whole Rs 30.47 cr estimate for the Rs 351 cr offer, OFS included, from the fresh issue, and the deviation statements call it "the company share". Rs 7.14 cr "expenses paid and recovered" from the parent in FY24 may be the reimbursement. Section 7 has the detail.
