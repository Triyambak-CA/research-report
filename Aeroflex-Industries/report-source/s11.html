Section 11

External headwinds and tailwinds

Aeroflex is an unhedged exporter of a stainless product, so the rupee, the steel price and US trade policy all reach its profit directly. In FY26 the rupee alone supplied all of the margin expansion. From FY27 a fourth driver joins them: the pace at which one platform builds liquid-cooled data-centre capacity in India. None of the sensitivities below is disclosed by the company; each is the report's arithmetic on filed figures, with inputs shown.

Global linkages

  • Export dependence: 60% of Q2FY27 revenue, down from 83% in H1FY25 because skids are domestic; Americas 66% and Europe 25% of exports. US exposure is therefore roughly 40% of revenue (inference: 60% x 66%, Americas taken as the US upper bound).
  • Tariff and trade policy: the sequence from Aug-2025 (50% combined reciprocal duty on India; Section 232 derivative listing of 8307.10.60) to Feb-2026 (framework, 18% announced and never applied), the 10% Section 122 surcharge (Feb-Jul 2026) and the 10% Section 301 duty (from 24-Jul-2026) moved Aeroflex's landed price in the US four times in a year. The rate payable today on its products is not verified. India's HS 8307 exports to the US still grew 17.2% in 2025 (Comtrade).
  • China: the main supplier of Aeroflex's coil, not a visible competitor in the record. A quality-control order or anti-dumping duty on Chinese stainless in India would raise Aeroflex's cost (Section 9).
  • Europe: the scaled incumbents are shrinking (Witzenmann -4.6% in 2025) or changing owner (Senior); demand weakness there is a headwind for volume and an opening for share.

The dominant tailwind and headwind

Tailwind: AI data-centre build in India, where measured additions were 85-130 MW in H1 2026 and liquid cooling has only just begun (Vertiv's first 30 MW deployment, Oct-2026). Each 10 MW supplied is worth about Rs 14 cr of skids on management's ratio. The rupee has also been a tailwind: Rs 15.03 cr of FY26 revenue was forex. Headwind: the same rupee reverses when it strengthens, because nothing is hedged; US trade policy has already cost an 8% price concession and three quarters of disruption against a promised "month or so" (notes-04 C18); and a single platform's India programme, not the market, sets skid volumes. Section 20 tests the industry numbers against the official record.
