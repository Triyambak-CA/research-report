Section 20

Official industry and macro data

The official record is thin for both of Aeroflex's markets: there is no government series for flexible hose, bellows or liquid cooling, and IBEF has no data-centre page. What exists supports the export story (India's HS 8307 exports are measured and growing 15% a year) and complicates the data-centre story: the Economic Survey gives two different figures for India's data-centre capacity, measured additions run far below announced pipelines, and management's own market-size figures disagree with its deck. The platform above Aeroflex

In skids, the layer above Aeroflex is the thermal-management platform that designs the secondary fluid network, owns the data-centre operator relationship and specifies the skid. Management names Vertiv as the industry leader in India and says Vertiv's design team works with Aeroflex's on the SFN design; Vertiv as the customer is an inference, not a disclosure. On that inference, the scale gap is the whole story. Vertiv's trailing revenue of USD 13.12bn is about Rs 1,16,000 cr at Rs 88.4 per USD, roughly 260 times Aeroflex's FY26 revenue and about 700 times its annualised H1FY27 skid revenue (inference). Its market value is USD 73.30bn against Aeroflex's Rs 6,545 cr. Its EBITDA margin (24.31%) is about the same as Aeroflex's, earned on the system rather than the fabrication.

Two facts reframe "competition" as a question of how long the platform chooses to buy. First, Vertiv, the probable customer on the report's inference, reported (secondary, 12-Oct-2026) that it set up its own Indian production facility to mass-manufacture loop skids for its first 30 MW deployment. Second, the platform holds the design and the IP; Aeroflex has no patent on the skid "since they are the principal". The skid business is therefore a share of a platform's make-or-buy decision, and that platform's India output is not disclosed anywhere in the record. In hose, the layer above is the US distributor, which owns the end customer; it is fragmented, so no single platform is sized.

Contradictions between official data and company statements

! 1. The Economic Survey gives India's data-centre capacity two ways

Chapter 7 (para 7.35): about 1.4 GW installed in Q2 2025, rising to about 8 GW by 2030 (citing S&P). Chapter 9 (para 9.47): about 1,280 MW in June 2025 across about 130 private and 49 government data centres, rising to about 4 GW by 2030 ("industry estimates"). Same document, same date (29-Jan-2026), a twofold gap in the 2030 figure. Any skid market sized on the official projection is uncertain by a factor of two before it starts. Both 2030 figures are projections and directional only.

! 2. Management's market sizes disagree with its own deck

Q4FY26 call (06-May-2026): the liquid-cooling market is "INR3-odd billion" growing to "about INR21 billion". Q2FY27 call (29-Oct-2026): "about USD 4 billion to 5 billion, growing more than 30%". The Q4FY26 deck, citing MarketsandMarkets: USD 2,843.6m (CY25) to USD 21,147.0m (CY32e). INR 3 bn is about Rs 300 cr; USD 2.84bn is about Rs 25,100 cr. The INR wording looks like a unit slip for USD, and the later USD 4-5bn oral figure sits above the deck's own base. Asked for the India market size, the MD said "exactly the numbers are not available" (Q2FY27 call). The company has no stable figure for the market it is building capacity for.

! 3. Measured additions trail announced pipelines, and Aeroflex's implied skid megawatts are large against them

JLL's path to 6 GW by 2029 needs about 1.26 GW a year against about 170 MW annualised in H1 2026, roughly 7x; CBRE's path to 3 GW by end-2028 needs about 0.5 GW a year against about 260 MW annualised, roughly 2x (notes-06 B2, derived). Against that measured base, Aeroflex's own numbers imply a lot of liquid cooling. At "about 40-odd skids" per megawatt (Q1FY27 call), the 2,515 skids shipped in H1FY27 correspond to about 63 MW, and the FY27 skid guide of Rs 150-200 cr at Rs 3.39 lakh a skid to about 110-150 MW (inference). That is the same order as India's entire measured data-centre additions in H1 2026 (85-130 MW, all cooling types), while the first large direct-to-chip deployment in India (30 MW) went live only in Oct-2026. The numbers can be reconciled if skids are built months ahead of commissioning, if the skids-per-MW ratio is loose, or if skids serve capacity under construction that the property consultants have not yet counted; the record does not say which. It is the single most useful question to put to management on skid demand.

! 4. The tariff rate management quoted is not in the official record

Management (Q4FY26 call): Section 232 "has been reduced ... with effect from February ... mid-February", "in the range of 10% to 15%". The White House fact sheet of 09-Feb-2026 does not mention Section 232; the Section 232 restructuring is dated 02-Apr-2026, and its annex treatment of HS 8307 was not seen. The 10-15% range is close to the 18% announced framework rate and the 10% Section 122 surcharge that began 24-Feb-2026, but management did not say which measure it meant. Neither confirmed nor refuted.

  • Export share (contradicted): "export will continue to contribute at least 80% to our overall sales" for 3-4 years (Q4FY24 p10). Actual: 74% FY25, 69% FY26, 60% Q2FY27. Benign: domestic grew faster, mostly skids.
  • Largest Indian exporter (consistent): about 29% of India's HS 8307 exports on the report's arithmetic supports "number one by far" in order of magnitude but does not establish it.
  • Peers in data centres (unsupported): management says Senior and Parker "are supplying" data-centre cooling; Senior's FY25 results release has no data-centre or liquid-cooling mention. Parker not checked.
  • Core demand (confirmed for H1FY27 only): the deck calls core hose demand "encouraging". Exports grew 6.2% in FY26, below India's HS 8307 line (+15.1% in 2025), then about +46% in H1FY27.
  • Input prices (no contradiction): management made no statement on stainless direction on the last three calls; nickel rose 9.4% in Apr-Oct 2026 on the FY26 average but was 9.6% below Mar-2026 by Oct-2026.
  • Policy (consistent): management claims no policy tailwind, and the Budget gives none to cooling hardware or hose.

Projections labelled P, including the 2030 capacity targets, the 2031-32 power-demand figure and all commissioned market forecasts, are directional only. The Economic Survey (ch 14) itself names power, water, finance and GPU supply as the binding constraints and warns against scale for its own sake.
