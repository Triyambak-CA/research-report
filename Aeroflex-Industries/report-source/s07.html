Section 07

Promoter, governance and capital allocation

Aeroflex has no individual promoter. It is controlled by a listed parent, Aeroflex Enterprises (formerly Sat Industries), which bought a sick, wilful-defaulter-listed company for about Rs 20.5 cr in 2018-2019, took out Rs 265 cr in 2023 through pre-IPO sales and the offer for sale, and has not bought a share since. Since the IPO the record is a mix: low pay, no pledge and a modest dividend on one side; on the other, a preferential issue whose use of money was described two ways on the same day, an 8x jump in sales to a parent-owned UAE trader with no balances disclosed, a five-month COO, and two of four independent directors who also sit on the parent's board.

History: from BIFR to a takeover at Rs 1.95 a share

The company was referred to BIFR as sick on 14-Jul-2010. SBI took possession of its assets, lenders ran SARFAESI and DRT proceedings, and the company sat on the CIBIL wilful-defaulter list (prospectus p.268-269). Sat Industries took control on 02-Apr-2018; lenders settled by compromise or one-time settlement (OTS), or assigned their debt to IARC and Edelweiss ARC; no-dues letters run from Mar-2018 to Sep-2018, and the CIBIL status was cleared only by 05-Jun-2023, under three months before listing. Sat paid about Rs 20.50 cr in all for 92.18%, including Rs 14.74 cr to founder Yusuf Mohammad Kagzi for 67,01,300 shares at Rs 22 (Rs 10 face value) on 22-Oct-2019, and Avigo and Bond Street preference positions bought at Rs 3.45 to Rs 80.84, partly from Park Continental Pvt Ltd, itself a 3.58% holder of the parent's promoter entity Sat Invest (prospectus p.92-94, p.208-209). The auditor-certified weighted average cost of the shares sold in the IPO was Rs 1.95 a share on the Rs 2 face value. Some RoC records for the 1994 allotment are untraceable (prospectus p.87).

The OTS still has a cost. An income-tax reassessment order of 25-Mar-2026 for AY 2018-19 treats the Rs 60.92 cr of waived working-capital interest as income and raises a demand of Rs 41.76 cr, with s.270A penalty proceedings initiated; the company has appealed (Reg 30, 27-Mar-2026, filed late, which the company attributes to festival leave). A separate s.74 CGST order of Rs 7.19 cr (tax plus equal penalty) disallows input credit on IPO expenses (Reg 30, 08-Apr-2026). Neither is provided for (Section 9). The legal case against the income-tax demand is reasonable: interest never allowed as a deduction under s.43B is arguably outside s.41(1), and most no-dues letters fall in AY 2019-20, not AY 2018-19. But the cost of a cheap 2018 entry is now a contingent claim on every shareholder, including IPO and preferential investors who paid 55 to 94 times the parent's entry price.

! A question for management: who paid for the offer for sale?

The prospectus says offer costs other than listing fees, stamp duty, audit fees and corporate advertising "shall be shared by our Company and the Promoter Selling Shareholder, on a pro rata basis, in proportion to the number of Equity Shares" (p.100, p.109): 46.15% company, 53.85% promoter. Yet it deducts the entire estimated offer cost of Rs 30.47 cr, which it describes as 8.68% of the whole Rs 351 cr offer (p.110), from the Rs 162 cr fresh issue to arrive at net proceeds of Rs 131.53 cr (p.101), and the later deviation statements say "the company share for Offer Expenses of Rs.30.473 crore was deducted". Actual costs borne by the company were Rs 25.99 cr: Rs 22.39 cr charged to securities premium plus Rs 3.60 cr taken as GST credit, the same Rs 3.60 cr the s.74 order now disallows (AR FY24 Note 33). At the estimate, a 46.15% share would have been about Rs 14.1 cr, so the company may have carried up to about Rs 11.9 cr of the promoter's costs, less whatever the carve-outs legitimately put on the company alone. Against that, the parent paid Aeroflex Rs 7.14 cr of "expenses paid and recovered" in FY24 (AR FY24 Note 36), which may be the reimbursement, and the drafting may simply be loose. The parent's own books cannot settle it: its FY24 "profit on sale of share" figures (Rs 264.24 cr and Rs 255.81 cr in two places) mix in other disposals. This is a question to put, not a finding.

The preferential issue, Feb-2026

! Rs 55 cr raised at the formula floor, with its use described two ways on the same day

Terms. 30,10,398 shares at Rs 182.70 = Rs 54.99 cr, allotted 03-Feb-2026 to six non-promoter investors: Ashish Kacholia 4,10,509; Bengal Finance and Investment 4,10,509 (beneficial owner stated as Suresh Kumar Agarwal, not Kacholia); Sanjay Surana 8,21,018; R K Investments (through Vaibhav Shah) 8,21,018; Madhu Silica 2,73,672; Nareshkumar Mehta 2,73,672. Kacholia and Bengal Finance were the two largest pre-IPO buyers at Rs 87.56; the other four held nothing before. Dilution 2.33%; no promoter, director or KMP interest declared, and none is shown in any filing read. Acting in concert is not established; Kacholia and Bengal Finance together hold 4.33%, below the 5% takeover-code threshold either way.

Price. The notice says only that Rs 182.70 is "higher than the calculated price". Replicated from NSE bhavcopy for the relevant date of 16-Dec-2025, the ICDR floor (the higher of the 90-day and 10-day volume-weighted averages) is Rs 182.46, so the issue was priced 24 paise (0.13%) above it; any window of 88 to 92 days gives Rs 182.31 to Rs 182.66. Rs 182.70 was above the Rs 177.31 close on the board day, 18-Dec-2025, so "cheap at issue" is not supported. What is supported: the board met from 5:00 to 5:55 pm that day and approved both the issue and a skid capacity jump from 2,000 to 15,000 units in the same meeting; the outcome was signed at 7:50 pm, after market hours; the next day the stock rose 9.2% to Rs 193.69 on 2.81 cr shares. The partner's demand forecast that sized the capex was, by management's later account, in the board's hands then (Q3FY26 call p16; Q1FY27 call p8); its value (a Rs 45 cr pipeline) was first stated publicly on 29-Jan-2026, after pricing and before allotment (Section 8). At Rs 494.60 the allottees' paper gain is about Rs 93.9 cr on Rs 55.0 cr, 2.71x, in about nine and a half months.

Use of money, two versions, same date. The board outcome of 18-Dec-2025 (Annexure A) says a Rs 97.56 cr capex plan is financed by internal accruals of Rs 42.56 cr and "Preferential issue - INR 55.00 Cr". The shareholders' notice dated the same day lists long-term working capital Rs 41.52 cr, general corporate purposes Rs 12.02 cr and issue expenses Rs 1.45 cr, and no capex. The 05-Jan-2026 corrigendum, issued after exchange observations, restated the same objects to the rupee and split the expenses into advisory fees of Rs 1,41,60,000 and meeting costs of Rs 3,40,000. Shareholders voted on the notice version. On the 29-Jan-2026 call an analyst said "we have raised the money to fund our capex"; the MD did not correct him (Q3FY26 call p9). The Q2 FY27 deviation statement measures use only against the notice objects, reports "no deviation", and says the money was fully used by 30-Sep-2026; only Rs 12.59 cr had been used by 31-Mar-2026 (AR FY26). Because the legal objects are working capital and general purposes, use on capex cannot be tested, and a "no deviation" statement is automatic either way. Cash is fungible, so no misuse is shown. The probable reason (inferred) is that a capex object needs project detail and a deployment schedule, and a later change needs a special resolution; working capital needs neither.

Fee and vote. The Rs 1.42 cr advisory fee (2.6% of a placement to six named investors; consistent with Rs 1.20 cr plus GST) has no named recipient in the notice, corrigendum, allotment intimation, deviation statements or AR FY26. Total preferential expenses charged to securities premium were Rs 1.79 cr, above the Rs 1.45 cr object. No monitoring agency was required (issue under Rs 100 cr). At the 15-Jan-2026 meeting the promoter voted 8,66,36,663 shares for (95.8% of votes cast); public institutions polled 28,83,082 shares (50.92% of their holding), all for; public non-institutions polled 9,41,554 shares (2.54%), 1,791 against. Non-institutional votes cast were fewer than either Kacholia's or Bengal Finance's pre-held shares, so neither voted those shares (scrutiniser's report p.7).

Context. A Rs 400 cr QIP authority passed on 15-Jan-2025 for acquisitions was never used; the MD had said "the QIP will only be done once the acquisition opportunity" is final (Q1FY26 call p11). No acquisition has been announced. The Rs 202 cr capex announced in Oct-2026 is to be funded by "internal accruals, bridge loan financing and equity fund raise if required" (Q2FY27 call p4), so further dilution is openly on the table. Counter-argument, fairly stated: the formula is mandatory, the price was above market on the day, the ramp request had been public since 29-Oct-2025 (Q2FY26 call p8), and Rs 55 cr is too small for an economic QIP.

Hyd-Air: a small acquisition, explained three ways

On 02-Apr-2024 Aeroflex announced the purchase of 100% of Hyd-Air Engineering (Chakan, Pune) for Rs 17,20,86,172; FY24 turnover Rs 8.06 cr, a loss of Rs 2.41 cr. The standalone books now carry the investment at Rs 27.80 cr: the Rs 17.21 cr price plus a Rs 10.59 cr loan converted into equity in FY25 (AR FY25 p161, CARO iii(a)), so the gap between the announced and booked cost is a post-closing infusion, not a revised price. A further Rs 5.25 cr loan was given in FY26 (Rs 5.40 cr outstanding, AR FY26 p145). Total committed: about Rs 33.2 cr. The price was about book value only after Hyd-Air booked an Rs 11.70 cr revaluation of its assets in its FY24 accounts, days before the share purchase agreement; before it, net worth was about Rs 4.24 cr. Goodwill (Rs 1.27 cr) is price minus the seller's book value, with no purchase-price allocation, and the acquisition is not shown separately in the cash flow (AR FY25 Note 40, p115). Hyd-Air earned PAT of Rs 0.49 cr in FY25 and Rs 0.24 cr in FY26, and lost Rs 0.27 cr in Q1 FY27. The funding was "entirely funded by internal accruals" (Q4FY24 call p3), then "mix of internal accruals and IPO proceeds" (Q1FY25 call p3); the thesis was Rs 30-35 cr of third-party sales by FY26 (Q2FY25 call p8-9), then captive use (Q4FY26 call p5), and on 28-Oct-2026 the board approved merging it into Aeroflex with an appointed date of 01-Apr-2026. The seller is not named; the founder stayed as Hyd-Air's MD and appears in Aeroflex's KMP list as a director (AR FY26 p131). Any related-party angle is unverified.

! Governance verdict

The alignment signals that are easy to see are good: no pledge, modest pay, no promoter selling since listing, a debt-free balance sheet. The ones that need digging are not. The controlling shareholder extracted 13 times its money before the public came in (whether the company also carried part of its offer cost is an open question, above); the only capital raised since was placed at the formula floor at the same board meeting that approved a price-moving capacity expansion, announced that evening, with its purpose told two ways on the same day and its adviser unnamed; sales to the parent's own UAE trader rose eightfold without balances; two of the four independent directors sit on the parent's board; the COO left within five months and the CFO is changing in the quarter of an accounting-method change. No rule has been shown to be broken. The pattern is of a board that, when it had a choice, chose the route most flexible for the controlling shareholder and least tested by the market.
