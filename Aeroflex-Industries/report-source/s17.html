Section 17

Capex deployment and return on capex

Aeroflex spent Rs 212 cr on fixed assets in FY24-FY26, 4.9 times its depreciation, and has announced Rs 202 cr more. Over FY23 to FY26 the money earned little: EBIT rose about Rs 26 cr, a pre-tax incremental return of 9-12% on the capital deployed, and 5-8% once the larger forex gain booked in revenue is stripped out, below a 12.5% cost of capital and below the business's own 18-21% average. Bellows and Hyd-Air have not earned their cost. The turn came in FY27 from skids: on the H1 FY27 run rate the incremental return could reach about 24%, a ceiling rather than a run rate, almost all of it from one customer's business, while hose capacity is being expanded at 65-80% utilisation.

Capex by purpose

The company reports one segment and does not split capex by purpose in any filing. Pieced together from the annual reports and calls:

  • Hose and braiding capacity, 11 to 20 mn metres a year (growth). 11 to 13.5 mn m by Mar-2024 for "an approximate CAPEX of about 30 crores"; 13.5 to 16.5 mn m for "between 35 to 40 crores" (Q3FY24 call p10); 16.5 to 20 mn m completed by Oct-2026, cost not separately stated. FY25 standalone additions were Rs 77.13 cr (land 0.60, buildings 13.83, plant and machinery 57.10; AR FY25 p186).
  • Metal bellows, Palava (growth). Guided at "another INR45 crores" in two phases over FY25-FY26 (Q2FY24 call p11) and "about 43 to 44 crores" (Q3FY24 call p10), later "most likely ... less than what we had budgeted" (Q1FY25 call p12). The actual cost is not disclosed. Miniature bellows: Rs 23 cr cut to Rs 10.5 cr with capacity cut from 240,000 to about 50,000 pieces (Q3FY26 call p2). Some bellows machines were redirected to liquid-cooling components (Q2FY26 call p4).
  • Hyd-Air (acquisition, booked as capex). About Rs 25.5 cr of acquired assets in FY25, of which land Rs 11.16 cr, after the seller's Rs 11.70 cr pre-sale revaluation. Aeroflex's total commitment is about Rs 33.2 cr: the Rs 17.21 cr price, a Rs 10.59 cr loan converted to equity, and a Rs 5.40 cr loan outstanding (Section 7).
  • Liquid-cooling skids (growth). 2,000 to 15,000 units a year "budgeted the capex of INR48 crores" (Q1FY27 call p9), inside the Rs 97.56 cr plan of 18-Dec-2025 that also covers robotic welding and annealing by Dec-2026 and, by the MD's account, working capital (Q3FY26 call p9). How much has been spent to Sep-2026 is not disclosed. The new Chakan plant appears to be leased (inferred from the right-of-use additions; the filings do not name the site).
  • Automation: two robotic welding lines commissioned by May-2026, about 16 months late (Section 8).
  • Announced, FY27-FY28: Rs 202 cr: Rs 124 cr to take hose capacity from 20 to 30 mn m (brownfield at Taloja plus a new hose-assembly facility) and Rs 78 cr to take skids from 15,000 to 30,000 by Mar-2028 (Q2FY27 call p3-4, p15). Funding: "internal accruals, bridge loan financing and equity fund raise if required".
  • Non-operating: none found. No head office, aircraft or land bank purchase; Hyd-Air's land came with the acquisition.

Replacement ratio

Not computable. Maintenance capex is not disclosed, and the statements show net PP&E only, without gross block or accumulated depreciation. On everything visible, capex is overwhelmingly growth: it ran at 2.7 to 9.2 times depreciation in each year, and FY26 cash capex reconciles to Rs 57.49 cr of PP&E additions, Rs 0.34 cr of intangibles and a Rs 13.26 cr rise in CWIP. There is no sign of an asset base being consumed faster than it is renewed; the risk here runs the other way, building ahead of demand. Two cautions on the depreciation base itself: plant and machinery is depreciated over 25 years, longer than the 15 years Schedule II indicates for general plant, and the switch from WDV to straight-line from 01-Apr-2026 cuts the annual charge by about Rs 17 cr (Section 6). Depreciation is therefore a weak proxy for replacement need in either direction.

  • Against the benchmarks. The FY23-FY26 increment, 9-12% pre-tax as reported and 5-8% without forex (roughly 4-9% after tax), sits below a 12.5% cost of equity and well below the return on the business's whole operating capital: FY26 operating EBIT of Rs 73.66 cr on average capital employed of about Rs 400 cr is 18.4% (21.0% net of cash; screener shows 18.9%). The new money earned less than the old.
  • Why FY26 understates it. FY26 carried WDV depreciation of Rs 26.08 cr, front-loaded on a young asset base; bellows and Hyd-Air were idle or near break-even; skids shipped only from Q3 FY26; and CWIP was Rs 23 cr. A forward test: H1 FY27 operating EBIT was Rs 75.53 cr (PBT 75.50 plus finance costs 0.95 less other income 0.92); restoring the Rs 8.41 cr of WDV depreciation gives Rs 67.12 cr, Rs 134.24 cr annualised. Against FY23 that is Rs 86.55 cr more EBIT on Rs 362.31 cr more capital (equity Rs 497.42 cr, borrowings Rs 0.85 cr and leases Rs 23.14 cr at Sep-2026, less Rs 159.10 cr at Mar-2023): about 24% pre-tax. The H1 forex gain is not disclosed and a fast-growing half annualised flatters, so treat this as a ceiling, not a run rate.
  • What made the difference. Almost all of the FY27 improvement is skids (Rs 82.42 cr of H1 revenue against nil a year earlier) and the core hose recovery in Q2 FY27. The deployments that management chose on its own initiative, bellows and Hyd-Air, roughly Rs 75-90 cr together on the guided figures, have earned close to nothing in two years.
! Hose capacity is growing faster than hose volume

Hose capacity went from 13.5 mn m (Mar-2024) to 17.5 mn m (Jan-2026) to 20 mn m (Oct-2026), and Rs 124 cr is now committed to take it to 30 mn m by Mar-2028. Utilisation over the same period: 87.9% (FY24), 75% (FY25), 70% (FY26) and 60-71% in the FY26 quarters (screener Insights, citing the decks; the company's own 15-Jan-2025 filing gave 85.18% for Apr-Dec 2024), "between 65% capacity utilization ... 65% to 66%" on 28-Jul-2026 (Q1FY27 call p4), and "about an 80% utilization" on 29-Oct-2026 (Q2FY27 call p15). The one strong quarter is real (non-skid revenue up 39% quarter on quarter in Q2 FY27), but on the record to Jun-2026 the hose line added a third more capacity while volume barely moved. A 50% expansion on top of that rests on demand that has been demonstrated for one quarter, from a customer base whose largest international member the MD describes as contributing "about 25% to 26% of our sales right now" (Q4FY26 call p11; whether the base is total, international or US sales is not clear).

! Capital-allocation verdict

Aeroflex has a reinvestment outlet, but it is narrow. Skids look like a high-return use of capital on management's own numbers, sized to one customer's forecast under a five-year India exclusivity that fixes the counterparty, not the volume (Section 18). The core hose business earns its cost of capital on average but its last two expansions have not yet filled. Bellows and Hyd-Air, the two bets the company made on its own, have not earned their cost. With payout at 9-10% and the next Rs 202 cr partly funded by bridge loans or new equity, the case for retaining rather than distributing rests on the skid returns holding and on the hose expansion filling faster than the last two did. There is insufficient disclosure (no skid margin, no bellows profit, no capex by line) to verify either; ask for capex and returns by business line.
