Section 15

Pre-mortem and anti-thesis

Assume it is Nov-2028 and the stock has halved. This is the most likely story of how that happened, argued as a short-seller would argue it.

The short case, argued sincerely

  1. One customer is the whole growth story, and it holds every card. Skids went from nothing to 24% of revenue in four quarters, all sold to one US multinational on a five-year India exclusivity that, on management's own description, fixes who supplies, not how much (Section 18). Firm visibility is about one quarter. The capex for 30,000 skids a year was sized on the customer's forecast. When that forecast is cut, or a second Indian source is qualified, the new plant sits idle and its straight-line depreciation does not.
  2. The core business was flat before skids arrived, and is now running below capacity. Exports grew 6% in FY26. Hose utilisation fell from 88% (FY24) to 65% (Q2 FY27) while capacity went from 13.5 to 20 mn metres, and a further Rs 124 cr is to take it to 30 mn. US tariffs on the main export market are unresolved. Strip out forex and FY26 operating margin did not move.
  3. The profit line was helped three ways in eighteen months. Rs 15 cr of exchange gains booked as operating revenue in FY26; a change of depreciation method worth about Rs 17 cr a year of pre-tax profit, booked entirely in one quarter, announced at the same board meeting that named a new CFO (the outgoing CFO's two-year term was ending, so the timing may be routine); an EBITDA definition that widened the FY26 beat. None was hidden, and none was explained on a call.
  4. The disclosures do not match what management says. The annual report says there is no concentration of credit risk, the customer base being widely distributed; the MD says one customer is 25-26% of sales and a second is 24% of the latest quarter. There is no major-customer disclosure. A related party in the UAE, held by the MD as nominee for the promoter group, bought eight times more in FY26 and was paid a new fee, with no year-end balances shown. Export receivables overdue beyond the FEMA realisation period ran at 30-46% of the receivables book.
  5. Management's promises on new things have a 25% hit rate. Bellows missed by 70-90%; miniature bellows cut by 79%; Hyd-Air's third-party growth thesis was dropped and the company is being merged in; robotics produced nothing; an acquisition has been "in progress" for four years; "we don't plan to take on any debt" became "bridge loan financing" in nine months.
  6. The history is a sick company. BIFR from 2010, SARFAESI action and a wilful-defaulter listing before the 2018 settlement whose interest waiver is now the subject of a Rs 41.76 cr tax demand. The company has had no credit rating since Oct-2023, a Rs 3 lakh statutory audit fee, and lost a COO after five months and a company secretary and a CFO since.
  7. The holders are momentum holders. Mutual funds went from 4.87% to 0.92%; the IPO anchor has left. The register is 1.2 lakh mostly retail holders plus the Feb-2026 preferential allottees, who are sitting on a 2.7x paper gain with their lock-in over. When growth slows there is no long-only buyer at 79x.

Thesis-killers (observable, specific)

For the bull case

  • Skid units below 1,500 in any quarter of 2027, after 15,000 capacity is installed in Dec-2026.
  • FY27 revenue below Rs 680 cr, a 5% miss on the Rs 715-725 cr guide.
  • Any disclosure that the skid customer has a second Indian supplier, or a skid price cut.
  • Export revenue down year on year in H2 FY27, with US tariffs the stated reason.
  • An equity raise below Rs 400 a share, or debt above Rs 100 cr to fund the Rs 202 cr plan.
  • Income-tax appeal on the AY 2018-19 demand lost at the first appellate stage.

For the bear case

  • A first international skid order from the same customer, with a value, in FY27 or FY28.
  • A second named data-centre customer for skids or SFN-adjacent products.
  • EBITDA margin excluding forex above 23% for two consecutive half-years.
  • Hose utilisation back above 80% on the 20 mn metre base by Q4 FY27, as guided.
  • Cash from operations above 75% of EBITDA in FY27 with receivable days below 100.
  • A major-customer note and related-party balances in the FY27 annual report.

Base rates

Indian small-cap component suppliers re-rated on one large new customer in a hot end-market have a poor record once that customer's ordering normalises: the pattern of a capacity build sized on the customer's forecast, a peak multiple set during the ramp, and then a pricing reset at the first renegotiation is familiar from Indian EMS, solar-module and telecom-equipment suppliers. The base rate for companies trading near 80 times earnings delivering the 29% compound growth the price needs for five years is low; most de-rate even when they grow. Aeroflex's own record adds a specific reason for caution: its revenue promises for new verticals are met a quarter of the time. The counterweight is real: the skid business so far has beaten its own first revenue guidance, because the customer, not management, sets the schedule.
