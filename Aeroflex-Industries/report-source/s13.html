Shareholding, positioning and flow analysis

The institutions that underwrote the IPO have left. Mutual funds held 4.87% in Mar-2025 and 0.92% in Sep-2026; the largest anchor, Nippon India Small Cap, is gone. Foreign portfolio investors rose from 0.51% to 3.93% after the data-centre story, two-thirds of it through Category II funds whose names are not public. The promoter has not moved, the two pre-IPO buyers who joined the preferential issue have not sold, and the four new allottees cannot be tracked. A Rs 6,545 cr company with no F&O contract, outside the Nifty 500, about 70% of whose daily volume is intraday.

Reading: the register has turned over from IPO institutions to retail, small foreign funds and a handful of known individual investors, with the promoter fixed at 65.47%. There is no institutional overhang left to sell, and an index promotion to the Nifty 500 would bring the first mechanical demand. But with about 34.5% free float, more than half of it with small retail holders (18.2% of capital), no derivatives and most volume intraday, the price has no long-only anchor, which is why it has moved between Rs 158 and Rs 614 in a year.
