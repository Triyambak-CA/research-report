Section 14

Valuation

At Rs 494.60 (NSE close, 18-Nov-2026) Aeroflex is priced at Rs 6,545 cr: about 79 times trailing profit measured on one consistent depreciation basis, and 45 times trailing EBITDA. Both sit at the 82nd percentile of the stock's own post-listing range and roughly twice the multiples of the global companies it supplies. The price already assumes profit compounding at 29-37% a year for five years and a premium exit multiple at the end of it.

The right multiple

Price to earnings on a trailing base that uses one depreciation method is the primary measure. The company is net cash (about Rs 8.5 cr after leases), so EV and market value barely differ, and the business is a manufacturer whose capex runs ahead of depreciation, which makes EV/EBITDA flatter it. The reported trailing P/E of 73-74 (screener) is not usable as it stands: it adds the Q2 FY27 depreciation catch-up to three quarters on the old method. The base used here is trailing profit of Rs 83.2 cr, with H1 FY27 restated to the old written-down-value method (Rs 49.06 cr; Section 5) plus H2 FY26 as reported (Rs 34.13 cr). On that base the P/E is 78.7. On straight-line depreciation throughout, a fairer forward-looking base, trailing profit would be about Rs 93-94 cr and the P/E about 70.

The test: what five-year profit path lets a buyer today earn 12.5% a year, the assumed cost of equity, with no dividends counted. A full free-cash-flow reverse DCF (WACC 12.5%, incremental return on capital 22%, five years of growth then a five-year fade to 5%) needs 67% a year for five years; it is punitive because growth above the return on capital makes free cash flow negative, so it is shown only as the outer bound.

Is it achievable? The 40x row, 29% a year to Rs 296 cr of profit in FY31, is roughly management's own plan: FY30 revenue of "about Rs 1,500 cr" at a 25% EBITDA margin (Q2 FY27 call p13-14) would give profit near Rs 270-280 cr after straight-line depreciation and tax. So the price is about where the arithmetic lands if the plan is delivered in full and the market still pays 40x in FY31. Against that: management's record on new-vertical revenue promises is 25% (Section 8); the plan depends on one skid customer whose firm visibility is about a quarter (Section 18); the core hose line ran at 65-70% utilisation in FY26-FY27 while capacity is being expanded 50%; and the 25% margin target has already slipped once (from FY27 to "blended", Section 8). At 30x, the multiple Parker and Vertiv earn on similar margins, the plan would need to be beaten by about a third.

Cross-checks

  • Against its own history: trailing P/E 73.4 on the screener basis against a post-listing median of 52.6 (82nd percentile); EV/EBITDA 45.4 against 32.8 (82nd percentile). The listing history is only 39 months, all of it inside one small-cap cycle, so the median is not a long-run anchor.
  • Against peers: no listed Indian pure-play exists (Section 3). Indian engineered-steel and thermal names trade at 21-23x EV/EBITDA (Ratnamani 22.5, Venus Pipes 21.6, Kirloskar Pneumatic 21.5); Indian data-centre-linked names at 37-53x (KRN 37.2, Netweb 43.1, Voltas 52.9). The global companies Aeroflex supplies or competes with trade at 16-23x (Vertiv 22.9, Parker-Hannifin 22.3, nVent 16.2), and Senior plc, owner of the closest operating comparable Senior Flexonics, was taken private in 2026 at about 22x earnings. Aeroflex at 46x is priced as a data-centre company, on a revenue base that is three-quarters hose. All multiples StockAnalysis, 18-Nov-2026 (secondary).
  • Sum of the parts: core hose and assemblies, on FY27 run-rate of about Rs 560 cr and a 22% margin, earn about Rs 85 cr of after-tax profit; at 30x, a premium to global hose makers, about Rs 2,550 cr. That leaves about Rs 4,000 cr of today's price for a skid business with one customer, Rs 101 cr of revenue in its first three full quarters, and no disclosed margin. At a 23% margin, Rs 4,000 cr is about 45x the skid profit the Dec-2026 capacity could earn at full use (15,000 units, Rs 509 cr of revenue).
  • Contingent items: tax demands of about Rs 49 cr (Section 9) are Rs 3.7 a share, under 1% of the price. They matter for governance reading, not for the valuation.

Margin of safety

None on the numbers as they stand. The probability-weighted value is about 11% below the price, the base case 16% below, and the price already needs management's FY30 plan delivered on time with a 40x exit. That is not the same as saying the price is wrong: a supplier that becomes the India source for a global thermal-management leader, then its export source, could earn the bull case. It is saying the buyer at Rs 494.60 is paying for that outcome now.
