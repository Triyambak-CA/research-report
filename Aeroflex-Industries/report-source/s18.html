Section 18

Order book, execution record and growth strategy

Aeroflex has never published an order book, and its MD has said so on the record. What exists instead is a short register: one exclusive five-year contract to supply liquid-cooling skids in India to the Indian subsidiary of a large US company, a handful of quantified skid orders and pipelines, and an unnamed largest international hose customer whose share the MD put at "about 25% to 26% of our sales", on an unclear base. Book-to-bill cannot be computed. The execution record splits unusually: where the customer sets the schedule (skids, core hose) the company delivers revenue roughly as guided; where it set its own course (bellows, Hyd-Air, robotics, exports of skids) it has missed by wide margins.

Proof there is no order book

A search of all 13 transcripts, 14 investor decks, the FY24-FY26 annual reports and the Reg 30 filings for "order book", "order backlog", "orders in hand", "order inflow" and "pending orders" finds no total order book in rupees in any company document (notes-02 section d; image-only results filings for Jun-2023, Dec-2023, Mar-2024 and Mar-2025 could not be searched). Reading: for forecasting, core hose revenue and EBITDA margin belong in the base case on management's record, with capacity dates pushed out a quarter or two. Skid revenue belongs in the base case only to the extent of the customer's visible schedule (about one quarter); the FY27 Rs 150-200 cr guide is credible on the H1 run rate. Bellows, Hyd-Air third-party sales, skid exports and acquisitions belong in an option bucket at best.

Growth strategy as management states it (Q1 FY27 and Q2 FY27 calls)

  1. Core hoses and assemblies: "capacity expansion, automation, product innovation and deeper market penetration" (Q2FY27 p4); 20 to 30 mn m by Mar-2028 for Rs 124 cr; base-business growth "about 15% to 20%" (Q4FY26 p19); peak hose revenue Rs 850-900 cr. Extension of demonstrated capability (an established export franchise in about 90 countries, by the company's description), but utilisation has to catch up first (Section 17).
  2. Scale liquid cooling: skids to 15,000 by Dec-2026 and 30,000 by Mar-2028 for Rs 78 cr; FY27 skids Rs 150-200 cr; peak Rs 300-350 cr (Q3FY26 p4). Manufacturing capability demonstrated (1,475 units a quarter); the commercial capability is the customer's. Design work has moved in-house, which Aeroflex says it now does.
  3. Technology and R&D: one patent granted (automated 3D modelling of braided hose assemblies); "more than 16 products under development" (Q4FY26 p3). Not yet visible in revenue.
  4. International skid supply to the same customer: sampling and pricing (Q2FY27 p10). Depends on winning against the customer's other global suppliers without exclusivity; not demonstrated.
  5. Products adjacent to the SFN skid, unnamed until approved (Q2FY27 p9). No evidence either way.
  6. Hyd-Air folded in as backward integration for fittings (Q2FY27 p8). Reverses the 2024 plan; a cost play, not growth.
  7. Acquisitions: "already working on certain potential acquisitions" (Q2FY27 p14), described as in progress since Q1 FY25 and "very soon" in May-2025. No acquisition other than Hyd-Air in four years, and Hyd-Air has not earned its cost. Requires capability the company has not shown.
  8. Funding: internal accruals, bridge loans and equity "if required" (Q2FY27 p4). Reverses "we don't plan to take on any debt" (Jan-2026).
  9. Targets: FY27 revenue Rs 715-725 cr (+60%); above Rs 1,000 cr in FY29; about Rs 1,500 cr in FY30 at 25% EBITDA (Q2FY27 p13-14). FY27 is on track; FY29-FY30 require items 1 and 2 at full utilisation plus growth not yet identified.

The common thread: items 2, 4 and 5 all rest on one customer relationship, governed by a contract that fixes exclusivity for five years in India and nothing about volume. The strategy is credible where it extends what Aeroflex already does at scale (braided hose, assemblies, and now skid assembly to a customer's design) and weakest where it needs new selling or deal-making capability (bellows sales, exports of skids, acquisitions), which the company has not hired for: its COO left after five months and no business head has appeared on a call.
