## 1. Ranked findings (most serious first)

### F1. Rs 48.95 cr of fresh tax demands from legacy events, one disclosed late (ESTABLISHED facts; outcome uncertain)
- Claim: in Mar-Apr 2026 the company received an income-tax demand of Rs 41.76 cr and a GST demand plus equal penalty of Rs 7.19 cr; together about 88% of FY26 consolidated PAT (Rs 55.5 cr).
- Evidence:
  - Income tax: order u/s 147 r/w 144B dated 25-Mar-2026, AY 2018-19, demand Rs 41,75,88,940; issue is the waiver of interest on working-capital loans in the 2018 one-time settlement, Rs 6,091.81 lakh, added as income; penalty proceedings u/s 270A initiated; appeal to CIT(A) filed 08-Apr-2026 (Reg 30 intimation dated 27-Mar-2026; AR FY26 consolidated Note 43.03; parent AR FY26 Note 34(b)(iii)).
  - The Reg 30 intimation was filed outside the 24-hour window; the company says the order arrived by e-mail at 10:17 pm on 25-Mar-2026 and staff were on festival leave on 26-Mar-2026 (same intimation, closing paragraph).
  - GST: order u/s 74 CGST Act (the fraud / wilful-misstatement section), demand Rs 3,59,70,285 plus penalty Rs 3,59,70,285 = Rs 7.19 cr, on input tax credit claimed on IPO expenses and related matters for Apr-2021 to Mar-2024 (Reg 30 intimation 08-Apr-2026). SCN reference ZD271124023695G.
  - Smaller: GST FY2019-20 Rs 1.36 cr on assignment of lease rights (order 27-Mar-2026) and Panvel property tax Rs 0.72 cr (AR FY26 Notes 43.02, 43.04). No Reg 30 filing for these two was found in the local set (UNVERIFIED whether one exists; they may sit below the materiality policy).
- Reconciliation that ties the GST demand to the IPO numbers (ESTABLISHED arithmetic): the fresh issue raised Rs 162.00 cr; net proceeds credited were Rs 136.011 cr (Statement of Deviation, Mar-2024 and Jun-2024 footnotes), so Rs 25.989 cr was deducted as issue expenses; the company charged only Rs 22.394 cr to securities premium (AR FY24 Note 33 and other-equity note). The gap, Rs 3.595 cr, equals the ITC now disallowed (Rs 3.597 cr). So the company took Rs 3.6 cr of IPO-expense GST as input credit instead of cost; if the order stands, that Rs 3.6 cr plus an equal penalty plus interest returns to the P&L.
- Inconsistencies inside the filings (ESTABLISHED): AR FY26 Note 43.01 dates the GST order 30-Mar-2026 and calls it FY2023-24; the Reg 30 intimation says uploaded 07-Apr-2026 and covers Apr-2021 to Mar-2024. Reg 30 cites "section 41"; the AR calls the addition "undisclosed business income".
- Why it matters: neither demand is provided for. The income-tax demand alone is about 75% of FY26 PAT and about 9% of 31-Mar-2026 equity (Rs 447.41 cr). A s.270A penalty, if levied, is 50% of the tax on under-reported income or 200% for misreporting. Prior ARs (FY24, FY25) carry no contingent-liability mention of a reassessment for AY 2018-19 (grep of AR-FY24/AR-FY25 for 148 / reassess / OTS found nothing); if the s.148 notice predates 31-Mar-2025, investors had no warning (UNVERIFIED: notice date not public in these files; s.153(2) timing suggests the notice was served in FY2024-25).
- Counter-argument (strong): (a) s.41(1) taxes a remission only of a liability earlier ALLOWED as a deduction; interest to banks is deductible only on payment under s.43B, so unpaid NPA-era interest was probably never allowed, and its waiver should not be income; Mahindra and Mahindra (SC, 2018) supports non-taxability of loan waivers outside s.41(1); (b) limitation for reopening AY 2018-19 is arguable; (c) most no-dues letters are dated Aug-Sep 2018 (SBI 30-Aug-2018, BOI 01-Sep-2018, ARCs 03/04-Sep-2018), i.e. AY 2019-20, while only UCO (28-Mar-2018) falls in AY 2018-19, so the year attribution itself looks contestable (prospectus p.268). The company states it expects no material impact. My read: the IT demand is legally weak but cash-relevant (a pre-deposit / stay is usually needed), and the GST s.74 demand is the more likely to stick in part.
- Mechanism for the reader: the 2018 OTS that let the parent buy a defaulted company cheaply (Section 3) is now generating a tax claim that falls on all shareholders, including IPO and preferential investors who paid 50-90 times the parent entry price.

### F2. Sales to the promoter-owned UAE trader jumped 8x in FY26, with no year-end balances disclosed (facts ESTABLISHED; leakage INFERRED as a risk, not shown)
- Claim: Aeroflex sells a growing volume to Italica Global FZC, a 100% subsidiary of the parent whose shares are held in the name of the MD as nominee, and the RPT notes omit the outstanding receivable.
- Evidence:
  - Sales to Italica (Rs cr): FY21 1.10, FY22 6.28, FY23 3.74 (prospectus p.33 / restated Note 41); FY24 5.81 (AR FY24 Note 36); FY25 2.84; FY26 22.56, plus a new "business development service" of Rs 0.36 cr paid TO Italica in FY26 (AR FY26 consolidated Note 36 and standalone RPT note). FY26 sales to Italica = about 5.1% of FY26 consolidated revenue (Rs 442 cr).
  - Ownership: "Asad Daud and Parag Shah (Nominee Shareholder) holds 99.00% and 1.00% respectively, on behalf of our Promoter" (prospectus p.210-211, promoter group table). Italica also holds 74,54,830 Aeroflex shares (5.63%) and is promoter group.
  - Business overlap: Italica is "engaged in trading and import and export of stainless steel hose and products" but the prospectus says it operates "only in markets which are different from the markets we operate in", hence "no common pursuits" (prospectus p.215-216). In FY26 Aeroflex starts paying it a business-development fee, i.e. Italica now sits inside the Aeroflex sales chain.
  - Italica economics: FY23 total revenue Rs 9.01 cr, PAT Rs 3.82 cr (42% margin) while buying Rs 3.74 cr from Aeroflex (company/Italica_Global_FZC FY2023-2021 statement; prospectus p.215). FY26 turnover AED 1,05,14,407 (about Rs 26.9 cr at Rs 25.55/AED) and PAT AED 1,27,966 (about Rs 0.33 cr) (parent AR FY26, AOC-1). So in FY26 about 84% of Italica turnover could be Aeroflex product.
  - Absence: neither the consolidated nor the standalone RPT note in AR FY26 shows closing balances with any related party (Ind AS 24 para 18(b) requires them). The FY24 AR also records Rs 32.35 cr of overdue export receivables "due to various business reasons" (AR FY24 consolidated additional information, item XI); whether any of it is Italica is UNVERIFIED.
  - Internal non-reconciliation in the parent: AOC-1 shows Italica PAT about Rs 0.33 cr, but the Schedule III table shows Italica contributing Rs 22.29 cr to consolidated profit (parent AR FY26). Mechanism unknown; it is not evidence of leakage from Aeroflex.
  - Data quality: Italica FY21 reserves are Rs 13.38 cr in the prospectus but Rs 10.76 cr in the company-website statement for the same year.
- Why it matters (mechanism): the Daud family economic interest in Aeroflex is about 52.45% x 65.47% = 34.3% (parent promoter stake x parent stake in Aeroflex), but about 52.45% in Italica. Every rupee of margin that sits in Italica rather than Aeroflex raises the family share of that rupee from 34 paise to 52 paise. Control (65.47% of Aeroflex votes) and economics (34.3%) diverge, which is the standard set-up for transfer-pricing leakage. Without balances and pricing data, investors cannot test it.
- Counter-argument: FY26 Italica PAT is only Rs 0.33 cr, so little margin was captured in FY26; the FY26 sales are below the Reg 23 material-RPT threshold (10% of turnover) and need only audit-committee approval, which the AR says was given on arm length terms; Italica may be a genuine Middle-East distributor. The gap is disclosure, not proven harm.

### F3. Preferential issue Feb-2026: priced at the floor, to a closed group that included the two largest pre-IPO buyers, with the use of funds described two ways on the same day (ESTABLISHED facts; motive not inferable)
- Pricing: relevant date 16-Dec-2025 (notice item 3). The notice states only that Rs 182.70 is "higher than the calculated price" and does not print the floor. My replication from NSE bhavcopy (exchange with the larger volume): 90-trading-day VWAP 05-Aug-2025 to 15-Dec-2025 = Rs 182.46; 10-trading-day VWAP 02-Dec-2025 to 15-Dec-2025 = Rs 176.86; ICDR Reg 164(1) floor = higher of the two = Rs 182.46. Issue price Rs 182.70 is 24 paise (0.13%) above. The result is robust to window choice: an 88 to 92 day window gives Rs 182.31 to Rs 182.66, all below Rs 182.70. Label: my replication, not the PCS figure (the PCS certificate link in the notice, aeroflexindia.com/wp-content/uploads/PCS-Certificate.pdf, now returns a web page, not the certificate).
- Market context (NSE bhavcopy): close 16-Dec-2025 Rs 179.83; 18-Dec-2025 Rs 177.31 (board met 5:00-5:55 pm, after market); 19-Dec-2025 Rs 193.69, up 9.2%, on 2,80,76,340 shares (about 21.7% of the 12.93 cr shares then outstanding) at VWAP Rs 198.01; allotment day 03-Feb-2026 Rs 185.71; 18-Nov-2026 Rs 494.60. Allottee paper gain at Rs 494.60: about Rs 93.9 cr on Rs 55.0 cr invested (2.71x) in about 9.5 months.
- Same-day disclosure conflict: board outcome 18-Dec-2025 Annexure A: liquid-cooling skid capacity 2,000 to 15,000 a year by Jun-2026 ("existing capacity utilisation: under trial production"), robotic welding and annealing by Dec-2026, total Rs 97.56 cr, financed by internal accruals Rs 42.56 cr and "Preferential issue - INR 55.00 Cr". EOGM notice of the same date: long-term working capital Rs 41.52 cr, GCP Rs 12.02 cr, issue expenses Rs 1.45 cr. Shareholders voted on the notice version. No document explains the difference.
- Inference on why (INFERRED, label as such): ICDR Schedule VI requires project-level detail and a deployment schedule for capex objects, and deviation from stated objects needs a special resolution; working capital plus GCP (GCP Rs 12.02 cr = 21.9%, inside the 25% cap) gives flexibility. In substance the money behaved like capex money: only Rs 12.59 cr was used by 31-Mar-2026 (AR FY26 CG report item h), the rest by 30-Sep-2026 (Statement of Deviation Q2 FY27), while CWIP rose Rs 23 cr to Rs 73.5 cr and bank balances fell Rs 50.66 cr to Rs 3.66 cr in H1 FY27 (working notes item 8). Cash is fungible, so no deviation can be proven.
- Allottees and links (ESTABLISHED from the notice, item 11-12, and prospectus p.92-96):
  - Ashish Kacholia (UBO himself): pre 25,98,080, allotted 4,10,509, post 30,08,589 (2.27%).
  - Bengal Finance and Investment Pvt Ltd (UBO stated as Suresh Kumar Agarwal, not Kacholia): pre 23,15,935, allotted 4,10,509, post 27,26,444 (2.06%). Several news sites call it "Kacholia firm"; the company UBO disclosure contradicts that.
  - Sanjay Babulal Surana 8,21,018; Vaibhav Jayantilal Shah for R K Investments (partnership) 8,21,018; Madhu Silica Pvt Ltd (UBO Darshak Rameshchandra Shah) 2,73,672; Nareshkumar Mahasukhlal Mehta 2,73,672. All four had nil prior holding.
  - Sequence of facts: Kacholia and Bengal Finance each bought exactly 23,15,935 shares from the promoter at Rs 87.56 under agreements dated 10-May-2023, transferred 15-May-2023 (prospectus p.92-96), three months before the Rs 108 IPO. Bengal Finance still held exactly 23,15,935 in Dec-2025 (SHP Dec-2025); Kacholia had added 2,82,145. Both then received identical preferential lots. The other four allottees came in identical pairs. None of the six is named in the prospectus promoter group list, and the notice declares no promoter, director or KMP interest. No link to the promoters is disclosed or found. Acting in concert is NOT established; the combined Kacholia plus Bengal stake (57,35,033 = 4.33%) is below the 5% SAST threshold either way.
- Advisory fee: Rs 1,41,60,000, consistent with Rs 1.20 cr plus 18% GST (my decomposition), = 2.57% of proceeds. Recipient NOT disclosed in the notice, corrigendum, allotment intimation, Statements of Deviation or AR FY26. Do not name anyone. Total preferential issue expenses charged to securities premium were Rs 1.79 cr (AR FY26 other-equity note), above the Rs 1.45 cr object; the Statement of Deviation shows only Rs 1.45 cr.
- No monitoring agency (issue under Rs 100 cr, notice item 17(i)). A Rs 400 cr QIP enabling resolution passed at the 15-Jan-2025 EOGM (Notice-of-EOGM-1) was never used; the Dec-2025 raise went to six non-QIB investors (notice item 17(c)).
- Vote, EOGM 15-Jan-2026 (Scrutinizer report, S.K. Jain and Co, page 7): promoter 8,66,36,663 for; public institutions 28,83,082 polled (50.92% of their holding), all for; public non-institutions 9,41,554 polled (2.54%), 1,791 against. No institution voted against. Promoter votes were 95.8% of votes cast. Counter-evidence on self-dealing in the vote: non-institutional votes polled (9.42 lakh) are fewer than either Kacholia (25.98 lakh) or Bengal Finance (23.16 lakh) held, so neither existing allottee voted its pre-held shares.
- Why it matters: the allottees bought at a price fixed by formula before the market saw the skid capacity plan, the use of funds was told two ways, and the fee recipient is undisclosed. Each is legal on its own; together they show a board that chose the most issuer-flexible and least market-tested route.
- Counter-argument: price was above the board-day close, the formula is mandatory, the Oct-2025 call had already disclosed an exclusive India contract and a customer request to ramp capacity (Q2 FY26 transcript, 29-Oct-2025, page 8), and a Rs 55 cr raise is too small for a QIP to be economic.

### F4. The company appears to have carried most of the IPO cost although 54% of the IPO was the promoter selling (text conflict ESTABLISHED; size of any excess INFERRED)
- Claim: the prospectus promised pro-rata cost sharing, but its own arithmetic and the later deviation statements put the whole estimated offer cost on the company.
- Evidence (ESTABLISHED):
  - Sharing clause: all offer costs other than listing fees, stamp duty, audit fees and corporate advertising "shall be shared by our Company and the Promoter Selling Shareholder, on a pro rata basis, in proportion to the number of Equity Shares" (prospectus p.100 and p.109). Pro-rata = 15.0 m / 32.5 m = 46.15% company, 53.85% promoter.
  - Estimated TOTAL offer expenses Rs 30.473 cr, shown as 8.68% of the TOTAL offer size of Rs 351 cr (prospectus p.110), i.e. the cost of the whole offer, including the OFS.
  - Net proceeds Rs 131.527 cr = Rs 162.00 cr less the entire Rs 30.473 cr (prospectus p.101), although "Net Proceeds" is defined as fresh issue less "our Company share of Offer related expenses" (prospectus p.9).
  - The Statements of Deviation (Mar-2024, Jun-2024) say: "the company share for Offer Expenses of Rs.30.473 crore was deducted"; actual issue expenses came in Rs 4.484 cr lower, so Rs 25.989 cr was borne by the company (Rs 22.394 cr to securities premium plus Rs 3.595 cr GST credit, see F1).
- Inference: at the estimate, a 46.15% share would have been about Rs 14.1 cr; the company bore about Rs 26.0 cr. The excess could be up to about Rs 11.9 cr, less any costs that the clause lets the company carry alone (listing fees, audit fees, roadshow and corporate advertising).
- What I could NOT pin down: the promoter share. The parent standalone FY24 cash flow shows "Sale of Investments" Rs 266.94 cr and "Profit on sale of share" Rs 264.24 cr (parent AR FY24, standalone cash flow), which exceeds the gross pre-IPO plus OFS receipts (Rs 265.14 cr), so other disposals are mixed in and the OFS cost deduction cannot be backed out. Aeroflex FY24 RPT shows "Expenses paid and recovered" Rs 7.14 cr from the parent (AR FY24 Note 36), but the same label is used for the FY23 comparative Rs 4.00 cr that the prospectus calls "loan taken and repaid" (prospectus p.33), so the label does not reliably mean recovery of OFS costs.
- Why it matters: if the excess is real, the IPO moved up to about Rs 12 cr of value from public shareholders to the selling promoter.
- Counter-argument: the carve-outs could be several crores for a 97x-subscribed Rs 351 cr IPO with roadshows; the Rs 7.14 cr from the parent may be exactly the promoter reimbursement; the "company share" wording may be loose drafting. Treat as a question for management, not a finding to publish.

### F6. Credit-rating sequence: sub-investment-grade CRISIL rating replaced by a two-notch-higher Infomerics rating, then no rating at all (ESTABLISHED sequence; no intent inferred)
- Sequence: CRISIL BB-/Stable 09-Aug-2019 and 06-Aug-2020; BB/Stable 24-Nov-2021 and 20-Dec-2021 (CRISIL 17-Oct-2022 rationale, rating history). Infomerics IVR BBB-/Stable assigned 27-Jul-2022 on Rs 37 cr. CRISIL BB/Stable "reaffirmed and withdrawn" at the company request on 17-Oct-2022 after bank NOC (Rs 55 cr facilities). For about 12 weeks both ratings were live, two notches apart, one either side of investment grade. Infomerics placed BBB- on watch positive 29-Aug-2023 (IPO), then withdrew it 16-Oct-2023 because the rated facilities were surrendered. AR FY25 and AR FY26 corporate-governance reports: "has not obtained any credit rating".
- Observations: Infomerics 27-Jul-2022 states "Status of non-cooperation with previous CRA: NA" and does not mention the live CRISIL BB. Its downward sensitivities are word-for-word CRISIL text ("Steep decline in revenue or profitability, leading to cash accrual less than Rs 8 crore"; "Any large, debt-funded capex or sizeable stretch in the working capital cycle"). The same FY22 metric differs: CRISIL gross current assets 194 days, Infomerics GCA 133 days.
- Stale disclosure: the "Credit Rating" link on the company website still serves the 29-Aug-2023 Infomerics watch release (company/Credit_Rating.pdf, file created 19-Apr-2024, six months after the withdrawal).
- Why it matters: a reader of the website sees an investment-grade rating on watch positive that no longer exists, and the only agency that rated through the stressed years rated it BB.
- Counter-argument: after the IPO the company was nearly debt-free and surrendered its rated facilities, so dropping ratings is rational; small and mid issuers often move to cheaper agencies; Infomerics criteria differ.

### F8. The promoter family is adding exposure through the parent at a deep holdco discount while the Aeroflex promoter line shows no activity (ESTABLISHED sequence [part secondary]; no impropriety implied)
- Parent market cap Rs 1,545 cr at Rs 136 (screener, 18-Nov-2026). Look-through Aeroflex stake: 8,66,36,663 shares (7,91,81,833 direct plus 74,54,830 via Italica) x Rs 494.60 = Rs 4,285 cr. The parent trades at about 36% of its Aeroflex stake alone, before Aeroflex Neu (58.56%), Aeroflex Finance, MRO sale cash and start-up holdings: a discount of at least 64%, before tax on unrealised gains.
- In June 2026 A Flex Invest Pvt Ltd (Shehnaz D Ali 60%, Asad Daud 40%, prospectus p.208) bought about 8.5 lakh parent shares in the open market at about Rs 113 [secondary: Reg 29(2) disclosures as reported by Trendlyne / Whalesbook]; parent promoter holding 51.59% (Mar-2026) to 52.30% (Jun-2026) to 52.45% (Sep-2026) (parent screener shareholding table). At about 0.766 Aeroflex share per parent share (8.66 cr / 11.31 cr parent shares at the prospectus date; current parent share count not verified), the family bought Aeroflex exposure at roughly Rs 148 per Aeroflex share when Aeroflex traded at Rs 489 to Rs 511.
- Why it matters: the family is net SELLER of Aeroflex at the opco level (2023, at Rs 87.56 and Rs 108) and a BUYER of Aeroflex exposure at the holdco level (2026, at a look-through Rs 148). Any future simplification (merger of parent and Aeroflex, or a parent buyback) would be priced against that discount.
