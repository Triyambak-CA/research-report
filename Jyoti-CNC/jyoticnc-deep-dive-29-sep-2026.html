Executive summary

Jyoti CNC is a real and growing Indian machine-tool business wrapped in two problems the headline numbers do not show. Its profits run years ahead of its cash because it books revenue on large machines while they are being built, and the unbilled balance that creates has grown nearly fivefold in three years, to ₹613 cr. Its French technology subsidiary, Huron, is under a criminal investigation that French prosecutors are reported to connect to dual-use exports to Russia via India. The price, 75 times earnings, assumes both problems resolve and that growth then compounds for a decade on far less capital than the company has ever needed.

The three strongest positives

  1. The Indian business is growing and profitable, and it is audited. Consolidated revenue ₹929 cr (FY23) to ₹2,093 cr (FY26); EBITDA margin 10.5% to 25.2%; machines 3,063 to 5,550 in two years with the plant above 90% used. Standalone profit rose 21% in Q1 FY27, the quarter the consolidated figure fell 20%.
  2. A product range no Indian rival matches, behind an export-control barrier. Simultaneous 5-axis and multitasking machines are licence-controlled; the nearest listed rival says 5-axis "will not be my business model". The Rajkot base is vertically integrated down to its own foundry, and capacity is due to rise from 6,000 to 16,000 machines a year from October 2026.
  3. Demand is real and funded. India's machine-tool consumption grew to ₹36,419 cr in FY26 (+15%), defence capital acquisition is up 24% for FY27 with 75% reserved for domestic industry, and electronics capex is subsidised by ECMS, under which Jyoti itself won approval for a ₹1,020.65 cr programme in Aug-2026. The pledged promoter-group stake was halved between Jun and Sep-2026.

The three sharpest risks

  1. Profit is running ahead of cash. Unbilled revenue ₹131 cr (FY23) to ₹613 cr (FY26), 29% of revenue; true debtor days 211, not the 104 screens show. The company gives none of the Ind AS 115 disclosures over-time revenue requires, and its FY26 audit report describes a point-in-time basis while its MD&A describes percentage of completion. Huron reversed ₹67 cr of such revenue in Q4 FY26.
  2. The French investigation. Four Huron staff in custody on 31-Mar-2026, the director general under formal investigation, EUR 3-4 mn and two properties seized, and press reports citing prosecutors on "exports to Russia, via India" from 2022. Parent exposure ₹882 cr funded plus ₹264 cr guaranteed, unimpaired, with a going-concern emphasis from the auditor. The company's Reg 30 letter left the Russia allegation out. Nothing is proven and the group denies wrongdoing.
  3. The price needs a different company. The reverse DCF requires about 36% revenue growth for ten years with capital turning at 1.0 against 0.42-0.63 achieved: FY36 revenue of about ₹45,000 cr, more than today's entire Indian market. At the capital intensity actually achieved, no growth rate reaches the price. FY26's incremental capital of ₹656 cr earned ₹22 cr more operating profit.
The brief's leads, and what the filings said

Pledge: one promoter-group holder (Anilkumar Virani, 14.45%, not a director), personal borrowing; 90.6% of his stake at Jun-2026, halved to 1.52 crore shares (6.70% of the company, 10.7% of the promoter group) by 16-Sep-2026, rotated from HDFC Bank to four NBFCs. Order intake below FY25 while revenue grew: confirmed; book-to-bill 1.44 to 1.13. Q1 FY27: the whole profit fall is the subsidiaries (-₹30.4 cr) plus lost forex income and doubled interest. Fire: company says a coating unit and 30-60 minutes; two local outlets say the assembly unit and hours.

The one line

This is a bet on India's only full-range CNC builder turning a fast-growing order book into cash, priced as if revenue compounds at about a third a year for a decade on capital that turns twice as fast as it ever has, and it breaks if unbilled revenue keeps rising or the French investigation of Huron goes against the group.

Industry and sector

India is now the world's fourth-largest consumer of machine tools and a growing one, but it still imports almost three-fifths of what it uses, and that share has risen, not fallen, since Jyoti listed. The prospectus told investors the reverse would happen. Domestic demand is real and funded; the question for an Indian builder is how much of it stays domestic.

Market size: two sources that are not measuring the same thing

The offer document's industry chapter was written by Frost & Sullivan under an engagement letter of 06-May-2022 and was, in the prospectus's own words, "exclusively commissioned and paid for by us" (prospectus p.178). Per the owner's standing instruction it is used here where it is the best source, flagged every time, and set beside the industry association's measured series. The two are not the same universe: F&S sized all Indian machine tools at US$ 2,108 mn for FY23, IMTMA at about US$ 3.0 bn for the same year. F&S may exclude metal-forming presses, which are 16% of FY26 imports; its definition is not stated.

Where in the cycle: domestic upcycle, global downcycle

The Indian demand indicators are unambiguously positive for FY26: index of industrial production for capital goods +8.3% (FY25 +5.6%), machinery and equipment +6.5%, motor vehicles +12.5%, other transport equipment +10.2% (MoSPI quick estimates, 28-Apr-2026); RBI capacity utilisation 74.8% in Q2 FY26 against a 72.9% pre-pandemic average (Economic Survey 2025-26 Table I.4); SIAM vehicle production +11.8%; ACMA component turnover +12.7%. Defence production reached a record ₹1.78 lakh cr (+15.6%) and the FY26 defence capital budget of ₹1.86 lakh cr was fully spent.

Outside India the cycle has turned down. Global machine tool production fell in 2024 (Gardner), DMG Mori's 2025 revenue fell 4.8%, and Hurco has lost money for three years (aggregator). Okuma grew 14.1% in the year to Mar-2026. Japanese and German builders facing a soft home market have every reason to push harder into the one large market still growing, and that is what the import data shows. Cycle position for Indian demand: mid-upcycle. Cycle position for the share of that demand going to domestic makers: flat to losing.

Structural shifts

Working for Jyoti

  • Defence indigenisation with money behind it. FY27 capital acquisition ₹1.85 lakh cr, about 75% reserved for domestic industry (PIB, 03-Feb-2026). Machine tools are an input to the supplier base that must build it; no official figure sizes that share.
  • Electronics manufacturing. The Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme (outlay ₹22,919 cr notified 08-Apr-2025) is pulling capex into mobile and component plants that buy vertical machining centres in volume. EMS is 1% of Jyoti's FY26 revenue but 13% of its Jun-2026 order book.
  • 5-axis scarcity. Simultaneous 5-axis machines above set accuracy thresholds are dual-use items under the Wassenaar Arrangement list and the EU dual-use regulation (general knowledge, not from a filing), and their controllers are export-licensed. A rival Rajkot builder told its own Jul-2026 call that its 5-axis export-control paperwork ran to "300 pages" and that it would not make 5-axis its business model (Macpower concall, 30-Jul-2026). That barrier protects Jyoti's high end.

Working against Jyoti

  • China is the largest and fastest-rising import source: 24% of imports in Q1 FY26, 28% in H1, 30% for FY26 (IMTMA, secondary). Management's Aug-2026 description of imports as "primarily from Japan, Europe and South Korea" omits it.
  • Protection is being taken away, not added. The machinery safety technical regulation that would have covered metalworking machines was rescinded on 14-Jan-2026 (S.O. 239(E)). No anti-dumping case on CNC machines was found. The Feb-2026 India-US joint statement commits India to "eliminate or reduce tariffs on all U.S. industrial goods", and the India-EU agreement was described on Jyoti's own Feb-2026 call as taking tariffs on EU machines "to near 0". The Economic Survey 2025-26 argues against protecting capital goods.
  • Export controls cut both ways. The same dual-use regime that bars low-end rivals from 5-axis is the regime under which French customs opened a judicial investigation into Jyoti's own subsidiary in Apr-2026 (Section 9).

Who captures the margin

The margin pool in machine tools sits at two ends: the controller and drive suppliers (Fanuc, Siemens, Heidenhain, Mitsubishi) whose parts are both costly and licence-gated, and the high-end builders with a brand. Jyoti buys CNC controllers, motors, linear guideways and ball screws from third parties and imports a meaningful part of them (prospectus pp.51, 65). Its 25.2% consolidated EBITDA margin for FY26 is an outlier: the global listed set earns 10-14% (Okuma 11.4%, Makino 13.7%, DMG Mori 10.4%), the largest Indian builder, Ace Designers, earned 9.75% in FY24 (CARE, 08-Jan-2025), Bharat Fritz Werner 5.5% in FY25 (ICRA, 29-Oct-2025), and LMW's machine tool and foundry segment about 8% (LMW FY26 annual report). Either Jyoti has a product mix and cost position unlike anyone else's, or its margin is measured differently. Section 6 tests the second possibility; Section 4 the first. The Aug-2026 intimation that Jyoti has ECMS approval for a ₹1,020.65 cr programme to make its own "electronic devices used to build CNC machines" is an attempt to move up this chain.

Business model and value chain

Jyoti designs, casts, machines and assembles computer-controlled metal-cutting machines in Rajkot, and owns a French 5-axis specialist, Huron, through which it bought its way into aerospace-grade technology in 2007. It reports one operating segment, "machine tools". The useful cut is three businesses: the Indian volume and mid-range machine business, the high-end 5-axis and aerospace business that leans on Huron, and after-sales service and spares.

Company level

Over 200 machine variants in 44 series: CNC turning and turn-mill centres, vertical machining centres, horizontal machining centres, simultaneous 5-axis machining centres and multitasking machines, priced from under ₹50 lakh to above ₹2 cr (FY26 annual report MD&A). Two plants in Rajkot on 237,408 sq m of industrial land of which 157,155 sq m is unused, plus Huron's plant near Strasbourg on 46,442 sq m (Q1 FY27 deck p.17). Installed capacity 6,000 machines a year in India; a Rajkot expansion to 10,000 a year was scheduled for June 2026 (FY26 annual report MD&A). The plant is vertically integrated: its own foundry, machine shop, sheet metal unit, paint shop and two assembly lines. It sells through 29 sales and service centres in 12 states, 2 domestic and 11 international dealers, and directly.

Section 04

Competitive advantage: the 6 Cs

Jyoti's real advantage is a product range no other Indian builder matches at the high end, built on a French acquisition, sold from a low-cost Rajkot base with its own foundry. That is a genuine but narrow moat, and its most valuable part now sits inside a criminal investigation.

Cost Moderate

Vertically integrated in Rajkot: own foundry, machine shop, sheet metal, paint and assembly (deck p.16), a low-cost labour market (AmbitionBox pay 3.7/5; advertised operator pay ₹19,000-42,000 a month) and 157,155 sq m of unused land. Gross margin rose from 42.7% (FY23) to 55.1% (FY26). Against that: the costliest inputs, CNC controllers and drives, are imported on a 12-month lead time, and Chinese machines now lead Indian imports on price. The in-house controller programme (HUMA, ECMS) would deepen this if it works.

Capacity Moderate

6,000 machines a year running above 90%; 10,000 more due from Oct-2026, which would make Jyoti the largest single machine-tool site in India by nameplate. But capacity is cheap to build here (about ₹4.5 lakh per machine of annual capacity), rivals are adding it too (BFW 10,000 at Hosur; Ace Micromatic's Ahmedabad plant), and Jyoti's own record on delivering capacity on time is poor (five missed dates). Time to replicate: about two years, which is not a barrier.

Capital Weak

An A+ rating (Infomerics) and bank lines at 1Y MCLR plus 0.25%, but a history of lender restructuring (2020-2023), negative net worth in FY22, and debt back at ₹850 cr within two years of the IPO. The business consumes rather than generates cash: three-year free cash flow minus ₹847 cr. The parent borrows from its Chairman's LLP at 15% while lending to its French subsidiary at 6.25%. In a downturn the balance sheet is a constraint, not a weapon.

Channel Moderate

Mostly direct selling through 27-29 branches in 12 states, with only 2 domestic and 11 international dealers: Jyoti owns its customer relationships, which is right for capital equipment. Service revenue grew 40% in FY26. Huron gives a European sales and service network. No evidence of channel stuffing: finished goods are small (₹51.7 cr) and the Q4 revenue share is falling. The weakness is that the company finances the channel: customer advances are under 2% of the order book.

Customer captivity Weak

Aerospace and defence buyers qualify machines and rarely switch mid-programme, which is real stickiness. But one unnamed customer is 22.5% of revenue, the top five were 48.6% of FY25 revenue, EMS demand is a handful of customers who have not taken delivery for two years, and machine tools are replaceable purchases that go to tender. The prospectus (p.48) says the company does not "generally enter into long term purchase contracts". Auto buyers, 26-35% of revenue, have many suppliers.

Compliance moat Moderate

Simultaneous 5-axis machines and their controllers are export-controlled dual-use items. Clearing that regime, plus aerospace customer qualifications, keeps most Indian rivals out of the high end (Macpower: 5-axis will "not be my business model"). The same regime is now the group's largest risk: the French investigation is about exactly this, and Huron's licences for Chinese and Turkish customers are stalled. A barrier that can be used against you is half a moat.

The most important moat, and its half-life

The 5-axis and multitasking product range, through Huron's technology. It is the main reason Jyoti's EBITDA margin (25%) is about double the global listed builders' (10-14%) and why its A&D share is twice the Indian market's (39% of revenue against about 20% of Indian CNC demand per the paid industry chapter). Absent the French case, its half-life would be long, perhaps a decade, because 5-axis design, precision and qualification take years to replicate and the export-control regime slows entrants. With the case open, the honest answer is insufficient data for conviction: if Huron emerges with its licences and management intact, the moat holds; if the French court finds against it, the high end of the range and the European aerospace door are both in question, and what remains is a very good Indian volume machine builder in a market where imports hold 59%.

Durability badges are the report's judgement on the evidence cited; the Ratings control hides them.

Because the parent is long euro and the subsidiary borrows in euro, a stronger euro raises reported profit (revaluation of the parent's euro claims on Huron, booked in other income) while also raising the rupee value of Huron's debt (booked in OCI). FY26 is the illustration: ₹43.39 cr of forex gain in profit, ₹18.01 cr of translation loss in OCI. Pre-mortem and anti-thesis

Suppose it is Sep-2028 and the shares have halved. This is the most likely story of how it happened, argued as a short-seller would argue it, sincerely.

The short-seller's case

  1. The profit was booked before it was billed. ₹482 cr of the ₹803 cr earned in FY24-FY26 sits in an unbilled balance whose basis the company does not disclose and describes in two different ways in one annual report. Huron's ₹67 cr reversal showed that percentage-of-completion revenue can be taken back. If a fraction of India's ₹465 cr follows, reported profit was pulled forward, not earned.
  2. The crown jewel is in a French criminal file. Prosecutors are reported to allege a scheme of dual-use exports to Russia via India from 2022; the director general is under formal investigation; the Chairman's own words on the call are ambiguous about 2022-23 dispatches. The parent has ₹882 cr funded and ₹264 cr guaranteed in the French sub-group, none of it impaired, and the auditor already flags a going-concern uncertainty. The company's disclosure left the Russia allegation out.
  3. Growth consumes more capital than it earns. Incremental sales-to-capital fell to 0.42 in FY26 and the incremental return to 3.4%. Three-year free cash flow is minus ₹847 cr; debt is back above its pre-IPO level; finance cost doubled in a year. The new plant multiplies the problem, because each machine needs about seven times its fixed capital in working capital.
  4. The order book is older than it looks. The A&D block has not grown in eleven quarters, ₹630 cr of EMS orders are 21-33 months old with customers who have not built their plants, a ₹135 cr EMS drop was never explained, and nothing on age, currency, GST or cancellations is disclosed. Customer advances cover under 2% of it.
  5. Management's dates and costs cannot be relied on. 17% of scoreable claims met; the capacity promise slipped five times and its cost doubled; a half-year operating cash flow given on a call ("crossed Rs. 50 crores") was nearly four times the filed a half-year cash-flow figure given on a call was four times the filed one#8377;13.15 cr; the CFO has not spoken on eleven calls.

  7. The price assumes a different company. 75 times earnings and 47 times EBITDA against 9-11 times for the global builders; a reverse DCF that needs revenue larger than today's entire Indian machine-tool market by FY36.
  8. The volume end is where China competes. Revenue per machine fell 15% in FY26 as mix moved to cheaper machines, while China became the largest source of Indian imports and India removed a non-tariff barrier.

Base rate

Judgement, not a statistical base rate. No systematic dataset of comparable setups was assembled for this report. Two reference classes point the same way. First, global machine-tool builders are cyclical and have traded at about 9-15 times EBITDA through the cycle; a 47 times multiple is a growth multiple that has to be earned by compounding for many years. Second, Indian capital-goods and engineering companies whose reported profit ran ahead of billing on percentage-of-completion accounting have historically seen their multiples compress when working capital did not normalise, whatever their order books said. Jyoti's Indian business is stronger than most of those cases; its disclosure is weaker than the premium requires. Where the historical parallels resolved well, it was because cash conversion improved within two to three years of the concern being raised. FY27 is that window here.

Section 16

Investment pitch

Both cases below are argued as their holders would argue them. The report takes no side: it gives no buy, sell or hold rating, no price target and no conviction level.

The 90-second case to buy

India is the fourth-largest machine-tool market in the world, still importing nearly 60% of what it uses, with defence, electronics and auto capex all rising and funded. Jyoti is the only Indian builder with a full range up to simultaneous 5-axis, it makes its own castings in a low-cost town, and its core business has grown revenue 125% in three years at a 25% EBITDA margin while running its plant above 90%. A plant that adds 10,000 machines a year opens within weeks, backed by a ₹4,848 cr order book and an ECMS approval that could subsidise a quarter of its next ₹1,020 cr programme and move it into making its own controllers. The standalone Indian business grew profit 21% in the very quarter the headline fell 20%: the damage is in France, it is a timing issue on about ₹100 cr of licensed machines, and management says more than fifty European companies have been through the same process. The pledge has already been halved. If the French case closes and working capital normalises as the new plant runs, FY27-FY28 earnings compound at 25-30% and cash conversion reaches the 50% management guides, and today's multiple is paid for the scarcest listed asset in Indian capital goods.

The 90-second case to exit or avoid

For three years the company has reported ₹803 cr of profit and generated minus ₹99 cr of operating cash; ₹482 cr of that profit sits in unbilled revenue whose basis it does not disclose and its own documents describe inconsistently. Its technology subsidiary is under a French criminal investigation reportedly about dual-use exports to Russia via India, with the parent's ₹1,146 cr of funded and guaranteed exposure unimpaired, a going-concern warning from the auditor, and a disclosure that left the allegation out. Debt has gone from net cash to ₹713 cr net since the IPO, the latest year's incremental capital earned 3.4%, and the new plant will need far more working capital than fixed capital to fill. Management has met 17% of its scoreable promises. At 75 times earnings the price needs revenue larger than the whole Indian machine-tool market by FY36. An existing holder does not need the French case to go badly to lose money: it only needs the H1 FY27 balance sheet to show unbilled revenue still rising.

Time horizon

The bull case needs about eighteen to twenty-four months to show itself: two half-year balance sheets (Sep-2026 and Mar-2027) to prove cash conversion, a year of output from the new plant, and some resolution in France, whose precedents management puts at two to three years. The bear case can show itself much sooner, in the H1 FY27 numbers due by mid-November 2026.

The one line

This is a bet on India's only full-range CNC builder turning a fast-growing order book into cash, priced as if revenue compounds at about a third a year for a decade on capital that turns twice as fast as it ever has, and it breaks if unbilled revenue keeps rising or the French investigation of Huron goes against the group.

This report gives no buy, sell or hold recommendation and none should be inferred from it. It presents the evidence found, the evidence that could not be obtained, and both sides of the argument. The scenario values in Section 14 are arithmetic on stated assumptions, not price targets. Insufficient data for conviction applies to the two items that decide the outcome: the contract terms and ageing behind the unbilled revenue, and the facts of the French investigation.
