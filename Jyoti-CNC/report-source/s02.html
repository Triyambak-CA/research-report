Section 02

Business model and value chain

Jyoti designs, casts, machines and assembles computer-controlled metal-cutting machines in Rajkot, and owns a French 5-axis specialist, Huron, through which it bought its way into aerospace-grade technology in 2007. It reports one operating segment, "machine tools". The useful cut is three businesses: the Indian volume and mid-range machine business, the high-end 5-axis and aerospace business that leans on Huron, and after-sales service and spares.

Company level

Over 200 machine variants in 44 series: CNC turning and turn-mill centres, vertical machining centres, horizontal machining centres, simultaneous 5-axis machining centres and multitasking machines, priced from under ₹50 lakh to above ₹2 cr (FY26 annual report MD&A). Two plants in Rajkot on 237,408 sq m of industrial land of which 157,155 sq m is unused, plus Huron's plant near Strasbourg on 46,442 sq m (Q1 FY27 deck p.17). Installed capacity 6,000 machines a year in India; a Rajkot expansion to 10,000 a year was scheduled for June 2026 (FY26 annual report MD&A). The plant is vertically integrated: its own foundry, machine shop, sheet metal unit, paint shop and two assembly lines. It sells through 29 sales and service centres in 12 states, 2 domestic and 11 international dealers, and directly.

? Unknown unknowns in this business
  • The controller supplier is a single point of failure. A few global firms supply CNC controls, and for multi-axis machines their export is licensed. The Aug-2026 call described German-controller machines needing German clearance. The ECMS-backed plan to make "electronic devices used to build CNC machines" in-house (17-Aug-2026 intimation) is the company's answer; the approval letter and product list are not disclosed.
  • EMS is a lumpy, few-customer market. A ₹304.9 cr order from one EMS entity sat in the Sep-2023 order book (prospectus); EMS was still only 1% of FY26 revenue but is 13% of the Jun-2026 book. Management said EMS customers are "waiting" for ECMS clearances before ordering. That makes a slice of the book conditional on someone else's subsidy approval.
  • Rajkot is one site. The 26-Sep-2026 fire in a coating facility was contained in 30-60 minutes with no stated loss, but it is a reminder that both Indian plants are in one town.

? Unknown unknowns in this business
  • Certification lock-in cuts both ways. The dual-use status that keeps low-end rivals out is the same status under which French customs intelligence opened a judicial investigation into Huron in Apr-2026, restricted its director general, seized about EUR 4 mn and placed machines under customs control (Section 9).
  • A&D is one customer deep. One unnamed customer's ₹470.7 cr is about 58% of FY26 A&D revenue (derived). If that customer is a defence programme, the revenue line is a procurement schedule, not a market.
  • Subsidiary capital leakage has a history. Huron had negative net worth from FY21 to FY24, received a conditional creditor waiver of ₹30.45 cr in FY23 that is reinstated if its finances improve, and needed ₹153.3 cr of parent loans converted to equity in FY23 (prospectus pp.47-48, 53).

? Business-level unknown unknowns
  • A machine-tool maker carrying ₹613 cr of unbilled revenue. A business that sells discrete machines normally bills on dispatch. Jyoti's consolidated balance sheet at 31-Mar-2026 carried ₹613.3 cr of "unbilled revenue receivable", more than its trade receivables, and its standalone Indian business ₹465.5 cr of it. Revenue is being recognised before invoices are raised on a scale that is central to the cash question in Section 6.
  • The order book implies 2.4 years of work. ₹4,732 cr at Mar-2026 against ₹1,978 cr executed in FY26. Machine tool lead times are months, not years. Either part of the book is long-dated defence work, or part of it is stale.
  • Growth has outrun capacity arithmetic. Management reported 5,550 machines sold in FY26 against 6,000 of Indian capacity (Section 19); the promised 10,000 has to arrive for FY27 growth.
