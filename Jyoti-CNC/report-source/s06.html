Section 06

Earnings quality and forensic checks

Earnings quality score: 3 out of 10

The answer to "why are profits not turning into cash" is in one balance sheet line. Jyoti recognises revenue on large machines while they are being built, and bills later. The unbilled balance that method creates rose by ₹482 cr in three years, 60% of the profit reported in those years. The company does not disclose how much of its revenue is recognised this way, how old the balance is, or what contract terms support it, and its own documents describe the method in contradictory ways.

What the company says the balance is. The Chairman, asked on 10-Nov-2025 why it had risen from ₹538 cr to ₹673 cr in six months: "these are the very large machines. It is partially built up like that ... this is all our five-axis and large machines cycles. There the cycles are very long, more than one year", billed "at milestone level". On 18-Nov-2024 he said it would "reduce drastically" by the fourth quarter; it trebled instead. The FY26 MD&A says revenue "is recognised over time ... by reference to the percentage of completion ... under input method". The accounting policy note carries two paragraphs: over time for fixed-price contracts and at a point in time for the sale of goods.

! The audit report describes a different basis from the MD&A, and from its own previous year

The FY25 key audit matter on revenue said "Estimated efforts is a critical estimate to determine revenue, as it requires consideration of progress of the contract, efforts incurred till date, efforts required to complete the remaining performance obligation": a percentage-of-completion audit. The FY26 key audit matter (consolidated p.88, standalone p.59) says "Revenue from the sale of goods is recognised at point in time when the control of the goods is transferred to the customers, which is on dispatch/delivery", and lists shipping-document and cut-off procedures, with no work on cost-to-complete estimates, while its opening sentence still refers to "the basis used to measure revenue recognised over a period". The FY26 consolidated notes contain none of the disclosures Ind AS 115 requires for over-time revenue: no split of revenue between over-time and point-in-time, no contract-asset reconciliation, no remaining performance obligations, no significant judgements. The balance is presented as an "other financial asset", not a contract asset. Verified against the text on 29-Sep-2026.

The innocent reading, stated fairly. Custom-built 5-axis and large machines can take more than a year and can carry contract terms that entitle the builder to payment for work done; for those, over-time recognition is correct Ind AS 115 and an unbilled balance is its normal footprint. The policy's over-time wording may be old boilerplate, and part of the balance may be goods dispatched and awaiting invoice. None of this shows revenue has been inflated. What it shows is an undisclosed and inconsistently described recognition basis on a balance that now equals 29% of revenue. Two facts make the benign reading harder to accept without disclosure: the prospectus says private customers pay "the balance amount at the time of delivery of the product, prior to dispatch" (p.69), which is not a right to payment for work done to date; and at Huron, ₹67 cr of over-time revenue was reversed in Q4 FY26 when machines were caught in the investigation, and the method was abandoned in Q1 FY27 without a note to the results.

The French exposure: a going-concern warning and no impairment

The standalone auditor's emphasis of matter says Jyoti SAS has accumulated losses and eroded net worth and that, with the judicial investigation and machines under customs control, there is "material uncertainty that may impact the subsidiary company's ability to continue as a going concern"; yet "carrying value investments, loans and other recoverable are not impaired". Management's impairment test (Note 42) rests on discounted cash flows and "the management's commitment towards the strategic nature of investments ... and its renowned brand value", with no inputs disclosed, and it names only ₹501.71 cr of the ₹881.66 cr funded exposure. The sub-group's net assets are negative ₹111.05 cr; Huron lost ₹80.16 cr on its own books in FY26 (the consolidated sub-group loss is ₹56.65 cr; the ₹20-24 cr gap is not explained in any document read) and the subsidiaries lost a further ₹30.4 cr in Q1 FY27.

! The consolidated accounts lack the disclosure the auditor points to

The consolidated auditor's emphasis of matter refers to "Note 40 of the accompanying Consolidated Financial Statements regarding the ongoing judicial investigation". Consolidated Note 40 is a routine balance-confirmation note; Note 41 says "No subsequent event has been observed". No consolidated note describes the investigation; the cash flow statement's reference for the seizure points to Note 5, which is Investments. The standalone accounts and the Reg 30 letter do disclose it, so the market knew; the consolidated accounts still carry the seizure and the reversal without the note.

The auditor

G.K. Choksi & Co. (FRN 125442W), partner Himanshu C. Vora, appointed at the 31st AGM on 30-Sep-2022 for five years; an earlier firm audited FY21. Opinions unmodified in every year, with emphasis of matter paragraphs on the subsidiaries' going concern since before the IPO (prospectus risk factor 6). For the French numbers the auditor relies on other auditors and an "independent chartered accountant". Reporting defects in the FY26 package, each individually small: CARO clause (ii)(b) says stock statements to banks showed differences "considered to be immaterial", while the Schedule III note says they "are in agreement with the books"; CARO clause III(c) says the subsidiary loan has "no stipulation of repayment" while clause III(f) says no such loans were granted; consolidated statements refer to "Accompanying notes to Standalone Financial Statements"; the MD&A calls a fall in inventory turnover from 2.06x to 1.22x "19%" (it is 41%); both the FY24 and FY25 annual reports were reissued with corrigenda. An analyst told the 07-Aug-2026 call "you should change the auditor"; the Chairman answered "improvement is always a continuous improvement".

Why 3 and not lower, or higher

Not lower because the Indian business shows real, auditable volume (5,369 machines made in Rajkot in FY26), real capacity is being built, FY26 operating cash flow was positive for the first time since listing, expected-credit-loss provisioning was raised rather than cut, payable days did not stretch, no factoring or bill discounting was found, and interest on the new plant is expensed. Not higher because more than half of three years' profit sits in an unbilled balance whose basis is undisclosed and inconsistently described; 9-12% of FY26 pre-tax profit is unrealised forex, arguably belonging in other comprehensive income and already reversing; and ₹882 cr of funded exposure to a loss-making, investigated subsidiary carries no impairment. The single most important unresolved question: how much of the ₹613 cr unbilled revenue at 31-Mar-2026 had been invoiced and collected by 30-Sep-2026. The half-year balance sheet with the Q2 FY27 results, due by mid-November 2026, is the first test.
