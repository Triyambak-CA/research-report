Section 03

Competitive landscape and peer benchmarking

There is no listed Indian company that looks like Jyoti. The two closest competitors by product and scale are unlisted, the listed ones are small, diversified or distressed, and screener's own peer list is built from sector tags, not products. The benchmark that matters is the global one, and on it Jyoti trades at four to five times the multiple of the established builders.

Ace was 1.65 times Jyoti's size in FY24 at under half the margin. Both Ace and BFW are volume players in auto tier-one turning and vertical machining; Jyoti sells a higher ticket and more defence. On Jyoti's Aug-2026 call an analyst cited BFW as "already set up with 10,000 machine capacity"; management declined to comment on a competitor and called competition "tough" as always. Two better-capitalised or larger domestic rivals are adding capacity into the same demand Jyoti is expanding for.

Peers in distress: an opening, not a threat

Lokesh Machines spent twenty months on the US sanctions list (OFAC, 30-Oct-2024 to 30-Jun-2026) and lost most of a principal auto customer's business in FY26. Premier has had no revenue since FY24. HMT is loss-making with negative net worth. BFW closed its Bengaluru plant. None has exited, but the weaker end of the domestic field is weaker than it was. The share that frees up is at the volume end, not Jyoti's premium end.
