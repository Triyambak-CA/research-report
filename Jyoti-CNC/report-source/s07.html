Section 07

Promoter, governance and capital allocation

An engineer-founder who built a genuine Indian machine tool brand, took it through a lender restructuring and negative net worth, and listed it with its debt cut by an IPO. Since listing: no promoter selling, modest pay, no dilution beyond the IPO. Against that: a credit rating trail the offer document left out, promoter loans at 12-15% whose principal the half-yearly filings do not show, a disclosure on the French case that omitted what French prosecutors were alleging, and a promoter-group pledge that went to 90% of one holder's stake before it was halved.

Background. The Chairman has run the business since the Jyoti Enterprise partnership of 1989, won national small-industry entrepreneurship awards in 2003-05 and has sat on the board of the Indian Machine Tool Manufacturers' Association since Dec-2015 (company deck). The history the prospectus had to disclose is not a clean one: losses in FY20-FY22 and negative net worth of ₹29.7 cr in FY22; lender repayments rescheduled under a Flexible Restructuring Scheme from Apr-2020 to Mar-2023; a debt service coverage ratio below 1.0 in every period from FY21 to H1 FY24; delays in depositing provident fund, TDS and GST "due to cash flow mismatch and lack of sufficient liquidity", 53 late GST payments in FY21 alone; a TDS default spanning roughly a decade, compounded for ₹9.07 cr in Jun-2024 with notices to all three executive directors; and 19 late overseas-investment filings for the French subsidiary, compounded with the RBI in 2013 (prospectus risk factors 10, 13, 20; FY25 annual report). None of this is current misconduct. All of it is the record of a company that ran short of cash for years and dealt with it by paying its obligations late.

! What the pledge is and is not

It is personal borrowing by a 14.45% promoter-group holder who is a Mumbai gem-and-jewellery businessman, not a director; it reached 90.55% of his stake at 30-Jun-2026; it was halved within eleven weeks while the share price rose from about ₹775 to ₹1,050; HDFC Bank's lines appear to have been fully released and replaced in part by four non-bank lenders whose loans against shares usually carry tighter margin calls; and three of the nine Jul-Sep 2026 disclosures reached BSE outside the seven working days SAST Reg 31(3) allows (the 22-Jun and 29-Jun creations, the early-July releases and the 12-Aug creation, all filed 17-Jul or 26-Aug-2026). Whether the NSE copies were earlier could not be checked. It is not a pledge by the Chairman, the LLP or any executive director, and not security for the company's own debt. The main risk is forced selling if the price falls sharply while NBFC margin terms bite: at 16-Sep-2026 the pledged shares were worth about ₹1,617 cr against loans of undisclosed size. Virani also signed a Reg 31(4) declaration on 06-Apr-2026 that he had disclosed every FY26 encumbrance; the FY26 filings could not be retrieved to test it.

The Brickwork actions were non-cooperation actions after the company moved its mandate to Infomerics in Mar-2023, at a time when the RBI had barred fresh Brickwork ratings for bank purposes (Oct-2022).
