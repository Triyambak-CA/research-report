Section 05

Financial analysis: eight quarters and five years

Revenue has nearly tripled since FY22 and the EBITDA margin has moved from single digits to the mid-twenties, all audited. The cash has not followed: three years of operating cash flow total minus ₹99 cr against ₹803 cr of profit, and borrowings are back above where the IPO found them. The Indian business is growing and profitable; the group numbers since Q4 FY26 carry a French subsidiary under investigation.

Margins: where 1,000 basis points came from

The step from 10.5% EBITDA margin in FY23 to 22.5% in FY24 and 27.0% in FY25 came from three things: operating leverage on a largely fixed cost base as revenue rose 96% in two years; a gross margin gain of about 950 basis points as the mix moved toward aerospace and defence and 5-axis; and Huron's turn from losses to a small profit in FY24-FY25. There is a fourth factor the company does not separate. Management says the over-time (percentage-of-completion) recognition on large machines began "18 months back" from May-2025, around the IPO window (notes-09 D5). Unbilled revenue booked under that method rose by ₹366 cr in FY25 alone, 76% of FY25's revenue increase (notes-07). Percentage-of-completion does not inflate a margin, but in a growing book it pulls revenue and margin forward. How much of the FY24-FY25 inflection is timing rather than economics cannot be separated from the disclosures.

Since the Q4 FY25 peak of 30.9% the margin has drifted to 24-27% and 21.4% in Q1 FY27. Management attributes the fall to Huron (standalone Q1 FY27 margin 27.2% reported). Input costs are rising: management told the 29-May-2026 call raw materials were up "2 to 3 percentage" on a fixed-price order book.

Cash flow

CFO to EBITDA: minus 16% (FY24), minus 21% (FY25), plus 10% (FY26; 17% before the ₹32.9 cr of seized French cash). Three-year free cash flow minus ₹846.6 cr. Capex has run at 3.5 times, 8.5 times and 6.4 times depreciation. FY26's turn to positive CFO came from the unbilled balance growing ₹85 cr instead of ₹366 cr (partly because of the Huron reversal) and from a ₹149 cr rise in liabilities, including statutory dues that more than tripled and interest on late income-tax payments of ₹5.80 cr. It did not come from faster collection. Management said on 10-Nov-2025 that first-half FY26 operating cash flow had "crossed Rs. 50 crores"; the filed half-year figure was ₹13.15 cr. Its FY27 guide is operating cash flow "close to 50% of EBITDA".
