Section 09

Regulatory and company-specific risks

One live matter dominates this section: a French criminal investigation into the group's 5-axis subsidiary over exports of dual-use machines, which French prosecutors are reported to connect to Russia via India. Nothing has been proven, the company denies wrongdoing, and the investigation is against the French subsidiary and certain of its staff, not the listed company. Its outcome is nonetheless the largest single unpriced variable in this report.

What management has said

On 29-May-2026 the Chairman described the matter as "a normal investigation", said "more than 50 companies are under investigation in last 3 years" in Europe, called it "a new normal", and said the company had "received a good order from Ukraine right now". Asked directly whether Jyoti's management was aware of "this alleged Russia via India re-export channel during the years 2022 to 2024", he answered, as transcribed:

Verbatim, 29-May-2026 transcript p.15

"First of all, we have never exported anything. The machines come from France to there to here -- to Russia there. By the way, for the input for you that whatever the machines Huron built there, almost 70% goods are coming here from India there. They does just the assembly there." ... "But we have not made any rerouted there. And that we are going to prove out there basically because all the machines, the Jyoti has been full capabilities and Jyoti only has dispatched at '22, '23 there. Then after we have not exported to Russia at all there." Asked whether movement to Russia had stopped: "Correct."

What this does and does not establish. The answer contradicts itself ("we have never exported anything"), and the speaker uses "there" as a verbal habit throughout. The most natural reading is that Jyoti itself dispatched machines to Russia in 2022-23 and has not since; the words can also be read otherwise. It is reported here as the Chairman's words, not as an admission, and no finding is made that Jyoti or any group company breached any law. Indian law did not prohibit exports to Russia in that period; EU export-control law governs the French subsidiary and EU-origin technology. On 07-Aug-2026 the matter was framed as delays in end-user certificates and licences in a "geopolitical situation".

Regulatory dependencies, tailwinds and concentrations

Dependencies

  • Export licences for 5-axis and multitasking machines (India's SCOMET list; the EU dual-use regulation for Huron; German licences for controllers). Their pace now sets Huron's revenue.
  • ECMS incentive of up to 25% on a ₹1,020.65 cr programme, subject to conditions in an approval letter not disclosed.
  • Defence procurement and domestic-content rules for the A&D book.

Concentrations

  • One town: both Indian plants are in Rajkot; the 26-Sep-2026 fire hit a coating facility (company) or the assembly unit (two Gujarati outlets).
  • One customer: ₹470.7 cr, 22.5% of FY26 revenue, unnamed; the annual report calls single-counterparty credit risk not significant.
  • One input: CNC controllers imported from Japan and Germany on a 12-month lead time (Infomerics).
  • One subsidiary for high-end technology, under investigation.

The rating agency's view

Infomerics: IVR A+/Stable and IVR A1 on ₹1,259.11 cr of facilities, reaffirmed on enhanced limits on 10-Feb-2026 and on a "material event" review on 21-Apr-2026, when it repeated the company's no-impact statement almost word for word and said it would monitor. Its rationales name what the company's decks never do: an operating cycle of 211 days with raw material held 228 days and work in progress 112 days (FY25), imported controllers on a 12-month lead time, about 26% of revenue overseas and unhedged exposure, and intense competition. Its downgrade trigger, "substantial debt funded capex impacting the debt protection metrics", was left word for word unchanged in Feb-2026 after the company switched its expansion from "entirely funded by the internal accruals" (Jul-2025) to a ₹300 cr term loan covering about 70% of the project; interest cover of about 11 times was judged comfortable. The rating uses a standalone approach with group support "not applicable", although the parent now backs ₹264 cr of the subsidiary's bank lines. Brickwork, which rated the same lines until 2023, carried the company at BB+ "issuer not cooperating", below investment grade, from Feb-2024 to at least May-2025; that was an information failure after the company moved agencies, not a credit view, and it is absent from the company's own documents (Section 7).
