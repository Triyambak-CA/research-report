Section 01

Industry and sector

India is now the world's fourth-largest consumer of machine tools and a growing one, but it still imports almost three-fifths of what it uses, and that share has risen, not fallen, since Jyoti listed. The prospectus told investors the reverse would happen. Domestic demand is real and funded; the question for an Indian builder is how much of it stays domestic.

Market size: two sources that are not measuring the same thing

The offer document's industry chapter was written by Frost & Sullivan under an engagement letter of 06-May-2022 and was, in the prospectus's own words, "exclusively commissioned and paid for by us" (prospectus p.178). Per the owner's standing instruction it is used here where it is the best source, flagged every time, and set beside the industry association's measured series. The two are not the same universe: F&S sized all Indian machine tools at US$ 2,108 mn for FY23, IMTMA at about US$ 3.0 bn for the same year. F&S may exclude metal-forming presses, which are 16% of FY26 imports; its definition is not stated.

Among them: text says CNC imports were "more than 50%" of consumption for four years while its own chart shows 45.9%; text says "over 60%" of domestic CNC output goes to autos while its own end-user chart shows 41%; its FY23 total does not close (production 1,295 + imports 915 - exports 128 = 2,082, printed 2,108), when every other year in the same chart closes exactly; and the CNC plus conventional split (US$ 1,125 mn + 485 mn) sums to US$ 1,610 mn against a stated US$ 2,108 mn. None of these changes the direction of the story, but they are the reason the report treats the chapter as the company's case, not as data. Full list: notes-11.

Where in the cycle: domestic upcycle, global downcycle

The Indian demand indicators are unambiguously positive for FY26: index of industrial production for capital goods +8.3% (FY25 +5.6%), machinery and equipment +6.5%, motor vehicles +12.5%, other transport equipment +10.2% (MoSPI quick estimates, 28-Apr-2026); RBI capacity utilisation 74.8% in Q2 FY26 against a 72.9% pre-pandemic average (Economic Survey 2025-26 Table I.4); SIAM vehicle production +11.8%; ACMA component turnover +12.7%. Defence production reached a record ₹1.78 lakh cr (+15.6%) and the FY26 defence capital budget of ₹1.86 lakh cr was fully spent.

Outside India the cycle has turned down. Global machine tool production fell in 2024 (Gardner), DMG Mori's 2025 revenue fell 4.8%, and Hurco has lost money for three years (aggregator). Okuma grew 14.1% in the year to Mar-2026. Japanese and German builders facing a soft home market have every reason to push harder into the one large market still growing, and that is what the import data shows. Cycle position for Indian demand: mid-upcycle. Cycle position for the share of that demand going to domestic makers: flat to losing.

Structural shifts

Working for Jyoti

  • Defence indigenisation with money behind it. FY27 capital acquisition ₹1.85 lakh cr, about 75% reserved for domestic industry (PIB, 03-Feb-2026). Machine tools are an input to the supplier base that must build it; no official figure sizes that share.
  • Electronics manufacturing. The Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme (outlay ₹22,919 cr notified 08-Apr-2025) is pulling capex into mobile and component plants that buy vertical machining centres in volume. EMS is 1% of Jyoti's FY26 revenue but 13% of its Jun-2026 order book.
  • 5-axis scarcity. Simultaneous 5-axis machines above set accuracy thresholds are dual-use items under the Wassenaar Arrangement list and the EU dual-use regulation (general knowledge, not from a filing), and their controllers are export-licensed. A rival Rajkot builder told its own Jul-2026 call that its 5-axis export-control paperwork ran to "300 pages" and that it would not make 5-axis its business model (Macpower concall, 30-Jul-2026). That barrier protects Jyoti's high end.

Working against Jyoti

  • China is the largest and fastest-rising import source: 24% of imports in Q1 FY26, 28% in H1, 30% for FY26 (IMTMA, secondary). Management's Aug-2026 description of imports as "primarily from Japan, Europe and South Korea" omits it.
  • Protection is being taken away, not added. The machinery safety technical regulation that would have covered metalworking machines was rescinded on 14-Jan-2026 (S.O. 239(E)). No anti-dumping case on CNC machines was found. The Feb-2026 India-US joint statement commits India to "eliminate or reduce tariffs on all U.S. industrial goods", and the India-EU agreement was described on Jyoti's own Feb-2026 call as taking tariffs on EU machines "to near 0". The Economic Survey 2025-26 argues against protecting capital goods.
  • Export controls cut both ways. The same dual-use regime that bars low-end rivals from 5-axis is the regime under which French customs opened a judicial investigation into Jyoti's own subsidiary in Apr-2026 (Section 9).

Who captures the margin

The margin pool in machine tools sits at two ends: the controller and drive suppliers (Fanuc, Siemens, Heidenhain, Mitsubishi) whose parts are both costly and licence-gated, and the high-end builders with a brand. Jyoti buys CNC controllers, motors, linear guideways and ball screws from third parties and imports a meaningful part of them (prospectus pp.51, 65). Its 25.2% consolidated EBITDA margin for FY26 is an outlier: the global listed set earns 10-14% (Okuma 11.4%, Makino 13.7%, DMG Mori 10.4%), the largest Indian builder, Ace Designers, earned 9.75% in FY24 (CARE, 08-Jan-2025), Bharat Fritz Werner 5.5% in FY25 (ICRA, 29-Oct-2025), and LMW's machine tool and foundry segment about 8% (LMW FY26 annual report). Either Jyoti has a product mix and cost position unlike anyone else's, or its margin is measured differently. Section 6 tests the second possibility; Section 4 the first. The Aug-2026 intimation that Jyoti has ECMS approval for a ₹1,020.65 cr programme to make its own "electronic devices used to build CNC machines" is an attempt to move up this chain.
