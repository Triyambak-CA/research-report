The platform above the company: imports

The spec asks for the competitor that sits above the company in the value chain to be sized. For an Indian machine tool builder that is not a platform but the import channel. At ₹21,628 cr in FY26, imports are 10.3 times Jyoti's consolidated revenue. They grew by ₹2,942 cr in the year, nearly nine times what Jyoti's standalone business added (₹334 cr, derived). The import substitution narrative is real as an opportunity and absent as a trend.

! The contradiction nobody is modelling

Management's own import-origin list leaves out the largest and fastest-rising source. China's share of Indian machine tool imports went from 24% in Q1 FY26 to 30% for the full year (IMTMA, secondary). At the same time the one non-tariff barrier that would have slowed low-cost imports was withdrawn, and trade deals are lowering tariffs on European and American machines. Jyoti's growth in FY26 (standalone +20.7%) kept pace with Indian production (+19%), so it has not lost share yet. The risk is to the volume end of its range, which is where most of its units are sold.

Measured versus projected

Treated as measured: IMTMA FY22-FY24 (primary), IIP, SIAM, MoD FY26 spending and production, RBI utilisation. Treated as directional only: IMTMA FY26 (revised estimate), Budget estimates for FY27, the ECMS cumulative approval total (press), F&S forecasts, the MD&A statement that India will be among the "top 3 machine tool markets by 2030", and every ministerial target, including the ₹3 lakh cr defence production goal for FY29. NITI Aayog's Trade Watch Quarterly editions read (Q1, Q2 and Q4 FY26) carry no machine-tool-level data, only the HS 84 aggregate, which is far too broad to use.
