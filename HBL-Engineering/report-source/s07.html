Section 07

Promoter, governance and capital allocation

HBL is a founder-run family company in which the founder's daughter alone votes 51.3% of the shares through the family trust. The family has never sold a share, never pledged one and never diluted anyone, which is rare and valuable. But public institutions have voted overwhelmingly against almost every family and related-party resolution for five years, and each has passed on the trust's votes; the founder's pay is set at the statutory ceiling and now exceeds the dividend paid to all public shareholders combined.

Who the promoters are

Dr Aluru Jagadish Prasad founded HBL in 1977 to make aircraft batteries India imported, and has been Chairman and Managing Director throughout; he is about 80. The promoter group is his family: daughter Kavita Prasad Aluru (director; Executive Director again from 01-Jul-2026), M S S Srinath (Executive Director from 27-May-2024; filings describe him only as related to Kavita Prasad Aluru and her children), and grandchildren Advay Bhagirath Mikkilineni (non-executive director 2021-2024, resigned "since occupied with pursuing higher studies") and Deeksha Mikkilineni (non-executive director from Aug-2023, profiled as "presently pursuing her masters"). No other listed group company, no political exposure and no promoter litigation was found in the exchange feed or annual reports. Past ventures that did not work: SCIL Infracon (a subsidiary dormant since 2019, Rs 12.48 cr fully impaired, whose former promoters hold an arbitration award against HBL on a 2011 loan), HBL Tonbo Pvt Ltd (a 51:49 JV that never traded, being struck off), and Torquedrive Technologies (the e-truck marketing subsidiary, Rs 3 cr impaired, being struck off).

Reg 44 category-wise voting results in the AGM outcome filings, read from the rendered pages (notes-06). The public as a whole voted 16.1% for the 2026 Executive Director resolution and 7.3% for the 2026 related-party resolution.

  • The CMD's re-appointment on 5% of profits to 2030 passed by 0.30 points. The family trust did not vote at the 2025 AGM; the only promoter votes cast were M S S Srinath's 19,56,920. Without them the resolution gets 72.7% and fails. Why the trust abstained is not disclosed.
  • The counting method changed when it mattered. In 2024, on a promoter-family appointment, all promoter votes were excluded as interested and the public decided. In 2026, on Kavita Prasad Aluru's own appointment and on the related-party authorisation, the scrutiniser excluded the six individual promoters' shares (exactly 2,13,93,317) but counted the trust's 14,24,58,992 votes, which she alone casts. Under the 2024 method both 2026 resolutions fail. For a director appointment the law does not bar interested members from voting, so this is not established as a breach; for the related-party resolution, which cites Reg 23(4) of the listing regulations (which bars related parties from voting), it is a live question unless none of the transactions was material and the vote was voluntary. Nobody has challenged it.
  • The scrutiniser's summary percentages for 2026 are computed on a base that includes excluded promoter shares, so "for" and "against" do not add to 100 and dissent looks smaller in the document most readers see first.

  • Within the law, at its edge. Section 197 allows one managing director more than 5% of net profit with a special resolution; the 2020 and 2025 special resolutions approve salary plus a 5% commission, and the executive directors together (about 5.2%) sit inside the 10% cap. From FY24 the board stopped paying below the approved rate: commission went from about 3% of the base to exactly the 5% cap in the year profits trebled. The commission is uncapped in rupees and runs to 2030.
  • The FY24 annual report gives the CMD's FY24 commission two ways: Rs 19.06 cr in the Board's report, Rs 3.78 cr (the FY23 figure) in the corporate governance report and its annexure: a Rs 15.28 cr understatement in two of the four places a reader would look, in the year the pay quadrupled. The auditor's governance certificate did not catch it.
  • Against shareholders: the FY26 commission (Rs 56.02 cr) exceeds the entire FY26 dividend paid to public shareholders (Rs 3 a share, about Rs 34 cr on the 40.89% float). The ratio of executive directors' pay to the median employee, required by Rule 5, is not disclosed for executives; on the BRSR's male-employee median (Rs 4.80 lakh), the CMD earns about 1,190 times the median.
  • Whether the Rs 11.79 cr of fair-value investment gains (including a provisional AIF valuation) sits inside the commission base, which s198 would exclude, is too close to call from the disclosures (unverified).

Board and key managers

Independent directors: K Venkat Sriram (chartered accountant; also interested as a director in Andhra Electronics Pvt Ltd, from which HBL bought Rs 4.40 cr of goods in FY26: an independent director whose company trades with HBL), Richa Datta (re-appointed in 2022 against 100% of public institutional votes), S Narsing Rao, Aparna Surabhi. Sitting fees only (Rs 0.15 cr in FY26). None of the independent directors' disclosed profiles is in railway signalling or defence procurement (profiles not checked in depth). KMP stability: CFO K Sridharan retired 30-Sep-2022; E Sairam appointed from 31-Mar-2023 (a six-month gap with no CFO named in the feed); Company Secretary M V S S Kumar retired 31-Mar-2023, succeeded by G B S Naidu. Independent director Dr R N Ramnath resigned 27-Jul-2022. No CXO exit since Apr-2023. The CFO sits as HBL's nominee on the boards of Tonbo and Xalten Systems, to which HBL lent Rs 3.50 cr.

Tonbo Imaging: the largest capital decision of the period

In Feb-2023 the board approved up to Rs 150 cr in compulsorily convertible preference shares of Tonbo Imaging India (thermal and electro-optic imaging for defence), for about 35% on conversion, and told the exchange the deal "would not fall within the definition of related party transactions" and that HBL had no shareholding in Tonbo. Five months earlier HBL and Tonbo had formed a 51:49 JV with Kavita Prasad Aluru as a director; the filing did not mention it. HBL paid Rs 86.67 cr by Sep-2023 (screener's "Rs 87 cr acquisition of preference shares") and then stopped: "a higher valuation was expected by Tonbo for the balance, which was not accepted by HBL" (07-Feb-2024). On conversion in Mar-2025 it received 11.13%, now 14.25% after a bonus and split; the implied value of Tonbo rose from about Rs 430 cr on the original terms to about Rs 780 cr on the stake received. In FY24 HBL sold about Rs 68.85 cr of goods to its associates and bought Rs 41.68 cr (screener's "Rs 42 cr purchases"), almost all Tonbo; associate trade was nil in FY25 and FY26. The H1 FY24 related-party statement omitted Tonbo although the money had gone, and Tonbo was classified as an associate only at year end, which let a full year of its profit be booked in Q4 FY24. The auditor has noted for three years that an inventory-policy difference at Tonbo "could not be ascertained". None of this is shown to be improper: Tonbo's revenue held at Rs 370-474 cr after HBL's trade stopped, which argues against round-tripping. It is, however, a related-party investment disclosed narrowly, bought at a rising price, and now reportedly heading for an IPO that HBL has not mentioned (Section 10).

Capital allocation over ten years

  • Debt: borrowings cut from Rs 686 cr (FY15) to Rs 67 cr (FY26), funded from operations. The strongest capital decision of the decade.
  • Dilution: none. Share capital has been 27.72 cr shares throughout; no QIP, preferential issue, rights issue, warrants or ESOP dilution. Reg 32 "deviation" statements are nil returns.
  • Capex: modest and battery-led; about Rs 200 cr into defence cells and a fuze plant ahead of approvals (Section 17).
  • Acquisitions and associates: SCIL Infracon (written off), Naval Systems & Technologies (41%, FY26 profit Rs 11.60 cr), Tonbo (Rs 86.67 cr), Xalten Systems (Rs 6.37 cr for 9.84% in Q1 FY27 plus a Rs 3.50 cr loan), a Rs 60.79 cr Category II AIF, and a marine JV. Realised returns are not disclosed for any of them.
  • Distribution: payout 5-13% in FY22-FY26 (Rs 3 a share in FY26). The policy of raising the dividend "by 5% every year" has been exceeded, but from a very low base.
  • Hoarding: cash and treasury Rs 824 cr at Mar-2026, up about Rs 510 cr in the year.
! Governance verdict

Aligned on the balance sheet (no pledge, no dilution, falling debt, a buying promoter), misaligned on the income statement (a commission at the statutory cap that outruns the public dividend) and on voice (the public is outvoted on every contested resolution, by a trust one family director votes, under a counting method that changed between 2024 and 2026). Add the absence of any earnings call, the FY24 pay misstatement and the narrow Tonbo disclosure, and the governance discount a buyer should apply is real, even though no rule has been shown to be broken.
