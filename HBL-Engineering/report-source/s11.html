Section 11

External headwinds and tailwinds

HBL's earnings are driven far more by one government budget line than by any market price. The commodity and currency exposures are real but second-order; the railway budget, and how the ministry splits it between vendors, is first-order.

Global linkages

  • Exports were 15.85% of standalone turnover in FY26 (BRSR), to 59 countries, mostly NiCad for utilities, oil and gas and data centres, plus defence batteries (Rs 101 cr of the defence segment). North America was "briefly impacted by import tariff uncertainty"; Middle East demand is "pent-up" pending the end of the West Asia conflict (AR FY26 MD&A). US tariff exposure is therefore modest: exports in total are about Rs 520 cr and North America is a part of that; the split is not disclosed.
  • China is both a supplier (lithium cells for every Li-ion product and the drivetrain; permanent magnets, whose supply problem forced the magnet-less motor redesign) and the competitor whose lithium-ion batteries displaced HBL's lead-acid in Indian telecom.
  • West Asia raised shipping costs and, by HBL's account, made imported lithium-ion less attractive against its lead products: a small tailwind that reverses when freight normalises.

The dominant tailwind and headwind are the same budget

Tailwind: Indian Railways capex is Rs 2.93 lakh cr (FY27 BE, +10.5%), signalling and telecom capital Rs 7,500 cr (+15%), Kavach Rs 2,066 cr (+23%), and the ministry is committed to network-wide Kavach. Electric locomotive output targets rise to 1,800 in FY27, each needing on-board Kavach and NiCad pantograph batteries. Headwind: the same ministry controls award size and timing, has qualified more vendors, and on the evidence of Dec-2025 will cancel rather than wait. A pause, a version change or a re-tender (the ~7,000 undelivered units from the 2024 tender "will be floated as a new tender. Date unknown") moves HBL's profit by hundreds of crores in a quarter, as Mar-2025 (electronics -20% margin) and Mar-2026 (14%) both show.
