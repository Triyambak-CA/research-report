Section 14

Valuation

At Rs 808 (29-Sep-2026 close) HBL is priced at Rs 22,406 cr: 27.5 times FY26 profit and about 51 times the latest quarter annualised. The reverse DCF says the price needs FY26's profit to be a floor that then grows about 18% a year for ten years. On management's own FY27-FY28 view (better than FY26, then a Kavach fade from FY29), the next two years roughly earn the price; the decade after them has to be carried by businesses that do not yet have revenue.

The right multiple

Price to earnings on a normalised year is the primary measure, because HBL is net cash (Rs 824 cr of cash and treasury against Rs 67 cr of debt), carries little fixed capital in its largest profit line, and has earnings that swing by a factor of three with one customer's delivery schedule. EV/EBITDA adds nothing that P/E misses here, and price to book (10.1x) is inflated by a year's retained windfall. Trailing P/E is misleading in both directions: it looks cheap (19th percentile of its own five-year range) because the denominator is a delivery peak, while the latest quarter annualised makes it look expensive because most of the 2026 orders had not yet been delivered.

Is it achievable? FY36 profit of about Rs 4,200 cr is five times FY26 and fifteen times FY25. The official record gives Kavach a real runway: only 2,633 route km of about 69,439 were commissioned on Ver 4.0 at 31-Jul-2026, trackside work on 21,794 km and on-board work on 7,190 locomotives was "taken up", and the ministry prices trackside at about Rs 50 lakh a km and on-board at about Rs 80 lakh a locomotive (PIB, 05-Aug-2026). On those ministerial costs the remaining network is a Rs 40,000-50,000 cr programme over many years (Section 20). But HBL will share it with at least three other vendors, management itself expects "sales and PBT could decline from FY 29", and the FY27 budget line for Kavach is Rs 2,066 cr for the whole country (the official spend series lags deliveries, Section 20, so the line understates the envelope, but not by an order of magnitude). The batteries franchise has grown revenue at single digits for a decade. Drivetrains, fuzes and marine propulsion have produced no revenue after up to twenty years. The price assumes the optimistic end of every one of these at once.

Cross-checks

  • Against its own history: trailing P/E 27.9 (five-year median 39.3, 19th percentile); EV/EBITDA 19.4 (five-year median 24.2); P/B 10.1 (ten-year median 2.1). The ten-year median P/E of 38.5 was set when profit was Rs 14-100 cr and every rupee of Kavach was still an option.
  • Against peers: Kernex, the only listed business selling the same product at the same price, trades at 15.4 times trailing profit; HBL at 28.0. Battery comparables trade at 18-37 times; defence electronics at 46-123 times. HBL's multiple is a blend that gives its battery and defence franchise a defence-electronics rating.
  • Sum of the parts: batteries and defence at about Rs 300 cr of normalised profit on 25x (Rs 7,500 cr), a Kavach annuity fading from Rs 700 cr of segment profit (present value about Rs 1,800-2,900 cr depending on the fade), Rs 824 cr of cash and treasury, and Rs 1,000-3,000 cr for the options (drivetrain, fuzes, marine, Tonbo) comes to Rs 11,000-14,000 cr, or about Rs 400-500 a share. The market is paying about Rs 8,000-11,000 cr more than that for a longer and larger Kavach than management guides to.
  • PEG and screener's intrinsic value (0.27 and Rs 670) are not used; Section 19 explains why.

Insufficient data for conviction on the Kavach fade: neither HBL nor the ministry publishes vendor-wise award data, HBL does not disclose unit counts on its 2026 orders, and there has been no earnings call since Jul-2024 at which the question could be asked.
