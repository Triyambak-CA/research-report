Section 15

Pre-mortem and anti-thesis

Assume it is Sep-2028 and the stock has halved. This is the most likely story of how that happened, argued as a short-seller would argue it.

The short case, argued sincerely

  1. One order made the year, and it is gone. Up to about Rs 1,148 cr of FY26 revenue, as much as 35% of the company, came from one CLW contract that ended in December 2025, a quarter of it forfeited. The 2026 replacement orders are twelve-month contracts on the same forfeiture terms, won against more competitors, one of whom has already beaten HBL on price.
  2. The moat was a head start, and it has been spent. HBL delivered more than half of all on-board sets in the 2024 round; it lost the largest 2026 round outright. Kernex now has a bigger CLW order, G G Tronics and Quadrant are close to approval, and the buyer deliberately multi-sources. Management's own guide has Kavach profit declining from FY29, and its guide has already moved once.
  3. The rest of the company is a 20-25% margin battery maker growing at single digits, facing lithium substitution in lead-acid and flat defence revenue. On FY25-FY26 numbers, batteries plus defence earn about Rs 400-420 cr of segment profit. At a battery multiple that is worth a third of today's price.
  4. The new businesses are a decade of dates. Rs 440 cr of drivetrain sales guided for FY24-FY26, none delivered; three sales dates missed; the drivetrain subsidiary being struck off; fuze approvals three years late; torpedo batteries written off. The 0% on-time record for new verticals is the base rate to apply to "substantial sources of sales and profits in a few years" (24-May-2026 letter).
  5. Governance is family control with the public outvoted. Public institutions vote 86-100% against family and related-party resolutions; they pass on the trust's votes, which one family director casts. The CMD's commission rose from Rs 18.95 cr to Rs 56.02 cr, and his re-appointment on 5% of profits passed with 75.30% against a 75% threshold. Surplus goes to associates, an AIF and a thermal-imaging stake bought at a price HBL itself later refused to pay more of.
  6. The numbers are not handled with care. Associates' profit shown gross of tax on the audited P&L face and net in the note, for three years; two versions of FY25 segment assets; results filed in a non-compliant format; a Jun-2025 shareholding pattern identical to March; a Kavach forecast ten weeks before year-end apparently missed by at least 13%, and never explained. No analyst has ever been able to ask about any of it on a call.
  7. Nobody who could defend the price owns it. No active mutual fund holds the stock; FPIs are 5.6%; the register is 3.5 lakh retail holders and index funds. When FY27 disappoints, there is no institutional buyer of last resort.

Thesis-killers (observable, specific)

For the bull case

  • Any forfeiture on the ICF, BLW, PLW or CLW Ver 4.0 orders (deadlines Jan-May 2027).
  • FY27 electronics revenue below Rs 1,600 cr, which would contradict "significantly better than FY 26".
  • HBL winning less than a quarter of the re-tender of about 7,000 on-board sets.
  • No Army grenade-fuze approval by Mar-2028, or a fourth drivetrain slip.
  • A downgrade of the CARE outlook from Positive, or a move of Kavach guarantees into invocation.

For the bear case

  • FY27 profit above Rs 1,000 cr with electronics margin above 45%: pricing held despite four vendors.
  • A second multi-year Kavach programme (trackside at 5,000+ route km a year) with HBL above a third.
  • Army and 155 mm fuze approvals in 2027 and defence revenue above Rs 400 cr.
  • Truck approval by Mar-2027 and a named fleet customer.
  • A payout ratio above 30% or a buyback, ending the associate-and-AIF pattern.

Base rates

Government-programme suppliers that re-rate on a single procurement wave have a poor record once the wave passes: the pattern of order-driven peak margins, a multiple set on the peak, and then multi-vendor pricing is familiar from Indian smart-meter, railway-wagon and telecom-equipment suppliers. The better outcomes came where the supplier used the windfall to build a second product line that reached revenue within two to three years. HBL's own record on second product lines (0 of 11 dates on time) is the most relevant base rate here. Insufficient data for conviction on a precise historical base rate: no clean sample of Indian single-programme suppliers at similar valuations was assembled for this report.
