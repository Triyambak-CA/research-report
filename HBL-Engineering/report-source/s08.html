Section 08

Management guidance scorecard

HBL has never held a quarterly earnings call. It does something unusual instead: it files precise sales and profit numbers in signed letters to the exchanges, and then its chairman says "we are careful not to say it is management guidance". Scored as what they are, 48 dated claims since Feb-2023 give a credibility ratio of 35%. The headline numbers are good when Kavach orders arrive and wrong when they do not; no new-business date has been met on time.

Concall forensics without concalls: language drift

  • The Kavach decline year moves out as it approaches: FY28 at the Sep-2025 AGM, FY29 in the FY26 annual report.
  • FY26 went from "exceptional ... should not be a basis for future expectations" (Nov-2025) to FY27 "significantly better" (May-2026). Both may have been honest at the time, because the order book changed; together they show the guidance is a reading of the current order book, not a plan.
  • Contradictions between annual reports: AR FY25 says artillery-fuze approvals were "obtained"; AR FY26 says 155 mm approval is expected in 2027. AR FY24 rejects selling drivetrain kits to truck makers; AR FY26 plans exactly that.
  • Deflection at the AGM: asked for drivetrain trial status and order timing, the chairman replied "I explained at great length in this MDA and I would only urge you to read it". Asked about the Mittelstand Technology Partners trust ("I think the trust is agreed, right?"), the proposal was later "dropped, because the standard regulations were too restrictive" (AR FY26).
  • Who answers: the founder-chairman, always. The CFO has signed one guidance letter (May-2023); every later one is signed by the Company Secretary. No analyst has asked a follow-up question on the record.

Counter-argument, fairly stated: the Feb-2023 deck carried its own warning ("There is many a slip, between the cup and the lip"); the FY25 misses trace to a customer decision (no Kavach tenders for 30 months) that HBL flagged as a condition in writing; and HBL volunteers its misses in filed letters, which most companies that do hold calls do not. The disclosure is unusual in being both more numerate and less accountable than the norm.
