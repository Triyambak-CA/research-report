Section 05

Financial analysis: eight quarters and five years

For five years HBL was a battery company earning 11-20% operating margins on Rs 1,200-2,200 cr of sales. In FY26 one product, on-board Kavach sets for locomotives, added about Rs 1,330 cr of electronics revenue at a segment margin above 50%, and consolidated profit trebled. Almost all of that came in two quarters. The numbers below separate the franchise from the wave.

! Management called FY26 a one-off, then called FY27 better

The board note filed with the Sep-2025 results reads: "Q2 of FY 26 has been extraordinarily good and management currently does not expect such good results in any single quarter in the next few years" and "FY 26 as a whole would be an exceptional year. Should not be a basis for future expectations of performance" (NSE, 08-Nov-2025). Six and a half months later, the letter filed with the FY26 results says: "FY 27 Management's cautious estimates for sales and profits show that Fy 27 sales and profit, would be significantly better than Fy 26" (NSE, 24-May-2026). What changed in between was the order book: about Rs 2,980 cr of new on-board Kavach orders (Section 18). The FY26 annual report adds that Kavach should hold its share in FY27 and FY28 and that "sales and PBT could decline from FY 29". The first quarter of FY27 did not start that way: profit Rs 109 cr against Rs 143 cr a year earlier.

What the numbers say

Revenue. Screener's "volume versus price" split is not disclosable: HBL publishes no unit volumes for any product. What the segment note does show is that the whole FY26 increase is Electronics. Segment revenue (consolidated, AR FY26 p.248): industrial batteries Rs 1,416 cr (FY25 Rs 1,397 cr), defence and aviation Rs 212 cr (Rs 227 cr), electronics Rs 1,626 cr (Rs 298 cr). Batteries and defence together shrank slightly. Exports within batteries grew from Rs 353 cr to Rs 402 cr, the one bright line in the franchise.

Margins. Gross margin (revenue less materials and inventory change) was 49.3% in FY25 and 58.2% in FY26. There is no raw-material story in that: it is mix, because Kavach electronics carry far lower material cost per rupee than lead and nickel batteries. The FY26 segment results bear it out: electronics about Rs 845 cr on Rs 1,626 cr (about 52%), industrial batteries about Rs 349 cr (24.6%), defence about Rs 73 cr (34.6%), unallocated a Rs 119 cr cost (derived from the identifiable and allocated expense lines, AR FY26 p.248; the note does not print a results line per segment, so these are arithmetic, labelled derived). Note that FY26 electronics profit is already struck after a Rs 92.75 cr Kavach warranty provision (note 40.1) and Rs 36.29 cr of installation charges against Rs 6.94 cr the year before, so adding those back would overstate the margin.

One-offs in FY26. Exceptional charge Rs 31.25 cr, of which Rs 26.49 cr was "unrecoverable costs incurred during development of high-performance batteries for torpedoes" (results note 3, 23-May-2026): a defence development write-off. A Rs 25.49 cr New Labour Codes gratuity charge sits in other expenses (note 4). Commission on profits to the Chairman and Managing Director, Rs 56.02 cr (FY25 Rs 18.95 cr), is a profit-linked cost that will fall with profit (Section 7).

The Mar-2026 quarter. Operating margin fell to 12% because electronics revenue fell from Rs 473 cr to Rs 179 cr while employee and overhead costs did not, the labour-code charge landed, and material cost ran at 51.4% of sales against 41.7% in the December quarter. Screener's 18% tax rate for the quarter is an artefact: filed tax was Rs 15.42 cr on PBT of Rs 67.45 cr (22.9%), and a Rs 11.71 cr share of associate profit (mainly Tonbo) lifted net profit. The only explanation is a letter filed the next day (24-May-2026): "Kavach contracts vary in profitability for many reasons ... beyond management control" plus "provisions for maintenance obligations on Kavach contracts, compliance with labour codes, R&D expenses charged off". There was no earnings call.

Returns. ROE went from about 10% to 37% on flat leverage: the whole improvement is margin, not balance-sheet gearing and only a little asset turn. Incremental return FY23 to FY26: EBIT rose about Rs 970 cr on about Rs 1,244 cr of added capital employed, a 78% incremental ROCE, but that is the Kavach wave. On the franchise alone (batteries plus defence), segment profit barely moved from FY25 while their segment assets rose from Rs 1,044 cr to Rs 1,457 cr.

Screener ratio table. Filed balances: trade receivables Rs 646 cr at 31-Mar-2026 (consolidated) against Rs 386 cr a year earlier, up 67% with revenue up 68%; inventory Rs 562 cr against Rs 534 cr. HBL files only half-yearly balance sheets, so an eight-quarter cycle cannot be built.

The cycle is long, as it always has been for a battery maker that holds nickel, cadmium and lead and sells to railways and defence on long credit. Receivables grew in step with revenue in FY26, which is reassuring, but the absolute rise (Rs 285 cr in the cash flow statement) is concentrated in the railway customer: railways were Rs 1,735 cr of standalone revenue in FY26 against Rs 530 cr in FY25 (Ind AS 115 disaggregation, AR FY26 p.171). The half-year balance sheets cannot show whether the Sep-2025 deliveries were collected before March; the full-year figure says most were.

Cash flow and the balance sheet

Operating cash flow before tax was Rs 1,038 cr in FY26 on operating profit of Rs 1,113 cr, a 93% conversion before tax and 66% after it. That is sound, and it includes Rs 148 cr of non-cash provisions added back (mostly the warranty and the unpaid CMD commission), so part of FY26's cash is timing: the warranty will be spent over 12-96 months. Capex was Rs 150 cr gross (Rs 126 cr net of capital advances released) against depreciation of Rs 41 cr, 3.1 times (Section 17). Free cash flow after capex was about Rs 590 cr.

The balance sheet is net cash: cash and bank Rs 552 cr, mutual funds Rs 186 cr, an Rs 61 cr stake in an alternative investment fund and Rs 25 cr of commercial paper, against Rs 67 cr of borrowings. Contingent liabilities are small (tax and excise claims about Rs 38 cr, 1.7% of net worth), but bank guarantees outstanding rose to Rs 303 cr (performance security on Kavach contracts, note 44.2), and non-fund limits rated by CARE rose to Rs 551 cr. Those guarantees are the off-balance-sheet line to watch: they are callable if Kavach contracts are delivered late or fail acceptance.
