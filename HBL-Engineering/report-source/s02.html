Section 02

Business model and value chain

HBL is a Hyderabad engineering company, founded in 1977 and incorporated in 1983 (it changed its name from HBL Power Systems in November 2024), that exists to make things India otherwise imports. It earns money three ways: it sells specialised batteries to industry, railways and exporters; it sells railway safety electronics, above all Kavach, to Indian Railways; and it sells batteries and electronics to the armed forces. A fourth line, electric drivetrains for heavy trucks and boats, has been in development for a decade and has no revenue.

Revenue mix

Industrial batteries 42.8% (FY25 70.8%) Defence and aviation 6.4% (FY25 11.5%) Electronics (Kavach, TMS) 49.1% (FY25 15.1%) Unallocated 1.7%

FY26 gross segment revenue Rs 3,311.32 cr (AR FY26 p.248). The mix swung on one product: electronics went from Rs 298 cr to Rs 1,626 cr while the other two segments were flat. Unit volumes and product-level revenue are never disclosed.

Value-chain position: a converter of commodity metals into qualified, specified products, with the margin captured by the qualification rather than the metal. HBL claims to be the second-largest industrial NiCad maker in the world and the only Indian PLT maker (AR FY26 MD&A pp.7-8, unverified). NiCad plant ran at 90% in FY25, lead-acid at 54% (CARE). Lead batteries for telecom are in decline as towers move to lithium-ion; HBL refuses to sell lithium packs to telecom because "the margins are too low to justify several years of warranty". Customer concentration is not disclosed; railways were Rs 196 cr of standalone battery revenue in FY26.

Electronics: Kavach and rail signalling
On-board and trackside train-collision avoidance (Kavach, formerly TCAS), traffic management (TMS/CTC)
Rs 1,626 cr · 49.1%
Inputs
  • Processor boards, radios, GPS, RFID tags, cabling, towers
  • RDSO specification and type approval
  • Installation labour, often via EPC partners (Shivakriti, IRCON)
›
What HBL does
  • Own design and software, developed with RDSO since the 2010s
  • Assembles, tests, installs and commissions; carries a defect-liability warranty
›
Customers
  • Locomotive factories: CLW, BLW, PLW, ICF (on-board)
  • Railway zones and EPC contractors (trackside)
  • Effectively one buyer: the Ministry of Railways
›
End use
  • Automatic braking to prevent signal overshoot and collisions across the network

Value-chain position: system integrator and design owner, which is why margins run above 50% in a delivery-heavy year. Railways bought Rs 1,539 cr of electronics from HBL in FY26 (standalone disaggregation, AR FY26 p.171): about 47% of company revenue from one ministry. Revenue is recognised mostly at a point in time (dispatch or delivery); only Rs 263 cr of FY26 standalone revenue was recognised over time. Orders run 12 months (on-board) to 18-24 months (trackside), and late delivery forfeits the balance: 541 of 2,200 CLW sets were cancelled in Dec-2025.

Value-chain position: qualified sole or lead source for several battery types (buyers keep "a second source"), sub-contractor for platforms. Revenue fell 7% in FY26 while about Rs 200 cr went into a defence cell plant and a licensed fuze factory in Telangana. Grenade fuze sales have begun; Army and 155 mm approvals are expected "during 2027".

Development: electric drivetrains
Complete motor, battery and electronics kits for 55-tonne trucks and, through a Cochin Shipyard joint venture, for ferries and tugs
Rs 0 · 0%

In development since 2016, trucks since 2019. Sales were promised from Oct-2026; now homologation by Mar-2027 and pilot sales from Jul-2027, after a supply problem with permanent magnets forced a magnet-less motor redesign. Marine sales "limited until end of September 2027". Green Maritime Propulsion Pvt Ltd (HBL 60%, Cochin Shipyard 40%) was incorporated on 11-Jun-2026 and was not capitalised by 30-Jun-2026; it has ordered batteries for two electric tugs from HBL.

Unknown unknowns

? Dependencies a first read would miss
  • One buyer, one approver. Kavach revenue depends on one ministry buying and one standards body (RDSO) approving each version. Version 3.2 to 4.0 cost the industry a year; the next version change could do the same. HBL now also carries upgrade contracts (Ver 3.2 to 4.0) on its own earlier installations.
  • Forfeiture, not penalty. The CLW contract did not merely charge liquidated damages; it cancelled undelivered sets. Twelve-month on-board terms and HBL's 75% on-time record together make every new order a partial option.
  • Warranty tail. Rs 92.75 cr provided for Kavach defect-liability warranty, to be spent over 12-96 months; field failures on a safety system would cost more than money.
  • Bank guarantees. Rs 303 cr of performance guarantees outstanding at 31-Mar-2026, callable on non-performance.
  • Input metals and China. Cadmium and nickel are exchange-priced and HBL does not disclose hedging or pass-through clauses; lithium cells for every Li-ion product, including the truck drivetrain, come from China, the same supply chain that just cost the drivetrain nine months over magnets.
  • Family control of the vote. Institutional shareholders vote against almost every family and related-party resolution; they pass on the promoter trust's votes (Section 7). Capital allocation is the family's call.
  • Associate capital. Money is flowing into associates (Tonbo Imaging, Naval Systems & Technologies, Xalten Systems) and a Category II AIF, none of which the operating business needs.
