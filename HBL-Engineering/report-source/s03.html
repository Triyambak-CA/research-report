Section 03

Competitive landscape and peer benchmarking

No listed company does what HBL does, so the peer set is built segment by segment. The one that matters is Kavach, and there the picture changed in January 2026: the largest on-board order of the year went to Kernex, not HBL, and two more vendors are in final trials. HBL delivered more than half of all on-board sets in the 2024 round; the 2026 awards on record give it about half, before counting Medha and orders Kernex has not filed.

Kavach: who holds the market now

RDSO approved three original Kavach vendors (HBL, Kernex, Medha; HBL's filing of 27-Jun-2023). For Version 4.0, the version the railways now buy, HBL was approved first (13-May-2025) and Kernex second (14-Oct-2025). G G Tronics said on 30-Jan-2026 that it was in "advanced stages" of final clearance; Quadrant Future Tek said on 12-Mar-2026 it was in passenger field trials. Both already hold on-board orders.

Two readings follow. First, pricing has held: if Kernex's figure includes GST, its per-set price (Rs 69.1 lakh ex GST) is almost exactly HBL's 2024 price (Rs 69.2 lakh), and Quadrant's is close (Rs 67.6 lakh). There is no evidence yet of the price war the MD&A anticipates for FY29. Second, share has moved: in the 2024 round HBL delivered 1,659 of about 3,000 sets supplied by five vendors (its own estimate, 18-Dec-2025). Of the on-board awards on record since Jan-2026 (about Rs 7,000 cr across the vendors above, grossed up to include GST where needed), HBL's are about half by value, and less if Medha's and Kernex's unverified orders are counted. HBL keeps the execution lead, which is what the railways penalised it for missing (541 cancelled sets), but it is no longer the default supplier.

Batteries and defence

In batteries HBL is a niche player beside two scale leaders: Exide and Amara Raja earn 10-11% EBITDA margins on Rs 13,500-18,000 cr of sales; HBL's battery segment earns about 25% on Rs 1,416 cr because it lives in NiCad ("second largest supplier ... globally"), PLT (the only Indian maker), aviation and defence chemistries where volumes are small and qualification is slow. The comparable niche business, High Energy Batteries, shows the downside of that niche without growth: flat sales for three years and margins falling from 37% to 21%. The threat named in HBL's own MD&A is lithium-ion substitution: telecom has already moved, creating "excess Industry capacity for 2V-VRLA batteries" and "pricing pressure extending even to remaining application sectors".

In defence electronics HBL is sub-scale (Rs 212 cr of segment revenue) against listed peers valued at 46-123 times earnings. The fuze market has a scale incumbent: Bharat Electronics signed a ten-year, Rs 5,336 cr electronic-fuze contract with the Ministry of Defence in Dec-2023 (media report; MoD release not opened). HBL's claim that "there is no other Indian company with this capability" in electronic fuzes sits awkwardly beside BEL's contract and Apollo's DRDO technology transfer for a grenade fuze.
