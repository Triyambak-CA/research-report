Section 17

Capex deployment and return on capex

HBL's reinvestment has two very different halves. Plant spending is modest and mostly into batteries and defence, and it has not yet earned anything visible: the segments it went into made no more profit in FY26 than in FY25. The other half is financial: about Rs 390 cr in three years into associates, a fund and treasury paper, including a stake in Tonbo Imaging, an unlisted defence-imaging company that HBL's own filings list among its related parties (Section 7).

Capex by purpose

The company does not split capex by purpose or segment in any filing; the segment note gives depreciation by segment but not additions. What can be pieced together:

  • Defence lithium-ion cells: "HBL has invested about Rs 200 crores for R&D and a modern production plant adequate for foreseeable demand ... technology was licensed from NSTL of DRDO" (AR FY26 MD&A p.9). This is growth capex with no disclosed revenue yet, and it is the main reason defence segment assets rose from about Rs 190 cr (Mar-2024) to Rs 428 cr (Mar-2026) while defence revenue fell from Rs 227 cr to Rs 212 cr.
  • Electronic fuze factory: "A licensed facility for the manufacturing of fuzes has been established in Telangana" (same page). Cost not disclosed. Only the grenade fuze has approval.
  • Batteries: PLT capacity "being doubled" for data centres, and NiCad capacity expansion "necessitating capacity expansion" for FY27 demand. Battery segment assets rose from Rs 747 cr to Rs 1,029 cr in FY26 alone (part of that is working capital).
  • Electronics / Kavach: little plant: Kavach sets are assembled from bought-in boards and radios, and the segment's rise in assets (Rs 351 cr to Rs 626 cr) is receivables and inventory, not plant.
  • Drivetrain: development spending since 2016, largely expensed through R&D and the Torquedrive subsidiary, now being struck off. Not separately quantified in any filing (a gap).
  • Non-operating: none found. No aircraft, head office or land purchase of note in FY24-FY26 (land additions Rs 2-3 cr a year, note 5).

Replacement ratio

Maintenance capex is not disclosed. Gross block rose from Rs 717 cr (Apr-2024) to Rs 841 cr (Mar-2026) with Rs 33 cr of disposals in FY26, and plant and machinery additions (Rs 65 cr in FY26) were more than double plant depreciation (Rs 25 cr). Whatever the maintenance share, the asset base is being renewed faster than it is consumed; there is no technical-debt problem here. The risk runs the other way: spending ahead of approvals in defence.

Aggregate incremental return

FY23 to FY26, EBIT rose by about Rs 970 cr on about Rs 1,244 cr of added capital (equity plus debt): 78%. Strip out electronics, whose profit came from a single order with almost no fixed capital, and the rest of the group added about Rs 1,000 cr of capital (including Rs 823 cr of cash and treasury at FY26) for segment profit that rose from about Rs 410 cr to about Rs 420 cr. On operating capital alone the franchise's incremental return over FY25-FY26 is close to zero, because the new defence and battery capacity has not yet produced revenue. Against a cost of equity of about 12.5%, the franchise is currently diluting returns, and the Kavach cash is financing it.

! Capital-allocation verdict

HBL has a reinvestment outlet in batteries (orders exceed capacity in NiCad and PLT) and a speculative one in defence cells and fuzes. It does not have an outlet for the Kavach windfall at anything like Kavach's return, which is why cash and treasury rose from about Rs 314 cr to Rs 823 cr in a year and why dividends rose to Rs 3 a share (payout about 10%). The honest reading is that FY26's cash should mostly be distributed or held; the record so far is that it is being held and spread into associates, a fund and long-dated development. Insufficient data for conviction on the defence spend until an Army fuze approval lands (expected 2027).
