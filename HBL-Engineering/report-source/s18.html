Section 18

Order book, execution record and growth strategy

HBL does not publish an order book in its results or annual reports. The only series is the one its rating agency prints, and it is enough to reconstruct what happened: a Rs 1,522 cr locomotive order carried FY26, HBL delivered three-quarters of it on time, the rest was cancelled, and the backlog was then refilled in 2026 with about Rs 3,000 cr of new on-board Kavach orders due within twelve months.

HBL's own filing of 18-Dec-2025: "HBL had a 2024 order for 2,200 Loco TCAS units. The last date for delivery was 13 Dec 2025. By that date, HBL delivered and installed 1659 (75.4 %); the undelivered 541 units are deemed cancelled according to the terms of PO." CARE (25-Feb-2026) repeats it. At Rs 69.2 lakh a set (Rs 1,522.40 cr / 2,200, ex tax), the delivered sets are about Rs 1,148 cr of revenue, about 71% of FY26 electronics revenue and 35% of the group's; the cancelled 541 sets are about Rs 374 cr. The same filing says the whole 2024 tender was 10,000 units across five suppliers, of which only about 3,000 were delivered by all of them together. HBL therefore delivered more than half of all on-board sets supplied industry-wide, which is the best evidence of its execution lead; and it still missed a quarter of its own order.

What the 2026 orders imply for FY27

Announced on-board Ver 4.0 orders since January 2026 total about Rs 2,982 cr ex GST, all with twelve-month terms. Add the trackside contracts awarded in 2025 (about Rs 1,940 cr ex GST at award, 18-24 month terms, part already billed) and FY27 electronics revenue could, on paper, exceed FY26's Rs 1,626 cr, which is what the MD&A says ("HBL expects some growth in sales over FY 26"). Against that: Q1 FY27 electronics revenue was only Rs 227 cr, so the remaining three quarters would need about Rs 1,400 cr to match FY26; the CLW Ver 4.0 order was placed in late May, and the 2024 experience shows twelve-month terms are tight. The December filing put visible industry demand at 18,429 on-board units (7,000 re-tendered plus 11,429 in three tenders). If HBL's 2026 orders are priced near the 2024 set price, they cover roughly 4,300 units, about 23% of that demand, down from more than half of actual 2024 deliveries: consistent with more suppliers now approved (Section 3).

Execution record: core versus new

Core (batteries, Kavach)

Batteries: steady Rs 335-390 cr a quarter for three years, NiCad utilisation 85-90% (CARE), exports up, no announced slippage. Kavach: pioneered with RDSO, first trackside orders in 2022, Ver 4.0 approval 13-May-2025, 1,659 of 2,200 locomotive sets by the deadline (75.4%) while the other four suppliers delivered about 1,300 between them. Wayside contracts: the MD&A says "some execution delays in wayside contracts are unavoidable"; the 2022 Eastern Railway contract had a 700-day term, and no completion has been announced. Verdict: reliable at scale for batteries; the best of a slow field for Kavach, but not on time.

New verticals

Electric drivetrain for 55 T trucks: in-house since 2016, focused on trucks since 2019; the FY25 annual report said sales could begin in Oct-2026; now approval by Mar-2027 and pilot sales from Jul-2027 after a magnet-less redesign (nine months' delay). The subsidiary named for it, Torquedrive Technologies, is being struck off (Q1 FY27 results note 4). Electronic fuzes: developed since 2006, grenade fuze approved by MHA, Army approval "during 2027". Torpedo batteries: Rs 26.49 cr written off in FY26. Marine drivetrain JV: incorporated 11-Jun-2026, not yet capitalised. Verdict: twenty years of development, one small approved fuze, no drivetrain revenue. These belong in an option bucket, not a forecast (Section 8 scores the dates).

Growth strategy as management states it (AR FY26 MD&A)

  1. Kavach and TMS/CTC - hold a top-two share of Kavach ("the top two ... are profitable. The third survives"); sell TMS/CTC, now under 2% of the network. Extension of demonstrated capability.
  2. Electric drivetrains for heavy trucks - sell a complete indigenous kit (motors, batteries from imported cells, electronics) to e-truck makers, off-highway users first. Requires capability not yet proven commercially: vehicle homologation, a dealer and service network, and a truck OEM partner, none of which is disclosed.
  3. Marine electric propulsion with Cochin Shipyard. Partly demonstrated (batteries ordered for two tugs); marine certification pending.
  4. Batteries - double PLT capacity for data centres, expand NiCad for domestic and export, Li-ion packs for Vande Bharat and a Siemens Germany development supply, defence cells made in-house. Extension of demonstrated capability.
  5. Defence - electronic fuzes (grenade, 155 mm), sonar and underwater electronics "from FY 28". Capability built; commercial approval pending.
