Official industry and macro data

The official record supports HBL's claim that Kavach demand is large and rising; it does not support the idea that HBL's FY26 sales were drawn from the Kavach budget line alone, and it gives no official support to the specific company claims on fuzes. The platform above HBL

There is no GeM-style intermediary here: the platform above HBL is the Ministry of Railways itself, acting through RDSO (approval), the production units (on-board purchases) and the zonal railways (trackside). Its scale relative to HBL: FY27 railway capex of Rs 2,93,030 cr is about 90 times HBL's FY26 revenue; the Kavach line alone (Rs 2,066 cr) is smaller than HBL's FY26 Kavach billing plus Kernex's. That asymmetry is the whole competitive story: one buyer, a published unit price, several approved sellers. The "competition" is not for customers, who are fixed, but for allocation within tenders, which is decided on price once a vendor is approved.

Contradictions between official data and company statements

! 1. HBL's railway billing exceeds the official Kavach spend

HBL billed railways Rs 1,538.61 cr of electronics in FY26 (standalone Ind AS 115 note). Official all-India Kavach spending was about Rs 814 cr from Mar-2025 to Feb-2026 (PIB, derived from cumulative spend of Rs 1,950 cr to Mar-2025 and Rs 2,763.90 cr to Feb-2026), and the whole FY26 Kavach allocation was Rs 1,673.19 cr, of which only 48.6% had been used by Feb-2026. One vendor's billing cannot exceed the programme's spending. The likeliest reconciliation is that on-board sets bought by the locomotive factories (CLW's Rs 1,522 cr order) are paid from the rolling-stock head, not the Kavach line, and that timing differs between billing and railway accounts. Unreconciled; it matters because it means the "Kavach budget" is not a ceiling on HBL's on-board revenue, but also that the rolling-stock budget, not the Kavach line, is the number to watch for on-board demand.

! 2. "No other Indian company" in electronic fuzes

HBL (AR FY26): "To our knowledge there is no other Indian company with this capability." ECIL states it has been "primary supplier of Electronic Fuzes to the Indian Army for the past 25 years, delivering over 2.2 million units", and Bharat Electronics holds a ten-year electronic-fuze contract. HBL's claim is defensible only in its narrowest reading (every component made in-house); as a statement about competition it is wrong.

! 3. The FY29 decline is not a demand cliff

HBL's MD&A says Kavach "sales and PBT could decline from FY 29" as competition rises (the 2025 AGM said FY28). The official scope sanctioned but unspent (about Rs 17,800 cr) lasts five to nine years at recent spending rates. The official record therefore supports management's stated reason (competition) and contradicts any reading that the market itself runs out by FY29. It also means the decline is within the company's control only through price and execution.

  • Input costs (consistent): the MD&A is silent on lead, nickel and cadmium; the measured series were benign in dollars through FY26 (lead -7.9%, nickel -18.6% against FY24) and turned up in FY27 (nickel +12.5%). Wholesale basic-metal prices rose 10.9% year on year in Aug-2026 (WPI, new base) with fuel and power up 22.9%: the cost headwind for FY27 is energy and the rupee, consistent with the MD&A's West Asia and shipping comments.
  • E-trucks (consistent): the ministry's own description of the e-truck market as "nascent", with nil incentives paid, agrees with HBL's slipped timetable.
  • Kavach demand "flood" (largely supported): the 2025 AGM claim that a "drought of Kavach orders has now become a flood" is borne out by awards and allocation, with two cautions: trackside scope "taken up" fell from 24,427 to 21,794 route km between the Mar and Aug-2026 statements, and FY26 allocation use was low.
  • IBEF: its railway page (seen only in search summaries; the site was unreachable) gives FY26 railway capex as Rs 3,02,000 cr, which is the Budget's internal-revenue figure, not capex (Rs 2,65,200 cr). The Budget figure is used throughout.

Projections labelled P above, including completion dates of 2030-2031 and the 44,000 km five-year target, are directional only. NITI Aayog Trade Watch Q3 and Q4 FY26 were checked and carry no battery or cell line. The Economic Survey 2025-26 lists "large-scale deployment of Kavach" among safety initiatives without numbers and has no defence chapter.
