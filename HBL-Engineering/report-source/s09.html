Section 09

Regulatory and company-specific risks and advantages

HBL has no live SEBI order, no material tax dispute and a credit rating that has risen three notches in four years. Its regulatory exposure is of a different kind: one standards body decides when its largest product can be sold, one ministry decides how much of it is bought, and one buyer can cancel what is late.

Industry-specific regulatory dependencies

  • RDSO type approval. Kavach is sold to a specification (RDSO/SPN/196/2020) that changes by version. The Version 4.0 specification was approved on 16-Jul-2024 and HBL's product on 13-May-2025; FY25 revenue fell 12% partly because of that wait (CARE: "delay in RDSO approval"). Every version change is a revenue gap for the incumbent and an entry window for rivals.
  • Contract terms. Railway on-board contracts forfeit undelivered quantities at the deadline. HBL lost 541 sets (about Rs 374 cr) this way in Dec-2025. Trackside contracts carry a defect-liability period, for which HBL provided Rs 92.75 cr in FY26.
  • Battery rules. Extended Producer Responsibility under the Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022 obliges HBL to collect and recycle a share of batteries sold (EPR provision Rs 17.37 cr at 31-Mar-2026). Lead is a hazardous input; CARE flags "stringent pollution control regulations". HBL cites zero liquid discharge for two decades.
  • Defence qualification. Fuzes must be proof-fired on real weapons at declared ranges; the Army and 155 mm approvals are expected "during 2027". Timing is controlled by range availability, not by HBL. A licensed fuze factory is required and has been built.
  • Vehicle homologation. The truck drivetrain needs approval (expected Mar-2027); marine drives need classification-society certification (sales "limited until end of September 2027").

Regulatory tailwinds

  • Kavach is the national automatic train protection system (adopted Jul-2020), with a rising budget line: Rs 1,112.57 cr (FY25), Rs 1,673.19 cr (FY26), Rs 2,066 cr (FY27 BE) (PIB, Lok Sabha replies).
  • Make in India preference in defence ("Defence has a strong purchase preference for Made In India", AR FY26), and the positive indigenisation lists.
  • Lithium-ion cell import costs have risen (Chinese prices, freight, rupee), which slows the substitution of HBL's lead-acid products (AR FY26 MD&A).

Concentration

Customer

Indian Railways bought Rs 1,735 cr of standalone FY26 revenue (batteries Rs 196 cr, electronics Rs 1,539 cr), 53% of the company; on-board Kavach went to four production units under one ministry. No single customer above 10% is disclosed because the company reports "Railways" as a category, not as a customer. In FY22 the top three customers (Indus Towers, Vertiv, Cummins) were 36% of sales (CARE).

Plant, supplier, geography

All seven plants are in Telangana (Hyderabad area: Shamirpet, Nandigaon, Thumkunta) and Andhra Pradesh (Vizianagaram and a Visakhapatnam SEZ unit) (AR FY26 p.10 and BRSR). Lithium cells and drivetrain magnets depend on China. Exports were Rs 520 cr (16% of standalone revenue) in FY26, with North America "briefly impacted by import tariff uncertainty" and Middle East demand held back by the West Asia conflict.

What changed between rationales: the upgrade trigger moved from growth and return tests (2022-2023) to a single "sustained" 18% margin (2024), then to an absolute Rs 2,800 cr revenue bar (Oct-2025) that only the Kavach year has cleared. The 2023 rationale's warning on associate investments ("the uncertainty surrounding its outcome has an inherent element of risk", on Tonbo) was dropped from the sensitivities after 2023 while the associate stakes kept growing. Risks CARE flags that the company's own documents do not: working-capital intensity (operating cycle 147 days in FY25), raw material at 50-60% of revenue with lead about 60% of battery cost and pass-through "with a time lag", and tender dependence ("TCAS tenders held up in FY25").
