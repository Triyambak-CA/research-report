Efftronics: nothing verified. Progota India (Concord Control) is a "developmental vendor" per a trade-press report (secondary).
3. Peer table below: HBL plus 10 listed peers (Kernex, Quadrant Future Tek, Siemens Ltd, Exide, Amara Raja, High Energy Batteries, BEL, Data Patterns, Apollo Micro Systems, Astra Microwave). Four more screened and excluded (Paras, Zen, Solar Industries, Bharat Dynamics), with reasons.
4. No official vendor-wise Kavach share exists in anything I could open. Parliament answers found give national totals only (secondary reports). Filed on-board loco order values by vendor are listed in section 4 for the reader to see the spread; tax bases differ so no share is computed.
5. 6. Distress or exit: Quadrant Future Tek is loss-making (FY26 EBITDA margin -25 percent, TTM net loss Rs 39 crore). Bharat Dynamics revenue is falling (TTM sales -19 percent per screener 3-yr/TTM block). High Energy Batteries has flat sales and margin compression, with a negative-EBITDA quarter in Sep-2025 and Jun-2026. No peer found exiting.

## 2. Why each peer is in (one paragraph each)

**Kernex Microsystems (Kavach)**: The only other listed original Kavach OEM and the closest like-for-like. Kavach 4.0 approved 14-Oct-2025 (Kernex NSE filing, source-docs/peers/KERNEX_14102025123337_intimation_RDSO_Approval.txt), with accumulated orders from 01-Apr-2024 of Rs 3,346.35 crore incl GST at that date. Share of revenue from rail signalling: not stated in any filing I read; the company is a rail-safety business (UNVERIFIED as a percentage). Small base and heavy execution lumpiness, so multiples are not stable.

**Quadrant Future Tek (Kavach aspirant)**: Listed rail electronics company (BSE 544336) that has on-board Kavach 4.0 orders but no final RDSO approval yet. Its own filing of 12-Mar-2026 (source-docs/peers/QUADRANT_12032026174527_QFTL_-_Kavach_Disclosure_Signed.txt) puts executable order book at Rs 897.88 crore incl GST at 28-Feb-2026, of which Rs 613.04 crore is on-board Kavach for 768 locomotives/EMUs due in FY27, and Rs 284.84 crore is trackside Kavach for 1,109.2 route km from RailTel. Kavach share of revenue today: nothing verified (revenue Rs 153 crore, loss-making). In the set as the likely third or fourth entrant that the AR-FY26 MD&A says will bring competition from FY29.

**Siemens Ltd (rail mobility, context only)**: The listed rail comparable with a mobility division, but not a Kavach vendor. Mobility revenue Rs 830 crore in Jan-Mar 2026 quarter against total Rs 4,620 crore, so 18.0 percent (derived from Siemens analyst-meet deck 28-May-2026, pages 9 and 13, source-docs/peers/SIEMENS_FY26_analyst_meet.txt). The deck attributes mobility growth to the 9K HP locomotive project, so signalling share is nothing verified. Weak comparable; kept because a reader will ask, and because AR-FY26.txt line 456 says Siemens Germany chose HBL as one of two lithium-ion suppliers for its trains.

**Exide Industries and Amara Raja Energy & Mobility (battery scale)**: The two listed lead-acid and industrial battery majors, and the only listed scale comparables for HBL's industrial battery segment (about Rs 1,400 crore a year of segment sales, screener segment popup). Neither is a specialised NiCad, aviation or defence battery maker, so they compare on lead-acid/VRLA pricing pressure, not on technology niche. AR-FY26.txt line 363 states excess 2V-VRLA industry capacity after telecom moved to lithium-ion. Share of revenue from industrial batteries within each: nothing verified.

**High Energy Batteries (India) Ltd**: The only listed specialised defence and aviation battery maker (silver-zinc, NiCad for helicopters, torpedo and missile batteries; customers Army, Navy, Air Force, DRDO, ISRO per trade summary, secondary). Direct competitor for HBL's defence battery line, which  "a second source" at defence buyers. Revenue Rs 84 crore, flat for three years, with margin falling from 37 percent to 21 percent TTM (screener). Shows what a captive defence battery niche looks like without a growth vertical.

**Bharat Electronics (defence electronics and fuzes)**: Only listed company with a verified electronic fuze contract at scale: Ministry of Defence signed Rs 5,336.25 crore for electronic fuzes with BEL for 10 years on 15-Dec-2023 (Free Press Journal, secondary; MoD release not opened). HBL makes electronic fuzes (AR-FY26.txt lines 495-504) and defence electronics. Scale is not comparable (about 8 times HBL revenue), so it is a sector anchor rather than a close peer.

**Data Patterns (defence electronics)**: Indigenous design-led defence electronics with 40 percent EBITDA margin and no debt. In the set because HBL's stated model is filling technology gaps with in-house design (AR-FY26.txt lines 245-250), and Data Patterns is the closest listed operating model, not the closest product overlap.

**Apollo Micro Systems (defence electronics and fuze)**: Closest listed match to HBL's defence segment scale (about Rs 900 crore revenue) and product overlap in fuzes: DRDO approved a transfer of technology for a mechatronic fuze for grenade on 16-Oct-2025 (blinkx, secondary). Order book Rs 1,704 crore as at Aug-2026 (secondary trade report, UNVERIFIED). Levered (gross D/E 0.41).

**Astra Microwave Products (defence RF electronics)**: Included as the fourth defence electronics name for breadth of scale (Rs 1,163 crore); overlap with HBL products is weaker (radar and RF subsystems). Lowest-relevance peer in the table.

**Not in the table but relevant**: Medha Servo Drives (unlisted, third original Kavach OEM, no financial or order data verified). G G Tronics India (Bengaluru; subsidiary of CG Power and Industrial Solutions, BSE 500093; on-board Kavach order Rs 433.34 crore incl taxes from Chittaranjan Locomotive Works announced 30-Jan-2026, one-year execution; not yet RDSO approved for 4.0, per its own press release). CG Power is diversified, so rail signalling share of revenue is nothing verified and no row is built. Saft (TotalEnergies) and HBL's NiCad competitors: unlisted or foreign, nothing verified on Indian shares.

## 6. Incidental findings

- HBL NSE filing of 27-Jun-2023 says three RDSO-approved OEMs. Three years on, the approved set for 4.0 is still only HBL and Kernex among those with a filing; Quadrant and G G Tronics are both in final trials, which supports HBL's own statement that competition rises from FY29.
- Quadrant announced on-board Kavach 4.0 orders directly from railways before final RDSO approval; Kernex and HBL took orders under 3.2 approval and upgraded. The order-award practice means approval status alone does not tell you who has orders.
- The screener premium extract states HBL's order book fell from Rs 4,479 crore (31-Jul-2025) to Rs 2,999 crore (31-Dec-2025), then HBL filed CLW Rs 1,714 crore ex GST on 28-May-2026 and Kernex reported a fall to Rs 3,641 crore by 13-Aug-2026. Order books at both companies are being run down by execution faster than intake, except when a big CLW lot lands.
