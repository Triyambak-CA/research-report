## Answers to the questions asked

1. **Tonbo.** HBL agreed on 13-Feb-2023 to invest up to Rs 150 cr in CCPS of Tonbo Imaging India (thermal and electro-optic imaging for defence; MoD/MHA supplier) for 34.46-35.96% on conversion; agreement signed 04-Apr-2023. It paid Rs 86.67 cr by 30-Sep-2023 for 1,12,156 CCPS (0.01% coupon), stopped because Tonbo wanted a higher valuation (07-Feb-2024). Stake: 15.83% fully diluted (FY24), 11.13% after conversion on 27-Mar-2025, 14.25% (FY26, after bonus and split; rise unexplained). Related party because it is an associate (board seat held by HBL's CFO plus SSHA reserved matters), not through a promoter link; an HBL-Tonbo JV with Kavita Prasad Aluru as director predated the deal. Carrying value: Rs 86.67 cr at cost standalone throughout (no fair-value change); equity-method Rs 97.28 cr / Rs 105.37 cr / Rs 112.63 cr (FY24-FY26). Yes, it is screener's Rs 87 cr FY24 "acquisition of preference shares". Business flow: FY24 sales Rs 68.85 cr to and purchases Rs 41.68 cr from associates (arithmetically Tonbo; this is screener's Rs 42 cr), nil in FY25 and FY26. Tonbo filed a DRHP (confirmed by HBL's FY26 secretarial audit; date 23-Dec-2025 per media, SECONDARY).
2. **Remuneration.** CMD totals: Rs 4.53 cr (FY22), Rs 4.74 cr (FY23), Rs 20.09 cr (FY24), Rs 19.98 cr (FY25), Rs 57.19 cr (FY26); salary about Rs 1 cr, the rest commission. 5% commission was approved by special resolution in Sep-2020 (to 30-Sep-2025) and again on 25-Sep-2025 (to 30-Sep-2030, EDs capped at 10% and all directors at 11% of net profit). FY21-FY23 commission ran at about 3% of profit; from FY24 it is about 5% of the approximated s198 base, which with salary puts the CMD at about 5.1-5.35%, allowed by the special resolutions (INFERRED compliant; the s198 computation is not published). The FY24 jump is profit tripling plus the rate moving to the approved cap; screener's FY23 "Rs 2 cr" is wrong (filed Rs 3.78 cr). No non-executive commission is paid. Dissent: see the table in s.1; public institutions 37.88% against the CMD's 2025 re-appointment, 86.24% against Kavita's 2026 ED appointment, 95-98% against every RPT resolution.
3. **Promoter family.** Board: Dr A J Prasad (CMD, 80), M S S Srinath (ED from 27-May-2024), Kavita Prasad Aluru (NED; ED from 01-Jul-2026; sole voting trustee of the 51.30% trust), Deeksha Mikkilineni (NED from 11-Aug-2023). **Findings (ESTABLISHED unless tagged):**

1. Every family-appointment and related-party resolution since 2022 drew a majority, often near-unanimous, "against" vote from public institutions (95-100% on RPT and s185 items in 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2026). Each passed only because roughly 14.2 crore promoter-group votes (about 51.4% of capital, consistent with the Aluru Family Private Trust block) were cast in favour.
2. **2026 RPT resolution (Item 8, Rs 700 cr omnibus) would have failed without promoter-group votes.** Promoter group cast 14,24,58,992 for. Public votes: 10,37,749 for against 1,32,59,319 against (7.3% for). The resolution cites Reg 23(4) of LODR, which bars *any* related party from voting on such a resolution "whether the entity is a related party to the particular transaction or not". Reg 2(1)(zb) treats every promoter-group entity as a related party. The scrutiniser excluded only 2,13,93,317 promoter votes (the "interested" promoters) and counted the rest. INFERRED: the counted promoter-group votes appear inconsistent with the Reg 23(4) voting bar. Counter-argument: if none of the listed transactions is "material" (above Rs 1,000 cr or 10% of consolidated turnover), Reg 23(4) approval was not legally required, and s188 only bars members who are party to the specific contract. On that reading the vote was voluntary and the bar arguably does not bite. Either way, the governance signal stands: public shareholders rejected it 92.7% to 7.3%. The same pattern (promoter trust votes counted on an RPT resolution citing Reg 23(4)) occurred on 28-Sep-2023 (Rs 500 cr) and 27-Sep-2022. Nobody appears to have challenged it.
3. **2026 Kavita Prasad Aluru executive appointment:** public institutions 86.24% against; public overall 23,00,732 for vs 1,19,96,352 against (16.1% for). The resolution also carries a s188(1)(f) office-of-profit approval for a related party. It passed on promoter-group votes.
4. **2025 CMD re-appointment passed by 0.30 percentage points.** Special resolution needs 75%; it got 75.2998%. The promoter trust did not vote at the 2025 AGM (promoter votes 19,56,920 of 16,38,52,309 held), so for once the public vote decided, and it very nearly failed. The resolution approves commission of 5% of s198 profit on top of salary for 01-Oct-2025 to 30-Sep-2030 (AR-FY25 notice, printed pp 26-27). UNVERIFIED why the trust did not vote in 2025; the trustee change (Section 3 below) is a candidate explanation.
5. **The treatment of promoter votes changed between 2024 and 2026, and the change was decisive.** In 2024 (MSS Srinath as ED, a promoter-group appointment) all 16,11,59,482 promoter votes were excluded as interested and the resolution passed on public votes (79.67%). In 2026 (Kavita Prasad Aluru as ED, also promoter group, plus a s188(1)(f) office-of-profit approval) only 2,13,93,317 promoter votes were excluded and 14,24,58,992 were counted for. Under the 2024 method the 2026 special resolution gets 16.1% and fails; the 2026 RPT resolution gets 7.3% and fails; the 2026 Srinath rotation (ordinary) gets 44.6% public support and fails. ESTABLISHED as to the numbers; INFERRED as to legal consequence. Counter-argument: for a director appointment under s196/197 the law does not bar interested members from voting, so the 2024 exclusion was voluntary conservatism and 2026 is the legal default. That defence does not cover the s188 and Reg 23(4) limbs.
6. The 2026 scrutiniser's summary percentages (`AGM-2026-scrutiniser.txt`, Items 3, 5 and 8) are computed on a base that includes excluded promoter votes, so "for" and "against" do not add to 100 (for example Item 8: 80.53% for, 7.44% against). The Reg 44 table in the same filing shows the correct 91.54% / 8.46%. Minor filing-quality point, but it understates dissent in the document most readers see first.

The 2023 AGM notice lists Tonbo among related parties for the Rs 500 cr omnibus RPT approval, purpose "Financial investment plus manufacturing" (AR-FY23 notice, printed p 39). Relationship basis: associate (Ind AS 24 / s2(76)) via board seat and reserved-matter rights under the SSHA, not via a common promoter. No promoter or promoter relative is disclosed as a Tonbo shareholder or director: UNVERIFIED either way beyond HBL's own filings.
3. **Business with Tonbo happened in one year only, and the numbers say it was Tonbo.** FY24 standalone RPT note: "Associate" sales of goods/services Rs 68.85 cr and purchases of goods Rs 41.68 cr (AR-FY24 note 43.9). Most of the Rs 68.85 cr sales must also be Tonbo (Naval's FY24 material cost was Rs 12.30 cr): INFERRED. **This is screener's "Rs 42 cr purchases FY24".** Associate trade in FY25 and FY26 was nil (AR-FY25 and AR-FY26 RPT notes). Delivered: one year of trade, then nothing.

ESTABLISHED. ## 9. What else a short-seller would pick up (Q8), including the 2025 AGM

1. **A recurring "exceptional" line that is really operating cost.** FY24: Rs 26.86 cr of assets written off plus Rs 3.00 cr Torquedrive impairment, net Rs 28.17 cr (AR-FY24 note 40).
