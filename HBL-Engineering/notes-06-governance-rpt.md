**Findings (ESTABLISHED unless tagged):**

1. Every family-appointment and related-party resolution since 2022 drew a majority, often near-unanimous, "against" vote from public institutions (95-100% on RPT and s185 items in 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2026). Each passed only because roughly 14.2 crore promoter-group votes (about 51.4% of capital, consistent with the Aluru Family Private Trust block) were cast in favour.
2. **2026 RPT resolution (Item 8, Rs 700 cr omnibus) would have failed without promoter-group votes.** Promoter group cast 14,24,58,992 for. Public votes: 10,37,749 for against 1,32,59,319 against (7.3% for). The resolution cites Reg 23(4) of LODR, which bars *any* related party from voting on such a resolution "whether the entity is a related party to the particular transaction or not". Reg 2(1)(zb) treats every promoter-group entity as a related party. The scrutiniser excluded only 2,13,93,317 promoter votes (the "interested" promoters) and counted the rest. INFERRED: the counted promoter-group votes appear inconsistent with the Reg 23(4) voting bar. Counter-argument: if none of the listed transactions is "material" (above Rs 1,000 cr or 10% of consolidated turnover), Reg 23(4) approval was not legally required, and s188 only bars members who are party to the specific contract. On that reading the vote was voluntary and the bar arguably does not bite. Either way, the governance signal stands: public shareholders rejected it 92.7% to 7.3%. The same pattern (promoter trust votes counted on an RPT resolution citing Reg 23(4)) occurred on 28-Sep-2023 (Rs 500 cr) and 27-Sep-2022. Nobody appears to have challenged it.
3. **2026 Kavita Prasad Aluru executive appointment:** public institutions 86.24% against; public overall 23,00,732 for vs 1,19,96,352 against (16.1% for). The resolution also carries a s188(1)(f) office-of-profit approval for a related party. It passed on promoter-group votes.
4. **2025 CMD re-appointment passed by 0.30 percentage points.** Special resolution needs 75%; it got 75.2998%. The promoter trust did not vote at the 2025 AGM (promoter votes 19,56,920 of 16,38,52,309 held), so for once the public vote decided, and it very nearly failed. The resolution approves commission of 5% of s198 profit on top of salary for 01-Oct-2025 to 30-Sep-2030 (AR-FY25 notice, printed pp 26-27). UNVERIFIED why the trust did not vote in 2025; the trustee change (Section 3 below) is a candidate explanation.
5. **The treatment of promoter votes changed between 2024 and 2026, and the change was decisive.** In 2024 (MSS Srinath as ED, a promoter-group appointment) all 16,11,59,482 promoter votes were excluded as interested and the resolution passed on public votes (79.67%). In 2026 (Kavita Prasad Aluru as ED, also promoter group, plus a s188(1)(f) office-of-profit approval) only 2,13,93,317 promoter votes were excluded and 14,24,58,992 were counted for. Under the 2024 method the 2026 special resolution gets 16.1% and fails; the 2026 RPT resolution gets 7.3% and fails; the 2026 Srinath rotation (ordinary) gets 44.6% public support and fails. ESTABLISHED as to the numbers; INFERRED as to legal consequence. Counter-argument: for a director appointment under s196/197 the law does not bar interested members from voting, so the 2024 exclusion was voluntary conservatism and 2026 is the legal default. That defence does not cover the s188 and Reg 23(4) limbs.
6. The 2026 scrutiniser's summary percentages (`AGM-2026-scrutiniser.txt`, Items 3, 5 and 8) are computed on a base that includes excluded promoter votes, so "for" and "against" do not add to 100 (for example Item 8: 80.53% for, 7.44% against). The Reg 44 table in the same filing shows the correct 91.54% / 8.46%. Minor filing-quality point, but it understates dissent in the document most readers see first.

**Findings:**

1. **The deal shrank and got dearer, and HBL did not file a Reg 30 update when it did.** Implied 100% equity value: Rs 417-435 cr at the Feb-2023 terms (Rs 150 cr for 34.46-35.96%); about Rs 547 cr at the price actually paid (Rs 86.67 cr for 15.83% fully diluted); about Rs 779 cr on the stake HBL actually received at conversion (Rs 86.67 cr for 11.13%). INFERRED arithmetic from filed figures. The conversion ratio of 0.7278 equity share per CCPS (fewer shares than CCPS) is not explained in any AR. Counter-argument: the SSHA ratchet (floor / cap valuation) plus later primary issuance by Tonbo can explain dilution; Tonbo's equity rose from Rs 230.13 cr to Rs 490.05 cr in FY25 while its PAT was Rs 72.67 cr, so it raised roughly Rs 187 cr of fresh equity that year (INFERRED). The rise from 11.13% back to 14.25% in FY26, with only a bonus and a split disclosed (both pro rata), is unexplained. The 04-Apr-2023 agreement was for "upto Rs 150 cr"; the curtailment surfaced only as item 5 of a results-day board outcome on 07-Feb-2024, not as a Reg 30 disclosure of a material change to an announced agreement. ESTABLISHED that no separate filing exists in the NSE index (`nse-ann-all.txt`).
2. **Tonbo was a related party in substance from day one of the plan, which the Feb-2023 disclosure did not mention.** HBL Tonbo Pvt Ltd, a 51:49 JV between HBL and Tonbo, was incorporated on 12-Sep-2022 with Kavita Prasad Aluru as a director (AR-FY23 note 9.3 and RPT note), five months before the 13-Feb-2023 filing said HBL had no shareholding in Tonbo and the deal was not an RPT. Both statements were literally true (the JV is HBL's subsidiary, not a Tonbo holding), but the existing JV was not disclosed. The JV never traded and is being struck off. HBL's CFO Sairam Edara sits on Tonbo's board as HBL's nominee (AR-FY24 note 43.9). The 2023 AGM notice lists Tonbo among related parties for the Rs 500 cr omnibus RPT approval, purpose "Financial investment plus manufacturing" (AR-FY23 notice, printed p 39). Relationship basis: associate (Ind AS 24 / s2(76)) via board seat and reserved-matter rights under the SSHA, not via a common promoter. No promoter or promoter relative is disclosed as a Tonbo shareholder or director: UNVERIFIED either way beyond HBL's own filings.
3. **Business with Tonbo happened in one year only, and the numbers say it was Tonbo.** FY24 standalone RPT note: "Associate" sales of goods/services Rs 68.85 cr and purchases of goods Rs 41.68 cr (AR-FY24 note 43.9). The only other associate, Naval Systems & Technologies, had total income of Rs 29.11 cr in FY24 (AR-FY24 consolidated note 10A), so it cannot have sold HBL Rs 41.68 cr of goods: ESTABLISHED that the purchases were from Tonbo. Most of the Rs 68.85 cr sales must also be Tonbo (Naval's FY24 material cost was Rs 12.30 cr): INFERRED. **This is screener's "Rs 42 cr purchases FY24".** Associate trade in FY25 and FY26 was nil (AR-FY25 and AR-FY26 RPT notes). The Feb-2023 deck promised "potential sales to Tonbo could reach a few hundred crores a year by FY 28" and that HBL would bid large contracts itself "with the purchase of necessary parts from Tonbo" (`PPT-b.txt` slides A11-A12). Delivered: one year of trade, then nothing.
4. **The H1 FY24 related-party disclosure omitted Tonbo even though the cash had gone.** HBL's Reg 23(9) RPT statement as on 30-Sep-2023 (`nse/04-Nov-2023_...txt`, RPT table) shows columns for subsidiaries, Naval Systems and KMP only; no Tonbo investment, no Tonbo sales. The same filing's cash flow shows Rs 86.67 cr paid in H1. The Q2 and Q3 FY24 consolidated results list only Naval as an associate and carry Tonbo as a plain investment. Tonbo was reclassified as an associate only at the FY24 year-end, and a full year of Tonbo profit share was booked in Q4 FY24: consolidated "Share of profit of associates" Rs 15.67 cr in Q4 FY24 against Rs 0.54 cr in Q3 FY24 (`nse/27-May-2024_...txt`, consolidated results line 10). ESTABLISHED. Why it matters: the associate call is a judgement (15.83% is below the 20% presumption); making it at year-end let HBL book a full year of Tonbo profit, including any pre-acquisition months, in one quarter. Ind AS 28 allows only post-acquisition profits: INFERRED overstatement of a few crore at most, since the tranche dates are not disclosed.
5. **The FY24 and FY25 consolidated P&L face books associates' share of profit BEFORE tax.** FY24: P&L line VIII "Share in profit of associates" Rs 17.69 cr = Naval 41% of PBT (Rs 2.95 cr) + Tonbo 15.83% of PBT (Rs 14.72 cr) (AR-FY24 note 10A). The auditor's own report on the FY24 results says "the Group's share of net profit after tax of Rs 1,282.42 lakhs" (`nse/27-May-2024_...txt`, consolidated audit report), i.e. Rs 12.82 cr. FY25: face Rs 13.91 cr (AR-FY25 directors' report table and P&L) vs note 9A Rs 10.19 cr on PAT basis; Rs 13.91 cr = 41% x Naval PBT + 11.13% x Tonbo PBT exactly. The associates' tax appears to have been pushed into group tax expense (standalone tax Rs 91.38 cr vs consolidated Rs 96.44 cr in FY24). ESTABLISHED that face and note disagree by Rs 4.87 cr (FY24) and Rs 3.72 cr (FY25). Net profit is probably unaffected; consolidated PBT and "share of associates" are overstated, and the tax line is understated as a rate. Ind AS 28 para 10 and Ind AS 1 require the post-tax share. The FY25 AR silently restated the FY24 comparative in the note (Rs 12.81 cr) without an Ind AS 8 disclosure.
6. **The auditor could not quantify an accounting-policy difference in Tonbo, three years running.** "There is a difference in accounting policy relating to inventory valuation followed by the Company and Tonbo. As of this reporting period, the financial impact of this difference ... could not be ascertained and consequently, no adjustments have been made." (AR-FY24, AR-FY25, AR-FY26 consolidated associate notes). Ind AS 28 para 35-36 require uniform policies. Impact is bounded by HBL's share (7.25 cr of profit in FY26) so it is small, but it is an unresolved note the auditor has accepted each year.
7. **Tonbo's revenue went from Rs 21 cr (FY22) to Rs 432 cr (FY24)** in the year HBL invested and traded with it, then fell to Rs 370 cr in FY26. HBL's Rs 68.85 cr of FY24 sales equals about 31% of Tonbo's FY24 material cost of Rs 219.93 cr. INFERRED only; the pattern (cash in, goods out, one-year spike, trade then stops) is what a short-seller would call circular. Counter-argument: HBL announced the contract-manufacturing plan publicly before investing; Tonbo's revenue held above Rs 370 cr in FY25-FY26 with no HBL trade, which is inconsistent with revenue that depended on HBL. On the filings available, round-tripping is not supported, and the claim should not be made.
8. **Tonbo IPO (SECONDARY).** Media reports (Inc42, Business Standard, 23-Dec-2025) say Tonbo Imaging India Ltd filed a DRHP with SEBI on 23-Dec-2025 for an offer-for-sale-only IPO of up to 1.81 crore shares (face value Rs 2), with CEAQ Technologies (two entities, including CEAQ Technologies Pte Ltd) selling up to 1.5 crore shares, and FY25 revenue Rs 469.1 cr / PAT Rs 72.8 cr; a later Inc42 headline says SEBI cleared it. HBL is not reported as a selling shareholder. Link to HBL's filings: HBL's "Investee Company" in the FY26 AR is CEAQ Technologies Pte Ltd (100 preference shares of USD 2.86, carried at nil), which replaced "Tonbo Imaging Pte Ltd, Singapore" of earlier years, i.e. HBL's nominal Singapore holding is in the main selling shareholder. Primary confirmation that the DRHP exists: HBL's FY26 secretarial audit report (MR-3, AR-FY26 printed p approx 58, text line 3111) says "BSE while considering the DRHP filed by an Associate Company of HBL, asked the Company for payment of ... outstanding SOP fines" (Rs 27,140 incl. GST, for Dec-2013 Reg 27(2), Mar-2014 and Sep-2020 Reg 23(9)); i.e. HBL's own old non-compliance fines surfaced only because Tonbo filed. HBL made no Reg 30 disclosure about the associate's DRHP (none in `nse-ann-all.txt`), and the FY26 AR mentions it only in that secretarial-audit remark. HBL carries Tonbo at Rs 86.67 cr standalone and Rs 112.63 cr consolidated; the IPO price will be the first market test. Not verified against the DRHP itself: UNVERIFIED as to HBL's disclosed shareholding and any lock-in.

**Tests and findings:**

1. **Why commission jumped from about Rs 3.8 cr to Rs 19 cr in FY24 (screener's "Rs 2 cr to Rs 19 cr").** Two things moved together: standalone PBT nearly tripled (Rs 126.33 cr to Rs 355.17 cr), and the Board/NRC stopped paying below the approved cap. The 2020 special resolution already allowed 5% of profit; FY21-FY23 commission was about 2.9-3.3% of the base (FY21 Rs 0.72 cr on PBT before exceptional Rs 24.84 cr, AR-FY22 line 5497 comparative). From FY24 it is about 5.0% every year. ESTABLISHED (figures) / INFERRED (that the rate is set at the cap; the NRC's basis is not published). Screener's "Rs 2 cr" for FY23 is wrong: the filed FY23 commission is Rs 3.78 cr (AR-FY23 RPT note 43.8: 377.60 lakh). SECONDARY vs primary disagreement recorded.
2. **Section 197 single-MD 5% limit: at or just over the line, covered by the special resolutions.** CMD total (salary + commission) is about 5.35% (FY24), 5.22% (FY25) and 5.08% (FY26) of my approximated s198 base. s197(1) second proviso allows more than 5% for one MD with a special resolution; the 2020 and 2025 special resolutions approve salary plus 5% commission, which necessarily exceeds 5% in total, so the package is approved. Weak point: both resolutions also say the pay is "subject to the overall limits laid down in Section 197", which is circular. INFERRED compliant; not a breach finding. EDs together (CMD + Srinath) about 5.2% in FY26, inside 10%; all directors inside 11%. Non-executive commission (1%/3% limits): none paid; IDs get sitting fees only (Rs 0.15 cr in FY26). Two women IDs (Preeti Khandelwal, Richa Datta) waived sitting fees in FY23-FY24.
3. **LODR Reg 17(6)(e)** (promoter ED paid more than Rs 5 cr or 2.5% of net profit needs a special resolution): triggered from FY24; covered by the 2020 special resolution (term to 30-Sep-2025) and the 2025 special resolution. INFERRED compliant.
4. **s198 base not disclosed, and notional gains may be inside it.** FY26 standalone P&L includes Rs 11.79 cr "Gains/(losses) on fair valuation or sale of investments", including AIF fair-value gains measured on a provisional NAV (AR-FY26 note 10, "Investment in AIF"). s198(3) excludes unrealised fair-value gains from the commission base. On my approximation the FY26 commission is 4.97% of a base that includes them and about 5.02% of a base that excludes them. Too close to call: UNVERIFIED. It matters because the commission is uncapped in rupees and runs to 2030.
5. **The FY24 annual report states the CMD's FY24 commission two different ways.** Board's report Rule 5 table: commission Rs 1,905.63 lakh, total Rs 2,008.98 lakh (AR-FY24 lines 2528-2545). CG report "Directors' remuneration for the year 2023-24": commission Rs 377.58 lakh, total Rs 480.93 lakh (AR-FY24 line 3933, CG report printed p 75), repeated in annexure "g. Remuneration to Managing Director" (AR-FY24 line 3154). Rs 377.58 lakh is the FY23 figure. A Rs 15.28 cr understatement of the promoter's pay in two of the four places a reader would look, in the year the pay quadrupled. ESTABLISHED. The auditor's CG certificate did not catch it.
6. **Rule 5(1)(i) ratio of each director's pay to the median employee is not disclosed for executive directors.** Item (a) in every year FY24-FY26 covers only non-executive directors ("Not Applicable as none of the Non-Executive Director was paid any remuneration"). On the FY26 BRSR male-employee median of Rs 4.80 lakh (AR-FY26 BRSR Principle 5 table), the CMD's Rs 57.19 cr is about 1,190 times the median employee and about 2,600 times the median male worker (Rs 2.18 lakh). INFERRED ratio; ESTABLISHED omission. The same BRSR table gives the Board's median pay as Rs 1.09 cr, which is only possible if the Rs 56.02 cr commission is left out.
7. **Scale against shareholders.** FY26 dividends: interim Rs 2 (declared 07-Feb-2026) + final Re 1 (approved 26-Sep-2026) = Rs 3 a share, about Rs 83.16 cr in total, of which the 40.89% public float receives about Rs 34.0 cr. The CMD's FY26 commission alone (Rs 56.02 cr) exceeds the entire dividend paid to public shareholders. INFERRED arithmetic from filed figures. Commission payable at 31-Mar-2026: Rs 61.76 cr (AR-FY26 RPT note), so FY25 and FY26 commission are paid in arrears.
8. **The 2025 explanatory statement is boilerplate.** The re-appointment of an MD then aged 79 (80 in the FY26 AR) needs a special resolution with a justification in the explanatory statement (s196(3)(a) proviso). The statement (AR-FY25 printed pp 38-40) gives no age justification beyond the Schedule V annexure's "rich and extensive experience", gives no estimate of the rupee value of 5% commission, and carries Schedule V text written for loss-making companies ("Inevitable market conditions are the key factors for inadequacy in profits") in a year of Rs 362 cr PBT. It copies the 2020 annexure almost word for word (AR-FY20 printed p 28). INFERRED weak disclosure; not a breach finding.
