Core Business Thesis

Crizac is an asset-light B2B international student recruitment platform connecting students, agents and global higher-education institutions. Its business is recruitment infrastructure, application processing, student matching and enrolment-linked monetisation, not university teaching.

The industry remains structurally attractive, but the near-term environment has shifted from simple volume growth to compliance-led growth.

Current Market Signal

UK remains a large corridor, while Germany, France and Ireland provide meaningful Europe ex-UK diversification. Policy, visa quality, and destination mix matter more than raw application growth.

Crizac's opportunity is strongest where it can redirect demand across destinations and improve quality screening.

What Is Working

  • Revenue, EBITDA and PAT scaled in FY26 with improved EBITDA margin.
  • Capital-light model produced strong returns with almost no debt.
  • Free cash flow remained positive after capex.
  • Applications processed increased to 3.94 lakh and active agents reached 5,389.
  • Revenue per enrolment improved to about Rs 4.22 lakh.

What Needs Proof

  • Applications grew much faster than enrolments.
  • Enrolments per active agent weakened.
  • Operating cash flow was positive but lower than FY25 despite higher profit.
  • Receivable and payable discipline must remain strong as revenue grows.
  • Acquisition-linked goodwill and other current financial assets need tracking.

Promoter and Management Read

The promoter and management assessment should be read with the business model: Crizac's strength depends on institution relationships, agent governance, destination strategy and disciplined cash conversion.

The IPO being a pure offer for sale means the thesis should be built around organic cash generation and execution durability rather than fresh-capital deployment.

Key Risks, Concerns and Watchpoints

  • Visa and destination-country policy risk.
  • University concentration and bargaining power of top institutional partners.
  • Non-exclusive agents and potential agent-productivity dilution.
  • Low-quality application volume hurting conversion, reputation and collections.
  • Data, documentation, compliance and student-outcome risks.
