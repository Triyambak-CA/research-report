An 85% spread between the pack's own two methods (Rs 148 vs Rs 278) means at least one of them is wrong. The sections below judge each on its own logic.

Verdict

The DCF is justified - as a conservative base case. Its assumptions are internally coherent and anchored to management's own disclosures: the 24% forecast EBITDA margin is management's stated sustainable band (24-25%), correctly refusing to annualise the FX-aided FY26 print of 27.1%; the growth taper to an 11.9% seven-year CAGR is consistent with conversion falling from 7.9% to 6.3% and FY27 guidance being deferred. Its bias is mildly punitive (risk charged in both cash flows and a 2% company-specific premium); on balanced settings the same engine gives Rs 160-175.

The CCM as constructed is not justified. Its Rs 245-280 band is produced by valuing Crizac at the multiples of two non-comparable Indian internet franchises while neutralising the only true peer, applying zero discount for concentration, moat and track record, and anchoring the selected band to the promoters' own IPO offer price. A coherent CCM from the same peer set lands at Rs 140-185 - converging with the DCF, not contradicting it.

Where value sits: weight the DCF 75-80% and use only a properly discounted CCM as cross-check. Fair value today is approximately Rs 150-185, centre near Rs 165. At Rs 199 the stock is fairly-to-fully priced with no margin of safety on conservative assumptions - not 24% undervalued as the CCM note concludes.

Justified  DCF - Rs 148

Management-anchored margin, evidence-based taper, clean mechanics, mid-year convention, all tie-outs pass. Read it as the floor of a Rs 148-175 band rather than a point estimate.

Not justified  CCM - Rs 245-280

Method failure, not math failure: wrong peer weighting, no comparability discount, circular IPO-price support. Usable only as an undiscounted bull-case reference.

Coherent  Market - Rs 199

11.9x EV/EBITDA = a 26% discount to the Indian-platform median and 2.3x the direct peer IDP. The market is applying exactly the discount structure the CCM refused to apply.

Why the DCF Passes and the CCM Fails

Judgment against the research pack's own evidence base: FY26 filings, four quarterly concalls, the operational-quality report and the red-flags report.

Test: are the assumptions consistent with the pack's own findings?

DCF - What It Gets Right

  • Margin honesty: 24% EBITDA margin is management's own sustainable band (Q2/Q4 concalls) rather than FY26's FX-aided 27.1%.
  • Growth realism: 18% tapering to 6% (11.9% CAGR) respects the pack's core finding - applications +43%, enrolments +13.8%, conversion down to 6.3%, guidance deferred.
  • Mechanics: FCFF, mid-year convention, 25.17% tax, terminal g 4% < WACC, TV only 50.2% of EV, all tie-outs pass.
  • Risk pricing: a 2% company-specific premium for ~97% UK concentration and policy exposure is a legitimate small-cap overlay.

DCF - Fair Critiques (none fatal)

  • Conservatism stacking: margin haircut + growth taper + 2% CSP charges some risk twice; beta 1.0 (light for a policy-cyclical name) partially offsets. Base is floor-ish.
  • Terminal capex 2.0% vs D&A 2.5%: a growing perpetuity should not depreciate more than it invests (~Rs 2.5/share).
  • Cosmetics: broken sensitivity-grid corner columns; equity bridge (Rs 196.0 cr) not harmonised with the CCM's Rs 133.8 cr.

CCM - Four Structural Flaws

FlawEvidence and consequence
1. The median IS IndiaMARTIn a three-company set the median on both metrics (4.84x / 15.98x) is literally IndiaMART's row. "Median result Rs 278" means rating an 11-month-listed microcap with ~97% UK concentration, 66% of revenue from ten universities and non-exclusive relationships at the multiple of a decade-listed, diversified, moated franchise. The pack's own red-flags report says this business "should not be valued as a clean, diversified, high-moat platform" - the CCM does exactly that.
2. The only true peer is neutralisedIDP Education - the one listed student-placement comparable - trades at 0.90x revenue / 5.19x EBITDA, implying Rs 61-91 per share. The note sets it aside as a "policy-led downturn"; but that downturn is the base-rate evidence of what tightening destination policy does to placement economics - the very risk Crizac holds in concentrated form. Dropping the direct peer while keeping the marketplace proxies can only produce a high number.
3. Zero comparability discountThe note itself lists Crizac's gaps versus the proxies (size, diversification, listing history, moat, commission-intermediation economics - management's own "do not compare us to high-operating-leverage platforms") and then applies no discount at all. Standard practice for this gap is 30-40%.
4. Circular IPO-price anchorThe Rs 245-280 band is "supported by the IPO-price multiple reference". Rs 245 was the promoters' ask in a pure offer-for-sale - a seller's number, not market evidence; the stock has traded below it since listing. The support is the thing the analysis was meant to test.

Market-Coherence Check

  • At Rs 199 the market pays 11.9x FY26 EV/EBITDA: a 26% discount to the platform median (15.98x) and 2.3x the direct peer IDP (5.19x).
  • That is the right intermediate rating for a concentrated, newly-listed, policy-exposed but fast-growing platform - the market is pricing the discount the CCM refused to apply.
  • The CCM reads the gap to Rs 245-280 as mispricing; the simpler reading is that its band is anchored and the market's relative pricing is rational.
  • The DCF workbook's own Conclusion sheet weights DCF 100% at Rs 148.46 - consistent with this verdict.

Final View

  • Method weighting: DCF 75-80%; discounted-CCM cross-check 20-25%; the as-published CCM band 0%.
  • Fair value today: ~Rs 150-185, centre near Rs 165.
  • At Rs 199: fairly-to-fully priced; no margin of safety on conservative assumptions. A fresh-purchase case needs either a lower entry or FY27 proof of the monitorables.
  • Judgment in one line: the DCF earns its number; the CCM asserts its number.
